## What must stay consistent (the non-negotiables)
- Terminology: every domain term must use exactly the name and definition from the glossary — no synonyms, no redefinitions mid-course
- Learner address: second-person ('you') vs. third-person must not switch; formality register must not drift between modules
- Objective traceability: every lesson must map to exactly one module objective; every module objective must map to exactly one course objective — no orphaned objectives, no double-coverage
- Scaffolding integrity: no module may use a concept, formula, or technique before the lesson that introduces it (per the prerequisite chain)
- Assessment format: formative checks must match the approved item types and pass thresholds from the assessment plan — no module may invent a new format
- Example and analogy ownership: a registered example may not be re-explained from scratch in a later module; it may only be referenced
- Difficulty trajectory: Bloom level of activities and assessments must follow the ramp defined in the bible — no summative-level tasks appearing in introductory lessons
- Capstone alignment: the capstone project brief must be answerable using skills and knowledge taught across the full course — no required concept may exist only outside the course content
