## What must stay consistent (the non-negotiables)
- Series thesis statement — the central claim must not be subtly restated to mean something different in later parts
- Named examples and case studies — if a company, person, or product is introduced as evidence for a claim in Part 2, it cannot be used as counter-evidence in Part 5
- Definitions of coined or special terms — a term defined one way in Part 1 must carry exactly that meaning in every subsequent reference
- Voice and POV — sentence length norms, use of second-person 'you', level of formality, and tone descriptors must not drift across installments
- Recurring structural elements — if Part 1 ends with a 'Key takeaway' block, every part ends with one; if a running analogy is introduced, it must be maintained and advanced
- Author's stated position on any contested claim — the series cannot appear to argue both sides of the same question across different installments
- Forward-references and their payoffs — every 'we will explore this in a future installment' promise must be fulfilled in the designated part
