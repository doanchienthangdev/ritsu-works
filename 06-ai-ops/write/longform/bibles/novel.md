# {{Title}} — novel bible

> **Purpose:** Single locked-before-split source of truth that every parallel chapter-drafting agent reads before producing any prose; prevents character drift, world inconsistency, timeline paradoxes, and tonal divergence across chapters written simultaneously.

> **Status:** DRAFT → **LOCKED** (no part drafting starts until locked; version-stamped).

## characters
_Full cast roster: name, role, physical description (hair/eyes/build/distinguishing marks), speech patterns and verbal tics, core wound + motivation + want vs need, arc endpoint, relationships to other characters, secrets they hold. Every agent drafting any chapter reads this in full before writing dialogue or action._

## world_and_rules
_Physical setting geography, political/social structure, technology or magic system with hard limits and costs, sensory texture (smells, sounds, light quality per location), what is impossible in this world. Rules stated here cannot be violated in any chapter._

## timeline
_Master chronology: anchored calendar or relative-day numbering for every scene in every chapter, plus backstory events that predate chapter 1. Prevents a character being in two places at once or a wound healing too fast._

{{fill}}

## pov_and_voice
_Whose POV governs which chapters (first/third limited/omniscient decision), head-hopping rules, sentence rhythm target (long and sinuous vs short and punchy), tense (past/present), and 5–10 forbidden words or cliches that break the intended voice._

## plot_and_arc
_Scene-by-scene beat sheet for the full novel: inciting incident, act breaks, midpoint reversal, dark night, climax, resolution. Each chapter assigned a dramatic function (establish, complicate, turn, rest, escalate) so parallel drafters know exactly what narrative work their chapter must accomplish._

## style_sheet

## glossary
_Every invented proper noun (place, person title, faction, artifact, concept) with pronunciation guide, meaning, and first-appearance chapter so agents do not invent alternate spellings mid-draft._

{{fill}}

## What must stay consistent (the non-negotiables)
- Character names and spellings (including nicknames introduced at first use)
- Physical descriptions of characters — eye color, scars, dominant hand, height relative to others
- Timeline and chronology — no scene can occur on a day that contradicts the master timeline
- World rules and their limits — a magic system's cost cannot silently disappear in a later chapter
- Character knowledge state — a character cannot act on information they have not yet learned within the story
- Tense and POV assignment — if chapter 3 is third-person limited from Mara's POV, no batch agent may draft it as omniscient
- Thematic motifs — a symbol introduced to carry a theme (e.g., a broken compass representing lost direction) must recur in the chapters designated in the bible, not disappear after chapter 2
- Emotional arc trajectory — a character in grief at the end of chapter 7 cannot open chapter 8 as inexplicably cheerful without a bridging scene
