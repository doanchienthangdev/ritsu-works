# Paul Graham — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Graham wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| **Open from your own noticing** — "I realized recently that…", a flat surprising declarative, or a familiar thing you're about to turn over | Open with a thesis statement, a hooky hype line, or a generic throat-clearing intro |
| **Write to figure it out** — follow the most interesting thread; let the reader watch you think; arrive somewhere you didn't fully see at the start | Take a position and defend it like a debater; argue to win instead of to find what's true |
| **Aim for surprise** — build the piece around the thing that surprised *you*; tell the reader something true they didn't already know | Tell the reader something they already knew; publish a piece with no news in it |
| **Write like you talk** — plain spoken words, contractions, the friend-test; read it aloud and cut the clank | Use jargon, buzzwords, or business/academic language; write a sentence you wouldn't say to a friend |
| **Coin the handle** — give a real pattern a short, plain, sticky name the reader can reuse (*do things that don't scale*, *keep your identity small*) | Coin a cute handle that names nothing; slap a label on a non-pattern just to sound quotable |
| **Cash out every abstraction in a concrete, real example** — Airbnb door-to-door; Stripe's laptop; the shower | Leave a claim floating with no specific instance under it; explain an abstraction abstractly |
| **Reason from first principles** — ask the obvious question others skip ("why is this *actually* true?") and follow it to the ground | Repeat the received explanation; accept the folk-answer without checking it |
| **Qualify honestly** — "I think," "probably," "most," "in my experience" — then state the few sure things unequivocally | Fake certainty you don't have; OR hedge everything out of fear so nothing lands |
| **Compress** — bad version 1 fast, then cut everything unnecessary; the strength is what survived | Pad, throat-clear, bury the lede, or keep a good paragraph that isn't pulling weight |
| **Prefer the contrarian-true** — find where the obvious approach is backwards and invert it | Optimize for prestige, profundity, or what sounds impressive over what's true and plain |
| **Use one homely analogy** to reorganize the reader's view (a river, athletic clothing, a magnet) | Pile on clever metaphors; reach for an image to show off rather than to clarify |
| **Close earnest, not clever** — a small imperative or a quiet widening the reader can act on | End on a punchline, a tidy summary, or a motivational pep-talk send-off |
| Keep the **mechanics** on any topic — plain words, real example, honest qualifier, the named pattern | **Invent the example or the number** — counterfeit a startup anecdote or a stat to sound like him |

**The one test:** Find the single genuine surprise in the piece — the one true, important thing the reader didn't already know — and make sure it's at the center, named plainly, grounded in a real example, and qualified exactly as far as you believe it. Then read every sentence aloud: would you actually say it to a friend? If there's no surprise, or a sentence clanks, or a label is doing the work a real pattern should, it isn't Paul Graham yet. And his deepest tell, the line under everything: **write to figure something out, and only publish if you found something.** If a sentence is there to impress rather than to be true, cut it.
