# George Orwell — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Orwell wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| **Find the lie first** — name the false or comfortable belief the piece exists to expose, and the fact you want attended to | Write a piece with no lie to expose and no verdict to deliver — mere information arranged pleasantly |
| **Open on the witnessed particular** — a scene with a place and time — or the flat contrarian declarative that turns on the received opinion | Open with throat-clearing, a definition from a dictionary, a hype hook, or anyone else's anecdote |
| **Let the meaning choose the word** — picture the thing wordlessly, then the fewest, shortest words that cover it | Surrender to the words — let ready-made phrases construct the sentences and think the thoughts |
| **Short Saxon words; active voice; the agent named** | Latinate dignity (*utilize, ameliorate, constitute*), agentless passives, noun-stacks where verbs should be |
| **Build the case as a list of particulars** — concrete instances, comma-strung, exhibited inside the sentence | Let a big abstraction (*engagement, efficiency, progress*) float unexamined where its contents should stand |
| **Demolish the euphemism** — particulars first, colon, then the soft phrase exhibited in scare quotes | Adopt the comfortable institutional phrase yourself; soften the unpleasant fact to spare anyone |
| **Land the blunt verdict sentence** — short, unhedged, earned by the evidence, quotable alone | End a paragraph on a qualification, a wind-down, or a both-sides shrug |
| **One fresh homely simile per abstraction** — physical, domestic, actually seen (cuttlefish, soft snow, Meccano) | Stale figures (*game-changer, double-edged sword*), mixed images, or two metaphors fighting in one sentence |
| **Hedge the facts, never the morals** — "probably," "so far as one can judge" on evidence; verdicts without qualification | Fake certainty about facts you haven't verified — or hedge the moral judgment you are sure of |
| **Confess first** — your own guilt, bias, vanity, rule-breaking, before judging anyone else's | Pose as superior to the faults you diagnose; flatter yourself, your side, or the reader |
| **Count it, price it** — exact figures as proof (syllables, pounds, yards), every number real | Invent the statistic, the price, or the witnessed scene — the one unforgivable move |
| **Define your terms** — "By 'X' I mean…", operationally, in what can be seen, heard, felt, reported | Argue with undefined fashionable words (*innovative, transformative, world-class*) doing the work |
| **End cold** — verdict, confession, or imperative; implicate yourself and the reader together | End with a summary, a pep talk, consolation, or any manufactured uplift |
| **Break any rule sooner than write something barbarous** — the discipline is judgment, not piety | Follow the rules into ugliness; treat the six rules as a checklist that replaces thinking |

**The one test:** Find the lie the piece exposes and the verdict it earns. Then check the windowpane: read each sentence and ask Orwell's six questions — what am I trying to say, what words will express it, what image will make it clearer, is the image fresh, can I cut it, is anything avoidably ugly? If an abstraction floats free of particulars, if a euphemism survives untranslated, if a verdict arrives unearned or hedged, if the writer escapes uncharged — it isn't Orwell yet. And his deepest tell, the line under everything: **the prose must be a windowpane over something true.** If a sentence is there to be admired rather than seen through, cut it.
