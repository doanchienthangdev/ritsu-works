# Robert B. Cialdini — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Cialdini wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| **Open on a puzzle or a confession** — a real behavior that makes no sense, told as a story, or your own susceptibility admitted first; hook *before* naming the principle | Open on a thesis, a definition, or an abstraction the reader hasn't been made to care about yet |
| **Name the hidden lever, then coin a sticky handle for it** (the way he gives us *expensive = good*, *Captainitis*, *click, run*) so the idea becomes portable | Leave the mechanism unnamed, or bury it in jargon the reader can't carry away |
| **Prove every claim with a real, named study and its exact number** — "social psychologist [Name] found [exact %]…"; the percentage IS the argument | **Invent a study, statistic, or percentage**, or assert "research shows" with no real, checkable source — the one unforgivable move |
| **Reach for the ethology / preset-program parallel** ("fixed-action patterns," trigger features, "*click, run*") to show the behavior is automatic, then turn it on us: *we run the same programs* | Describe an automatic behavior abstractly, with no concrete animal/everyday parallel that makes it feel like biology rather than insult |
| **Turn the mechanism physical with one vivid metaphor** — jujitsu (the persuader uses the force's own strength), the lever, the weapon, the trigger | Let the psychological force stay invisible and abstract, with no single image the reader can see |
| **Write "you and I"** — put author and reader in the same boat, equally wired, equally susceptible | Write "you are susceptible" (accusing) or "people are susceptible" (distancing); never exempt yourself |
| **Be fair to the shortcut** — say plainly it's rational most of the time ("a kinder view"); we'd "stand frozen" without it | Mock or shame the reader for being fooled; adopt the cheap "people are dumb" tone |
| **End on the defense** — teach the reader to tell the genuine signal from the counterfeit and to say *no* to the "profiteer" | Drop the ethics-and-defense beat and read as a how-to-manipulate manual — you become the profiteer the voice warns against |
| **Let the science ride on a story** — every abstraction gets a concrete carrier (an anecdote, an animal, an everyday scene), often a first-person "reader's report" | Stack claims with no human scene; present the mechanism as bare theory |
| **Keep the diction warm and the term explained** — the precise behavioral-science word, then the homely picture, in the same breath; italicize the one load-bearing word | Perform erudition; leave a technical term unexplained; round a sourced number into a vague "most people" |
| **Land on the lever and the defense** ("now you can see the machinery, and now you can refuse it") | Close on a motivational flourish or an inspirational send-off |

**The one test:** Find the section's single real study (with its number) and its single human story. Is there a *checkable* finding doing the persuading — named scientist, exact figure — riding on a concrete scene a reader has lived? And have I *armed* the reader (named the lever, taught the defense) rather than just impressed or, worse, equipped them to manipulate? If a statistic is unsourced or invented, if the mechanism floats free of any story, or if the piece reads like a manipulation manual with no defense, it isn't Cialdini yet. And his deepest tell, the line under everything: **the authority is real evidence, and the author is the chief patsy.** If any sentence asserts more than a real study supports, or talks down to the reader instead of sitting beside them, cut it.
