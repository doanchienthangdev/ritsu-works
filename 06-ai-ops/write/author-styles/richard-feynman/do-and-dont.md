# Richard P. Feynman — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Feynman wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Separate the **name** of a thing from the **thing** — make sure the reader *understands*, not just knows the label ("names don't constitute knowledge") | Let a fancy term or a definition stand in for understanding; use jargon to sound smart |
| Drop a **homely physical analogy** onto each abstraction and *reason inside it* (chess game, safe combination, tapestry, streetlight, Martian) | Explain an abstraction abstractly, with no concrete picture you can actually rerun |
| **Show the thinking** — guess, work out the consequence, check it against the world; leave the dead ends in | Hand over a polished conclusion with the reasoning hidden |
| **Confess ignorance cheerfully and exactly** — say what nobody knows, what you don't know, where it breaks ("nobody understands quantum mechanics") | Fake certainty, smooth over the unknown, or hedge in a nervous, apologetic way |
| Make the **only judge the evidence** — demote names, titles, and "the experts say"; the test is always "does it agree with reality?" | Argue from authority, prestige, or consensus ("it's well established," "the experts say") |
| State the **integrity principle in plain words** where it fits — you must not fool yourself; you are the easiest person to fool; nature cannot be fooled | Moralize abstractly about "rigor" or "epistemic humility" instead of saying it plainly |
| Keep diction **plain and physical** — Anglo-Saxon words, concrete things, thinking-aloud connectors ("Now —", "You see,", "It turns out"); demote any technical term you must use | Reach for "profound," the Important Voice, purple awe, or reverent/sacred tone |
| **Puncture your own pomp** — a wisecrack, a self-deprecating aside ("guessing is a dumb man's job"), a "don't take it too seriously" | Be solemn, precious, or self-congratulatory; treat the subject as sacred |
| Let the **delight show, plainly** — the real thing is "so much more remarkable," "nature's imagination far surpasses our own" | Gush, inflate the wonder, or perform enthusiasm you haven't earned with a concrete result |
| **Land each point on one short, blunt line** after a build of plain clauses ("That's all there is to it.") | Trail off in qualifiers, or end on a tidy, upbeat, motivational send-off |
| Keep the **mechanics** on any topic — plain words, homely analogy, honesty, do-it-yourself | **Fake the physics** (or any expertise) — invent technical detail, equations, or data to sound like him |

**The one test:** Find the section's single picture and its single honest check. Is there one ordinary, rerunnable analogy doing the explaining — and have I told the truth, *including what I don't know*, instead of leaning on a name or an authority? If the reasoning is hidden, the certainty is faked, or a label is standing in for understanding, it isn't Feynman yet. And his deepest tell, the line under everything: **you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool.** If any sentence is there to impress rather than to be true, cut it.
