## The opening move: widening frame, modest task, dated case

> "To science we owe dramatic changes in our smug self-image."
> — *The Worst Mistake in the History of the Human Race* (Discover, 1987)

**Demonstrates:** The widening-frame opening: a series of what-science-taught-us clauses (astronomy, biology) that lands on the provocation ("Now archaeology is demolishing another sacred belief"). The first sentence already implicates "our" self-image.

---

> "I've set myself the modest task of trying to explain the broad pattern of human history, on all the continents, for the last 13,000 years."
> — Edge.org talk-essay (1997)

**Demonstrates:** The Modest Task: an enormous question stated as a bounded, concrete puzzle (all the continents, 13,000 years) with deadpan ironic humility.

---

> "In 1992, when Daniel Wemp was about twenty-two years old, his beloved paternal uncle Soll was killed in a battle against the neighboring Ombal clan."
> — *Vengeance Is Ours* (The New Yorker, 2008)

**Demonstrates:** The in-medias-res case opening: year, name, age, relationship, and place locked into one sentence before any thesis appears.

## The question as engine

> "Why weren't Native Americans, Africans, and Aboriginal Australians the ones who conquered or exterminated Europeans and Asians?"
> — Edge.org talk-essay (1997)

**Demonstrates:** The counterfactual self-posed question that drives the whole investigation; posed straight, answered immediately after.

---

> "If one could choose between being a peasant farmer in Ethiopia or a bushman gatherer in the Kalahari, which do you think would be the better choice?"
> — *The Worst Mistake in the History of the Human Race* (Discover, 1987)

**Demonstrates:** The second-person thought experiment: the reader is handed the choice, making an abstract historical claim personally consequential.

## The announced enumeration

> "There are at least three sets of reasons to explain the findings that agriculture was bad for health."
> — *The Worst Mistake in the History of the Human Race* (Discover, 1987)

**Demonstrates:** Commit to the count before delivering ("at least three" buys honest room), then signpost: First... Second... Finally. The enumeration lives in the syntax, not bullets.

---

> "I now have a checklist of five factors that I run through when trying to understand why Society fails or succeeds"
> — UCSB "Voices" lecture on Collapse (UCTV, 2005)

**Demonstrates:** The framework announced before it is populated; the spoken-lecture version of the same move, with the checklist as explicit scaffolding.

## Do the numbers: compound risk made checkable

> "do the numbers suppose the risk of that tree falling on me tonight is 1 in 1,000 but if I sleep under dead trees for a Thousand Nights in three years I'm going to be dead"
> — Royal Institution talk (2013; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** Intuition converted into arithmetic: unit risk, multiplied across real exposure, landing on a blunt three-word verdict. The New Guinea lesson he calls constructive paranoia.

---

> "the most dangerous thing I did today was to to take a shower"
> — Royal Institution talk (2013; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** The domestic application that makes the exotic principle operative in the listener's morning routine; deadpan delivery of an apparently absurd claim he then proves ("that means 5,475 showers ahead of me").

## The eyewitness scene and the witness's own words

> "Why should we, when there are so many mongongo nuts in the world?"
> — a Bushman's reply, quoted in *The Worst Mistake in the History of the Human Race* (Discover, 1987)

**Demonstrates:** The person-on-the-ground quotation that collapses an entire abstract argument (agriculture's superiority) into one vivid human utterance.

---

> "it took three years, twenty-nine more killings, and the sacrifice of three hundred pigs before Daniel succeeded in discharging this responsibility"
> — *Vengeance Is Ours* (The New Yorker, 2008)

**Demonstrates:** Exact-count specificity as rhetorical climax: the logistics of revenge enumerated until the abstraction ("revenge culture") becomes legible and heavy.

## The uncomfortable mirror

> "just as we Americans are not eating horses kidneys and hearts"
> — People I (Mostly) Admire, ep. 55 (2023; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** The self-inclusive comparison: the Norse starving beside fish they refused to eat is mapped onto the audience's own food taboos before anyone can feel superior. "We," not "they."

## The rescale device

> "Suppose that an archaeologist who had visited from outer space were trying to explain human history to his fellow spacelings."
> — *The Worst Mistake in the History of the Human Race* (Discover, 1987)

**Demonstrates:** The Time-Machine Rescale: deep time compressed onto a 24-hour clock (agriculture arrives at 11:54 p.m.) so the reader feels the recency of everything they assume is permanent.

---

> "there's a thin slice on top the five countries in the North temperate zone there's a thin slice on the bottom the five countries in the South temperate zone"
> — the "Africa is like a Big Mac" passage, Upfront Summit talk (2020; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** Continental wealth distribution mapped exactly onto a homely physical object; parallel syntax ("there's a thin slice... there's a thin slice...") carrying the geometry.

## Dry wit, deadpan

> "the sole animal domesticated within sub-Saharan Africa was [you guess] a bird, the Guinea fowl"
> — Edge.org talk-essay (1997)

**Demonstrates:** The bracketed prompt makes the reader a co-investigator one beat before the punchline; professor's dry irony without a joke anywhere in sight.

---

> "I have not detected average differences in ego between new guineans and Americans but then I don't have a reliable ego meter"
> — Royal Institution talk (2013; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** Mock-technical humor ("a reliable ego meter") used to decline an unanswerable question honestly and lightly: the hedge itself is the punchline.

## The close: open questions or quantified hope

> "As our second midnight approaches, will the plight of famine-stricken peasants gradually spread to engulf us all?"
> — *The Worst Mistake in the History of the Human Race* (Discover, 1987)

**Demonstrates:** The open-question close with stakes: the essay's final move is a question the evidence has made urgent, not a summary or a sermon.

---

> "I'm cautiously optimistic I see the chances as at least 51 percent that will have a happy ending and only 49 will have a sad end"
> — People I (Mostly) Admire, ep. 55 (2023; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** The Cautious-Optimist Close: hope with a number on it. Calibration in place of uplift; "cautiously optimistic" as a precise epistemic position, not a mood.

---

> "forgiving is difficult but not forgiving is harder"
> — Royal Institution talk (2013; transcript verbatim)

**Demonstrates:** The aphoristic landing after a long narrative (the restorative-justice widow story): the single plain sentence that compresses several hundred words of testimony.
