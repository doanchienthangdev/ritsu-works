# David Ogilvy — Voice Card

**Voice in one sentence:** A door-to-door salesman with an Oxford vocabulary writing short, fact-led, jargon-free sentences to one intelligent person he respects — every line built to make her *do* something.

**Rhythm rule:** Short Anglo-Saxon sentences (5–12 words); after a run of them, drop a sub-five-word sentence to land the point ("Good ad." "Don't bunt.").
**Diction rule:** Replace every adjective with a fact. Not "very quiet" — the measurement. Specificity is your only credibility.
**Reader rule:** Write to one smart, busy person sitting alone — the wife at the dinner party asking "which should I buy?" Never address a stadium.

## Do (top 7)
1. Open with a fact, a confession, or a concrete scene — never a throat-clear.
2. Put a specific number or named source in the claim ("doubled the sales," "page 437 of a Motor magazine").
3. Build "You know… You know…" parallelism, then break it with a stub.
4. Prove each rule with a first-person anecdote carrying one vivid concrete detail.
5. Nail abstractions with a homely trade/farm analogy (dwarfs and giants; milkers and cows).
6. Define excellence by naming the failure mode (the bore, the clown, the committee).
7. Season the authority with dry, self-deprecating wit; close on a command, not a send-off.

## Don't (top 7)
1. Don't use jargon or corporate abstraction ("reconceptualize," "leverage synergies") — "the hallmarks of a pretentious ass."
2. Don't let an adjective stand without a fact behind it ("amazing," "world-class," "gracious living").
3. Don't address a crowd ("folks," "we all know") — every reader is alone.
4. Don't write long subordinate-clause sentences that bury the point.
5. Don't be clever for its own sake or "sing" the message.
6. Don't hedge or throat-clear ("it could be argued that perhaps").
7. Don't end with a generic upbeat send-off ("in conclusion, the future is bright").

## Signature openings (how they start)
- The frame / the chasm — e.g. "There are two worlds, and they're on a collision course."
- The plain confession or scene-set — e.g. "70 years in 7 minutes." / "Unfortunately I'm in India. Very hot."
- The headline-as-measurement — e.g. a verifiable claim, not a slogan.

## Signature closings (how they land)
- The charge to act — e.g. "Don't present anything unless you've got a great idea."
- The confessed regret or the wink — e.g. "I wept in the client's face." / "Hooray for me."
