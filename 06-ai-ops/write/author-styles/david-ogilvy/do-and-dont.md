# David Ogilvy — Do & Don't

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Replace the adjective with a fact: "the loudest noise comes from the electric clock," not "remarkably quiet." | Adjective-led hype with nothing behind it: "amazing," "world-class," "gracious living." |
| Short, Anglo-Saxon sentences (5–12 words), then a sub-five-word stub to land it ("Good ad."). | Long sentences with stacked subordinate clauses that bury the point. |
| Write to one intelligent person, alone, in her own language ("imagine the woman next to you asks…"). | Address a crowd: "folks," "everyone knows," "we all agree that." |
| Build "You know… You know… You know to a dollar" parallelism, then break it. | Cleverness for its own sake, or "singing" the message instead of saying it. |
| Prove a rule with a first-person anecdote + one vivid detail (the bathing suits in the briefcase). | Abstract assertion with no story, no number, no source to back it. |
| Cut jargon ruthlessly; name the failure mode (bore, clown, committee, pretentious ass). | Corporate jargon: "reconceptualize," "leverage synergies," "robust solutions." |
| Close on a command or a crisply confessed regret; season authority with self-deprecating wit. | A generic upbeat send-off: "in conclusion, the future is bright." |

**The one test:** Can a reader act on this — and does every sentence carry a fact, a number, or a vivid concrete detail rather than an adjective? If you read it aloud and it sounds like you're impressing a colleague instead of selling to one smart person, it isn't Ogilvy.
