# {{Full Name}} — Style DNA

## 0. Voice in one breath
Guidance: One dense paragraph (4–6 sentences) that, read alone, lets a writer hear the voice.
Not a bio — a *voice*. What does a sentence by this person sound like, and why?

## 1. Identity & stance
Guidance: Who they are as a writer and their relationship to the craft. Their core belief about
*what writing is for*. Their posture toward the reader (peer? teacher? provocateur? salesman who
respects you?). One or two sentences of worldview that shapes every choice.

## 2. Diction & vocabulary
Guidance:
- **Reaches for:** the specific words/word-classes they favor (concrete nouns? plain verbs? a signature lexicon?).
- **Refuses:** words/registers they avoid (jargon? adjectives? hype?).
- **Register:** formal ↔ colloquial; where they sit and when they shift.
- **Concreteness:** abstraction tolerance. Do they name the specific thing or gesture at the category?

## 3. Syntax & sentence mechanics
Guidance:
- **Sentence length:** the actual distribution (e.g. "mostly 6–12 words, with occasional one-word lines").
- **Rhythm:** how short and long alternate; what cadence feels like them.
- **Fragments / parallelism / repetition:** which they use and how.
- **Punctuation habits:** their real punctuation fingerprint (and note: the humanizer strips em-dashes
  regardless — capture what they'd use *instead*).
- **Paragraphing:** length and break logic.

## 4. Structure & architecture
Guidance: How a piece is built.
- **Openings:** how they earn the first line (their actual hook patterns).
- **Development:** how the middle moves (argument? story? accretion of examples?).
- **Closings:** how they land (and what they never do — e.g. no generic upbeat send-off).
- **Headers/lists/length:** their structural tendencies.

## 5. Rhetorical devices (signature moves)
Guidance: Summarize the named moves; full detail lives in `signature-moves.md`. List the 4–8
recognizable techniques (e.g. inversion, the specific-fact-as-proof, the rule-of-one).

## 6. Tone & emotional register
Guidance: Warmth, authority, humor, edge, vulnerability, generosity. Where on each axis, and how it
shows up mechanically (not "warm" — *how* the warmth appears in the words).

## 7. Themes & worldview
Guidance: What they keep returning to. Their recurring mental models and arguments. The 3–6 ideas
that, if absent, mean it doesn't sound like them.

## 8. Reader relationship
Guidance: Who they imagine reading, and how they treat that reader. Do they flatter, challenge,
respect-by-default, assume intelligence? This governs second-person usage and tone.

## 9. Formatting & medium habits
Guidance: Title style, typical length, how they handle evidence/citations/quotes, visual habits.

## 10. Tells of authenticity
Guidance: The hard-to-fake specifics that make it unmistakably them — a recurring phrase, a structural
tic, a way of being wrong on purpose, a kind of example they always reach for.

## 11. Anti-patterns (what they would NEVER do)
Guidance: As important as the do's. The moves that instantly break the illusion. Be concrete.

## 12. How to write AS {{Full Name}} (operational checklist)
Guidance: A numbered, actionable checklist the orchestrator can apply per section. The single most
useful section for the writer. 8–14 imperative rules, each falsifiable.

## 13. Calibration
See `voice-card.md` (load-every-run), `signature-moves.md`, and `samples.md` (the excerpt bank).
