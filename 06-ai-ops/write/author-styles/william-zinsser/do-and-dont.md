# William Zinsser — Do & Don't

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Cut every word that does no work; aim to halve the first draft. "Be grateful for everything you can throw away." | Inflate to sound important: "presently anticipating experiencing precipitation" for "it may rain." |
| Reach for the short Anglo-Saxon word and the precise active verb: "help," "now," "resign / retire / get fired." | Reach for the pompous synonym or the vague verb: "assistance," "facilitate," "stepped down." |
| Let an adjective or adverb in only when it does work the noun/verb can't ("garish daffodils," "drab house"). | Restate the root: "blared loudly," "tall skyscraper," "totally flabbergasted," "effortlessly easy." |
| Write lean and confident; state the thing flat. "Be confused. Be tired. Be depressed. Be annoyed." | Hedge with qualifiers: "a bit," "sort of," "rather," "quite," "very," "kind of bold." |
| Write to one intelligent reader, in the first person, in words you'd say aloud ("I," "you"). | Address a crowd, hide behind "one" and "it is to be hoped," or say "indeed"/"moreover" you'd never speak. |
| Exhibit a real bad-writing specimen and let it convict itself; teach with a good-model/bad-model pair. | Assert "this is wordy" or "avoid jargon" abstractly with no specimen, no example to make it land. |
| Vary sentence length on purpose; drop a three-word stub to land the point; read every line aloud. | Let every sentence "sound as if it came out of the same mold," or trust the eye over the ear. |
| Start a turn with "but"/"yet"/"still"; use the dash to amplify (render as a period + short sentence in output). | Bolt on a decorative "style," chase "gaudy similes and tinseled adjectives" — "there is no style store." |
| Refuse cliché and journalese; choose the original over the swift. "The race in writing is to the original." | "Fire off a note," "send shock waves," call the police "New York's finest," or write the breezy "just folks" voice. |
| Close on a concrete image, a small imperative, or an unsoftened hard truth (DiMaggio; "Simplify, simplify."). | End on a generic upbeat send-off, or flag your own joke with an exclamation point. |

**The one test:** Read it aloud. Is every sentence stripped to its cleanest components — plain words, active verbs, no qualifier, no restated modifier — and does it sound like one real person talking to one real reader? If a word would impress a colleague more than inform the reader, or if you couldn't comfortably say the sentence in conversation, it isn't Zinsser. (For Ritsu's learning content: this is the voice that makes an explanation *land* — it cuts the fog, respects the learner's intelligence, and leaves the concept as a clear, hearable sentence.)
