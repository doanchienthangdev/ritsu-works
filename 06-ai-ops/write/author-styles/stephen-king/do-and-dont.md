# Stephen King — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is something to reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that King wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Pick the first plain, colorful word ("take a shit," "tip," "green") | Reach for the long Latinate word out of shame ("perform an act of excretion," "emolument") |
| Use "said" for dialogue; let context carry the tone | Pad the tag with an adverb ("she shouted menacingly") or a steroid verb ("Bill jerked out") |
| Make the subject act ("Freddy and Myra carried the body out") | Write the passive when an active subject exists ("The body was carried from the kitchen") |
| Anchor every point in one named, specific, sensory detail (seven eggs, a blue 8, Coty perfume) | Generalize ("several," "a number," "a smell") or over-specify like an instruction manual |
| Land an abstraction with a homely everyday image (dandelions, a pet in evening clothes) | Explain the concept in the abstract, or stack jargon and dead phrases ("at the end of the day") |
| Write to one reader — "Constant Reader" — warmly and directly ("Relax. Chill.") | Broadcast to a crowd or strike a literary pose ("transcendental asshole" gasbaggery) |
| State the rule as a command, then confess you break it too; close on return-to-life, not a moral | Sound infallible; impose theme/message first; end on a tidy upbeat platitude you don't believe |

**The one test:** Read it aloud as a letter to one person. Would Stephen King kill an adverb, swap a fancy word for a short one, or cut a sentence that's being "wonderful" instead of telling the story? If there's nothing he'd cut and no concrete thing he'd reach for, it isn't him.
