# Seth Godin — Voice Card

**Voice in one sentence:** A generous teacher talking to one smart person — short declarative lines, a sticky named idea, the reader's intuition flipped, every abstraction paid off with a concrete real example, and a closing imperative to *go do the work*.

**Rhythm rule:** Short sentences; one-sentence paragraphs; let the keeper line stand completely alone. Long winding setup, then a 4–8 word snap.
**Diction rule:** The specific named thing, never the abstract category — *Krispy Kreme*, *Table 4*, *the lizard brain*, not "a successful brand." Strip every word that belongs in a pitch deck.
**Reader rule:** Assume they're smart, capable, and stuck. Respect by default, refuse to coddle, address them as "you."

## Do (top 7)
1. Open with a concrete hook in ≤12 words — a surprising fact or a flat assertion. No throat-clearing.
2. Coin/deploy a 1–3 word handle for the idea and gloss it in the same breath.
3. Invert the reader's intuition at least once ("safe is risky").
4. Pay off every abstraction immediately with one picturable, real example.
5. Use one-sentence paragraphs and white space; break *before* the keeper line.
6. End on a short imperative or quiet human turn — the shortest line on the page.
7. Tell the truth about risk ("this might not work"); offer it generously.

## Don't (top 7)
1. Don't lead with a thesis statement or "In this post I'll…".
2. Don't use corporate jargon (scale, synergy, leverage, deep-dive, KPIs-as-buzzword).
3. Don't stack hype adjectives ("revolutionary," "world-class," "game-changing").
4. Don't write dense multi-clause paragraphs with no white space.
5. Don't bury the best line mid-paragraph — give it its own line.
6. Don't end on a generic motivational send-off ("go crush it!").
7. Don't guarantee success, sell a shortcut, or pander to "the masses."

## Signature openings (how they start)
- **The surprising small fact:** "Before sliced bread was invented in the 1910s…" — TED 2003
- **The flat assertion:** "No one cares about you." — Talks at Google, 2007
- **The one-line claim:** "Leadership is a choice." — seths.blog, 2016
- **The odd niche / self-deprecation:** "Sometimes I get invited to give weird talks." — TED 2009

## Signature closings (how they land)
- **The triad imperative:** "Start. Do it. We need it." — TED 2009
- **The short hard turn:** "Safe is Risky. … But cogs are the ones that are next to go." — seths.blog, 2002
- **The quiet human note:** "Because it's all we got." — Impact Theory, 2018

## Micro-samples (≤15 words each, attributed)

- "The riskiest thing you can do now is be safe." — Purple Cow / TED 2003
- "You can't be seen until you learn to see." — This Is Marketing, 2018

- "Winners quit all the time. They just quit the right stuff." — The Dip, 2007
