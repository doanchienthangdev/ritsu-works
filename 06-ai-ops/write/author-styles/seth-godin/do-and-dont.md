# Seth Godin — Do & Don't

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Coin a sticky 1–3 word name for the idea and gloss it in the same line (Purple Cow, the Dip, sheepwalker). | Describe the idea in abstract multi-clause prose with no handle to repeat. |
| Flip the reader's intuition once ("safe is risky"; the smallest audience is the *safest* move). | Confirm the conventional wisdom or hedge so nothing gets upset. |
| Pay off every abstraction with one picturable, real example — a named brand, person, or price (Table 4, Krispy Kreme, $5.82 ad). | Argue from statistics, data tables, or "studies show" with no concrete picture. |
| Write one-sentence paragraphs; break *before* the keeper line so it stands alone in white space. | Bury the best sentence inside a dense wall-of-text paragraph. |
| Keep load-bearing lines to 6–14 words; make punch lines the shortest sentences on the page. | Pad with qualifiers, jargon (scale, synergy, leverage), or hype adjectives. |
| Address one capable "you," respect by default, and tell an uncomfortable truth plainly ("you're probably not good enough — and no one is"). | Pander to "the masses," coddle the reader, or talk down. |
| Close on a short imperative or quiet human turn ("Start. Do it. We need it."); flag risk honestly ("this might not work"). | End on a generic upbeat send-off or guarantee a quick-fix outcome. |

**The one test:** Is there a single short line a reader would screenshot and tell a friend — a *named idea* or a *flipped intuition* — sitting alone in white space, paid off by one concrete real example? If the piece has no such line, or the line is buried, abstract, or jargon-laced, it isn't Seth Godin.
