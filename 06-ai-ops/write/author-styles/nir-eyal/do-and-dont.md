# Nir Eyal — Do & Don't

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Open inside one named person's vivid scene, a confession of your own failure, or a test the reader's intuition gets wrong ("How did she get there?") | Open with a thesis, a definition, throat-clearing context, or a long warm-up before the point lands |
| Relocate the cause one level upstream and coin the corollary ("all motivation is the desire to escape discomfort" → "time management is pain management") | Treat the surface problem as the real problem, or leave the diagnosis at "you're just lazy / undisciplined" |
| Say the load-bearing line, flag the repeat, and say it again verbatim ("I'll say it again") | Vary the slogan each time you restate it, or bury the key line in the middle of a paragraph |
| Cash out every claim into a *named* expert and a decimal-precise number ("240 times more persistent"; "Gabriele Oettingen"; "it's called mental contrasting") | Write "studies show," "research suggests," or "experts agree" with no named researcher, institution, or technique |
| Introduce each technical term in bold, then translate it in plain English with an everyday example | Use insider jargon to sound smart, or let a psych term sit on the page untranslated |
| Flatten the moral panic in one blunt line ("that's just not true") before offering the constructive frame | Blame the technology, the app, or the modern world as the root cause; catastrophize; moralize ordinary behavior |
| Concede the kernel of the popular fix, then flip it to the real mechanism ("there is some truth to this… But how, exactly?") | Strawman the opposing view, or skip to the rebuttal before the reader feels its pull |
| End every concept with exactly one named, do-it-today tool (the 10-minute rule, the timebox, surfing the urge), framed as something you do | Leave a principle abstract ("be more mindful"), hand over a menu of ten tactics, or end on a concept |
| Build the insight as an antithesis ("not X, but Y"); land sections on short fragments; stand a hard sentence alone as its own paragraph | Pile up qualifiers and hedges around the moral conclusion; pad with adverbs ("hugely," "massively") instead of one italic word |
| Under-promise honestly ("effective and hard"); route any outside study back to your own model; close on a callback image or a portable slogan | Promise an easy or magic fix; sell hard; manufacture urgency or scarcity; preach visualization, affirmations, or manifesting as the cure |

**The one test:** Cover the byline and read one paragraph aloud. Does it open in a scene or a question rather than a thesis? Is the cause relocated upstream and cashed out into a *named* source and a real number? Is there exactly one antithesis you could quote and one tool you could use tonight? Does it hand the reader agency instead of shame, and refuse to blame the technology? If it reads like hype, a vague "studies show," or a sermon with no tool at the end, it isn't Nir Eyal.
