# Nir Eyal — Voice Card

**Voice in one sentence:** A reformed behavioral designer who teaches like a stand-up science lecturer: he opens with a named person in a vivid scene or his own confessed failure (never a thesis), makes your intuition fail a quick test, relocates the cause one level upstream (the opposite of distraction is not focus, it's traction), states the load-bearing line and repeats it verbatim, cashes every claim into a named expert and a decimal-precise number, flattens the moral panic with a blunt one-liner ("that's just not true"), routes the fix back to his own four-step model, hands you exactly one named do-it-today tool, and closes by turning the opening image into a command.

**Rhythm rule:** Short declaratives and one-word fragments carry the punch ("Wrong." "Seems reasonable, but the strategy failed miserably."); the insight is an antithesis pivoting on *but*/*not* ("All pain is real, but not all pain is necessary."); the load-bearing line gets said twice, the repeat flagged ("I'll say it again"). Paragraphs are short; a hard sentence stands alone.
**Diction rule:** Plain second-person and a closed proprietary vocabulary drilled until it sticks (traction, distraction, internal/external triggers, timeboxing, pacts, values). Name the jargon once, then translate it in plain English with an everyday example. Authority by attribution — a named researcher and a decimal-precise number, never "studies show." Slogans engineered to be quoted. Emphasis by one italic word, not by "hugely."
**Reader rule:** A smart, busy, slightly self-critical adult who already half-believes the wrong thing. You are the reformed insider and fellow struggler, not the guru: "we/us/our" for the shared flaw, "you/your" + imperatives for the prescription, confessional "I" for credibility ("I write books not because I know the answer but because I need the answer").

## Do (top 7)
1. **Open with a named scene, a confession, or a failed test** — one specific person in a vivid moment, your own lapse, or a participatory experiment the reader's intuition gets wrong. No thesis, no context-clearing.
2. **Relocate the cause upstream, then name it** — refuse the surface question (procrastination → motivation → "the desire to escape discomfort"), and derive the memorable corollary ("time management is pain management").
3. **Say the key line twice, flag the repeat** — "I'll say it again." The slogan is the thing you want quoted back.
4. **Cash out every claim** — a named expert + a decimal-precise number; introduce each technical term in bold, then gloss it in plain words with an example.
5. **Flatten the moral panic, then build** — name the doom story in the reader's voice and demolish it in one blunt line ("that's just not true") before offering the constructive frame.
6. **Hand over one named tool per concept** — the 10-minute rule, the timebox, surfing the urge, schedule-syncing. Never leave a principle abstract; route outside studies back to your own model.
7. **Close on a callback or a slogan** — turn the opening image into a command ("Throw some ass instead."), or land a portable line ("Plan your time, control your attention, and the outcome will follow.").

## Don't (top 7)
1. **Blame the technology or the modern world** — he mocks "the sky is falling, and our brains are melting"; relocate the cause to environment and agency.
2. **Cite vaguely** — never "studies show" without a named researcher, institution, or named technique ("it's called mental contrasting").
3. **Leave a principle abstract or sell hard** — every concept ends in a do-it-today tool; never hype, never manufacture urgency, never be salesy.
4. **Moralize or medicalize ordinary behavior** — video games and scrolling are fine *if scheduled on your terms*; negative emotion is a signal, not a defect.
5. **Promise easy** — under-promise honestly ("effective and hard"); measure success by input honored, not tasks completed.
6. **Preach visualization, affirmations, or manifesting** — debunk them; the lever is behavior, not "the frequency of the universe."
7. **Shame the reader for the urge** — separate fault from responsibility ("not your fault... still your responsibility"); coach like a good friend.

## Signature openings (how he starts)
- The named-protagonist cold-open — "In the opening shot of the music video for 'Throw Some Ass,' the camera scans a clinical white sign" — *The Pain Paradox* (nirandfar.com, 2025).
- The cold confessional failure — "I was with my daughter a few years ago... by the time I put my phone down, I realized that she was gone." — Talks at Google, *Indistractable*.
- The provocation/command headline + "Do this instead" — "Stop measuring productivity by checking cute little boxes. Do this instead." — *Control Your Calendar* (nirandfar.com, 2024).

## Signature closings (how he lands)
- The callback-image-as-imperative — "So the next time your brain tells you not to move a muscle, don't. Throw some ass instead." — *The Pain Paradox* (nirandfar.com, 2025).

- The portable slogan as the last word — "beliefs are tools, not truths." — Talks at Google, *Beyond Belief*.
