# Tim Urban — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Tim Urban wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Personify the invisible force as a named, capitalized character with a personality and a (described) drawing — the Instant Gratification Monkey, the Panic Monster, the Yearning Octopus | Explain an abstract force abstractly — define "procrastination" or "desire" or "AI risk" with no character, drawing, or thought experiment to hold it |
| Run one thought experiment and push it past where a sane writer stops; let it get gloriously absurd (the recursive 1750→1500→12,000 BC time machine) | Make a flat assertion and move on; assume the reader will accept an unintuitive claim without walking the steps |
| Drop one hyper-specific, faintly ridiculous tangent mid-point — name the exact rabbit hole (gorilla vs tiger; the Feynman-to-Bieber spiral) | "I got distracted" / vague hand-waving; specificity is the joke, so generic kills it |
| Build-then-drop: a long escalating riff capped by a short deadpan beat ("Daunting." / "I have problems.") | Uniform, even, same-length sentences; burying the punch in the middle of a paragraph |
| Zoom out to a vast scale (time, mortality, the real numbers, the cosmos) at least once — the Life Calendar / 100 Blocks move | Stay at human eye-level the whole way; tell the reader "life is short" instead of *showing* the boxes |
| Be the specimen — cast yourself as the dumbest / most-procrastinating / most-confused person, and flag your uncertainty out loud | Pose as the authority; pretend certainty; hide your confusion or your sources |
| Talk straight to one *you*; voice the reader's skeptical question and answer it; turn corners on "So." / "Now." / "Here's the thing." | Write at the reader from a podium; ignore the obvious objection forming in their head |
| Do the real research, credit thinkers by name, then re-render it as a cartoon or thought experiment; coin a reusable handle (a unit, an acronym, a character) | Hand over conclusions with no reasoning (be the "cook"); skip attribution; leave the reader with no souvenir concept |
| Keep the fun load-bearing — the joke is how the hard idea gets past the guard; close by zooming out, then wink | Be boring to seem serious; pad with corporate filler ("in today's fast-paced world"); end on a tidy motivational bow |

**The one test:** Find the section's single character (or thought experiment or grid) and its single zoom-out. Is there one invisible force made *visible* — a named character, a pushed-too-far hypothetical, a drawing-in-words, a scale grid — and at least one moment where the camera pulls back far enough that the reader feels the real stakes? If the idea is abstract with nothing to see, or earnest with no fun to carry it, it isn't Tim Urban yet. And if it reads as shallow — the inch-deep version everyone already repeats — delete it and go deeper: his deepest tell is the refusal to skate over anything worth understanding.

**Ritsu calibration:** Tim Urban's occasional profanity ("spicy meatball" is fine; the harder words are not) is authentic to his blog but clashes with Ritsu's brand voice (`00-core/brand_voice.md`: never casual-careless). Keep every other lever — the absurd tangent, the goofy hyperbole, the deadpan drop, the irreverent register — and just drop the swearing on Ritsu surfaces. The voice survives the cut intact.
