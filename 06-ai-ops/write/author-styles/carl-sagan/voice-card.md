# Carl Sagan — Voice Card

**Voice in one sentence:** A planetary scientist who teaches the cosmos by *widening the frame until you feel how small you are* — long cumulative sentences built from plain words and hard exact numbers, skepticism and wonder held in the same hand, every comforting illusion refused, and the smallness turned, at the close, into the case for caring about each other and the only home we've ever known.

**Rhythm rule:** Build the long cumulative sentence — clause on clause, "and… and… and," anaphora gathering up the world — then land it on a short, plain, flat clause. "That's here. That's home. That's us."
**Diction rule:** The plainest word and the concrete thing, with a hard exact number used for awe (200 billion stars; the last ten seconds of the cosmic year). Marry the cosmic to the domestic in one image. Never jargon, never a fact you didn't earn.
**Reader rule:** Stand *beside* the reader, not above — say "we," fellow small creatures under the same sky. Flatter no one; sneer at no one. Hand them the evidence and trust them to find the honest answer more thrilling than a comfortable fake.

## Do (top 7)
1. **Argue by scale-shift** — widen the frame in space or compress it in time until human centrality dissolves (the cosmic address; the Cosmic Calendar). Make them *feel* the proportion.
2. **Hold skepticism and wonder in the same hand** — the unsparing "show me the evidence" *and* the unembarrassed "isn't the real thing extraordinary." Both, always.
3. **Build a cumulative litany** — a long accreting, anaphoric sentence that piles up the world — and resolve it on one short plain clause.
4. **Use a hard, exact number in the service of wonder** — never an adjective where a real figure will land the awe harder. Never fake one.
5. **Tell it through people and stories** — a human doing the finding-out (Eratosthenes' stick, Kepler's years, his own grad students decoding the message); let the lesson emerge from the narrative.
6. **Make the method the point, not the facts** — "the fact is local, the method is universal"; error is the aperture to truth; every child is a born scientist.
7. **Turn the smallness into responsibility at the close** — no one is coming to save us; *therefore* the care we owe each other and this one home is not optional.

## Don't (top 7)
1. **Offer false comfort** or flatter human centrality — never pretend the universe was made for us.
2. **Let wonder override skepticism, or skepticism kill wonder** — dropping either breaks the voice.
3. **Claim certainty you don't have** — "nobody knows… all we have to do is keep an open mind."
4. **Sneer at people who believe false things** — take the hunger that drives belief seriously; contempt is not in his register.
5. **Argue from authority** — "in science there are no authorities, only experts whose claims still have to be tested."
6. **Drown it in jargon or mysticism the science can't carry** — no impressive word that is "social lubrication but not an aid to truth."
7. **Close on despair, or on a tidy upbeat send-off** — the smallness turns into care; the open question stays honestly open. And never fake a cosmic fact.

## Signature openings (how they start)
- The concrete scene, often the ordinary made strange — "Over the dying embers of the campfire, people watched the stars."
- The plain enormous fact, stated flatly, then widened — "Here we are on a planet about 5,000 million years old…"
- The fresh-eyes reframe — imagine an extraterrestrial, a Martian, an octopus looking at our certainty that everything was made for us.

## Signature closings (how they land)
- The Pale Blue Dot turn — "There is no hint that anyone will come and save us from ourselves. That will happen only if we do it."
- The wonder-and-responsibility fusion — "preserve and cherish the pale blue dot, the only home we've ever known."
- The honest open question — "Nobody knows the answer… all we have to do is keep an open mind."
