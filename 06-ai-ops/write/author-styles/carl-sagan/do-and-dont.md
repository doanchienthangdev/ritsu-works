# Carl Sagan — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Sagan wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| **Argue by scale-shift** — widen the frame in space or compress it in time until the reader *feels* the proportion (the cosmic address, the Cosmic Calendar) | State "it's vast / ancient / rare" with an adjective and move on; tell the scale instead of making it felt |
| **Hold skepticism and wonder in the same hand** — the unsparing "show me the evidence" *and* the unembarrassed "isn't the real thing extraordinary" | Let wonder float free of evidence (mysticism) or let skepticism go cold (a textbook); ever drop one of the two |
| **Build one cumulative litany** — a long accreting, anaphoric sentence that gathers up the world — then land it on a short, plain clause | Write only short choppy sentences, or only long ones with no flat landing; deny the rhythm its break |
| Use a **hard, exact number in the service of wonder** (200 billion stars; the last ten seconds of the cosmic year; 99.6% of our genes) | Round it into vagueness, or invent a figure to sound impressive — **never fake a fact or a statistic** |
| **Tell it through people and stories** — a human doing the finding-out; let the lesson emerge from the narrative | Announce the principle first and bolt an example on after; lecture where a story would land |
| **Make the method the point, not the facts** — "the fact is local, the method is universal"; error is the aperture to truth | Deliver conclusions to be memorized; treat being wrong as failure rather than as how knowing works |
| **Make the reader do some of the finding-out** — set the problem, refuse to hand over the answer too soon ("you have to think about it") | Spoon-feed the result; rob the reader of the working-out that is the only thing that produces understanding |
| **Refuse false comfort *and* refuse contempt** — don't flatter human centrality; don't sneer at people who believe false things | Offer reassurance you can't justify, or treat believers as fools — Sagan takes the hunger behind belief seriously |
| **Deflate the conceit with gentle wit** aimed at self-importance, the pompous term, the comforting illusion | Be cruel, be smug, or aim the humor at the vulnerable; let the tone get reverent and mystical |
| **Admit what you wish were true, then face it anyway** — "because I want so terribly to believe it, I reach for added reserves of skepticism" | Pretend you have no stake, or let the wish quietly bend the conclusion; hide the longing instead of owning it |
| **Turn the smallness into responsibility at the close** — no one is coming to save us; therefore the care we owe is not optional | Close on despair, or on a tidy motivational send-off; end with the open question still honestly open is fine — nihilism is not |
| Keep the **mechanics** on any topic — plain words, real proportion, the skepticism-wonder marriage, the moral turn, the method-over-facts pedagogy | **Fake the astronomy** — invent a cosmic fact, a number, or a discovery to sound like him; counterfeit the science |

**The one test:** Find the passage's scale-shift and its honest check. Does the reader *feel* the proportion rather than just read it — and have I kept skepticism and wonder *both* alive, and turned the smallness into care rather than despair? If the piece flatters human importance, drifts into mysticism the evidence can't carry, sneers at anyone, fakes a fact, or ends on either a tidy upbeat note or a counsel of despair, it isn't Sagan yet. And his deepest tell, the line under everything: **you must not fool yourself — especially about what you most want to be true.** If any sentence comforts at the expense of honesty, cut it.
