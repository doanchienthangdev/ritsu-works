# Nassim Nicholas Taleb — Do & Don't

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Open with a law, a coined term, or a refused premise ("Wind extinguishes a candle and energizes fire."; "a lot of people think X; in fact...") | Open with context, a definition, a literature survey, or a soft scene-set |
| Sort the subject into a named two- or three-column split early (fragile/robust/antifragile; doer/talker; passive/active), then walk it across domains | Argue in one undifferentiated flow with no binary the reader can sort themselves against |
| Build long (commas, semicolons, parentheses), then land bare on a short maxim you refuse to qualify ("Compromising is condoning.") | Hedge the verdict ("arguably," "it depends," "may in some cases") or bury the claim under caveats |
| Pivot every overturned cliché on a semicolon, mirrored syntax, one swapped word ("an error is information; ... an error is an error") | Use "but," a comma splice, or an em-dash where the chiastic flip needs a semicolon |
| Cash out every abstraction in a concrete prop (coffee cup, deadlift, doorman, slice of pizza) and ask "can I generalize?" | Let an abstraction float ("forces," "factors," "innovation") with no object and no name attached |
| Refuse to forecast the event; name the fragility instead ("foolish to say which truck; not foolish to say the bridge is fragile") | Predict a specific event, price, or date, or pretend the Black Swan is a hard problem rather than the wrong question |
| Ground authority in lived exposure / skin in the game ("tell me what's in your portfolio"), not citation | Argue from authority you read ("studies show"); treat the library as the origin of an idea |
| Coin or borrow a Capitalized handle, gloss it once, reuse it; aim contempt at a method or an institution, deadpan | Decorate with empty Capitalized terms; praise "resilience"/"stability" as the goal; write in one even register |
| Close on a coined law or a duty, set apart ("If you see fraud and do not say fraud, you are a fraud.") | Close on hope, uplift, a call to action, or "in conclusion"; explain the aphorism after the punch |

**Ritsu brand-safety rider:** Taleb's voice names real people and whole professions as frauds, and asterisks the occasional profanity. That naming-and-insult is genuinely part of the voice, but when this style is applied to Ritsu content, aim the contempt at *methods and ideas* (rereading, the fluency illusion, "study hacks," passive consumption), never at named individuals, and keep the profanity out. The combat survives; the target changes. This stays inside `00-core/brand_voice.md` (honest, concrete, never cruel-for-cruelty, never manufactured).

**The one test:** Cover the byline and read one paragraph aloud. Does it sort the world into named columns, cash every abstraction into a concrete object or a name, pivot at least one cliché on a semicolon, and land on a bare maxim it refuses to soften, with the contempt aimed at a method rather than hedged into neutrality? If the prose is evenly hedged, abstract, citation-backed, or ends on uplift, it isn't Taleb.
