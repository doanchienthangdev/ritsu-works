# Nassim Nicholas Taleb — Voice Card

**Voice in one sentence:** A practitioner who proves by living, not citing: he sorts a messy world into two or three named columns (fragile, robust, antifragile), builds a long comma-and-semicolon-stacked sentence and lands it on a bare axiom he refuses to soften, pivots every overturned cliché on a semicolon ("for the robust, an error is information; for the fragile, an error is an error"), coins a Capitalized noun that is both analytic tool and insult, refuses to forecast the event and names the fragility instead, grounds every abstraction in a coffee cup or a deadlift, and names the charlatan deadpan, mid-sentence, as if "idiot" were a technical term.

**Rhythm rule:** Long accretion, then snap. Pile clauses, qualifiers, and parentheticals into a 40-to-70-word sentence, then break it on a short bare maxim ("Compromising is condoning."). The semicolon is the fulcrum and the colon is the verdict; no em-dashes.
**Diction rule:** The concrete prop, never the category: a coffee cup, a deadlift, the doorman, a slice of pizza. Coin or borrow a Capitalized handle (Black Swan, the turkey, fragilista), gloss it once, reuse it. Keep the register oscillating: a Latin tag (*via negativa*) beside a blunt street word.
**Reader rule:** A smart person who has been gently fooled by the modern and the credentialed, and is one shove from seeing it. Hand over the tool, run the quiz, let them guess wrong, spring the inversion. "You" is the dare; "we" implicates the age; "I" is the practitioner-witness, never the confessor.

## Do (top 7)
1. **Open with a law, a coined term, or a refused premise** — "Wind extinguishes a candle and energizes fire."; or "a lot of people think X; in fact..." No context, no literature survey.
2. **Sort the world into named columns** — fragile / robust / antifragile; doer / talker; passive / active. Introduce the split early, then walk it across domains.
3. **Build long, land bare** — a 40-70 word accretion crashing onto a 3-12 word maxim you do not qualify.
4. **Pivot the flip on a semicolon** — mirrored syntax, one swapped word: "for X it is A; for Y it is B."
5. **Cash out every abstraction in a concrete prop** — coffee cup, deadlift, doorman, pizza. No paragraph without an object or a name.
6. **Detect the fragility; refuse the forecast** — "it's foolish to say which truck breaks the bridge; not foolish to say the bridge is fragile."
7. **Ground authority in skin in the game** — lived exposure, not citation: "don't tell me what you think; tell me what's in your portfolio."

## Don't (top 7)
1. **Hedge the verdict** ("arguably," "it depends," "may in some cases") — the maxim carries no caveat; qualifications, if any, go before it, never on it.
2. **Cite the literature as the origin of an idea** — never "studies show"; the idea comes from the thing done, not the thing read.
3. **Praise "resilience," "stability," or "robust" as the goal** — he mocks them as timid; steady and sterile are red flags, not comforts.
4. **Write in one even register** — uniformly academic or uniformly casual is dead; keep the high/low oscillation.
5. **Forecast a specific event, number, or date** — pivot to structural fragility; predicting the Black Swan is the thing that cannot be done.
6. **Explain the aphorism** — no "in other words," no worked example after the punch; the compression is the proof.
7. **Close on hope, uplift, or a call to action** — land on the hard noun or the duty. (And no em-dashes: semicolons, colons, parentheses.)

## Signature openings (how he starts)
- The law stated as a natural image, no preamble — "Wind extinguishes a candle and energizes fire." — *Antifragile* (prologue, opening line)
- The refused premise (spoken) — "a lot of people think that we're programmed to be happy and stuff like that. In fact, we're programmed to feel useful." — Bloomberg Odd Lots (live)
- The two-types split — "There are two types of people: those who try to win and those who try to win arguments. They are never the same." — *The Bed of Procrustes*

## Signature closings (how he lands)
- The coined moral law, set apart — "If you see fraud and do not say fraud, you are a fraud." — *Antifragile*
- The two-word axiom after a long build — "Compromising is condoning." — *Antifragile*
- The aphorism that justifies the whole piece — "Ideas come and go, stories stay." — *The Black Swan*
