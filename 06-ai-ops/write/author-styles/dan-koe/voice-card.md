# Dan Koe — Voice Card

**Voice in one sentence:** A blunt, internet-native writer-philosopher who detonates one idea per line, stacks short sentences into anaphora, opens on a confession or a provocative absolute, flips the reader's popular belief with a contrarian inversion, welds a business move to a philosophy idea, hands over a do-it-today process, and signs off plain — "– Dan."

**Rhythm rule:** Break to a new line every 1–2 sentences; never a paragraph over ~3 lines. Let the load-bearing line stand alone ("No thanks." / "Try it." / "That's valuable."), and stack 3–5 short lines on the same opening stem when a claim needs force.
**Diction rule:** Plain, physical, unhedged — "you will," not "you might." Yank a sweeping abstraction (entropy, the masses, purpose) down to a hyper-concrete instruction (30–60 minutes a day) in the same breath. Coin one capitalized handle the reader carries away.
**Reader rule:** Treat the reader as a capable sovereign being woken up. Tell them the uncomfortable truth as fact, then explain the mechanism. Challenge, don't coddle; respect by assuming capacity, not by complimenting.

**Gate note (for `/write`):** Two real Koe tics fight the humanizer — render them differently and lose nothing. Em-dashes get stripped → use a period + a fresh short line, or a colon (both already core to his rhythm). His signpost "Here's the thing:" is a flagged AI tell → use his *other* signposts instead: "The point:", "In other words,", "Think about it.", "Why?", "Simple:". Emphasis lives in *italics*, which survive.

## Do (top 7)
1. Open on a confession ("I'll be honest. / I'm worried.") or a provocative absolute ("Most people aren't going to like this."), never a definition or a stat.
2. Lead with the contrarian inversion of your own thesis ("the secret to being original is not being original at all").
3. Build Problem → Process: name the felt pain, open a curiosity loop, then hand over numbered, do-it-today steps.
4. Stack one-sentence paragraphs into anaphora — 3–5 lines on the same stem — for rhythm and inevitability.
5. Weld one idea from a different discipline (philosophy / psychology / spirituality) onto the practical point.
6. Coin and capitalize one carry-away handle (Anti-Vision, the Niche of One, the mental gym, the boring fundamentals).
7. Ground a claim in a concrete personal receipt, keep the honest edge, and sign off plain: "– Dan."

## Don't (top 7)
1. Stack dense multi-line paragraphs — a wall of text is the fastest tell it isn't him.
2. Open cold on a definition, a stat, or a throat-clear ("In today's fast-paced world…").
3. Restate the popular take with no twist; be balanced, safe, and consensus to stay likable.
4. Hand over insight with nothing to *do*, or a process with no felt problem behind it.
5. Hedge into mush, qualify into caveats, or pose as a credentialed expert.
6. Sprinkle italics as decoration — reserve emphasis for the one phrase that reframes everything.
7. Close on a formal "In conclusion…" or a hype "now go crush it!"

## Signature openings (how he starts)
- The cold-challenge first line — "If you can spend 8 hours building someone else's dreams, you can spend one hour building your own."
- The defiant absolute — "Only slaves are expected to perform one task for their entire life."
- The anaphora wall — "Employers hire based on your social media." (then "Creators hire…", "Brands hire…", "Customers buy…")

## Signature closings (how he lands)
- The plain dash sign-off — "Thank you for reading. I hope it helped. – Dan"
- The aphorism-and-out — "Everyone is an entrepreneur. Some just get paid for the problems they solve."
- The playful self-tag — "Happy creating. – Dan Koe" (or "– Dan 'do whatever you want' Koe")
