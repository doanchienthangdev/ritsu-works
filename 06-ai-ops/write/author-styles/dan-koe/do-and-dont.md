# Dan Koe — Do & Don't

<!-- The fastest reference during drafting + the humanizer's voice-fidelity checklist.
     Each "do" is a move to actively reach for; each "don't" would break the illusion that Dan Koe wrote it. -->

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Break to a new line every 1–2 sentences; let key lines stand alone ("No thanks." / "Try it." / "That's valuable.") | Stack dense multi-line paragraphs; a wall of text is the fastest tell it isn't him |
| Open on a confession or a flat provocation — "I'll be honest. I'm worried." or "Most people aren't going to like this."| Open cold on a definition, a stat, or a throat-clear ("In today's fast-paced world…") |
| Lead with the contrarian inversion of your own thesis — "Original thinking is largely a myth."; "You can't think outside the box if there is no box."| Restate the popular take with no twist; be agreeable, balanced, and consensus to stay likable |
| Build Problem → Process: name the pain, open a curiosity loop, then hand over numbered do-it-today steps | Hand over insight with nothing to *do*, or a process with no felt problem behind it |
| Bolt a different discipline onto the practical point — marketing as natural selection, "just look at peacocks"| Keep the piece flat and single-domain; explain the business move with only business words |
| Coin and capitalize one carry-away handle (The Pull, Value Creator, anti-vision, the mental gym) | Leave the core idea abstract and unnamed, with no noun the reader can repeat |
| Ground a claim in your own numbered receipt — "first $50K month working 3 hours a day… max"| Assert success in the abstract; motivational-poster lines with no mechanism or proof |
| Keep the edge and the honest swear when it's true — "It's just so fucking boring"; "Source? I made it up."| Pull the punch, hedge into mush, or pose as a credentialed expert; close on a formal "In conclusion…" or a hype "go crush it!" |
| Close plainly and human — a short thanks, then "– Dan" | End on a dramatic CTA or a dry academic summary |

**The one test:** Read the draft aloud and watch two things — paragraph length and the angle. Is almost every paragraph 1–3 lines, with the load-bearing sentences sitting alone? And does it lead with an inversion that makes the reader's popular belief flinch — the contrarian turn ("original thinking is largely a myth," "you don't *find* a niche, you create one") — backed by Problem → Process and one personal receipt? If it's dense, or it just agrees with what the reader already thinks, it isn't Dan Koe yet.
