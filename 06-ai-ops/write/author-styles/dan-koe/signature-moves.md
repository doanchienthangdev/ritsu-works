## 1. The Stacked One-Liner Anaphora (his core rhythmic instrument)

**What:** Build an idea not by elaborating one paragraph but by stacking single-sentence paragraphs — each on its own line with white space around it — often sharing a repeated opening stem so the repetition becomes a drumbeat that escalates toward a turn. This is the single most recognizable thing on a Koe page: the vertical column of short, parallel declaratives. The line breaks *are* the rhythm.

**When:** Whenever a claim needs force, momentum, or inevitability — especially to make an abstract point land emotionally, or to corner the reader into agreement before the pivot. Reach for it on a thesis, a list of proofs, or an emotional crescendo.

**Example:** "You describe your weekend and present a certain version of yourself. / You persuade your friends to go to a specific dinner spot. / You plead your case at a job interview. / You recommend your favorite show. / You choose what to wear on a first date. / You get your kids to eat their vegetables. / *You tell stories.*"
— "Every ambitious person must learn this skill," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "Social media is a necessity of modern life. / It *is* virtual reality. / It *is* a digital society. / It *is* the new economy."
— "The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People)," thedankoe.com (2023)

**Example:** "Purpose is the divine force by which you conduct your life. / Purpose is the intrinsic energy that makes positive choices seamless. / Purpose is the compass that guides you inward — unmoved by external circumstance."
— "How To Profit From Solving Your Own Problems," thedankoe.com (2022)

## 2. The Contrarian Inversion (set up the obvious, then flip it)

**What:** State the expectation the reader holds, then invert it in the same breath — the value isn't the new claim, it's the *reversal* of an assumed truth. The move is always "the thing you think is X is actually not-X," delivered flat, often as a section's hinge or a one-line thesis.

**When:** To reframe a tired topic, puncture a common belief, or make a fundamentals-are-underrated argument feel fresh. Best as a pivot line right before you deliver the real lesson.

**Example:** "But sometimes, the secret to being original is not being original at all."
— "How To Think Originally," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "Make a habit of the boring fundamentals, but realize that the boring fundamentals are not boring… it's mastery over misery."
— "How To Get Ahead Of 99% Of People (In 6-12 Months)," YouTube (2022)

**Example:** "You don't 'find' a profitable niche. You create a profitable niche through persuasion. … Free people don't choose a niche, they create one."
— "The Future-Proof Skill Stack (Learn In This Order)," thedankoe.com (2024)

## 3. The Provocative Absolute / Reframe-the-Mundane (the jolt)

**What:** A flat, near-offensive universal claim — no hedging, often bolded and standing alone as its own line/subhead — that reframes something mundane in a jarring, status-threatening way. Or the inverse: take a familiar tool and rename it with a loaded metaphor that recategorizes it. The absolute is designed to make the reader flinch, then keep reading to see if he can defend it (he then does).

**When:** As an opening hook, or to detonate a section. Use when a gentle version of the claim would be ignored. The provocation must be backed immediately, or it reads as a cheap guru line.

**Example:** "**Only slaves are expected to perform one task for their entire life.**"
— "The Future-Proof Skill Stack (Learn In This Order)," thedankoe.com (2024)

**Example:** "most people treat AI as a slot machine rather than something you can program to do exactly what you want it to. … you need to think of AI as a *digital employee that will do exactly what you tell it to do.*"
— "How to use AI better than 99% of people," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "The starving artist is a modern myth. Historical artists were great marketers, and that's why you know their name today."
— "Every ambitious person must learn this skill," thedankoe.com (2025)

## 4. Problem → Process (his stated writing framework)

**What:** Structure any piece (and most sections) as a pain point first, a procedure second. Open with a problem to "open a curiosity loop" and qualify the reader ("what's in it for me?"), then deliver a clean, usually numbered, repeatable process to resolve it. He names this as his explicit method, not a vibe.

**When:** Essentially always — it's the macro skeleton of a Koe letter and the micro skeleton of his sections. Whenever you'd otherwise lead with an idea, lead with the problem the idea solves instead.

**Example:** "Almost everything I write or speak stems from a simple framework: / Problem and Process. … Why start with a problem? / - It captures attention / - It qualifies the reader or listener (what's in it for me?) / - It is the start of any good story / - It opens a curiosity loop"
— "How To Think Originally," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "1. Identify a problem in your life / 2. Set a goal to reveal your present purpose / 3. Experiment with different solutions / 4. Find what works and create your own / 5. Talk about your journey in public / 6. Offer your solution with a price tag on it"
— "Solve Your Own Problems & Sell The Solution," thedankoe.com (2023)

## 5. Cross-Disciplinary Synthesis (business × philosophy/psychology/spirituality)

**What:** Take a concrete business or productivity mechanic and fuse it with a concept from a "higher" domain — philosophy, psychology, spirituality, evolutionary biology, systems theory — so a commodity idea becomes novel. He frames himself as a "synthesizer" and says this fusion is the source of his best work and his moat against AI content.

**When:** When a topic is over-covered and you need a fresh angle, or to add depth that short-form can't. The rule he gives: study the thing *adjacent* to what you're writing about, not the thing itself.

**Example:** "If you watch any of my most popular videos, you'll notice that I attempt to weave a business concept like 'niching down' with another concept like ego development theory or identity. … when I came across the book *Awareness* by Anthony De Mello, it was pretty incredible how novel ideas started to spring up, which brought a new dimension to a boring business concept."
— "How To Think Originally," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "If you're into marketing, study psychology or spirituality. / If you're into philosophy, study business and social media. / Write about the connections you make."
— "How To Think Originally," thedankoe.com (2025)

## 6. The Confrontational Second-Person Diagnosis (tell them the unflattering truth)

**What:** Address the reader directly with an uncomfortable diagnosis stated as plain fact — not "some people" but "you" — usually exposing a hidden mechanism behind their behavior (survival wiring, approval-seeking, self-deception). It implicates the reader without insulting them, because the next move always explains the mechanism compassionately. He often pre-empts the dodge: "don't act like you're immune."

**When:** Early in a piece to create stakes, or to break a reader's defensiveness so the teaching lands. The diagnosis must be true and immediately followed by the *why*, or it reads as an attack.

**Example:** "You don't have your own opinion because your survival is dependent on agreeing with others' opinions. / Think about it. / You needed *approval* if you didn't want to be cast out of the 'tribe.'"
— "How To Think Originally," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "And don't act like you're immune to this. / Nobody is immune to this. / I'm not immune to this right. / I click on things mindlessly on my phone and then up watching for a minute before I realize, like, what the fuck am I doing?"
— "The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One)," YouTube (2023)

## 7. The Coined Capitalized Framework (give the idea a proprietary handle)

**What:** Package an idea as a capitalized, ownable term the reader can carry and that becomes his IP — Anti-Vision, the Niche of One, the Future-Proof Skill Stack, the Experience Model, the digital renaissance, the one-person business. Often the coinage *is* the title or the section head, and the body defines it. The naming converts a loose concept into a memorable, repeatable, monetizable handle.

**When:** When an idea is central enough to be a takeaway, a product, or a worldview anchor. Name it the moment you want the reader to remember and re-use it.

**Example:** "Many of you know that I'm very big on creating a vision for your future, but the best way to do that is by also creating an antidote… So to create your anti vision, sit down with a notebook, become a mad scientist, draw out everything… write down every single thing that you do not want in this life."
— "How To Get Ahead Of 99% Of People (In 6-12 Months)," YouTube (2022)

**Example:** "That is your niche. YOU are your niche. YOU, at this very moment, are interested in a select few things that intersect. That intersection is where your audience is built."
— "How To Profit From Solving Your Own Problems," thedankoe.com (2022)

## 8. The "Ahead of 99% / Observe the Masses" Framing (aspiration + simple math + the sleeping majority)

**What:** Frame the whole piece as a path to beat the overwhelming majority, paired with the claim that the bar is shockingly low because almost no one tries. Two halves: the **aspiration** ("get ahead of 99% of people"), often with a concrete timeline and small daily input, and the **sleeping-majority** contrast — telling the reader to *look at* the masses on the default path and see where mindless action leads. The math is deliberately simple to make the goal feel attainable.

**When:** As a hook/title and as a motivational frame mid-piece. Deploy when you want to convert vague ambition into "this is actually doable if you just do the boring thing most people won't."

**Example:** "Here's how you get ahead of 99% of people in 6 to 12 months become brutally aware of two things. … Observe the masses and see where mindless action leads. / It's not pretty at all by any means."
— "How To Get Ahead Of 99% Of People (In 6-12 Months)," YouTube (2022)

**Example:** "Most people don't try to become the top 5% in any skill because they don't realize that the 95% is comprised of people who put 0% effort outside of anything they were told to do by their parents or society. / 30-60 minutes a day for 6-12 months puts you ahead of almost everyone."
— "The Future-Proof Skill Stack (Learn In This Order)," thedankoe.com (2024)

## 9. The Escalating ">" Ladder (drill from symptom to root)

**What:** Chain a sequence of deepening statements with literal greater-than signs, each one pushing past the surface answer to the more honest, more painful one underneath — until it bottoms out at a core fear or driver. The `>` is doing visible work: it signals "go deeper" on the page. He uses the same ladder logic to compress a multi-stage progression ("project → product," "external to internal") into a single rhythmic line.

**When:** To model honest self-inquiry, root-cause a problem, or show the reader that the real motivation is several layers below the stated one. Use sparingly, at the moment you want to reach the emotional truth under a topic.

**Example:** "'I don't like how I look' > 'I'm uncomfortable taking my shirt off' > 'My wife/husband deserves better' > 'I don't have the energy to build the life I want' > 'People won't respect me if I keep doing this' > 'I don't want to end up alone'"
— "How To Think Originally," thedankoe.com (2025)

**Example:** "That means working from external to internal. / From superficial to profound. / Surface level to the core. / Tip of the iceberg to the bottom of the ocean."
— "How To Profit From Solving Your Own Problems," thedankoe.com (2022)
