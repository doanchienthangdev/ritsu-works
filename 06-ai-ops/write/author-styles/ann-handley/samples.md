# Ann Handley — Samples

<!-- Short, attributed, fair-use excerpts (≤25 words) from the copyrighted book Everybody Writes
     (2nd ed., Wiley, 2023). Each teaches a specific mechanic. Never reproduced at length. -->

## The personal-confession open

> "Last Tuesday, for the first time in my life, I did a push-up."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** The opening hook as small vulnerable confession — concrete, first-person, and the seed of the book's governing metaphor.

---

> "You might be picking this up for the first time. (Hi, new friend.)"
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** Greeting one reader directly; the parenthetical aside carrying the warmth.

## The fragment as percussion

> "Great writing makes us feel. Period."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** A short declarative landed with a one-word fragment for emphasis.

---

> "No. It does not."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023 (on whether good writing equals good grammar)

**Demonstrates:** The two-beat fragment answer — terse, conversational, decisive.

## Rhetorical question, answered curtly

> "Feels formulaic, right? It is. Feels boring? That, too."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** Posing the reader's half-thought and answering in two words to keep pace.

## The homely / grotesque analogy

> "If writing can feel like birthing a Honda Civic, then writing a book is like birthing a car dealership full of them."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** The everyday-object metaphor stretched for comic scale.

---

> "Most will be isht. That's okay. Keep those Franken-steps lurching forward."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023 (on the ugly first draft)

**Demonstrates:** Plain colloquial diction + coined label ("Franken-steps") + reassurance, all in three short lines.

## The before/after teaching pair

> "Tell Me: Ready-to-use Asian sauces and spices. Tell Me Without Telling Me: Meet your new pantry staple."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023 (via Omsom)

**Demonstrates:** Her default teaching unit — flat version vs. better version, then a one-line "why."

## The portable craft rule

> "The simplest version of a word is the strongest version of a word."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** A rule compressed into one balanced, memorable sentence a reader can pin to the wall.

---

> "Writing is editing."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** Maximal compression — two words carrying the whole argument of a chapter.

## Name-the-enemy, then reassure

> "Harsh, I know. But no hate. I'm here for you."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023

**Demonstrates:** The hard truth softened instantly so the standard lands without resentment.

## The generous close

> "Done is better than perfect."
> — *Everybody Writes*, 2023 (Epilogue)

**Demonstrates:** The four-word mic-drop ending — action-forward, no throat-clearing send-off.
