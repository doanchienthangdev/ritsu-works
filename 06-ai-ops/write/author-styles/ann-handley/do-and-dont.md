# Ann Handley — Do & Don't

| Emulate (do) | Avoid (don't) |
|---|---|
| Open with a small confession, a vivid scene, or a literal "Hi, new friend." | Open with "According to a study…", "I'm excited to announce…", or "The purpose of this post is…" |
| Keep sentences short (6–14 words); drop 1–4-word fragments on their own line to land a point. | Write long, modifier-clogged sentences that bury the idea and make the reader work. |
| Run one homely governing metaphor through the whole piece (push-ups, a pool, a Honda Civic). | Gesture at abstractions ("engaging," "world-class," "impactful") instead of showing the thing. |
| Teach with a before/after pair: "This is okay… This is way better…" + a one-line "why." | Assert the rule bare and expect it to land as a platitude. |
| Swap company-centric "we/I" for reader-centric "you"; make the reader the hero. | Broadcast to a crowd in cold corporate voice; address "folks" in a stadium. |
| Put the joke and the warmth in the parentheses. | Strip out personality to please a committee (Hot Dog Writing). |
| After any hard truth, reassure immediately ("Harsh, I know. But no hate."). | Be clever or cutting at the reader's expense. |
| Choose the simplest version of every word; hunt and kill jargon, Frankenwords, and Weblish. | Bulk words with -ization/-ize/-ment ("prioritization," "incentivize," "learnings"). |
| Format for the scroller: short paragraphs, subheads, bold, white space. | Stack big impenetrable blocks of text with no air. |
| Close on momentum or generosity — ideally a four-word fragment that flips focus to the reader. | End on a generic upbeat send-off ("in conclusion, the future is bright"). |

**The one test:** Could you read this aloud to one nervous-about-writing friend across a kitchen table — greet them, make them laugh once, teach them one thing, and reassure them — without sounding like a press release? If not, it isn't Ann Handley. (And: could you cut the throat-clearing opener and the jargon and lose nothing? Then you must.)
