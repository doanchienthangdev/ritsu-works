# The anatomy of a real McKinsey deliverable — the GENRE-KEYED format system (v2.2)

> **Rebuilt from THREE full primary-source reports read end-to-end** (v2.2, capability thinking-toolkit) — not blog deconstructions (the v2.1 baseline). `mckinsey-sell` reads this to render a deliverable that matches what McKinsey actually ships. Every `grounded_in` citation in `knowledge/mckinsey-templates.yaml` traces here.
>
> **The three real reports (the short codes used throughout):**
> - **[SIG]** — *Sustainable and inclusive growth impact report 2025* (21 pp.) — a **brand / corporate-impact** report. Near chart-free; template-driven; big-number-led.
> - **[AI25]** — *The state of AI in 2025: Agents, innovation, and transformation* (QuantumBlack, Nov 2025, 30 pp., 19 exhibits) — a **survey/data** report. Exhibit-dense; action-title exhibits.
> - **[B2B26]** — *The surprising economics of B2B growth* (Growth Marketing & Sales, Jun 2026, 18 pp., 7 exhibits) — a **thought-leadership POV** report. Tight single-spine SCR argument.
>
> **The single most important meta-lesson:** McKinsey does **NOT** have one house format — it has a **genre-keyed format system**. The three reports diverge on header style, front-matter device, and exhibit density *by genre*, while converging on a small set of non-negotiables (source footer on every exhibit, one-message-per-exhibit, named close, city-tagged author box, governing-thought-first).

## 0. The genre matrix (encode this FIRST — it governs every choice below)

| Dimension | **[SIG]** brand/impact | **[AI25]** survey/data | **[B2B26]** thought-leadership POV |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Front-matter device** | Visual 5-tile TOC + persistent web-style top nav; NO exec summary — a **managing-partner letter** + a big-number "approach" page substitute | **NO TOC**; large-type **abstract panel** + a numbered **"Key findings" 1–6** list IS the exec summary | **NO TOC**; a boxed **"At a glance"** with 5 bold-italic-lead-in bullets IS the exec summary |
| **Section header style** | **Two-register**: short serif **topic label** + a declarative first-person standfirst sentence beneath | **Full declarative ACTION-TITLE sentences** at both section AND exhibit level | **Two devices on purpose**: section headers = serif **topic labels**; exhibit titles = full **action-title sentences** |
| **Exhibit density** | ~0 data charts (hero illustrations + 1 stat stack + 1 timeline) | **19** numbered data exhibits, ~1/page | **7** numbered data exhibits |
| **Narrative arc** | 5 equal proof-pattern pillars (repeated 2×2 lens) | One thesis in 5 named movements + 6 expert commentary panels | Classic **SCR** spine: situation → complication (raised baseline) → resolution (3 engines + integration) |
| **Close** | Emotional letter close + back-cover URL/email (no checklist) | **Single reflective paragraph** (prescribe-by-example; no checklist) | Distributed **"Here is what leading organizations do differently"** action-bullet lists + urgency historical-analog coda |
| **Methodology / authors** | Light: ONE p.4 footnote + per-spread inline `Source:`; single signed author (Sternfels) | **"About the research"** box (field dates, N, GDP-weighting) + city-tagged multi-author byline | Methodology in **exhibit footnotes** (not a box) + city-tagged author box |

**Rule for `mckinsey-sell`:** classify the deliverable into one of these three genres FIRST, then pull the matching column. The most common bug (re-confirmed here) is applying [AI25]/[B2B26] action-title discipline to a [SIG]-genre brand piece — or applying [SIG]'s topic-label two-register headers to a data report whose exhibit titles MUST be action sentences.

## 1. Front matter & the In-Brief substitute (the biggest upgrade over v2.1)

The physical device differs by genre, and two of three reports have NO classic exec summary:

- **[B2B26] — the canonical "At a glance" box (p.2).** Header `At a glance` in serif; **five em-dash bullets, each a BOLD-ITALIC lead-in sentence then a roman explanation.** Verbatim lead-ins: *"B2B sales and marketing have reached a decisive moment." / "Omnichannel is no longer a differentiator—it is assumed." / "E-commerce powers the commercial core." / "A widening performance gap is emerging—driven by three reinforcing engines." / "Together, hyperpersonalization, scaled AI, and disciplined commercial governance form a new self-reinforcing operating system…"* → **the gold-standard In-Brief: bold-italic lead-in + roman gloss, em-dash bulleted, answer-first.**
- **[AI25] — the "Key findings" numbered list IS the exec summary (p.2).** A large-type abstract on a **pale-blue panel** then a **numbered 1–6 list**, each item = **bold lead-in phrase + a sentence containing a HARD STATISTIC** (verbatim finding 3: *"Positive leading indicators…64 percent say AI is enabling their innovation. However, just 39 percent report EBIT impact at the enterprise level."*). Drop-cap opener on the following page. → **for survey/data, the In-Brief is a NUMBERED key-findings list with a stat in every bullet, not prose.**
- **[SIG] — NO exec summary; a signed letter + a stat page.** The "Message from our global managing partner" (Sternfels, p.3, ink-signed) + an "Our approach to impact" stat page (p.4). A **visual 5-tile thumbnail TOC** + a persistent top-nav bar (web-microsite styling). → **brand pieces substitute a signed framing letter + a headline-stat page; a visual TOC is acceptable.**

**TOC rule:** long brand report → **visual thumbnail TOC** [SIG]; ≤30-pp. article/POV → **NO TOC** [AI25, B2B26]. (v2.1's "always include a TOC" was wrong for the article genres.)

## 2. Narrative arc

- **[B2B26] — the textbook SCR spine** (the analytical/POV model): Situation (3rd inflection after consumerization + COVID) → Complication (the baseline rose; the divide widens to **60% vs 21%** double-digit growth) → Resolution (3 reinforcing engines — hyperpersonalization → AI amplification → ABM governance → "a new operating system for B2B sales"). Each engine section: **oversized blue pull-quote → exhibit → "Here is what leading organizations do differently" action-bullet list.** Closes on a 2016 historical analog forcing urgency.
- **[AI25] — one thesis in 5 named movements + interleaved human voice.** Thesis = *broad adoption, thin enterprise value; high performers show the way via ambition + workflow redesign.* The data is broken by **6 "McKinsey commentary" expert panels** (pale-blue, circular headshot) + **large standalone blue pull-quotes** that restate the prior exhibit's takeaway. → **for survey reports, interleave expert-commentary + restating pull-quotes between exhibit clusters; never run exhibits back-to-back with no human "so-what."**
- **[SIG] — a repeated proof-pattern, not a linear argument.** Five equal pillars; every topic recurs through the SAME four fixed lenses; emotional-bookended open/close.

## 3. Header discipline — action-title AND topic-label, per genre

- **[AI25] section headers ARE full declarative sentences** (serif, sentence case): *"AI use continues to broaden but remains primarily in pilot phases"; "Organizations with ambitious AI agendas are seeing the most benefit."* **EVERY exhibit title is the chart's one-sentence takeaway** + a descriptor+unit subtitle: *"No more than 10 percent of respondents report scaling AI agents in any individual function."*
- **[B2B26] exhibit titles are full sentences ending in a period**: *"Omnichannel breakdowns are the primary reasons why buyers switch suppliers."; "An AI flywheel is emerging—and budgets are reinforcing it."* — BUT its **section headers are serif TOPIC LABELS** (noun phrases): *"The new survival threshold"; "The widening performance divide."* Sub-section heads can be bold run-in labels (*"Engine one: Hyperpersonalization as a performance multiplier"*).
- **[SIG] uses a TWO-REGISTER header**: a short serif topic label (*"Jobs and reskilling"*) + a declarative first-person standfirst (*"We equip people with the skills and access they need to secure higher-paying jobs."*).

**Encodable header rule:**
```
genre = brand        → header = {topic_label (serif) + first_person_standfirst}; exhibits = hero illustrations
genre = survey/data  → section headers = ACTION SENTENCES; exhibit titles = ACTION SENTENCES; interleave pull-quotes + commentary
genre = pov/analytical → section headers = TOPIC LABELS (noun-phrase); exhibit titles = ACTION SENTENCES (period-terminated); interleave pull-quotes
```
**The pull-quote** is a third header-class device [AI25, B2B26]: an oversized blue serif/sans quote between sections lifting the single most important sentence verbatim. Add a `pull_quote` IR element. **The "Pro tip"** [B2B26]: a recurring left-margin device (label `Pro tip` in blue, set off by thin rules).

## 4. Exhibit conventions & the source footer (pinned verbatim from two real reports)

**Exhibit scaffold (identical across [AI25] and [B2B26]):** small grey **`Exhibit N`** kicker → **bold takeaway-sentence TITLE** → **descriptor + unit SUBTITLE** (`% of respondents`, base **n**) → superscript ¹/² **FOOTNOTES** → **`Note:` line** → **SOURCE FOOTER** → wordmark **`McKinsey & Company`** lower-left. One exhibit per page, no borders, generous white space; the body lead-in paragraph sits ABOVE and cross-references it in parentheses (*"…(Exhibit 2)"*).

- **The `Note:` rounding line (near-universal on stacked):** *"Note: Figures may not sum to 100%, because of rounding."*
- **SOURCE FOOTER — two verbatim forms:**
  - **analysis form** (the internal canonical): `Source: <data source>; McKinsey analysis`.
  - **survey form** [AI25]: *"Source: McKinsey Global Survey on the state of AI, 1,993 participants…, June 25–July 29, 2025"*; [B2B26] spells the **full 13-country list every time**: *"Source: McKinsey Global B2B Pulse Survey, Dec 2025, n = 3,664; Australia, Brazil, Chile, China, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Spain, UK, US."*
- **Footnote-1 convention [B2B26]:** fn1 is the **verbatim survey QUESTION**.
- **[SIG] brand-genre:** no numbered exhibits; per-spread inline `Source:` footers, grey-italic portrait captions, blue hyperlinks with ▶ play glyphs, recognition badges as micro-exhibits, and the p.4 **big-number stat stack** (`16% / 1M / >80%`).

## 5. The close — genre-keyed (NONE uses a generic bulleted recommendations list)

- **[B2B26] (analytical model):** DISTRIBUTED **"Here is what leading organizations do differently:"** action-bullet lists at the end of each section + an **urgency coda** (a 2016 historical analog: "5× faster growth, 30% higher acquisition efficiency"; then *"Clearing the baseline keeps organizations in the game. Building an integrated, data-driven revenue engine determines who sets the pace."*).
- **[AI25] (survey model):** a single reflective **"prescribe-by-example" paragraph** ("emulate the high performers"), no bullets, no CTA.
- **[SIG] (brand model):** an emotional close + recurring video CTAs + a **back cover** with the report URL + feedback email.

**Rule:** `pov/analytical` → distributed per-section action bullets + urgency-analog coda; `survey` → one prescribe-by-example paragraph; `brand` → emotional close + back-cover surface. A generic bulleted "recommendations" list is **memo/deck-only**.

## 6. Methodology & the city-tagged author box (the rigor that earns authority)

- **[AI25] "About the research" box (p.29):** field dates, N=1,993 in 105 nations, **GDP-weighting** — + the **city-tagged byline** (*"Alex Singla is the global leader of QuantumBlack… and a senior partner in McKinsey's Chicago office; … Lareina Yee is a senior partner in the Bay Area office, where Michael Chui is a senior fellow…"*) + a named editor + the Insights-App QR back cover.
- **[B2B26] — methodology in EXHIBIT FOOTNOTES, not a box** (Ex 1 fn1 spells the k-means clustering + segment sizes). Same city-tagged author box + named editor + `Copyright © 2026 McKinsey & Company`.
- **[SIG] — light:** one p.4 footnote + a signed letter (no research box).
- **The constant across all three:** `<Name> is a <title> in McKinsey's <City> office`; a collaborative byline up front; the back-matter `<Month Year> / Copyright © <Year> McKinsey & Company / Designed by McKinsey Global Publishing` + Insights-App QR.

## 7. How `mckinsey-sell` uses this (the actionable diff over v2.1)

1. **Classify the genre FIRST** (§0) — route header/front-matter/close/methodology by the genre column.
2. **Genre-keyed front-matter** (§1): visual-TOC+letter [brand] / numbered-key-findings [survey] / "At a glance" bold-italic box [POV].
3. **Genre-keyed headers** (§3): exhibit titles = action sentences for survey+POV always; **section** headers = action sentences for survey but **topic labels** for POV; brand = two-register. Add `pull_quote` + `pro_tip` elements.
4. **Source footer = two verbatim forms** (§4) + the `Note:` rounding line + question-in-footnote-1.
5. **Genre-keyed close** (§5), not a generic recommendations list.
6. **Methodology in a box [survey] OR exhibit footnotes [POV]**; city-tagged author box + Insights-App QR universal.
7. **Exhibits via `/dataviz`** (v2.2 integration): each exhibit's action-title is the `--message`+`--title`, the survey/analysis line is `--source`, `--style` flows the brand — so the chart matches the genre's caption style (action sentence for survey/POV; topic label for brand) and carries the source footer the renderer already mandates.

Refresh when McKinsey materially changes house style (the 2024 Bower/McKinsey-Sans rebrand is partially visible in [AI25]'s serif-title/sans-body split).
