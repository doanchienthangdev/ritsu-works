# Emirates maintained premium pricing through experience differentiation while competitors cut fares

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 22-24, chunk 1/7 (confidence 0.8, pending founder review).
>
> "Most airlines have had to reduce their fares considerably to remain competitive. Not Emirates. The company has maintained fares while managing healthy yields thanks to its customer value–driven marketing approach and its service proposition, for which customers are still willing to pay a premium."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What happened.** The Emirates case opens Chapter 1 as the primary example of customer value-driven marketing. While most airlines reduced fares considerably to remain competitive, Emirates maintained fares and achieved healthy yields by pursuing a customer-focused value proposition: "The Emirates Experience."

**The mechanism.** Rather than competing on price or aircraft quality, Emirates competed on emotional differentiation. "Beyond the functional benefit of air travel, Emirates marketed its services as 'The Emirates Experience,' a genuine passion for comfort and attention to detail. Customers didn't just fly Emirates; they experienced it." Specific tools: the ICE in-flight system, Chauffeur Drive service in 70+ cities, premium lounge access, and the Skywards loyalty program (the first of its kind in the industry).

**The shift from broadcast to engagement.** Emirates moved from outspending competitors on traditional media to building brand "tribes" via social media. The Hello Tomorrow campaign (2012) reached 43 million viewers across 85 countries via BBC, CNN, and Yahoo. Awareness jumped from 38% to 69% among viewers. The campaign positioned Emirates as a lifestyle catalyst — connecting "people's dreams, hopes, and aspirations" — rather than a transport provider.

**The result.** Emirates became the world's 4th most valuable airline brand at an estimated $7 billion (2018 Brand Finance Global 500), the only non-American airline in the top five.

**Relevance.** This case grounds the chapter's central thesis: companies that create superior customer value can maintain pricing power. The pattern — emotional differentiation + community building + lifestyle positioning — is the template the chapter returns to throughout.
