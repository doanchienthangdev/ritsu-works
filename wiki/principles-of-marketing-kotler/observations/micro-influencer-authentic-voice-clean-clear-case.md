# Micro-Influencers (500-1,500 Followers) Drove 19% Sales Lift for Clean & Clear

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 618–623, chunk 5 (confidence 0.8, pending founder review).
>
> "a recent campaign by Johnson & Johnson featured teens with only 500 to 1,500 Instagram followers as stars in videos for its Clean & Clear skincare products."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

J&J's Clean & Clear used teens with 500-1,500 Instagram followers (authentic voices, not follower counts) → 3.2M YouTube views in 9 months, +19% overall sales, +50% on one product after years stagnant.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "In the nine months following launch, the influencer campaign helped boost Clean & Clear's sales by 19 percent. One product in particular—Clean & Clear oil-absorbing sheets—saw a 50-percent sales lift after years of stagnant growth."
>
> "instead of using mega-celebrities as influencers, J&J opted for influencers who weren't famous per se but are doing things that other kids responded to authentically, letting them tell their story and building the products and brands from there."
>

## For Ritsu

Directly applicable. Budget-constrained EdTech startups shouldn't chase mega-celebrity study influencers. Identify students with 500-2,000 followers in class Discord servers / subject subreddits who genuinely use Ritsu — give them early access + referral incentive, let them create authentic content. Measurable: free-to-paid conversion from their referral links.
