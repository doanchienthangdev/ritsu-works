# Channel Design Decisions Are Long-Term Commitments — Costly to Reverse

> **Source.** [Chapter 12](../chapters/chapter-12-marketing-channels.md), book pp. 346–358, chunk 7 (confidence 0.9, auto-accepted).
>
> "Distribution channel decisions often involve long-term commitments to other firms. Companies such as Ford, McDonald's, or Nike can easily change their advertising, pricing, or promotion programs. But when they set up distribution channels through contracts with franchisees, independent dealers, or large retailers, they cannot readily replace these channels."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Unlike ads/pricing/product, channel decisions involve long-term commitments. Once set up via contracts with franchisees/dealers/retailers, cannot be readily replaced with company-owned stores. Evaluate alternatives on economic + control + adaptability. Long-term commitment must be greatly superior on economic+control to justify reduced adaptability.

## Additional quotes

> "Channels often involve long-term commitments, yet the company wants to keep the channel flexible so that it can adapt to environmental changes. To be considered, a channel involving long-term commitments should be greatly superior on economic and control grounds."
>

## For Ritsu

Before signing any LMS integration agreement or app-store exclusivity deal, treat as long-term commitment — not quick growth hack. Evaluate on economic (rev share math), control (who owns user relationship, pricing, data), adaptability (can Ritsu exit if LMS declines?). Bad integration deal harder to unwind than bad ad campaign.
