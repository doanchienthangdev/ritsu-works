# Personal Sources Most Effective in Info Search Despite Commercial Sources Being Most Available

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 169–170, chunk 8 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Traditionally, consumers have received the most information about a product from commercial sources—those controlled by the marketer. The most effective sources, however, tend to be personal. Commercial sources normally inform the buyer, but personal sources legitimize or evaluate products for the buyer. Few advertising campaigns can be as effective as a next-door neighbor leaning over the fence and raving about a wonderful experience with a product you are considering."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Consumers receive most info from commercial sources but find personal sources most effective (legitimize/evaluate vs merely inform). Yelp's 171M+ reviews = digital 'neighbor's fence.'

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Increasingly, that 'neighbor's fence' is a digital one. Today, consumers share product opinions, images, and experiences freely across social media."
>

## For Ritsu

For Ritsu, the most powerful acquisition signal is a student telling a classmate 'I used this and got an A.' Investing in referral mechanics, shareable quiz results, study group features pays higher return than equivalent paid-ad spend. Freemium already reduces commercial-source friction; missing piece is designing for word-of-mouth amplification inside study groups.
