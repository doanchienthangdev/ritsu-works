# Disintermediation Consistently Destroys Incumbents Relying on Structural Advantage Alone

> **Source.** [Chapter 12](../chapters/chapter-12-marketing-channels.md), book pp. 353–354, chunk 6 (confidence 0.9, auto-accepted).
>
> "Once-mighty Blockbuster fell into bankruptcy and shuttered its doors as Netflix surged."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

3 converging cases. Blockbuster (shelf space, brand) destroyed by Netflix. Toys 'R' Us (pioneered superstore) bankrupted by Walmart then Amazon (28% toy purchases moved online). Music downloads fell 24% as streaming rose 42% in one year. Pattern: structural advantages erode when new channel format better serves customer needs at lower cost.

## Additional quotes

> "Toys'R'Us pioneered the superstore format that once made it the go-to place for buying toys."
>
> "Music downloads fell 24 percent last year while on-demand audio streaming rose 42 percent."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's ICP students currently use Anki, Quizlet, Notion — all incumbents with structural advantages (large user bases, habit, integrations). Pattern suggests these incumbents vulnerable precisely at point where new format (AI-native, multi-format, single-upload) better serves active-learning need. Move fast on incumbent channels (flashcards, quizzes) before more AI-native flashcard tool does same to Ritsu.
