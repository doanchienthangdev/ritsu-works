# Price Increases Can Signal Quality and Paradoxically Raise Demand

> **Source.** [Chapter 11](../chapters/chapter-11-pricing-strategies-additional.md), book pp. 153–153, chunk 2 (confidence 0.9, auto-accepted).
>
> "In the very, very early days, we charged 1,200 dollars for the Peloton bike for the first couple of months. And what turned out happening is we heard from customers that the bike must be poorly built if you're charging 1,200 dollars for it. We charged 2,000 dollars for it, and sales increased, because people said, 'Oh, it must be a quality bike.'"
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Peloton raised bike price $1,200 → $2,000 → demand INCREASED. Lower price signaled poor quality; higher price signaled premium. Price functions as quality signal when buyers lack direct quality info.

## For Ritsu

Counter-intuitive Ritsu lesson: under-pricing the paid tier may signal poor quality. If 'serious learners' (specialty product framing) are target, price below $10/mo may be SELF-SABOTAGE. Test premium price ($25-30/mo) — may convert better than $7.
