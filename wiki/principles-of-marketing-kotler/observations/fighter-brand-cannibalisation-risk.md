# Fighter Brands Protect vs Low-Price Rivals but Risk Cannibalizing Premium

> **Source.** [Chapter 11](../chapters/chapter-11-pricing-strategies-additional.md), book pp. 1072–1119, chunk 4 (confidence 0.85, auto-accepted).
>
> "The company might launch a low-price 'fighter brand' — adding a lower-price item to the line or creating a separate lower-price brand."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

P&G launched Luvs, Charmin Essentials, Tide Simply Clean to counter private labels. Risk: image spill (degrades premium quality) + internal cannibalization (budget buyers switch from full-margin to lower-margin).

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Companies must use caution when introducing fighter brands, as such brands can tarnish the image of the main brand. In addition, although they may attract budget buyers away from lower-priced rivals, they can also take business away from the firm's higher-margin brands."
>

## For Ritsu

Pre-PMF: don't launch fighter brand. If ChatGPT/Notion AI commoditize features, response is differentiation (active-learning depth), not cheap Ritsu variant. Fighter brand introduces brand confusion at scale Ritsu can't afford.
