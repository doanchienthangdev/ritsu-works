# Digital/social media shifted marketing from broadcast to continuous engagement

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 42-49, chunk 6/7 (confidence 0.8, pending founder review).
>
> "Greater consumer empowerment means that companies can no longer rely on marketing by intrusion. Instead, they must practice marketing by attraction—creating market offerings and messages that engage consumers rather than interrupt them. Hence, most marketers now combine their mass-media marketing efforts with a rich mix of online, mobile, and social media marketing that promotes brand-consumer engagement, brand conversations, and brand advocacy among customers."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What the text observes.** By the late chapter section on digital, mobile, and social media, a clear before/after pattern emerges:

- **Old model**: Outspend competitors on big media ads and celebrity endorsers who "talk at rather than to customers."
- **New model**: Customer-engagement marketing — fostering direct and continuous customer involvement in shaping brand conversations, brand experiences, and brand community.

The text states: "Greater consumer empowerment means that companies can no longer rely on marketing by intrusion. Instead, they must practice marketing by attraction — creating market offerings and messages that engage consumers rather than interrupt them."

**Quantitative texture from the chapter.** The digital context is stark:
- More than 4 billion people (55% of the world's population) are online.
- 71% of Americans keep their mobile phone next to them when they sleep.
- 79% of smartphone users have made a purchase online using a mobile device.
- More than 40% of all online purchases are now made from mobile devices.
- Mobile advertising accounts for more than two-thirds of all digital ad spending.

**The Red Bull social media model.** Red Bull has 50M+ Facebook followers, 2M Twitter, 8M Instagram. Its social pages "hardly mention the company's products at all. Instead, they promote Red Bull's pedal-to-the-metal lifestyle." Lifestyle first, product second.

**Relevance for Ritsu.** Ritsu operates in a digital-native context where this shift is already complete. The implication: the product's social proof strategy should be attraction-based (user-shared mastery moments, result screenshots, community spaces) rather than intrusion-based (paid ads, cold outreach). At 100-paying-PMF scale, engineering advocacy from delighted users is the highest-leverage marketing motion available.
