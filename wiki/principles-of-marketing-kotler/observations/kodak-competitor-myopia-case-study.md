# Kodak's Collapse as Canonical Competitor Myopia Case

> **Source.** [Chapter 18](../chapters/chapter-18-creating-competitive-advantage.md), book pp. 528–529, chunk 3 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "It wasn't competing film makers that brought Kodak down. It was the competitor Kodak didn't see soon enough — digital photography and cameras that used no film at all."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Kodak (85% camera, 90% film share) went bankrupt 2012 by defining competition narrowly (other film makers). Missed real competitor = digital photography needing no film. INVENTED first digital camera 1975, shelved fearing cannibalization. Failure = competitive definition + willingness to cannibalize.

## Additional quotes

> "Failing to recognize the mass-market potential of digital photography and fearing that digital technology would cannibalize its precious film business, Kodak shelved the digital project."
>
> "They were a company stuck in time. Their history became a liability."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's Kodak risk: being proud of 'we generate flashcards from PDFs' while missing when AI makes document-based learning obsolete (voice, AR, ambient AI). Lesson: define success as 'student achieves mastery' not 'student uploads PDF.' Watch emerging UX paradigms even if they threaten upload-and-quiz model.
