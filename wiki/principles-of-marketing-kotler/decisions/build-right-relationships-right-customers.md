# Segment customers by profitability × loyalty and invest accordingly — not uniformly

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 34-42, chunk 5/7 (confidence 0.9, auto-accepted).
>
> "The point here is an important one: Different types of customers require different engagement and relationship management strategies. The goal is to build the right relationships with the right customers."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**The recommendation.** Chapter 1 explicitly presents a decision framework (Figure 1.5) for managing customer equity. The underlying strategic directive: "The goal is to build the right relationships with the right customers."

**The 2×2 matrix:**
- **Strangers** (low profitability, low projected loyalty): Don't invest. Make money on every transaction if possible, but no relationship investment.
- **Butterflies** (high profitability, low loyalty — good fit but transient): Enjoy them for the moment. Create satisfying and profitable transactions. Do not invest in converting them to loyal customers — "efforts to convert butterflies into loyal customers are rarely successful."
- **True friends** (high profitability, high loyalty — strong fit): Make continuous relationship investments to delight, engage, nurture, retain, and grow. Turn them into "true believers" who return and spread word-of-mouth.
- **Barnacles** (low profitability, high loyalty — limited fit): Attempt to improve profitability via upsell, fee increase, or reduced service. If they cannot be made profitable, "fire" them.

**The actionable implication.** Before investing in CRM programs, loyalty tools, or retention campaigns, a company must first *classify* its existing customers. Applying retention tactics uniformly across all four groups wastes resources — spending on Barnacles and Strangers is destructive; the highest return is investing in True friends and capturing Butterflies at peak transactional value.

**For Ritsu.** At 100-paying-PMF scale, Ritsu likely does not yet have enough data to formally run this matrix. But the framework suggests an immediate diagnostic: among the paying users, which ones exhibit high engagement (True friends) vs. low engagement despite paying (Barnacles)? The founder-led onboarding (SOP-CUSTOMER-006) is the right tool to identify True friends empirically — not through data alone, but through direct conversation.
