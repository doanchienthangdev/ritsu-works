# Segment Beyond Birth Year: Use Lifestyle, Life Stage, Values Within Cohorts

> **Source.** [Chapter 3](../chapters/chapter-03-analyzing-the-marketing-environment.md), book pp. 94–94, chunk 3 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Marketers need to form more precise age-specific segments within each group. More important, defining people by their birth date may be less effective than segmenting them by lifestyle, life stage, or the common values they seek in the products they buy."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Generational cohort labels are useful starting points but insufficient as sole segmentation. Each generation spans decades/socioeconomic levels. Overlay lifestyle, life stage, and values/benefits sought for actionable segments.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Some experts warn that marketers need to be careful about turning off one generation each time they craft a product or message that appeals effectively to another."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's 6 ICP personas (students, self-learners, engineers, researchers, educators, professionals) already implement this — each defined by life stage and learning purpose, not age. GTM should resist collapsing to 'Gen Z students' alone — a 35-year-old engineer learning a new framework has different activation triggers and WTP.
