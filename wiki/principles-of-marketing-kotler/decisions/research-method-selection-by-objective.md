# Match Research Method to Objective: Observation→Exploratory, Survey→Descriptive, Experiment→Causal

> **Source.** [Chapter 4](../chapters/chapter-04-managing-marketing-information.md), book pp. 125–128, chunk 4 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Whereas observation is best suited for exploratory research and surveys for descriptive research, experimental research is best suited for gathering causal information."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Direct mapping: exploratory → observational (incl. ethnographic), descriptive → survey, causal → experimental. Surveys tell you what; experiments tell you why. Microsoft Bing A/B test: new headline → +12% ad revenue within hours.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Online controlled experiments can be simple and inexpensive to run with immediate and revealing results."
>
> "The objective of exploratory research is to gather preliminary information that will help define the problem and suggest hypotheses. The objective of descriptive research is to describe things. The objective of causal research is to test hypotheses about cause-and-effect relationships."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu PMF sprint: (1) Exploratory — observe 5 users completing first session end-to-end (ethnographic, no survey). (2) Descriptive — survey: 'Which activity type did you use most? Why did you stop?' (3) Causal — A/B test onboarding flow variants in ops-supabase experiment table. Do not survey to find root cause of churn; A/B test the hypothesis derived from ethnographic observation.
