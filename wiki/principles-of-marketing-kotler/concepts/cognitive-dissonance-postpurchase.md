# Cognitive Dissonance (Postpurchase Conflict)

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 171–172, chunk 8 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Almost all major purchases, however, result in cognitive dissonance, or discomfort caused by postpurchase conflict."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Discomfort from postpurchase conflict — satisfied with chosen brand benefits but uneasy about forgone alternatives. Marketers manage via after-sale communications (evidence, support). Bad word of mouth travels farther/faster than good.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "consumers feel at least some postpurchase dissonance for every purchase."
>
> "Bad word of mouth often travels farther and faster than good word of mouth. It can quickly damage consumer attitudes about a company and its products."
>
> "The larger the negative gap between expectations and performance, the greater the consumer's dissatisfaction. This suggests that sellers should promise only what their brands can deliver so that buyers are satisfied."
>
