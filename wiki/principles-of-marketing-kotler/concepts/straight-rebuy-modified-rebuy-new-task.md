# Three Business Buying Situations

> **Source.** [Chapter 6](../chapters/chapter-06-business-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 185–186, chunk 3 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "In a straight rebuy, the buyer reorders something without any modifications. It is usually handled on a routine basis by the purchasing department."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

3 archetypes: straight rebuy (routine reorder, no modification, handled by purchasing); modified rebuy (changes to specs/price/terms/supplier — 'in' feels pressure, 'out' sees opportunity); new task (first-time purchase, most participants, full 8-stage process, greatest opportunity + challenge).

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "In a modified rebuy, the buyer wants to modify product specifications, prices, terms, or suppliers."
>
> "A company buying a product or service for the first time faces a new task situation. In such cases, the greater the cost or risk, the larger the number of decision participants and the greater the company's efforts to collect information."
>
> "The new task situation is the marketer's greatest opportunity and challenge."
>
> "The buyer makes the fewest decisions in the straight rebuy and the most in the new task decision."
>

## For Ritsu

University/corporate adoption of Ritsu = NEW TASK (highest complexity). Implies: more decision participants, more info-seeking, longer cycle. Implication for early Ritsu B2B: invest in pilot programs (lowers risk perception) and detailed proof points before approaching first institutional sale.
