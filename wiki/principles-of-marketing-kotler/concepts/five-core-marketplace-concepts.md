# Five core marketplace concepts

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 26-30, chunk 3/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "We examine five core customer and marketplace concepts: (1) needs, wants, and demands; (2) market offerings (products, services, and experiences); (3) value and satisfaction; (4) exchanges and relationships; and (5) markets."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** Chapter 1, Objective 1-2, explicitly enumerates the five core customer and marketplace concepts that form the analytical foundation for marketing strategy.

**The five concepts:**

1. **Needs, wants, and demands.**
   - *Needs*: states of felt deprivation — physical (food, warmth), social (belonging), individual (knowledge, self-expression). Marketers did not create these.
   - *Wants*: the form needs take as shaped by culture and personality (an American *needs* food, *wants* a Big Mac).
   - *Demands*: wants backed by buying power. People demand products with benefits that add up to the most value.

2. **Market offerings** — some combination of products, services, information, or experiences offered to satisfy a need or want. Includes physical products, services, and also persons, places, organizations, information, ideas, and causes.

3. **Value and satisfaction** — customers form expectations and buy accordingly. Satisfied customers buy again; dissatisfied ones switch and disparage. Marketers must set the *right* level of expectations — too low = insufficient buyers; too high = disappointed buyers.

4. **Exchanges and relationships** — exchange is the act of obtaining a desired object by offering something in return. Marketing consists of actions to create, maintain, and grow desirable exchange relationships.

5. **Markets** — the set of all actual and potential buyers sharing a particular need or want that can be satisfied through exchange relationships.

**Marketing myopia warning.** Within concept 2, the text introduces *marketing myopia*: the mistake of paying more attention to the specific products offered than to the benefits and experiences they produce. The canonical example: a customer does not need a quarter-inch drill bit — they need a quarter-inch hole.

**For Ritsu.** The needs/wants/demands distinction is directly applicable: users *need* to learn (need), they *want* an easy, engaging way to do it (want), and they have at least some willingness to pay (demand varies by segment). Ritsu's positioning as active-learning-vs-passive-reading is a strong needs-level insight. The marketing myopia risk: fixating on specific activity types (flashcard, quiz) rather than the underlying experience of genuine mastery.
