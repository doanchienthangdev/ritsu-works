# Global Pricing: Price Escalation + Local Market Strategies

> **Source.** [Chapter 19](../chapters/chapter-19-the-global-marketplace.md), book pp. 571–572, chunk 6 (confidence 0.93, auto-accepted).
>
> "An Apple 11-inch iPad Pro that sells for $799 in the United States goes for $995 in the United Kingdom. Why? Apple faces a price escalation problem."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Foreign prices typically higher than domestic due to transport, tariffs, importer/retailer margins — can require 2-5x home price. Strategies: simpler product versions, local manufacturing to avoid tariffs, tiered pricing. Internet makes global price differences visible → standardization pressure.

## Additional quotes

> "Depending on these added costs, a product may have to sell for two to five times as much in another country to make the same profit."
>
> "The internet is making global price differences more obvious."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's freemium has price escalation in reverse — digital marginal cost near zero but PPP across Vietnam/India/US varies 5-10x. PPP-adjusted pricing tiers = SaaS equivalent of Samsung Galaxy J strategy.
