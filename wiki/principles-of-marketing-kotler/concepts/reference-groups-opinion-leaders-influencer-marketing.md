# Reference Groups, Opinion Leaders, and Influencer Marketing

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 157–159, chunk 5 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Reference groups serve as direct (face-to-face interactions) or indirect points of comparison or reference in forming a person's attitudes or behavior."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Reference groups serve as direct/indirect comparison points shaping attitudes. Opinion leaders exert disproportionate social influence. Influencer marketing formalizes this via enlisting established or new influencers. ~$100B annual global spend. Authenticity > follower count.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Marketers of brands subjected to strong group influence must figure out how to reach opinion leaders—people within a reference group who, because of special skills, knowledge, personality, or other characteristics, exert social influence on others."
>
> "Influencer marketing involves enlisting established influencers or creating new influencers to spread the word about a company's brands."
>
> "companies are spending nearly 100 billion dollars annually on influencer campaigns. Brands love influencer marketing because it builds on existing relationships between influencers and their followers."
>
> "brands must build long-term relationships with influencers who resonate authentically with customers."
>
