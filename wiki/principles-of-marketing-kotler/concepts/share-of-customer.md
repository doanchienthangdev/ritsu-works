# Share of customer

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 34-42, chunk 5/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Beyond simply retaining good customers to capture customer lifetime value, good customer relationship management can help marketers increase their share of customer—the share they get of the customer's purchasing in their product categories."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** Share of customer is the share a company gets of the customer's purchasing *in their product categories* — not their share of the overall market. The terminology is playful and memorable: banks want "share of wallet," supermarkets want "share of stomach," car companies want "share of garage."

**How to grow it.** Firms can increase share of customer by offering greater variety to current customers, or by creating programs to cross-sell and up-sell. The Amazon example in the chapter is precise: a recommendation system that influences ~35% of all sales, combined with Amazon Prime (which converts "casual shoppers" into "Amazon addicts"), drives share of wallet deep into customer purchasing behavior.

**Relationship to retention.** Share of customer is the second outcome of effective customer relationship management (after loyalty and retention). It captures the idea that retaining a customer is not enough — the goal is to grow the *depth* of the relationship over time.

**For Ritsu.** Ritsu's share of customer opportunity is clear: a student who uploads one textbook chapter could upload the entire textbook, then multiple courses, then recommend it to study groups. The product's multi-format ingestion (PDF, video, slides, URL) is a structural enabler of growing share of a student's learning wallet. The strategic question is which cross-sell or up-sell triggers are most natural — e.g., "You've completed 3 quizzes on Chapter 1; want to start Chapter 2?" — and how to design the free-to-paid upgrade as a share-of-customer expansion moment.
