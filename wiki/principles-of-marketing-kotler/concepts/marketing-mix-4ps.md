# The marketing mix (4 Ps)

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 30-34, chunk 4/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "The major marketing mix tools are classified into four broad groups, called the four Ps of marketing: product, price, place, and promotion. To deliver on its value proposition, the firm must first create a need-satisfying market offering (product). It must then decide how much it will charge for the offering (price) and how it will make the offering available to target consumers (place). Finally, it must engage target consumers, communicate about the offering, and persuade consumers of the offer's merits (promotion)."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** The marketing mix is the set of marketing tools a firm uses to implement its marketing strategy. The major tools are classified into four broad groups — the four Ps of marketing:

- **Product**: a need-satisfying market offering. Includes physical goods, services, and experiences.
- **Price**: how much the firm charges. Must create real customer value (not just cover costs).
- **Place**: how the offering is made available to target consumers — distribution channels, supply chain management.
- **Promotion**: how the firm engages target consumers and communicates about the offering to persuade them to act.

**How they work together.** The firm must blend all four into a comprehensive integrated marketing program that communicates and delivers the intended value to chosen customers. No single P operates in isolation. The text explicitly notes that selling and advertising (a subset of promotion) are "only part of a larger marketing mix" — a corrective against equating marketing with promotion alone.

**Value proposition link.** The 4 Ps are the operational translation of a company's value proposition. The strategy says *what* value to deliver; the marketing mix says *how* to deliver it.

**For Ritsu.** Mapping Ritsu's current state:
- *Product*: the AI tutor platform — strong, actively built.
- *Price*: freemium — exists, but free-to-paid conversion mechanics (pricing experiments) are still early.
- *Place*: web/app — direct digital distribution; no physical channel needed.
- *Promotion*: the weakest P at current scale — organic growth, limited paid acquisition.

The 4 Ps framing helps diagnose where marketing investment is missing. Given the PMF goal of 100 paying users, *promotion* (how potential users discover and engage with Ritsu) and *price* (what triggers conversion from free to paid) are the highest-leverage levers right now.
