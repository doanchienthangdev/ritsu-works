# Promotion Budget-Setting Methods

> **Source.** [Chapter 14](../chapters/chapter-14-engaging-consumers-imc.md), book pp. 422–423, chunk 6 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Affordable method: Set budget at whatever the company can afford after operating expenses."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

4 methods. Affordable (whatever's left — ignores effect). Percentage-of-sales (treats promotion as effect not cause). Competitive-parity (matches competitors — no strategic logic). Objective-and-task (define obj → tasks → cost → sum). Last is most defensible.

## Additional quotes

> "Percentage-of-sales method: Wrongly views sales as the cause of promotion rather than as the result."
>
> "Objective-and-task method is the most logical budget-setting method."
>

## For Ritsu

At pre-PMF Ritsu, objective-and-task method mandated. Every dollar tied to measurable awareness or activation objective. Affordable = drift. Percentage-of-sales = sales are near-zero so budget is near-zero (wrong). Objective-and-task forces explicit ROI assumptions per task.
