# Derived Demand

> **Source.** [Chapter 6](../chapters/chapter-06-business-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 183–183, chunk 2 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Business demand is derived demand—it ultimately derives from the demand for consumer goods. For example, demand for Gore-Tex fabrics derives from consumer purchases of outdoor apparel brands made from Gore-Tex."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Business demand ultimately derives from demand for consumer goods. B2B marketers must track and sometimes stimulate end-consumer demand. Gore-Tex advertises to end consumers to pull B2B demand from outdoor apparel brands.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "So to boost demand for Gore-Tex, Gore advertises to final consumers to educate them on the benefits of Gore-Tex fabrics in the brands they buy."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's institutional channel (universities, L&D) can be activated via derived demand — students who use Ritsu personally request it from their institutions, creating pull on procurement. The consumer channel generates the institutional demand.
