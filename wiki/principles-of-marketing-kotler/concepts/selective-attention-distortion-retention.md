# Selective Attention, Distortion, Retention (Perception Filters)

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 163–165, chunk 7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Selective attention—the tendency for people to screen out most of the information to which they are exposed—means that marketers must work especially hard to attract the consumer's attention."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Consumers filter 3,000-10,000 daily stimuli through 3 perceptual processes: selective attention (most stimuli screened out), selective distortion (info bent to fit existing beliefs), selective retention (remember confirming info). Marketers must break through all 3 layers.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Selective distortion describes the tendency of people to interpret information in a way that supports what they already believe."
>
> "Selective retention means that consumers are likely to remember good points made about a brand they favor and forget good points made about competing brands."
>
> "individuals are exposed to an estimated 3,000 to 10,000 ad messages daily"
>

## For Ritsu

Students who already believe 'AI tools are distracting' will distort any Ritsu message toward that belief (selective distortion). Onboarding must address this attitude head-on: Ritsu is structured/focused, not open-ended like ChatGPT. Getting past selective attention requires in-context placement — Reddit study threads, Discord servers, class group chats — not banner ads.
