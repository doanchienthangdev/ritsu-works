# Societal Marketing: 4 Product Categories

> **Source.** [Chapter 20](../chapters/chapter-20-sustainable-marketing.md), book pp. 598–600, chunk 5 (confidence 0.97, auto-accepted).
>
> "Products can be classified according to their degree of immediate consumer satisfaction and long-run consumer benefit."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

2 axes (immediate satisfaction × long-run benefit) = 4 categories. (1) Deficient: neither (bad-tasting ineffective medicine). (2) Pleasing: high immediate, hurts long-run (cigarettes, junk food). (3) Salutary: low immediate, high long-run (bike helmets). (4) Desirable: BOTH high (tasty nutritious food). Goal: move all to desirable.

## Additional quotes

> "Deficient products, such as bad-tasting and ineffective medicine, have neither immediate appeal nor long-run benefits. Pleasing products give high immediate satisfaction but may hurt consumers in the long run."
>
> "Companies should try to turn all of their products into desirable products."
>

## For Ritsu

Powerful positioning tool. Passive re-reading apps = 'pleasing products' (high satisfaction, low long-run benefit). Ritsu's active-learning quizzes + Feynman-mode = 'desirable products' (engaging + durable memory). Framework justifies anti-passive-learning marketing posture directly.
