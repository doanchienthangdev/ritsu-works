# Customer relationship management (CRM)

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 34-42, chunk 5/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Customer relationship management is perhaps the most important concept of modern marketing. In the broadest sense, customer relationship management is the overall process of building and maintaining profitable customer relationships by delivering superior customer value and satisfaction. It deals with all aspects of acquiring, engaging, and growing customers."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** CRM is defined as "perhaps the most important concept of modern marketing." In the broadest sense: the overall process of building and maintaining profitable customer relationships by delivering superior customer value and satisfaction. It deals with all aspects of acquiring, engaging, and growing customers.

**Relationship levels and tools.** The chapter distinguishes relationship depth by market context:
- Many low-margin customers → basic relationships (brand advertising, social media, product experiences).
- Few high-margin customers → full partnerships (P&G sales reps working directly with Walmart, Kroger).
- In between: loyalty programs, frequency marketing, personalized apps (Hilton HHonors).

**Customer-engagement marketing** is introduced as the modern evolution of CRM. It is "fostering direct and continuous customer involvement in shaping brand conversations, brand experiences, and brand community." The old marketing involved marketing brands *to* consumers; the new marketing makes the brand a meaningful part of consumers' conversations and lives.

**Customer brand advocacy** is the downstream outcome: satisfied customers who initiate favorable interactions with others about a brand. In the digital age, this is organic social proof and is the lowest-cost acquisition channel.

**Consumer-generated marketing** is a sub-form: consumers play roles in shaping brand experiences — from uninvited social media exchanges to company-invited co-creation (Oreo #MyOreoCreation, My Starbucks Idea). The chapter cautions that this can backfire (McDonald's #McDStories hashtag hijacked by negative experiences).

**For Ritsu.** At 100 paying users, CRM is not a software category — it's founder-led. The chapter's Collison install protocol equivalent: personally onboard each paying user to understand their use case, measure their satisfaction, and create the first loop of delight-to-advocacy. Every paying user who becomes an advocate amplifies reach without acquisition cost. The risk of over-automation: moving to basic-relationship tools (email newsletters, drip sequences) before the "true friends" segment is properly identified and delighted.
