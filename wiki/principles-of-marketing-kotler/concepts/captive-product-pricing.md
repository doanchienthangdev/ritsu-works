# Captive-Product Pricing

> **Source.** [Chapter 11](../chapters/chapter-11-pricing-strategies-additional.md), book pp. 315–342, chunk 3 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Producers of the main products (razors, e-readers, printers, single-cup coffee brewing systems, and video-game consoles) often price them low and set high markups on supplies and supplemental products."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Main product priced low to drive adoption, then capture margin via high-priced consumables/subscriptions. Razors+blades model. Risk: customers feel trapped → resentment + switching (Dollar Shave Club).

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Consumers trapped into buying expensive captive products may come to resent the brand that ensnared them."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu freemium with usage limits = captive-product analog. Free tier hooks users; paid tier captures margin. Avoid resentment trap: free tier limits must feel reasonable, not artificially crippling. Dollar Shave Club lesson: if users feel trapped, they switch.
