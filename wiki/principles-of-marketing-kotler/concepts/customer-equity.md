# Customer equity

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 34-42, chunk 5/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Customer equity is the total combined customer lifetime values of all of the company's current and potential customers. As such, it's a measure of the future value of the company's customer base. Clearly, the more loyal the firm's profitable customers, the higher its customer equity. Customer equity may be a better measure of a firm's performance than current sales or market share. Whereas sales and market share reflect the past and present, customer equity suggests the future."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** Customer equity is the total combined customer lifetime values of all of the company's current and potential customers. It is a measure of the *future* value of a company's customer base. Whereas sales and market share reflect the past and present, customer equity suggests the future.

**Why it matters more than market share.** The Cadillac vs. BMW illustration is the chapter's sharpest example. In 1976, Cadillac had 51% of the luxury car market — rosy by market share metrics. But measuring customer equity would have painted a bleaker picture: Cadillac buyers were getting older (average age 60) and many were on their last cars. Customer lifetime value was falling. BMW, with a younger customer base (average age ~40), was building higher customer lifetime values. "BMW overtook Cadillac in the 1980s." Moral stated explicitly: "Marketers should care not just about current sales and market share. Customer lifetime value and customer equity are the name of the game."

**The relationship to customer classification.** Not all loyal customers are good investments. The text introduces a 2×2 profitability × loyalty matrix (Figure 1.5): *Strangers* (low profit, low loyalty — don't invest), *Butterflies* (high profit, low loyalty — enjoy short-term, don't convert), *True friends* (high profit, high loyalty — invest to delight and grow), *Barnacles* (low profit, high loyalty — upsell or fire).

**For Ritsu.** At 100-paying-PMF scale, customer equity is the most forward-looking signal available. A student cohort that converts in their freshman year and stays through graduation represents dramatically higher equity than a one-semester churn. The customer classification matrix maps directly to Ritsu's freemium funnel: free users who never engage are Strangers; engaged free users who convert are True friends in the making. The question is: which activation behaviors predict the transition from Butterfly to True friend?
