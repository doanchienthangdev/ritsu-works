# Price Elasticity of Demand

> **Source.** [Chapter 10](../chapters/chapter-10-pricing-understanding-customer-value.md), book pp. 309–310, chunk 4 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Marketers also need to know price elasticity — how responsive demand will be to a change in price. If demand hardly changes with a small change in price, we say demand is inelastic. If demand changes greatly, we say the demand is elastic."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

How responsive demand is to price change. Inelastic = barely changes with price (high unique value). Elastic = changes significantly (commodity-like). Internet+comparison tools increased consumer price sensitivity.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Forces such as deregulation and the instant price comparisons afforded by the internet and mobile and other technologies have increased consumer price sensitivity."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's elasticity varies by persona. Students = elastic (price-sensitive, budget-constrained). Engineers/researchers = inelastic (ROI-framed, expensable, high-value skill acquisition). Single price under-monetizes inelastic segment + excludes elastic mass market. Segment pricing required.
