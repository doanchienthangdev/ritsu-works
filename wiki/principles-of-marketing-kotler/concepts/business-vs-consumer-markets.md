# Business Markets vs. Consumer Markets

> **Source.** [Chapter 6](../chapters/chapter-06-business-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 183–184, chunk 2 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Business markets differ in many ways from consumer markets. The main differences are in market structure and demand, the nature of the buying unit, and the types of decisions and the decision process involved."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Business markets: orgs buying to produce other goods/services or for resale. Differ in 3 dimensions: market structure/demand (fewer/larger buyers, derived/inelastic/fluctuating demand), buying unit (professional purchasing, committees), decisions (more complex, formal, longer process, deeper interdependence).

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "The business marketer normally deals with far fewer but far larger buyers than the consumer marketer does."
>
> "Many business markets have inelastic and more fluctuating demand. The total demand for many business products is not much affected by price changes, especially in the short run."
>
> "Business purchases often involve large sums of money, complex technical and economic considerations, and interactions among people at many levels of the buyer's organization."
>
