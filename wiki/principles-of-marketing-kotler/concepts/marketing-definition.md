# Marketing: the core definition

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 24-26, chunk 2/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "we define marketing as the process by which companies engage customers, build strong customer relationships, and create customer value in order to capture value from customers in return."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** The chapter opens with the simplest version — "Marketing is engaging customers and managing profitable customer relationships" — before providing the formal definition: marketing is a social and managerial process by which individuals and organizations obtain what they need and want through creating and exchanging value with others. In a business context this tightens to: the process by which companies engage customers, build strong customer relationships, and create customer value in order to capture value from customers in return.

**The twofold goal.** Attract new customers by promising superior value, and keep and grow current customers by delivering value and satisfaction. The Drucker insight is embedded here: "The aim of marketing is to make selling unnecessary" — effective marketing should mean products sell themselves because customer needs are genuinely understood.

**What marketing is not.** A common misconception is that marketing equals selling and advertising. The text explicitly corrects this: selling and advertising are "only the tip of the marketing iceberg" — part of a larger marketing mix, not the whole of it.

**For Ritsu.** Ritsu's mission — turn any document into a personal AI tutor — maps cleanly onto this definition. The product creates value (active learning in 30 seconds) to capture value in return (subscription revenue, word-of-mouth). The risk: fixating on the product's features (17+ activity types) rather than the underlying customer need (genuine understanding, not just information consumption). Drucker's framing is directly applicable: if Ritsu truly satisfies learning needs, conversion should become low-friction.
