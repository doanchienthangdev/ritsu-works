# Competitor Analysis Framework — Identify, Assess, Select

> **Source.** [Chapter 18](../chapters/chapter-18-creating-competitive-advantage.md), book pp. 526–529, chunk 2 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Competitor analysis involves first identifying and assessing competitors and then selecting which competitors to attack or avoid."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

3 steps: (1) identify key competitors (industry + market-based definitions), (2) assess objectives/strategies/strengths-weaknesses/reaction patterns, (3) select which to attack/avoid. Market-based view = broader, surfaces latent competitors. Competitor myopia = primary failure mode.

## Additional quotes

> "Companies must avoid 'competitor myopia.' A company is more likely to be 'buried' by its latent competitors than its current ones."
>
> "Cinnabon realized that it doesn't just sell cinnamon rolls in malls. Instead, it sells 'irresistible indulgence.'"
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu's competitor set must be defined at need-level ('help me understand this material') not product-level ('flashcard app'). Latent competitors: YouTube tutors, Khan Academy, even student's professor. Ritsu sells 'true mastery from any material in 30s' not flashcards.
