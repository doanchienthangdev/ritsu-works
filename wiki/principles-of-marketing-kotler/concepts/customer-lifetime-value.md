# Customer lifetime value (CLV)

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 34-42, chunk 5/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Customer lifetime value [is] the value of the entire stream of purchases a customer makes over a lifetime of patronage. Losing a customer means losing more than a single sale. It means losing the entire stream of purchases that the customer would make over a lifetime of patronage."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** Customer lifetime value (CLV) is the value of the entire stream of purchases a customer makes over a lifetime of patronage. The textbook definition is crisp and the illustration is memorable.

**The Stew Leonard's example.** Stew Leonard, supermarket operator, saw $50,000 flying out of his store every time he saw a sulking customer. Why? His average customer spent $100/week, shopped 50 weeks/year, and remained in the area for 10 years — totaling $50,000 in lifetime revenue. The loss could be much greater if the disappointed customer spread the bad experience to others.

**Industry benchmarks the chapter cites.**
- Lexus: a single satisfied and loyal customer is worth more than $600,000 in lifetime sales.
- Starbucks: estimated lifetime value of a customer is more than $14,000.

**The acquisition-retention cost ratio.** Research cited in the text shows it is *five times cheaper* to keep an old customer than to acquire a new one. This is the economic foundation for prioritizing retention and delight over pure acquisition.

**The broader implication.** A company can lose money on a specific transaction but still benefit greatly from a long-term relationship. Customer delight creates an emotional relationship with a brand, not just a rational preference — and that relationship keeps customers coming back.

**For Ritsu.** At freemium scale, most of Ritsu's users are free. But the CLV lens immediately surfaces the right question: what is the estimated lifetime value of a *paying* Ritsu user? If average paid plan is $10/month, and a retained student uses Ritsu for 3 years of their academic career, CLV = ~$360. That implies the acquisition cost ceiling per paid user is well below $360, and that churn reduction is economically more powerful than pure acquisition spending at this stage. The Stew Leonard's logic: every churned paid user is not just a lost month — it's a lost multi-year relationship.
