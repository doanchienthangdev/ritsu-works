# Ethnographic Research

> **Source.** [Chapter 4](../chapters/chapter-04-managing-marketing-information.md), book pp. 127–128, chunk 4 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Ethnographic research involves sending observers to watch and interact with consumers in their 'natural environments.'"
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Observational research where trained observers watch/interact with consumers in natural environments — reveals unconscious actions and unexpressed needs that structured methods miss. Intuit's 'follow-me-home' = 10,000 hrs annual observation, no interview.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Observational research can generate fresh customer and market insights that people are unwilling or unable to provide. It provides a window into customers' unconscious actions and unexpressed needs and feelings."
>
> "What you get from a follow-me-home you can't get from a data stream. You've gotta look somebody in the eye and feel the emotion."
>

## For Ritsu

For Ritsu's first 30 paying users, Collison install protocol should include ethnographic elements: watch users go through first session on their own device, in normal study context — not a demo call. Gap between what users SAY they want ('more quiz types') and what they DO ('quit after generating, never attempt') only shows up in observation.
