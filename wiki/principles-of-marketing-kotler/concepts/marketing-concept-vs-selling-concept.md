# Five marketing management orientations (incl. marketing vs. selling concept)

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 30-34, chunk 4/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "The marketing concept holds that achieving organizational goals depends on knowing the needs and wants of target markets and delivering the desired satisfactions better than competitors do. Under the marketing concept, customer focus and value are the paths to sales and profits. Instead of a product-centered make-and-sell philosophy, the marketing concept is a customer-centered sense-and-respond philosophy."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** Organizations can adopt one of five competing philosophies to guide their marketing strategy. The choice of orientation fundamentally shapes what the company optimizes for.

**The five orientations:**

1. **Production concept** — consumers favor products that are available and affordable; focus on production and distribution efficiency. Risk: leads to marketing myopia by focusing too narrowly on operations.

2. **Product concept** — consumers favor products that offer the most quality, performance, and innovative features; focus on continuous product improvements. Risk: "build a better mousetrap" thinking — ignores that the better product still needs effective packaging, pricing, distribution, and communication.

3. **Selling concept** — consumers will not buy enough unless the firm undertakes large-scale selling and promotion. Typically used for unsought goods (insurance, blood donations). Creates sales transactions rather than long-term relationships; sells what the company makes rather than making what the market wants.

4. **Marketing concept** — achieving goals depends on knowing the needs and wants of target markets and delivering desired satisfactions better than competitors. **Outside-in** perspective: starts with a well-defined market, focuses on customer needs, integrates all marketing activities. As Southwest Airlines' founder put it: "We don't have a marketing department; we have a customer department." The job is "not to find the right customers for your product but to find the right products for your customers."

5. **Societal marketing concept** — marketing strategy should deliver value to customers in a way that maintains or improves both the consumer's and society's well-being. Balances three considerations: company profits, consumer wants, society's interests.

**The critical distinction.** Figure 1.3 in the text contrasts the selling concept (factory → existing products → selling and promoting → profits through sales volume) vs. the marketing concept (market → customer needs → integrated marketing → profits through customer satisfaction).

**For Ritsu.** Early-stage startups often slip into selling-concept mode: pitching features at users rather than deeply understanding what they need. The marketing concept demands starting with a well-defined segment ("students preparing for exams" vs. "all learners") and delivering value tailored to that segment's real needs. At 100-paying-PMF scale, the customer-driving variant is most relevant: understanding latent needs that users can't articulate yet (e.g., users don't ask for "adaptive difficulty" — they just want to finally feel confident they've mastered the material).
