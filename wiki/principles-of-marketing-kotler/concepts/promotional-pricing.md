# Promotional Pricing

> **Source.** [Chapter 11](../chapters/chapter-11-pricing-strategies-additional.md), book pp. 577–607, chunk 4 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "With promotional pricing, companies will temporarily price their products below list price — and sometimes even below cost — to create buying excitement and urgency."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Temporarily price below list (sometimes below cost) for urgency/short-run sales/decision barriers. Forms: flash sales, rebates, low-interest financing, loss leaders. Risk: deal-prone customers + brand devaluation.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Constantly reduced prices can erode a brand's value in the eyes of customers. And used too frequently, price promotions can create 'deal-prone' customers who wait until brands go on sale before buying them."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu: use sparingly. Time-bounded launch promos OK. Constant discounts train users to wait. Better: feature gating + value-added paid tier (per Ch 10) than promotional discounts on existing tiers.
