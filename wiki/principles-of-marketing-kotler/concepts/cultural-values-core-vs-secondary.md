# Core vs. Secondary Cultural Values

> **Source.** [Chapter 3](../chapters/chapter-03-analyzing-the-marketing-environment.md), book pp. 107–109, chunk 6 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "People in a given society hold many beliefs and values. Their core beliefs and values have a high degree of persistence."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Core beliefs persist across generations (individual freedom, hard work, achievement). Secondary beliefs are more open to change (when to marry vs whether to marry). Marketers can influence secondary values but rarely core values.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Secondary beliefs and values are more open to change. Believing in marriage is a core belief; believing that people should get married early in life is a secondary belief. Marketers have some chance of changing secondary values but little chance of changing core values."
>

## For Ritsu

Ritsu is attempting to shift a secondary cultural value: the belief that passive re-reading is 'studying.' The core value — achievement through learning matters — is stable and Ritsu can leverage it. The secondary belief about HOW to study is where Ritsu can effect change through product experience and marketing narrative.
