# The 5-step marketing process

> **Source.** [Chapter 1 — Marketing: Creating Customer Value and Engagement](../chapters/chapter-01-marketing-creating-customer-value.md), book pp. 24-26, chunk 2/7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "In the first four steps, companies work to understand consumers, create customer value, and build strong customer relationships. In the final step, companies reap the rewards of creating superior customer value. By creating value for consumers, they in turn capture value from consumers in the form of sales, profits, and long-term customer equity."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

**What it is.** Figure 1.1 presents the spine of the entire textbook: a five-step model of the marketing process for creating and capturing customer value.

**The five steps:**
1. **Understand the marketplace and customer needs and wants** — research customers and the marketplace, manage marketing information.
2. **Design a customer value-driven marketing strategy** — select target markets (segmentation + targeting), decide on a value proposition (differentiation + positioning).
3. **Construct an integrated marketing program that delivers superior value** — the marketing mix (4 Ps: product, price, place, promotion).
4. **Engage customers, build profitable relationships, and create customer delight** — customer relationship management, customer-engagement marketing, partner relationship management.
5. **Capture value from customers to create profits and customer equity** — customer loyalty and retention, share of customer, customer equity.

**The underlying logic.** Steps 1–4 all flow toward creating value *for* customers. Step 5 is when the company captures value *from* customers in return — in the form of sales, profits, and long-term customer equity. This create-to-capture loop is the central concept of the entire chapter.

**For Ritsu.** Ritsu currently operates primarily in steps 3–4 (building product, acquiring early users). The 100-paying-PMF goal is step 5. But the chapter's logic suggests step 5 is unreliable unless steps 1–2 are solid: does Ritsu have a precise understanding of which customer segment to serve first (students? self-learners? engineers?) and a distinct value proposition per segment? The answer to "what customers will we serve?" is especially urgent at the current scale.
