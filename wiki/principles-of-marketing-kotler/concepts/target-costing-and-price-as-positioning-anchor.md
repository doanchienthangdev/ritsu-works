# Target Costing and Price as Positioning Anchor

> **Source.** [Chapter 10](../chapters/chapter-10-pricing-understanding-customer-value.md), book pp. 307–308, chunk 4 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Target costing reverses the usual process of first designing a new product, determining its cost, and then asking, 'Can we sell it for that?' Instead, it starts with an ideal selling price based on customer value considerations and then targets costs that will ensure that the price is met."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Target costing REVERSES design-then-price: start with ideal selling price based on customer value, then engineer costs to fit. Price also functions as positioning anchor — defines competitive space, target, design.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "A firm can set prices to attract new customers or profitably retain existing ones."
>

## For Ritsu

For Ritsu: price first (what would 'mastery in 30 days' be worth to a med student?), then engineer the AI model + features + ops cost structure to be profitable at that price. Premium positioning ($30/mo) defines target ICP and design quality.
