# Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs as Motivation Framework

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 163–164, chunk 7 (confidence 0.95, auto-accepted).
>
> "Maslow's answer is that human needs are arranged in a hierarchy, from the most pressing at the bottom to the least pressing at the top. They include physiological needs, safety needs, social needs, esteem needs, and self-actualization needs."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

5-level hierarchy: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualization. Person satisfies most pressing lower need first; once satisfied, next level activates. Marketers position products against active need tier of target.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "A person tries to satisfy the most important need first. When that need is satisfied, it will stop being a motivator, and the person will then try to satisfy the next most important need."
>
> "starving people (physiological need) will not take an interest in the latest happenings in the art world (self-actualization needs) or in how they are seen or esteemed by others (social or esteem needs)"
>

## For Ritsu

Student Maslow mapping: during exam season, anxiety activates Safety/Esteem needs ('I need to pass'). Ritsu's 'Ace your exam in 3 days' workflow maps directly. Free users have satisfied physiological/safety needs; esteem need ('I want to actually understand, not just pass') is the upgrade trigger that pushes free → paid.
