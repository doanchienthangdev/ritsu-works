# Ritsu's Compatibility Risk: Active Learning vs Entrenched Passive Study Habits

> **Source.** [Chapter 5](../chapters/chapter-05-consumer-markets-and-buyer-behavior.md), book pp. 174–175, chunk 9 (confidence 0.65, pending founder review).
>
> "Compatibility. The degree to which the innovation fits the values and experiences of potential consumers."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Compatibility — fit with existing values/practices — is most likely friction point for Ritsu adoption. Students have deeply habituated passive behaviors (re-reading, highlighting). Ritsu's active model is superior (relative advantage) but unfamiliar. Reframe: Ritsu works WITH existing materials, REPLACES the passive behavior of what you do with them.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "people acquire beliefs and attitudes. These, in turn, influence their buying behavior. Attitudes are difficult to change. A person's attitudes fit into a pattern; changing one attitude may require difficult adjustments in many others. Thus, a company should usually try to fit its products into existing attitude patterns rather than attempt to change attitudes."
>
> "A need can also be triggered by external stimuli. For example, an advertisement or a chat with a friend might get you thinking about buying a new car."
>

## For Ritsu

Single most important positioning insight from Ch 5 for Ritsu. The anti-passive-learning stance in product charter ('Reading, watching, highlighting — none of it is learning') is strategically correct but carries attitude-change risk. Softer compatibility message: 'Start with what you already have — your PDF, your notes, your lecture recording. Ritsu takes it from there.' Fits existing practices (you start with source material) while changing downstream behavior.
