# Ritsu Freemium = Good-Value Tier; Paid = Value-Added Tier

> **Source.** [Chapter 10](../chapters/chapter-10-pricing-understanding-customer-value.md), book pp. 299–300, chunk 3 (confidence 0.8, pending founder review).
>
> "Good-value pricing — offering the right combination of quality and good service at a fair price."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

Kotler framework maps onto Ritsu's freemium. Free = good-value pricing (right quality+service at zero, users genuinely learn). Paid = value-added (adaptive modes, emotional AI, unlimited uploads). Conversion = value ceiling hit, not paywall core.

## Additional quotes from the chapter

> "Value-added pricing strategies. Rather than cutting prices to match competitors, they add quality, services, and value-added features to differentiate their offers and thus support their higher prices."
>

## For Ritsu

Avoid EDLP trap of signaling 'cheap' via over-discounting. Make free tier genuinely good (trust + WoM). Paid tier adds demonstrably higher-outcome features. Anchor: show paid price alongside 'hours of tutoring equivalent' cost (human tutor $50/hr, Ritsu produces equivalent mastery in 2 hrs = $100 anchor, not competitor SaaS prices).
