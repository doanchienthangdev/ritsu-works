# Position Active Learning as 'Desirable Product' in EdTech

> **Source.** [Chapter 20](../chapters/chapter-20-sustainable-marketing.md), book pp. 598–600, chunk 5 (confidence 0.9, auto-accepted).
>
> "Desirable products give both high immediate satisfaction and high long-run benefits, such as a tasty and nutritious fast-casual food."
>
> — Kotler/Armstrong/Opresnik, *Principles of Marketing* (18e Global, Pearson 2021)

## Summary

4-product classification = precise competitive positioning framework. Most EdTech = 'pleasing' (YouTube lectures, TikTok study) or 'salutary' (traditional SRS — effective but unengaging). Ritsu's thesis: active-learning quizzes + AI tutoring = BOTH highly engaging AND produce durable memory → unique 'desirable' quadrant.

## Additional quotes

> "The challenge posed by pleasing products is that they sell very well but may end up hurting the consumer."
>
> "Sustainable marketing calls for products that are not only pleasing but also beneficial."
>

## For Ritsu

Direct positioning implication: Ritsu's anti-passive-learning posture ('reading/highlighting — none is learning') maps to 'pleasing vs desirable' distinction. Framework elevates marketing claim to academically-grounded product design principle. Strengthens credibility with educators/researchers/discerning students.
