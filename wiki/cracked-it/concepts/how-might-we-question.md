# "How Might We" Design Goal

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

A 'How Might We' (HMW) question is an open-ended, exploratory question that caps the Define phase and launches ideation. It flows from a POV statement and design imperatives. A well-formed HMW question is broad enough to stimulate many divergent solution ideas yet narrow enough to provoke specific and novel thinking. Research cited in the chapter shows that exploratory framing ('could' / 'might') generates more options and better solutions than directive framing ('should' / 'must'). The HMW question is the bridge from the problem space to the solution space.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path](../chapters/chapter-08-redefine-the-problem-the-design-thinking-path.md)** (PDF pp. 155–183, confidence 0.95):

  > How might we turn MRI scans for children who fear medical treatment into an adventure?

  > A good "how might we" question is broad enough to stimulate many ideas for solutions, but narrow enough to provoke you and your team to think of specific, novel ideas.

## Ritsu relevance

HMW framing is the right entry point for Ritsu's product ideation sessions: e.g., 'How might we make the transition from passive re-reading to active quizzing feel like a natural next step rather than extra work?' This connects directly to the `/think` skill ideation prompts.
