# Judgment Call Transparency

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 7 — Solve the Problem: Eight Degrees of Analysis

## Summary

The discipline of explicitly labeling irreducible judgment calls as such, rather than allowing an audience to believe that judgments are backed by facts. Degree 8 of the analysis continuum: when no amount of additional analysis can eliminate uncertainty, the best practice is to surface the judgment explicitly so the problem owner can weigh in. The alternative — disguising judgments as facts — leads to unpleasant surprises.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Solve the Problem: Eight Degrees of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-07-solve-the-problem-eight-degrees-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 133–154, confidence 0.95):

  > The alternative (which too many problem solvers adopt) is to use judgments, but let the audience believe, implicitly, that those judgments are based on facts, even though no facts are available. This can lead to unpleasant surprises.

  > Pretending we can be certain of things that are difficult to predict isn't confidence—it's foolishness.
