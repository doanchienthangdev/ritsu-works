# Miscommunication Pitfall

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving

## Summary

The fifth pitfall is that a correct, well-reasoned solution fails to produce action because it is communicated unpersuasively, while an erroneous solution may spread because it is communicated compellingly. Being right is insufficient; a solution must be sold. The fat-vs-sugar nutrition case shows both failure modes: Yudkin's correct sugar hypothesis languished for decades due to poor communication, while Keys's incorrect fat hypothesis spread globally due to brilliant communication at a politically critical moment.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving](../chapters/chapter-02-the-five-pitfalls-of-problem-solving.md)** (PDF pp. 32–51, confidence 0.95):

  > Being right isn't enough. Solving the problem is worthless if you can't convince decision-makers to adopt the solution.

  > An unconvincing recommendation is as ineffective as an irrelevant solution.

  > brilliant communication of the wrong answer is even more harmful than poor communication because it leads to misguided and even detrimental actions.
