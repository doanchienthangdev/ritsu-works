# TOSCA Problem Framing in Practice

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 12 — The 4S Method in Action

## Summary

The chapter applies the TOSCA checklist (Trouble, Owner, Success, Constraints, Actors) explicitly to the Kangaroo case to produce a precise problem statement. The exercise reveals that the 'trouble' is an acquisition opportunity rather than a dysfunction, the 'owner' is a specific skeptical executive (Brenda), and 'success' means enabling her to take a clear, defensible stand — not simply recommending whether to buy.

- **From [Ch 12 — The 4S Method in Action](../chapters/chapter-12-the-4s-method-in-action.md)** (PDF pp. 263–281, confidence 0.95):

  > To define the problem, let's look at the TOSCA checklist:

  > The purpose of the problem-solving effort is either to convince Brenda to approve the deal or to provide her with solid arguments to reject it.

  > We can state the problem in the following way: Is Kangaroo's business attractive enough and its strategic plan convincing enough to justify Cherry Holdings' interest in acquiring it, assuming a fair agreement on the other aspects of the deal?
