# Cartesian Method as Foundation for Issue-Driven Structuring

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 5 — Structure the Problem: Pyramids and Trees

## Summary

The chapter grounds issue-driven problem structuring in Descartes's four rules from Discourse on the Method (1637): accept nothing without questioning; divide each issue into parts; order from simple to complex; and ensure nothing is omitted. Rules 2 and 4 map directly to MECE — mutual exclusivity enables solving issues one by one, and collective exhaustiveness ensures nothing is omitted. The chapter uses this as intellectual legitimacy for the issue tree approach.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Structure the Problem: Pyramids and Trees](../chapters/chapter-05-structure-the-problem-pyramids-and-trees.md)** (PDF pp. 86–110, confidence 0.9):

  > Issue-driven problem structuring draws heavily from the Cartesian method for conducting a systematic search of the truth.

  > The last two rules are what we described as the MECE principle. Elementary issues must be mutually exclusive so you can solve them one by one. You ensure nothing is omitted if all elementary points cohere into a combination that covers all the facets of the problem.
