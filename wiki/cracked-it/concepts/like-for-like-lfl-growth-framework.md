# Like-for-Like (LFL) Growth Framework

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 6 — Structure the Problem: Analytical Frameworks

## Summary

LFL (same-store sales) growth measures the revenue variation for stores open in both the current and prior year, isolating organic format performance from network expansion effects. The chapter uses this retail industry framework to show how applying the right industry-specific value driver lens transforms an impractical revenues-vs-costs decomposition into an actionable issue tree: LFL profit improvement vs. new store portfolio changes vs. non-store opportunities.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Structure the Problem: Analytical Frameworks](../chapters/chapter-06-structure-the-problem-analytical-frameworks.md)** (PDF pp. 111–132, confidence 0.95):

  > LFL growth measures the variation in sales for stores that were open the previous year and indicates the growth (or decline) that a chain would experience if there were no store openings or closures.

  > It also captures a capital-efficient (and more profitable) source of growth, as same-store growth typically requires considerably less investment than new store openings.
