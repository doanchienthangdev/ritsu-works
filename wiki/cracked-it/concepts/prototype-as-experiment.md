# Prototype as Experiment

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 9 — Structure and Solve the Problem Using Design Thinking

## Summary

A prototype is a tangible approximation of at least one attribute of a solution that embodies a hypothesis from the Ideate phase. Prototypes serve three functions: learning (richer feedback than verbal descriptions; forces clarity of thinking; surfaces unexpected problems), risk management (reduces uncertainty before committing resources), and communication (aligns stakeholders around evidence). The chapter recommends early prototypes be fast, rough, and cheap, progressively increasing fidelity as learning accumulates.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 9 — Structure and Solve the Problem Using Design Thinking](../chapters/chapter-09-structure-and-solve-the-problem-using-design.md)** (PDF pp. 184–210, confidence 0.95):

  > Prototyping is an essential part of design thinking and disciplined problem solving for three reasons: learning, risk management, and communication.

  > Early prototypes, however, should be fast, rough, and cheap. This allows you to learn rapidly and explore multiple solution possibilities. IDEO encourages its designers to 'fail often to succeed sooner.'
