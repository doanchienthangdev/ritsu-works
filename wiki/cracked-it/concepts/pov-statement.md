# Point-of-View (POV) Statement

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

A POV statement is the central output of the Define phase—an actionable problem definition framed from the user's perspective. It has the structure: '[user] needs to [need] because [insight].' The three components are: a specific user type, a fundamental need expressed as a verb, and an insight (an unexpected finding about how users act, think, or feel that explains why the need exists). POV statements reorient the problem-solver's perspective, opening up previously unimaginable solutions. Each persona developed during the Define phase yields one POV statement.

- **From [Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path](../chapters/chapter-08-redefine-the-problem-the-design-thinking-path.md)** (PDF pp. 155–183, confidence 0.95):

  > A POV statement identifies a particular type of user, a fundamental need the user is trying to fulfill, and insights about why the need exists and what is important to the user in fulfilling the need.

  > A young child with cancer needs to play and have fun to feel like a normal kid when receiving an MRI because kids participate when they perceive it as an adventure.
