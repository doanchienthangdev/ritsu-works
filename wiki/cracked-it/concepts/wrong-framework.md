# Wrong Framework

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving

## Summary

The third pitfall is selecting a conceptual lens that directs attention toward certain variables while systematically excluding others that may be more important. Frameworks carry implicit causal assumptions that tell us what to attend to and, crucially, what to ignore. The call-center HR case shows how a vendor's personality-based model implicitly assumed personality drives tenure, hiding the more important causal factors of pay, management quality, and working conditions.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving](../chapters/chapter-02-the-five-pitfalls-of-problem-solving.md)** (PDF pp. 32–51, confidence 0.95):

  > Frameworks are like theories—they're a way of seeing and understanding our world. They carry with them implicit assumptions about what causes what.

  > "A way of seeing is also a way of not seeing."

  > Our choice of frameworks can blind us to important aspects of a problem, leading us to develop ineffective and costly solutions.
