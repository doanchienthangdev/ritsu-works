# Hypothesis-Driven vs. Issue-Driven Problem Structuring

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 5 — Structure the Problem: Pyramids and Trees

## Summary

Two complementary but distinct approaches to structuring a problem. Hypothesis-driven starts with a candidate solution and validates it top-down; it is fast, intuitive, and easy to sell but prone to five cognitive pitfalls and logical errors. Issue-driven starts from open questions and disaggregates MECE, requiring more time and rigor but generating deeper insights and avoiding confirmation bias. The chapter's default recommendation is issue trees unless compelling reasons favour hypothesis pyramids.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Structure the Problem: Pyramids and Trees](../chapters/chapter-05-structure-the-problem-pyramids-and-trees.md)** (PDF pp. 86–110, confidence 0.95):

  > our recommendation here is simple: the "default choice" should be to use an issue tree, unless there are compelling reasons to be content with a hypothesis pyramid.

  > Limit the use of hypothesis-driven thinking to two cases only: 1. You have strong reasons to believe in your hypothesis. … 2. You don't have the luxury of building an issue tree.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's `/cla` capability lifecycle uses hypothesis-driven structuring when an architecture option is pre-identified and issue-driven structuring during open domain analysis (Phase 2). Knowing when to switch between the two prevents premature narrowing of the solution space.
