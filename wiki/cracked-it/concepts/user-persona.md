# User Persona

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

A user persona is a vivid, realistic description of a fictional composite character representing a segment of real observed users. It synthesizes their needs, values, aspirations, limitations, lifestyle, attitudes, and background, supplemented by demographic details (age, occupation, name, photo) to make the archetype concrete and emotionally resonant. The chapter recommends developing no more than three personas to keep solution development focused. Personas overcome the tendency to design from the developer's own perspective and create a shared mental model across the problem-solving team.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path](../chapters/chapter-08-redefine-the-problem-the-design-thinking-path.md)** (PDF pp. 155–183, confidence 0.95):

  > A persona is a vivid and realistic description of a fictional character that represents a composite of real users. It represents the needs, values, aspirations, limitations, lifestyle, attitudes, and background of a hypothetical group of users synthesized from observations of actual users.

  > Personas are powerful because they put a human face and story to otherwise complex and impersonal data.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's six homepage personas (Students, Self-Learners, Engineers, Researchers, Educators, Professionals) are marketing archetypes, not design-thinking personas grounded in observation. Building genuine observation-based personas for the US STEM student segment—the first-100-paying wedge—would sharpen the product roadmap and reduce assumption-driven feature building.
