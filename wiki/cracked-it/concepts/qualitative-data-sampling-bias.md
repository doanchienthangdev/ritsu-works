# Qualitative Data Sampling Bias

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 7 — Solve the Problem: Eight Degrees of Analysis

## Summary

The systematic distortion introduced by non-random sample selection in qualitative analysis such as customer interviews. Common patterns include convenience sampling (easiest-to-reach respondents), sales-force selection (their best relationships), and opt-in biases. The chapter emphasizes that qualitative facts are still facts, making sampling discipline just as critical for qualitative as for quantitative data.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Solve the Problem: Eight Degrees of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-07-solve-the-problem-eight-degrees-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 133–154, confidence 0.95):

  > If, as is often the case, you select them based on how easy they are to find, your sample will probably be biased. Relying on the sales force to organize interviews, for example, is likely to produce meetings with the salespeople's best friends—not a tough crowd.

  > Such sampling biases are frequent in qualitative analysis, and often go unnoticed.
