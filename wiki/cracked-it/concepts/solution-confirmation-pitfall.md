# Solution-Confirmation Pitfall

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 4 — State the Problem: The TOSCA Framework

## Summary

The solution-confirmation pitfall occurs when a problem is defined in terms of a proposed solution, predetermining the answer before analysis begins. The chapter identifies this as a frequent mistake made with good intentions — managers who are 'results-oriented' may restate the solution as the problem. The TOSCA success-criteria component is the primary antidote: asking what success looks like forces separation of problem from solution.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 4 — State the Problem: The TOSCA Framework](../chapters/chapter-04-state-the-problem-the-tosca-framework.md)** (PDF pp. 70–85, confidence 0.95):

  > When we define a problem so it suggests the solution, we're in great danger of blindly confirming that conclusion.

  > To properly define a problem, we must resist the urge to solve it too quickly.
