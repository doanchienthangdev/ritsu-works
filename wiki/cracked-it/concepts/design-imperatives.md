# Design Imperatives

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

Design imperatives are statements of what a solution must do and how users should experience it, phrased independently of any specific implementation or form. They are the design thinking equivalent of 'Success Criteria' in the TOSCA framework. They emerge from synthesizing user needs and insights across the Define phase and serve as the guiding vision that constrains and focuses the Ideate, Prototype, and Test phases. The chapter distinguishes between a user need ('play and have fun to feel like a normal kid') and the corresponding imperative ('involve the child in an adventure'), illustrating that imperatives translate needs into solution-shaping requirements.
