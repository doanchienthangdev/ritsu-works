# Maslow's Hammer (Law of the Instrument)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving

## Summary

A cognitive bias, described by Abraham Kaplan as the 'law of the instrument' and named 'Maslow's hammer' after Abraham Maslow, in which a solver over-applies a familiar tool or framework to every problem regardless of fit. Maslow captured it as: if the only tool you have is a hammer, treat everything as if it were a nail. The chapter uses it to explain why the wrong-framework pitfall is so persistent: familiarity with a method creates a systematic blind spot.

- **From [Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving](../chapters/chapter-02-the-five-pitfalls-of-problem-solving.md)** (PDF pp. 32–51, confidence 0.95):

  > I suppose it is tempting, if the only tool you have is a hammer, to treat everything as if it were a nail.

  > This cognitive bias goes by at least two names: "the law of the instrument," coined by Abraham Kaplan (another philosopher), and "Maslow's hammer," after the eminent psychologist Abraham Maslow
