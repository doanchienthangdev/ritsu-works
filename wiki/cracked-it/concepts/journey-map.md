# User Journey Map

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

A journey map (also called an experience map) is a time-ordered model of every activity a user performs and experiences when interacting with an artifact, plus their thoughts and feelings at each step. The chapter uses the 5Es framework to structure any experience: Entice (triggers interest), Enter (onboarding cues), Engage (core interactions), Exit (offboarding cues), and Extend (post-exit follow-ups). Analysts look for bright spots (delightful moments), hot spots (frustration points), and gaps (transition failures) to identify needs and insights for design imperatives.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path](../chapters/chapter-08-redefine-the-problem-the-design-thinking-path.md)** (PDF pp. 155–183, confidence 0.95):

  > A journey map is a time-ordered model of the activities a person performs and experiences in using a particular artifact, such as a service or product, and what they think and feel about each activity.

  > look for bright spots, hot spots, and gaps. A bright spot is a point in the experience that the user truly enjoys. A hot spot is the opposite—a point in the experience where the user is uncomfortable, frustrated, anxious, or upset.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's activation funnel (upload → plan generated → first quiz completed) is a natural journey map. Mapping the hot spots between upload and first quiz—and the Extend phase (what brings users back on day 7)—would directly inform the design of habit-forming mechanics and retention SOPs.
