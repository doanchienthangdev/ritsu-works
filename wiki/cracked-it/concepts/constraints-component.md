# Constraints (TOSCA Component)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 4 — State the Problem: The TOSCA Framework

## Summary

Constraints are the limits on what can be done when solving the problem. The chapter identifies three categories: (1) conflicts with other objectives and commitments that qualify the success criteria, (2) resource and capability gaps that limit solution scope, and (3) constraints on the problem-solving process itself (time, budget, confidentiality). Constraints should be surfaced early but treated as provisional — overly rigid constraints can make a problem unsolvable, so they must be revisited and potentially relaxed.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 4 — State the Problem: The TOSCA Framework](../chapters/chapter-04-state-the-problem-the-tosca-framework.md)** (PDF pp. 70–85, confidence 0.95):

  > You should define constraints from the perspective of the problem owner.

  > First, there are always constraints on the success criteria, arising from conflicts with other objectives and commitments.

  > The need to reevaluate constraints, and possibly to relax them, is one of the reasons to revisit the problem definition periodically throughout the problem-solving process.

## Ritsu relevance

For Ritsu capability proposals (/cla), constraints map directly to budget caps (ROLES.md monthly_cap_usd), timeline (sprint count), and capability gaps — all of which bound solution options in exactly the three-category pattern the chapter prescribes.
