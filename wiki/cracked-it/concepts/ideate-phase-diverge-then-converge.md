# Ideate Phase: Diverge Then Converge

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 9 — Structure and Solve the Problem Using Design Thinking

## Summary

The Ideate phase has two steps: first diverge by generating as many solution concepts as possible without judgment, then converge by evaluating and selecting the most promising concepts to prototype. The goal in divergence is volume and variety, not average quality — one outstanding idea among many duds beats 100 mediocre ideas. In convergence, concepts are rated against structured criteria of desirability, feasibility, and viability.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 9 — Structure and Solve the Problem Using Design Thinking](../chapters/chapter-09-structure-and-solve-the-problem-using-design.md)** (PDF pp. 184–210, confidence 0.95):

  > The Ideate phase consists of two steps. In the first, you'll diverge by generating as many concepts as possible. The goal is to go big, regarding both the volume and the variety of ideas you develop.

  > Developing one outstanding idea and 99 duds is better than 100 good ideas. In innovative problem solving, the extremes of the distribution matter, not the average.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's feature-generation cycles (new activity types, tutoring modes) benefit from separating divergent ideation from structured evaluation. The desirability-feasibility-viability triad maps directly onto Ritsu's product prioritization criteria.
