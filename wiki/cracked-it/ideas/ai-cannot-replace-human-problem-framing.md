# AI Cannot Replace Human Problem Framing

**Type:** idea · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 13 — Conclusion: Becoming a Master Problem-Solver

## Summary

The opening paragraphs make a pointed claim: AI and big data change how problems are solved but cannot replace the human work of defining, framing, structuring, and judging problems. The authors position quantitative analysis as 'a small part of the problem-solving process, not the whole approach.' This is a deliberate rebuttal of both techno-optimist and apocalyptic AI narratives.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 13 — Conclusion: Becoming a Master Problem-Solver](../chapters/chapter-13-conclusion-becoming-a-master-problem-solver.md)** (PDF pp. 282–285, confidence 0.95):

  > complex and non-routine problems can't be solved by expertise alone: they must be defined and structured first.

  > It takes a human to identify and frame problems, to structure and monitor a comprehensive problem-­solving effort, to plan analyses, to critically assess and interpret evidence, to imagine novel solutions, to use judgment to evaluate them

## Ritsu relevance

Aligns with Ritsu's 'AI can't learn for you' belief: the AI generates the practice materials and feedback, but the mastery work — sense-making, application, retention — must be done by the human learner.
