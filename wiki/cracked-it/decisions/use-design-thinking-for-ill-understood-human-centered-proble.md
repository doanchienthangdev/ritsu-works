# Use Design Thinking for Complex, Ill-Understood, Human-Centered Problems

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

The chapter provides a four-question diagnostic for choosing the design thinking path: (1) Is the problem human-centered? (2) Is it complex with interlinked explanations? (3) Are you uncertain about the causes? (4) Are you struggling to state the problem precisely? A majority of 'yes' answers signals insufficient understanding to effectively state the problem, and the design thinking path should be chosen. The chapter also notes design thinking tools can be adopted partially—entering at the Ideate phase when the problem is defined but structuring or solving it proves elusive.
