# Prioritize Make-or-Break Hypotheses First

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 7 — Solve the Problem: Eight Degrees of Analysis

## Summary

When planning analytical work, start with the critical hypotheses whose falsification would collapse the entire hypothesis pyramid, especially when they are relatively easy to test. This principle prevents wasted effort on peripheral analyses when the core premise may be wrong. In the Solar example, verifying that there are no customer synergies between Uranus and Solar's core units is the highest-priority test.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Solve the Problem: Eight Degrees of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-07-solve-the-problem-eight-degrees-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 133–154, confidence 0.95):

  > First, start with the critical, "make or break" hypotheses, especially when they're relatively easy to test.

  > Good work planning should give priority to the analyses that can change the overall answer or solution to the problem.

The /cla options-generator and sprint-planner phases should apply this discipline: identify which assumption, if wrong, kills the proposed capability entirely — and validate it first before investing full sprint effort.
