# Empathize with Extreme Users, Not Only Average Users

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

The chapter prescribes deliberately targeting extreme users—experts, complete novices, power users, rejectors, rabid fans—during the Empathy phase rather than focusing on the typical or average user. Focusing on the bell-curve center confirms existing beliefs rather than generating new insights. Extreme users reveal unanticipated workarounds and hacks invisible to average users, and solutions that satisfy extreme constraints tend to work better for mainstream users as well.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path](../chapters/chapter-08-redefine-the-problem-the-design-thinking-path.md)** (PDF pp. 155–183, confidence 0.95):

  > To create divergence at the Empathy phase and expand your understanding of the problem space, increasing your opportunities for an innovative solution, consider focusing on extreme users.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu should explicitly recruit extreme users for any future empathy research: the student who uploads a 500-page textbook (power user) and the student who signed up and never returned after seeing the upload screen (extreme avoider). Both reveal design failures invisible in the average new user flow.
