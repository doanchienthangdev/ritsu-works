# Pilot-Test Solutions Before Full Rollout When Facing Human-Centered Problems

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving

## Summary

When a problem is complex and involves human behavior that the solver understands imperfectly, the chapter prescribes generating multiple candidate solutions, prototyping them, and running small-scale tests before committing resources to full implementation. The J.C. Penney case demonstrates the catastrophic cost of skipping this step even when the solver is highly experienced.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — The Five Pitfalls of Problem Solving](../chapters/chapter-02-the-five-pitfalls-of-problem-solving.md)** (PDF pp. 32–51, confidence 0.95):

  > we should also resist the temptation to zero in on one solution, and instead generate multiple potential solutions to the problem at hand. We can then avoid "betting the farm" on one idea that may not work by prototyping and testing potential solutions to identify the best one.

  > Johnson could have deployed his new strategy in a gradual, learn-as-you-go manner, for instance, by running small-scale pilot tests in a few stores to gauge customer acceptance
