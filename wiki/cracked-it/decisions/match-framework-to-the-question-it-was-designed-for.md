# Match Each Framework to the Specific Question It Was Designed For

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 6 — Structure the Problem: Analytical Frameworks

## Summary

The chapter prescribes applying frameworks only to the exact class of problem they were designed for. Porter's Five Forces answers exactly one question — 'Is this industry attractive for investment?' — and applying it to questions about brand futures or acquisition merit creates confusion. The entry point for framework selection must be the problem (the nail), not the framework (the hammer).

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Structure the Problem: Analytical Frameworks](../chapters/chapter-06-structure-the-problem-analytical-frameworks.md)** (PDF pp. 111–132, confidence 0.95):

  > Porter's Five Forces model, for instance, is useful to address only one question: "Is this industry attractive for investment?" If you face an issue that consists of determining the future of a brand or discussing the merits of an acquisition, trying to break down your question into five forces will only create confusion.

  > Intentionally, the entry point into this table of frameworks is the problem to which the framework applies, not the framework: the nail, not the hammer.
