# LEGO's Ethnographic Research Identified Three Unmet Needs That Drove Turnaround

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 8 — Redefine the Problem: The Design Thinking Path

## Summary

By 2005 LEGO was losing approximately $1 million a day after losing touch with its core customers. CEO Knudstorp commissioned an ethnographic research project ('Find the Fun') with ReD Associates, involving months of in-home visits with families in the UK, USA, and Germany. The research yielded three unexpected insights: childhood play is fundamentally social (not just solitary); self-ranking and status hierarchies are natural and desirable for children (not negative); and children have an innate desire to master skills and demonstrate mastery to peers. These insights led to new product lines including LEGO Friends (2012).

## Ritsu relevance

LEGO's discovery that 'mastery demonstration to peers' is a primary driver of child engagement maps directly onto Ritsu's core value proposition: active learning produces demonstrable mastery. The social dimension of mastery (sharing, competing, demonstrating) is a product feature space Ritsu has not yet built—LEGO Friends succeeded precisely by addressing the social layer.
