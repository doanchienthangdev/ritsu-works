# Ancel Keys Conflated Selling with Solving in the Fat-Sugar Debate

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 3 — The 4S Method

## Summary

The chapter uses Ancel Keys's Seven Countries Study as a cautionary illustration of what happens when solution-selling contaminates problem-solving. Keys focused on countries that confirmed his fat-causes-heart-disease hypothesis and disregarded disconfirming ones, because he had already mentally switched to 'selling' mode. This is presented as a real-world demonstration of the confirmation bias amplified by premature solution-selling.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 3 — The 4S Method](../chapters/chapter-03-the-4s-method.md)** (PDF pp. 52–69, confidence 0.95):

  > because he thought he knew the solution, he didn't use his empirical work as a problem-solving device to test his hypothesis. Instead, he used it as a sales pitch to convince decision-makers.

  > The confirmation bias led him to confuse problem solving and solution selling.
