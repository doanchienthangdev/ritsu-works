# Starbucks: Revenues-vs-Costs Split is Practically Useless

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Cracked It! How to Solve Big Problems and Sell Solutions Like Top Strategy Consultants* (Bernard Garrette, Corey Phelps, Olivier Sibony, 2018), Ch 6 — Structure the Problem: Analytical Frameworks

## Summary

The chapter uses the Starbucks France profitability question to demonstrate that decomposing profit into revenues and costs — while arithmetically MECE — is managerially impractical because the two levers are causally entangled. Price increases reduce volume; cost reductions often impact service and thus revenue. The decomposition creates an issue tree in which branches overlap in practice, violating the independence requirement for useful MECE analysis.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Structure the Problem: Analytical Frameworks](../chapters/chapter-06-structure-the-problem-analytical-frameworks.md)** (PDF pp. 111–132, confidence 0.95):

  > while revenues and costs are a MECE decomposition of profit, changes in revenues and costs are not. Everything else being equal, price increases drive volumes down.

  > Ideas you pursue on the cost side and the revenue side are intertwined. An issue tree that starts this way is not MECE: it is messy.
