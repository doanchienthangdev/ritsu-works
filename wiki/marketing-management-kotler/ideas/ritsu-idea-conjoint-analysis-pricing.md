# Idea: Run Conjoint Analysis to Resolve Ritsu Freemium Limit and Pricing Tier Design

**Type:** idea · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 111–undefined)

## Summary

MM 16e names conjoint analysis as the key survey-based technique for understanding how consumers value attribute bundles. For Ritsu, a $400-500 Qualtrics conjoint study across 200 respondents segmented by ICP persona (student vs engineer vs educator) would resolve three open questions: (1) which feature limitations in the free tier drive the highest upgrade intent; (2) what price points maximize revenue per segment; (3) which activity types (quiz, flashcard, mindmap) command the most willingness-to-pay. This directly feeds SOP-PRODUCT-002 (N=10 strangers observed) and informs pricing-experiments sub-pillar.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> One popular survey-based statistical technique used in market research is conjoint analysis, which helps determine how consumers value different attributes (product features, service benefits, and price) that make up a particular offering.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Direct product-ops recommendation: before the next pricing revision, run a conjoint study. Budget: ~$400 Qualtrics panel. Output: per-feature willingness-to-pay by persona, enabling the freemium limit and paid tier design to be evidence-based rather than intuition-based.
