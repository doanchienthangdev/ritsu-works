# Decision: Depth of Localization — What to Standardize, What to Adapt

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 20 — Tapping into Global Markets (pp. 452–453)

## Summary

Companies must explicitly decide which elements of the marketing mix to standardize globally (brand positioning, core product features, visual identity) versus localize (product formula, pricing, messaging, distribution channels). The decision matrix: standardize for economies of scale and brand consistency; adapt for preference fit and conversion. The cost of each adaptation must be compared against the incremental revenue it enables.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> International companies must decide how extensively to adapt their marketing strategy to local conditions. At one extreme is a standardized marketing program worldwide, which promises the greatest consistency across individual countries. At the other extreme is a localized marketing program in which the company, consistent with the marketing concept, believes consumer needs vary and tailors its marketing to each target group.
>
> Having a standardized marketing program offers multiple advantages. These include economies of scale in production and distribution, lower marketing costs, consistency in brand image, ability to leverage good ideas across different markets, and uniformity of marketing practices.
>
