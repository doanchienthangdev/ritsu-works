# Singapore Airlines 40-30-30 Resource Allocation Rule

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 9 — Designing and Managing Services (pp. 192–193)

## Summary

Singapore Airlines, consistently rated the world's best airline, allocates its service resources in a 40-30-30 split: 40% to training and motivating staff, 30% to reviewing processes and procedures, and 30% to creating new product and service ideas. New recruits receive four months of training (twice the industry average) and existing staff get three weeks of annual refresher training. The airline was first to launch on-demand entertainment in all classes, book-the-cook service, and a $1M simulator to discover how taste changes at altitude.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> SIA applies a 40-30-30 rule: 40 percent of resources go to training and motivating staff, 30 percent to reviewing process and procedures, and 30 percent to creating new product and service ideas.
>
> New SIA recruits receive four months of training (twice the industry average), and existing staff get nearly three weeks of refresher training a year (costing $70 million).
>

## Ritsu relevance

Analogous allocation for Ritsu's AI service: 40% of AI development effort on prompt quality and AI model training (the 'staff'), 30% on reviewing generation quality and monitoring failures (process review), 30% on new activity types and feature innovation. As Ritsu scales, this ratio prevents over-indexing on new features at the expense of core quality.
