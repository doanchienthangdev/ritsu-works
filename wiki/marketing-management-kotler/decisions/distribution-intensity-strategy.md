# Distribution Intensity: Exclusive/Selective/Intensive

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 15 — Designing and Managing Distribution Channels (pp. 331–332)

## Summary

Strategic brand-equity decision, not just coverage. Exclusive = fewest intermediaries (luxury/complex). Selective = some willing intermediaries (brand control + reach). Intensive = max outlets (convenience but brand-equity erosion risk). Moving toward intensive = tempting but damaging long-term.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Exclusive distribution severely limits the number of intermediaries.
>
> Intensive distribution places the goods or services in as many outlets as possible. Manufacturers are constantly tempted to move from exclusive or selective distribution to more intensive distribution to increase coverage and sales. This strategy may help in the short term, but if not done properly, it can hurt long-term performance.
>
> When Gucci found its image tarnished by overexposure from licensing and discount stores, it decided to end contracts with third-party suppliers, control its distribution, and open its own stores.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's freemium = intensive distribution analog. Risk: over-distribution erodes premium positioning. Counterstrategy: paid tier as 'exclusive' brand experience even though tech delivery is same.
