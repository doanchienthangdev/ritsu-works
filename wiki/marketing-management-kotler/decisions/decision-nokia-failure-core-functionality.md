# Nokia's Collapse: Failing to Defend Core Functionality

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 8 — Designing and Managing Products (pp. 162–163)

## Summary

Nokia dominated mobile phone sales globally for 14 years before being overtaken by Samsung and Apple. Despite $40B in R&D and early smartphone patents, Nokia chose not to invest in the iPhone form factor because it failed the company's drop test and was deemed too expensive to manufacture. The result: consumers associated Nokia with an earlier technology era — a fatal blow in fast-moving markets.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Nokia did not respond to the wildly successful iPhone and the shifting consumer demand that accompanied it. The company thought the iPhone was too expensive to manufacture and was not up to its own product standards.
>
> Without the right new products, Nokia began to be associated by consumers with an earlier era of technology, a fatal blow in the fast-moving, technologically intensive smart-phone market.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Cautionary signal for Ritsu: EdTech tools that fail to continuously evolve their core AI quality face Nokia-style displacement. If a better AI model produces dramatically better quiz/explanation quality and Ritsu doesn't upgrade, users will associate Ritsu with 'the old AI.'
