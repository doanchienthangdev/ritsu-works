# Kellogg's Brand Audit 'Project Signature': Systematic Repositioning

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 214–215)

## Summary

Facing declining cereal consumption as consumers ate on-the-run and worried about GMO ingredients, Kellogg launched a yearlong brand audit called 'Project Signature' with brand consultancy Interbrand. Outcomes: new tagline ('Let's Make Today Great'), updated logo and design, clarified core purpose ('power of breakfast'), explicit incorporation of the Kellogg master brand across all campaigns, and consolidation of 42 global websites into one. The audit then drove specific programs: cause-related marketing, social media debunking myths, Olympic sponsorship. This case demonstrates that brand audits should precede strategic shifts, not follow them.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> An extensive brand audit, called 'Project Signature,' was launched to provide strategic direction and creative inspiration. A year of collaboration with brand-consulting partner Interbrand resulted in a new tagline, 'Let's Make Today Great'; an updated design and more contemporary logo; clear identification of the brand's core purpose as highlighting the 'power of breakfast.'
>
> A brand audit is a focused series of procedures to assess the health of the brand, uncover its sources of brand equity, and suggest ways to improve and leverage its equity. Marketers should conduct a brand audit when setting up marketing plans and when considering shifts in strategic direction.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu should run a lightweight brand audit before its first major marketing push. Key questions: what associations do current users actually have with Ritsu? Do they associate it with 'active learning' (intended) or 'AI quiz generator' (functional but shallow)? The audit would inform whether to consolidate messaging before spending on growth marketing.
