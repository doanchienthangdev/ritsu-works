# Decision: B2B Brand Investment Is Justified Because It Transfers Trust to Purchasing Stakeholders

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 88–89)

## Summary

MM 16e makes a clear normative argument: business marketers should invest in brand because 'Brands give managers peace of mind by ensuring product quality and thus make it easier to justify the purchase of established brands to the company stakeholders.' The 'Nobody gets fired for buying IBM' principle means brand serves as decision insurance for Approvers and Buyers in the buying center. ABB's 'one company, one brand' rebranding and SAS's crossing from IT into C-suite consciousness illustrate the pattern. This is a decision available to Ritsu as it enters institutional channels.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Business marketers are increasingly recognizing the importance of their brands. Brands give managers peace of mind by ensuring product quality and thus make it easier to justify the purchase of established brands to the company stakeholders. As the old saying goes: 'Nobody gets fired for buying IBM.'
>
> To extend the company's reach beyond IT managers with PhDs in math or statistical analysis, the company needed to connect with C-level executives in the largest companies - people who either didn't have a clue what SAS software was or didn't think business analytics was a strategic issue.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's brand investment in EdTech credibility (partnerships with universities, peer-reviewed outcome data, awards) enables Approvers and Buyers in institutional buying centers to say yes without career risk. Brand is a B2B decision lubricant.
