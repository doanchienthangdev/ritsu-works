# Decision: How to Organize for Innovation (Departments, Ventures, Communities, Cross-Functional)

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 18 — Developing New Market Offerings (pp. 400–400)

## Summary

Companies choose among five organizational forms for NPD: managers of existing products (fast but distracted), dedicated new-product departments (structured but siloed), innovation centers (geographically embedded), venture teams or skunkworks (entrepreneurial, protected budget/timeline), and communities of practice (knowledge-sharing across functions). Choice depends on stage of company, innovation ambition, and how much protection from the core business is needed.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Venture teams. Another approach to driving innovation involves assigning new-product development to venture teams—cross-functional groups charged with developing a specific product or business. These intrapreneurs are relieved of other duties and given a separate budget and a longer time frame than is typical. They are also often organized in a 'skunkworks' setting—informal workplaces, sometimes garages, where entrepreneurial-minded teams work to develop new products.
>
> Cross-functional teams composed of both engineers and marketers help ensure that a company's research and development efforts are not driven to create a 'better mousetrap' when potential customers do not need or want one.
>
