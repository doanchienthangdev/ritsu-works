# Observation: Five Proven Use Cases Where Data Mining Creates Customer Value

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 109–110)

## Summary

MM 16e catalogs five operational data mining applications: (1) prospect identification from response data; (2) targeting customers for specific offers by matching ideal-customer profile; (3) deepening loyalty by personalizing gifts and discount coupons; (4) reactivating purchases via automatic lifecycle marketing (birthday cards, off-season promotions); (5) avoiding serious relationship mistakes (a bank that missed that a late-payment customer was also a major depositor). The fifth case is the most distinctive — data mining as a defensive tool preventing costly relationship failures, not just offensive targeting.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Companies can use their data in several ways to create customer value and gain competitive advantage: To identify prospects... To decide which customers should receive a particular offer... To deepen customer loyalty... To reactivate customer purchases... To avoid serious customer mistakes.
>
> A major bank confessed to a number of mistakes it had made by not using its customer database well. In one case, the bank charged a customer a penalty for a late payment on his mortgage, failing to note that he headed a company that was a major depositor in this bank. The customer quit the bank.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's ops.knowledge_pages and user behavioral data should enable at minimum use cases 1-4. Use case 5 (avoid relationship mistakes) is especially relevant: Ritsu should flag when a churning user is a heavy-engagement user who hit a friction point, not a disengaged free user — the response is very different.
