# Observation: Online and Offline WOM Obey Different Mechanics

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 13 — Designing an Integrated Marketing Campaign in the Digital Age (pp. 863–890)

## Summary

Research finds that online WOM is driven more by novelty, excitement, and surprise (brand perceived as interesting). Offline WOM is driven by salience and top-of-mind availability — brands mentioned in face-to-face conversation are those that come easily to mind regardless of novelty. Positive WOM is shared by users from their own experience; negative WOM is more often transmitted as second-hand reports about others' experiences. Implication: offline and online WOM require different seeding strategies.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> More interesting brands are more likely to be talked about online, but whether a brand is seen as novel, exciting, or surprising has little effect on whether it is discussed in face-to-face, oral communications. The brands discussed offline are often those that are salient and visible and come easily to mind.
>
> Research has shown that consumers tend to generate positive word of mouth themselves and to share information about their own positive consumption experiences. They tend to transmit negative word of mouth only by passing on information they have heard about others' negative consumption experiences.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu needs two WOM strategies: (1) online — make the product genuinely surprising so students post about it (the 'you got it right' emotional moment, the beautiful mindmap); (2) offline — be the name students mention when asked 'what do you use to study,' which requires salience (repeat exposure, not just virality).
