# Observation: Ethnographic Research Revealed GE Was in an Artisanal Industry, Not a Commodity

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 84–undefined)

## Summary

MM 16e uses GE's plastics-fiber ethnographic research as a canonical case: GE assumed it was competing on price in a commodity market; ethnographic research revealed it was actually in an artisanal industry where customers wanted early-stage collaboration. This led to GE completely reorienting its customer interaction model. The lesson is that B2B market positioning errors (commodity vs. specialty) are discoverable only through deep qualitative insight, not price benchmarking.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> GE's ethnographic research into the plastic-fiber industry revealed that the firm wasn't in a commodity business driven by price, as it had assumed. Instead, it was in an artisanal industry whose customers wanted collaboration at the earliest stages of development. This resulted in GE completely reorienting the way it interacted with companies in the industry.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu may be making a similar positioning error with Educators — treating it as a price competition with Quizlet when Educators actually want curriculum collaboration, co-design of assessments, and early-stage product input. Ethnographic interviews with 5 Educators would surface this.
