# Observation: Avon's Market Share Collapse from Social Media Laggard Status

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 14 — Personal Selling and Direct Marketing (pp. 963–984)

## Summary

Avon's North American sales fell from $2.2B to $1.0B between 2010-2014 partly because the company was slow to adopt online marketing and social networking. Only in 2014 did Avon attempt to revamp its website (which had not been updated in a decade) and create social media marketing materials. Millennials preferred online-first experiences and were following social media influencers — a channel Avon had not facilitated for its reps. The lesson: in direct sales, digital fluency of the distribution network is as important as product quality.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> One of the reasons for the decline in Avon's market share was that the company was slow to pick up on the proliferation of online marketing and social networking. Only in 2014 did Avon attempt to refurbish its website, which hadn't had a makeover in a decade.
>
> Millennials, a rapidly growing market, preferred online to in-home events and were paying attention to Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter influencers—the type of social media marketing that Avon had failed to facilitate.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Inverse lesson for Ritsu: Ritsu IS a digital-native product. Its competitive moat against any incumbent EdTech is precisely the social-media-first distribution model. The risk is the opposite — being too platform-dependent on TikTok/Instagram and losing distribution if algorithms change.
