# Observation: Awareness-to-Satisfaction Diagnostic for Communication Priorities

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 12 — Managing Marketing Communications (pp. 289–295)

## Summary

The chapter presents a diagnostic matrix comparing two brands: Brand A has 80% awareness but only 20% satisfaction among those who tried — implying a product quality problem, not a communication problem. Brand B has 40% awareness but 80% satisfaction among triers — implying the communication campaign needs to drive trial. The framework prevents misallocating communication spend when the real bottleneck is product or vice versa.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> We find that 80 percent of the consumers in the total market are aware of Brand A, 60 percent have tried it, and only 20 percent who tried it are satisfied. This indicates that the communication program is effective in creating awareness, but the product fails to meet consumer expectations. In contrast, 40 percent of the consumers in the total market are aware of Brand B and only 30 percent have tried it, but 80 percent of them are satisfied. In this case, the communication program might benefit from focusing on creating awareness and encouraging brand trial.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu resembles Brand B: strong early user NPS/satisfaction signals with low awareness. The diagnostic tells us: do NOT optimize the product further before fixing awareness. Spend goes to communication, not R&D, at this stage.
