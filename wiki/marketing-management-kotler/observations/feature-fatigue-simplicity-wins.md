# Feature Fatigue: More Options Can Hurt Conversion

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 8 — Designing and Managing Products (pp. 162–176)

## Summary

Research shows that larger product assortments are not always better. More options can lead to information overload, choice overload, and unmet expectations (consumers expect to find the perfect option and leave empty-handed when they don't). Consumer response to assortment size depends on expertise: experts benefit from variety; novices unfamiliar with the product category benefit from fewer, simpler options.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> To avoid 'feature fatigue,' the company must prioritize features and tell consumers how to use and benefit from them.
>
> Larger assortments are more likely to lead to choice overload, which can occur when the consumer finds more than one satisfactory option in the available choice set.
>
> Empirical research shows that there are many instances when fewer options can make purchase more likely, lower the rate of return, and lead to more satisfied customers.
>
