# New Coke: Emotional Brand Attachment Invisible to Taste Tests

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 211–212)

## Summary

In 1985, Coca-Cola replaced its century-old formula with New Coke after extensive research and blind taste tests showed consumers preferred the sweeter version. The launch provoked a national uproar — angry letters, protests, and lawsuits. Market researchers had measured taste but failed to measure consumers' deep emotional attachment to the brand. The reversal, reintroduced as Classic Coke, actually strengthened the brand's market position, resulting in higher market share and more favorable attitudes. The lesson: brand equity resides in emotional and identity dimensions that product features alone cannot capture.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The company spent $4 million on market research, and blind taste tests showed that Coke drinkers preferred the new formula. But the launch of New Coke provoked a national uproar.
>
> Market researchers had measured the taste but failed to adequately measure the emotional attachment consumers had to Coca-Cola.
>
> Ironically, the failed introduction of New Coke ended up giving the old formula measurably stronger status in the marketplace, with more favorable attitudes and greater sales as a result.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's emotional dimension is built into the product (stickers, personality modes, milestone messages). This is not decoration — it is brand equity infrastructure. Users who feel emotionally attached to Ritsu's personality will resist switching to a technically superior but emotionally sterile competitor. Never A/B test away the emotional moments without measuring brand attachment.
