# Negatively Correlated Benefits: The Positioning Trade-off

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 7 — Crafting a Customer Value Proposition and Positioning (pp. 154–155)

## Summary

Many POPs+PODs inversely correlated in consumer minds (cheap vs quality, powerful vs safe, nutritious vs tasty, ubiquitous vs exclusive). Solutions: better product genuinely doing both; separate campaigns; endorser/secondary association lending credibility (Miller Lite 'tastes great + less filling').

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> One common challenge in positioning is that many of the benefits that make up points of parity and points of difference are negatively correlated.
>
> The challenge is that consumers typically want to maximize both of the negatively correlated attributes or benefits.
>
> Consider these examples of negatively correlated attributes and benefits: low price versus high quality, powerful versus safe, taste versus low calories.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu negatively correlated pair: 'fast/easy' (30s start) vs 'deep learning' (mastery takes effort). Users may perceive contradiction. Correlational POP problem. Solution: messaging explicitly resolves — 'Ritsu makes setup fast so you spend your time on the hard part: actually doing the work.'
