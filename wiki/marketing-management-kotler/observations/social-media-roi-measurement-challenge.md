# Observation: Social Media ROI Measurement Is Structurally Difficult — Most CMOs Cannot Quantify It

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 13 — Designing an Integrated Marketing Campaign in the Digital Age (pp. 1075–1135)

## Summary

A survey of CMOs shows social media accounts for 12% of marketing budgets (projected 20%+ in five years), yet 44% of CMOs cannot measure its impact on business. Only 20% have quantified it. Of brands that claim to measure social engagement, only 21% measure actual goal/revenue conversions. Four benefit categories exist: short-term financial (revenue + cost reduction), short-term digital (SEO lift, community support deflection), long-term brand lift, and long-term risk avoidance. The industry conflates vanity metrics (likes, shares) with business outcomes.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> According to a survey of CMOs by MDG Advertising, social media now account for 12 percent of marketing budgets. Of the CMOs surveyed, 44 percent say they have been unable to measure social media's overall impact on their business. And although 36 percent feel they have a good sense of the qualitative impact, only 20 percent have actually quantified the impact of social media on their business.
>
> Although 58 percent of brands state that they measure social engagement, only 21 percent measure goal or revenue conversions.
>
> Some marketing pundits compare social media to a phone: How would you assess the ROI of all the different calls you make?
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu should instrument social → signup → first-quiz-completion as the minimal viable attribution chain before scaling any social spend. Without this funnel instrumentation, all social spend is a cost center masquerading as a growth engine.
