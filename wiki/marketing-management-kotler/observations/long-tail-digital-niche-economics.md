# Long Tail: Digital Platforms Make Small Niches Viable

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 6 — Identifying Market Segments and Target Customers (pp. 135–136)

## Summary

Anderson's thesis: e-commerce shifts demand from hits to niches via lower distribution + better discovery. 80-20 → 50-50 in digital. Caveat: poor recommendations doom tail products to obscurity.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Anderson asserts that, as a result of the growth of e-commerce, the long tail holds significantly more value than before. The internet has directly contributed to the shifting of demand 'down the tail, from hits to niches.'
>
> With a new ability to match potential customers to niche offerings tailored to their tastes, a number of companies have started to derive increasing value from the long tail.
>
> Poor recommendation systems render many very-low-share products in the tail so obscure and hard to find that they disappear before they can be purchased frequently enough to justify their existence.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's multi-format strategy (YouTube, PDF, PPTX, URL, code) = long-tail play. Anderson's caveat: if Ritsu doesn't actively surface these capabilities via recommendation, remain invisible to users who'd value them.
