# Observation: Individualism vs Collectivism Drives Channel and Return Behavior Differently

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 3 — Analyzing Consumer Markets (pp. 54–55)

## Summary

A longitudinal study of 30,000 customers across 30 countries found that individualistic-culture consumers use multichannel purchasing (including online and catalog), return products readily, and buy for self-expression, while collectivist-culture consumers prefer brick-and-mortar, value long-term supplier reputation, and shop for others. This is an empirically grounded culture-segmentation finding with direct implications for EdTech distribution strategy in collectivist markets (e.g., Vietnam, where Ritsu operates its founding team).

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Consumers in individualistic societies like Australia and the United States are more likely to buy for themselves, follow trends, use multiple purchase channels (including online and catalog) to find the best deal, and return items that fall below expectations, whereas consumers in collectivist countries (e.g., Portugal, Mexico, and Turkey) tend to follow the crowd, value long-term reputation, shop for their families, buy from trusted retailers, and prefer traditional brick-and mortar stores.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's freemium conversion strategy in Vietnam and Southeast Asia should weight social proof and long-term reputation signals more heavily than in US/AU markets, where self-directed trial and comparison are the natural triggers.
