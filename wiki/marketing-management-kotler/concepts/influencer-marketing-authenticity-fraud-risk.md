# Influencer Marketing: Paid-Endorsement-in-Social-Context + Fraud Risk

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 13 — Designing an Integrated Marketing Campaign in the Digital Age (pp. 645–670)

## Summary

Influencer marketing pays popular online figures to promote brands within their social media feed — a hybrid of publicity and paid endorsement. Has grown to a multibillion-dollar industry with rising costs (top influencers exceed $100,000 per endorsement). Two structural problems: (1) demand has inflated prices faster than ROI evidence; (2) a global marketplace for social media fraud (bot followers, fake views) makes authenticating real reach essential. Marketers must verify follower authenticity, real engagement rates, and audience-product alignment before committing budget.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Influencer marketing refers to the use of a popular online figure to promote a product, service, or brand within his or her social media feed. The company pays the endorser to promote its offerings, but rather than using this endorsement in its own communications, the company relies on the influencer's own social media networks to disseminate the message.
>
> Influencer marketing has grown rapidly in recent years to become a multibillion-dollar industry. The demand for influencers has gone up, and the price of securing an endorsement has increased manyfold to reach over $100,000 for some of the top influencers.
>
> A growing number of companies specialize in selling Twitter followers and retweets, views on YouTube, and endorsements on LinkedIn to users who want to appear more popular and influential in the social media space. In one case a single company was found to be using over 3.5 million automated accounts, each sold to multiple customers, to provide influencers with more than 200 million Twitter followers.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's best influencer play is micro-influencers (study YouTubers, student TikTokers) rather than macro-celebrities. Authentication matters: follower count means nothing if the audience is not actively studying. Evaluate by comment quality and engagement-to-follower ratio, not raw numbers.
