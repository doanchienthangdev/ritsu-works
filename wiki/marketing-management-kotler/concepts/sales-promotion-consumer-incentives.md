# Sales Promotion: Consumer Incentives and Their Strategic Risks

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 11 — Managing Pricing and Sales Promotions (pp. 254–256)

## Summary

Sales promotions are short-term tools designed to stimulate quicker or greater purchase. Consumer incentives include price reductions, coupons, cash refunds/rebates, price packs, premiums, frequency programs, prizes/contests, tie-in promotions, seasonal discounts, and favorable financing. Key strategic risks: promotions produce high short-run sales response but little permanent volume gain; incessant discounting devalues the offering in buyers' minds; post-promotion dip often follows the initial spike. Some tools are 'consumer franchise building' (frequency awards, product-feature coupons, premiums related to the product); others are not (price-off packs, unrelated premiums, sweepstakes). Push strategy (trade incentives) works when brand loyalty is low; pull strategy (consumer advertising/promotion) works when brand loyalty and involvement are high.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Sales promotions can produce a high sales response in the short run but little permanent gain over the longer term.
>
> Incessant price reductions, coupons, deals, and premiums can have a longer-term negative impact by devaluing the company's offering in buyers' minds.
>
> Ideally, consumer incentives have short-run sales impact and long-run effects on brand equity.
>
> A push strategy uses the manufacturer's sales force, trade promotion money, or other means to induce intermediaries to carry, promote, and sell the product to end users. This strategy is particularly appropriate when there is low brand loyalty in a category.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu should use promotions sparingly and only with brand-building mechanics. Recommended: frequency programs (usage streaks → unlock premium features) rather than price-off discounts (which signal low value). Student referral programs with a reward tied to Ritsu usage (e.g., extra plan credits) are franchise-building. Avoid permanent coupon codes that establish a new reference price lower than list price.
