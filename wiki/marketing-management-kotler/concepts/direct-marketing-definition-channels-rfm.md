# Direct Marketing: Consumer-Direct Channels + RFM Customer Selection

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 14 — Personal Selling and Direct Marketing (pp. 689–776)

## Summary

Direct marketing uses consumer-direct channels (direct mail, catalog, telemarketing, kiosks, websites, mobile) to reach customers without intermediaries, seeking a measurable response (typically an order). Key selection tool: RFM formula — Recency (how recently a customer purchased), Frequency (how often), Monetary amount (how much spent). Higher RFM scores = better prospects for offers. E-mail direct marketing generates purchase rates at least 3x that of social media ads when timely, targeted, and relevant. Direct marketing's main advantage over traditional advertising is testability of every offer element under real market conditions.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Direct marketing is the use of consumer-direct channels to reach and deliver goods and services to customers without using intermediaries.
>
> Most direct marketers apply the RFM (recency, frequency, monetary amount) formula to select customers according to how much time has passed since their last purchase, how many times they have purchased, and how much they have spent since becoming a customer.
>
> E-mails can be very productive selling tools. The rate at which they prompt purchase has been estimated to be at least three times that of social media ads. To be effective, e-mails must be timely, targeted, and relevant.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's email list of free users is a high-value direct marketing asset. RFM segmentation: Recency = last login date; Frequency = sessions per week; Monetary = plan tier. Users who were active 2 weeks ago but haven't returned are the highest-priority re-engagement segment — more likely to convert than cold prospects.
