# Specific Investments, Lock-In, and Opportunism in B2B Relationships

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 93–94)

## Summary

When buyers and suppliers make relationship-specific investments (dedicated equipment, shared systems, tailored processes), both parties face lock-in risk. Buyers risk holdup due to switching costs; suppliers risk IP expropriation. Opportunism - 'a form of cheating or undersupply relative to an implicit or explicit contract' - becomes a threat when monitoring is hard and contingencies are difficult to anticipate. The presence of a long time horizon and solidarity norms converts specific investments from expropriation risk to bonding (reduced opportunism). This framework from transaction cost economics is more analytically precise than Principles' buyer-seller relationship coverage.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Specific investments are those expenditures tailored to a particular company and value-chain partner (investments in company-specific training, equipment, and operating procedures or systems).
>
> Opportunism is a 'form of cheating or undersupply relative to an implicit or explicit contract.'
>
> The presence of a significant future time horizon and/or strong solidarity norms typically causes customers and suppliers to strive for joint benefits. Their specific investments shift from expropriation (increased opportunism on the receiver's part) to bonding (reduced opportunism).
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's institutional contracts should be designed with long-term orientation signals (multi-year pricing, shared roadmap input, customer advisory board) to shift the relationship from transactional to bonding. Specific investments by the institution (custom content libraries, LMS integrations) increase switching costs favorably for Ritsu.
