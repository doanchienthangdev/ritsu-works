# Four Pricing Objectives: Profit, Penetration, Skimming, Quality Leadership

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 11 — Managing Pricing and Sales Promotions (pp. 243–244)

## Summary

A company's pricing objective determines its price-setting approach. (1) Short-term profit maximization: price to maximize current profit, cash flow, or ROI — risks ignoring long-run performance and competitive reactions. (2) Market penetration: set a very low price to maximize market share, assuming high price sensitivity; requires the market to be price-sensitive and production costs to fall with scale. (3) Market skimming: set a high price to skim maximum willingness-to-pay, then lower over time — works when initial demand is strong, high price signals quality, and the high price doesn't immediately attract competition. (4) Quality leadership: charge a premium to fund R&D, production quality, and service delivery; builds an intensely loyal customer base.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Four common pricing objectives are current profit, market penetration, market skimming, and quality leadership.
>
> Companies that choose penetration pricing want to maximize their market share. They believe a higher sales volume will lead to lower unit costs and higher long-run profit, so they set a very low price, assuming the market is price sensitive.
>
> In market skimming, a company is setting a relatively high price in an attempt to 'skim the cream' off the market by making the offering affordable only to customers with the highest willingness to pay.
>
> To maintain quality leadership, a company must charge a relatively high price in order to be able to invest in research and development, production, and service delivery.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's current objective is penetration (freemium = near-zero price to maximize trial) with a quality-leadership aspiration for the paid tier. The tension: penetration pricing trains users to expect free value, making upgrade conversion harder. Resolution: the free plan must be genuinely useful but must have clear, felt limitations (e.g., page limits, activity count limits) that create organic upgrade motivation.
