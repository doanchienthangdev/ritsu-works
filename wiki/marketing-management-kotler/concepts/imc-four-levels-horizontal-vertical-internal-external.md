# IMC Four Integration Levels: Horizontal, Vertical, Internal, External

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 13 — Designing an Integrated Marketing Campaign in the Digital Age (pp. 283–285)

## Summary

Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) operates on four distinct levels: (1) Horizontal — all marketing actions (pricing, packaging, sales promotions, distribution) coordinated with the communication campaign; (2) Vertical — communication objectives aligned with company's overarching marketing strategy goals; (3) Internal — product development, market research, sales, and customer service insights shared with the communication team; (4) External — the company's communication activities coordinated with external partners (agencies, PR firms, event organizers, co-sponsors). All four levels must be simultaneously managed or the result is 'a random compilation of unrelated and sometimes conflicting messages.'

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Integrated marketing communications (IMC) is an approach to managing a communication campaign through a coordinated use of different communication tools that work in concert and reinforce one another to enable the company to achieve its strategic goals.
>
> Horizontal integration involves coordinating all relevant marketing actions—including packaging, pricing, sales promotions, and distribution—with the communication campaign to achieve maximum customer impact. Vertical integration involves aligning the communication objectives with the higher-level goals. Internal integration involves sharing the relevant information from different departments with the communication team. External integration coordinates a company's communication activities with those of the external collaborators.
>
> Without an explicit focus on consistency, a company's communication can easily become a random compilation of unrelated (and sometimes even conflicting) messages, designed by different creative teams working in isolation from one another, and distributed though disparate media channels in a way that fails to emphasize their joint impact and might ultimately confuse rather than inform and persuade target customers.
>
