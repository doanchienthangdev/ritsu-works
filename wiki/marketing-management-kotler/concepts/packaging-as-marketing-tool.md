# Packaging as a Marketing Tool

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 8 — Designing and Managing Products (pp. 171–173)

## Summary

Packaging functions as a five-second commercial. Good packaging attracts attention, describes features, creates consumer confidence, and makes a favorable impression. Key factors driving packaging importance: self-service shopping dynamics (50-70% of purchases made in-store), consumer affluence, company/brand image (billboard effect on shelf), and innovation opportunity. Packaging must achieve multiple objectives: identify the brand, convey information, facilitate logistics, and aid consumption.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Packaging is important because it is the buyer's first encounter with the product. A good package draws the consumer in and encourages product choice. In effect, it can act as a 'five-second commercial' for the product.
>
> In an average supermarket, which may stock 15,000 items, the typical shopper passes some 300 products per minute. Given that 50 percent to 70 percent of all purchases are made in the store, effective packaging must perform many sales tasks: attract attention, describe the product's features, create consumer confidence, and make a favorable overall impression.
>
