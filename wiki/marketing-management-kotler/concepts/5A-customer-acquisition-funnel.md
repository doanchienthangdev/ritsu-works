# 5-A Customer Acquisition Funnel: Awareness, Appeal, Ask, Act, Advocate

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 19 — Building Customer Loyalty (pp. 420–421)

## Summary

A five-phase model replacing the linear AIDA funnel: Awareness (encounter), Appeal (shortlisting into consideration set), Ask (seeking additional information from external sources), Act (purchase and consumption experience), Advocate (loyalty and recommendation post-consumption). Conversion rates between phases identify specific bottlenecks. Advocates can exist without being purchasers.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The customer acquisition funnel can be represented in terms of five relatively distinct phases: awareness, appeal, ask, act, and advocate.
>
> The advocate phase reflects customers' reactions following their consumption experience. Ideally, customers' reactions will involve a certain level of loyalty to the company and its offering. These reactions can range from simply repurchasing the offering to ultimately advocating the offering to others. Active advocates recommend products, services, and brands they love without even being asked.
>
> By calculating conversion rates—the percentages of customers at the different stages who move to the next stage—managers can identify any bottleneck stage or barrier to building a loyal customer base.
>
