# Waterfall vs Sprinkler International Expansion Sequencing

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 20 — Tapping into Global Markets (pp. 444–445)

## Summary

Waterfall approach enters countries sequentially — careful planning, less resource strain, preferred for complex or high-investment products. Sprinkler approach enters many markets simultaneously — required when first-mover advantage is critical and competitive intensity is high. Technology-intensive firms and digital companies are increasingly born global, marketing worldwide from launch. Selection of approach depends on competitive dynamics and internal resource constraints.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Typical entry strategies are the waterfall approach, gradually entering countries in sequence, and the sprinkler approach, entering many countries simultaneously. Increasingly, firms—especially technology-intensive firms and online ventures—are born global and market to the entire world from the outset.
>
> Matsushita, BMW, General Electric, Benetton, and The Body Shop followed the waterfall approach, which allows firms to carefully plan expansion and is less likely to strain human and financial resources. But when first-mover advantage is crucial and a high degree of competitive intensity prevails, the sprinkler approach is better.
>
