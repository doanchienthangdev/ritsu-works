# Marketing Dashboards — Four Measurement Pathways

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 113–114)

## Summary

MM 16e defines marketing dashboards as 'a concise set of interconnected performance drivers to be viewed in common throughout the organization.' They should include two key market-based scorecards: one reflecting performance, one providing early-warning signals. Four measurement pathways structure the dashboard: (1) Customer Metrics - prospect-to-repeat-purchaser funnel, customer experience; (2) Unit Metrics - sales by product line/geography, marketing cost per unit sold, margin optimization; (3) Cash-Flow Metrics - program ROI, net present value of marketing investments; (4) Brand Metrics - perceptual health and financial health of the brand. 'Over time the number of metrics on the dashboard will be reduced to a few key drivers.'

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Marketing dashboards are 'a concise set of interconnected performance drivers to be viewed in common throughout the organization.'

> Company input to the marketing dashboard should include two key market-based scorecards: one that reflects performance and one that provides possible early warning signals.

> There are four common measurement 'pathways' marketers pursue today: the customer metrics pathway, the unit metrics pathway, the cash-flow metrics pathway, and the brand metrics pathway.
