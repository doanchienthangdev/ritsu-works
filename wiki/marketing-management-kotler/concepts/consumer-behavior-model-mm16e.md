# Model of Consumer Behavior (MM 16e Framing)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 3 — Analyzing Consumer Markets (pp. 53–54)

## Summary

MM 16e frames consumer behavior as a two-stage filter model: marketing tactics and market context pass through consumer characteristics (cultural, social, personal) and consumer psychology (motivation, perception, emotions, memory), producing a buying decision process (problem recognition through postpurchase) and a granular purchase decision (product, brand, store, quantity, timing, payment method). The distinctive contribution vs Principles is the explicit 'market context' input block (economic, technological, legal, political, sociocultural, physical) feeding the psychology layer simultaneously with marketing tactics.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The tactics shaping the offering and the context of the market in which the offering will be sold are filtered through the cultural, social, and personal lenses of target customers, as well as being influenced by consumer motivation, perception, emotions, and memory.
>
> Research on consumer behavior explores how individuals, groups, and organizations select, buy, use, and dispose of goods, services, ideas, or experiences to satisfy their needs and wants.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu sits at the intersection of motivation (esteem/self-actualization), perception (how the product is positioned vs ChatGPT/Anki), and memory (spaced repetition). Understanding which layer of the model Ritsu needs to move determines whether messaging or product design is the lever.
