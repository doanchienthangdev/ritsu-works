# Luxury Branding: Principles and Management

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 230–232)

## Summary

Luxury brands command premium prices based on quality, uniqueness, craftsmanship, heritage, authenticity, and history. Key management principles: all marketing decisions (product, pricing, promotions, distribution) must align with the brand image; luxury brands typically create aspirational, premium imagery; luxury brands span categories and must aggressively protect identity against counterfeits; all brand attributes (names, logos, packaging, associations) must align with the brand image. The luxury market has shifted from pure social status signaling to style and self-expression in developed markets. China became the world's largest luxury market and its consumers have evolved from logo-driven to quality-conscious.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The common denominators of luxury brands are quality and uniqueness. A winning formula for many is craftsmanship, heritage, authenticity, and history (often critical to justifying a high price).
>
> All marketing decisions associated with luxury brands — product, service, pricing, sales incentives, communication, and distribution — must be aligned to ensure that purchase and consumption experiences are consistent with the image of the brand.
>
> In the end, luxury brand marketers have to remember they are often selling a dream, anchored in product quality, status, and prestige.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu is not a luxury brand, but the principle of alignment applies: Ritsu's freemium pricing, UX quality, support responsiveness, and marketing copy must all signal the same value tier. Mixed signals (premium marketing language + free-tier-quality AI output) create a luxury-brand-equivalent gap that destroys trust faster than in mass market products.
