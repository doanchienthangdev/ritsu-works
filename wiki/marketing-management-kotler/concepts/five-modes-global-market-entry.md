# Five Modes of Foreign Market Entry: Escalating Commitment-Risk-Control-Profit Spectrum

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 20 — Tapping into Global Markets (pp. 449–452)

## Summary

Companies enter foreign markets through five modes in increasing order of commitment, risk, control, and profit potential: indirect exporting (via intermediaries), direct exporting (own export department or local subsidiaries), licensing (IP/brand for fee/royalty), joint ventures (shared ownership with local partner), and direct investment (ownership of foreign manufacturing or service facilities). Most firms start with exporting and escalate as market size and confidence grow.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Once a company decides to target a particular country, it must choose the best mode of entry for its brands. Its broad choices are indirect exporting, direct exporting, licensing, joint ventures, and direct investment. Each succeeding strategy entails more commitment, risk, control, and profit potential.
>
> Many companies use direct or indirect exporting to 'test the waters' before building a plant and manufacturing their product overseas.
>
> The ultimate form of foreign involvement is direct investment: The foreign company can buy part or full interest in a local company or build its own manufacturing or service facilities.
>
