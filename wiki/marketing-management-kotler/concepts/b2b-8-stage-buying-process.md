# Eight-Stage Business Buying Process — From Problem Recognition to Performance Review

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 84–85)

## Summary

MM 16e presents a canonical 8-stage B2B buying model: (1) Problem Recognition, (2) Need Description, (3) Product Specification - via product value analysis engineering teams, (4) Supplier Search - including e-hubs, buying alliances, and spot markets, (5) Proposal Solicitation, (6) Supplier Selection - using weighted-score vendor evaluation models and economic value analysis (EVA), (7) Contract Negotiation - blanket contracts and vendor-managed inventory, (8) Performance Review. Key distinctive elements vs Principles: the Product Value Analysis step in Stage 3, the explicit EVA framework in Stage 6, and blanket contracts/vendor-managed inventory in Stage 7.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> One popular model delineates eight distinct stages of the decision process in business markets.
>
> Product value analysis is an approach to cost reduction that studies whether components can be redesigned, standardized, or made by cheaper methods of production without adversely affecting product performance.
>
> Economic value analysis (EVA) is a tool that helps monetize the functional benefits of a company's offering, such as performance, reliability, and warranty.
>
> A blanket contract establishes a long-term relationship in which the supplier promises to resupply the buyer as needed, at agreed-upon prices, over a specified period of time.
>

When Ritsu targets Educators, the buying process maps to these 8 stages. Stage 3 (Product Spec) requires Ritsu to provide a clear curriculum-alignment document. Stage 6 (Supplier Selection) requires EVA framing: Ritsu saves 3h/week of flashcard creation at $X/student.
