# Media Planning: Reach, Frequency, Impact and GRP Trade-offs

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 12 — Managing Marketing Communications (pp. 712–745)

## Summary

Three media planning variables: Reach (R) = number of unique persons exposed at least once; Frequency (F) = average exposures per person; Impact (I) = qualitative value of an exposure in a given medium. Total exposures = R x F = GRP (Gross Rating Points). Weighted exposures = R x F x I. Core trade-off: a fixed budget forces choice between wide reach at low frequency or narrow reach at high frequency. Reach is paramount for new launches; frequency is paramount when competitive noise is high or message is complex. Forgetting rate drives repetition requirements.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Reach (R). The number of different persons or households exposed to a particular media schedule at least once during a specified time period. Frequency (F). The number of times within the specified time period that an average person or household is exposed to the message. Impact (I). The qualitative value of an exposure through a given medium.
>
> Reach is most important when launching new products, extensions of well-known brands, or infrequently purchased brands, or when going after an undefined target market. Frequency is most important when there are strong competitors, a complex story to tell, high consumer resistance, or a frequent purchase cycle.
>
> A key reason for repetition is consumer forgetfulness. The higher the forgetting rate associated with a brand, product category, or message, the higher the warranted level of repetition.
>
