# Brand Equity Measurement: Cost, Market, and Financial Approaches

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 213–215)

## Summary

Three primary approaches exist for measuring brand equity financially. The cost approach calculates equity from historical or replacement costs of brand building (marketing research, design, communication, legal costs). The market approach measures the revenue premium of a branded versus unbranded identical offering. The financial approach calculates the net present value of future brand earnings, typically in three steps: compute future cash flow, estimate brand's contribution to that cash flow, adjust for risk/volatility. No universally agreed method exists; most companies use multiple approaches. Brand valuation differs from brand equity — valuation estimates total financial value while equity focuses on consumer-facing sources.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Although the importance of brand equity is undisputed, there is no universally agreed-on method for assessing the equity of a brand accurately.
>
> The cost approach calculates brand equity by examining the costs of developing the brand, such as marketing research, brand design, communication, management, and legal costs.
>
> The financial approach evaluates brand equity as the net present value (NPV) of a brand's future earnings and usually encompasses three key steps: computing the company's future cash flow, estimating the brand's contribution to the company's future cash flow, and adjusting this cash flow using a risk factor.
>
> John Stuart, cofounder of Quaker Oats, said, 'If this business were split up, I would give you the land and bricks and mortar, and I would take the brands and trademarks, and I would fare better than you.'
>
