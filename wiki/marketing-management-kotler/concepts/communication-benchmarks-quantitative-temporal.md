# Communication Benchmarks: Quantitative + Temporal Specification

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 12 — Managing Marketing Communications (pp. 320–325)

## Summary

Effective communication objectives require two benchmark types: quantitative (level of awareness, strength of preference, specific action) and temporal (timeframe). Example: 'Create awareness of new movie among 40% of Millennials prior to premiere.' Without both, design and measurement are impossible. Benchmarks serve double duty as design guides and post-campaign evaluation yardsticks.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> A communication objective must have a clearly articulated focus as well as clearly defined quantitative and temporal benchmarks.
>
> Quantitative benchmarks quantify a particular objective. Temporal benchmarks define the timeframe within which a particular outcome has to be achieved. Quantitative and temporal benchmarks are closely related and interdependent: Setting a particular timeframe is contingent on the magnitude of the desired outcome.
>
> Create awareness of the new James Bond movie (focus) among 40 percent of Millennial consumers (quantitative benchmark) prior to the movie premiere (temporal benchmark).
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's comms lack explicit benchmarks. Applying this: 'Grow unaided awareness of Ritsu among college students studying for exams from ~0% to 15% within 90 days' is a valid Q+T benchmark that forces channel and budget decisions.
