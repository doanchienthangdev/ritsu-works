# Brand Mantra: Heart and Soul of the Brand

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 215–216)

## Summary

A brand mantra is a three-to-five word articulation of the heart and soul of the brand, closely related to 'brand essence' and 'core brand promise.' It is an internal document — unlike consumer-facing slogans — designed to guide employee actions and filter marketing decisions. Good mantras meet three criteria: communicate what is unique about the brand, simplify the brand's essence (short, crisp, vivid), and inspire employees. Nike's mantra 'authentic athletic performance' filtered all product decisions; Disney's 'fun family entertainment' screened licensing deals. The mantra creates a mental filter to screen out brand-inappropriate actions.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> A brand mantra is a three- to five-word articulation of the heart and soul of the brand and is closely related to other branding concepts like 'brand essence' and 'core brand promise.' The mantra's purpose is to guide the actions of all employees within the organization.
>
> Brand mantras must economically communicate what the brand is and what it is not.
>
> Nike marketers adopted the three-word brand mantra 'authentic athletic performance' to guide their marketing efforts. Thus, in Nike's eyes, its entire marketing program — its products and the way they are sold — must reflect that key brand value.
>
> Disney moved quickly to ensure that a consistent image — reinforcing its key brand associations — was conveyed by all third-party products and services. To that end, Disney adopted the internal brand mantra 'fun family entertainment' to filter proposed ventures.
>
