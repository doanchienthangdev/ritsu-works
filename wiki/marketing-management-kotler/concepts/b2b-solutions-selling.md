# Systems Selling — Transitioning from Products to Total Problem Solutions

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 87–88)

## Summary

MM 16e describes systems selling (or solutions selling) as providing a 'turnkey solution' where the buyer 'simply had to turn one key to get the job done.' The Japanese cement factory example illustrates the power of taking the broadest view of customer needs (economic development vs. just building a factory). Systems contracting is a variant where a single supplier manages all MRO requirements and customer inventory. The key insight: suppliers who influence spec development early and offer the broadest solution scope win contracts that pure product sellers cannot. Adobe's shift to cloud subscriptions is the software parallel.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Many business buyers prefer to buy a total problem solution from one seller.
>
> The Japanese viewed the problem not just as building a cement factory (the narrow view of systems selling) but as contributing to Indonesia's economic development. They took the broadest possible view of the customer's needs, which is true systems selling.
>
> Suppliers, however, are not just at the mercy of customer demands. Ideally, they're active early in the process to influence the actual development of the specifications.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's educator channel should offer a full course-creation solution: upload syllabus → AI generates quiz bank, flashcard set, student performance dashboard → teacher gets weekly learning analytics. This is systems selling vs just 'give students a quiz tool.'
