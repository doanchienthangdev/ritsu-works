# Product Line Stretching and Filling

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 8 — Designing and Managing Products (pp. 169–171)

## Summary

Companies lengthen product lines via two strategies. Line stretching moves the line beyond its current price/market range — downward (to capture lower-tier growth or block competitors), upward (for higher margins or full-line positioning), or both ways simultaneously. Line filling adds items within the existing range to capture incremental profits, satisfy dealers, or plug competitive gaps. Over-filling risks cannibalization and customer confusion.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Line stretching occurs when a company lengthens its product line beyond its current range, whether down-market, up-market, or both ways.
>
> A firm can also lengthen its product line through line filling — that is, by adding more items within the existing range. Motives for line filling include reaching for incremental profits, satisfying dealers who complain about lost sales because of items missing from the line, addressing consumers' desire for variety, trying to become the leading full-line company, and plugging holes to keep out competitors.
>
> Line filling is overdone if it results in cannibalization, causes confusion, or merely does not address the needs of any customer segment.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's freemium tier structure is a downward stretch question: the free plan is the down-market product protecting against ChatGPT, while paid tiers are the up-market. Risk of line filling: adding too many activity types without clear segmentation could confuse new users.
