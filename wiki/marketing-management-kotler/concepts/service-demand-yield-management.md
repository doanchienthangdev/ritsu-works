# Service Demand and Yield Management

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 9 — Designing and Managing Services (pp. 187–189)

## Summary

Because services cannot be stored, demand-supply mismatches create direct revenue loss. Yield management optimizes pricing and capacity to match demand to available supply. On the demand side: differential pricing (shifting demand off-peak), cultivating non-peak demand, reservation systems, complementary services during waits. On the supply side: part-time employees for peaks, consumer self-service participation, shared services, and capacity pre-investment. Dynamic pricing (Uber surge, zoo variable admission) is the modern dominant form.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Demand or yield management is critical; the right services must be available to the right customers at the right places at the right times and at the right prices to maximize profitability.
>
> Differential pricing will shift some demand from peak to off-peak periods.
>
> Services cannot be stored, so their perishability can be a problem when demand fluctuates.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's AI compute costs spike during exam seasons when thousands of students simultaneously generate study plans. Yield management applies: consider seasonal pricing, time-based usage caps, or queuing during peak demand. Free plan users could be offered 'off-peak' processing with longer wait times, while paid users get priority — a form of service-tier yield management.
