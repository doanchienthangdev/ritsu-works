# Channel Design as System Optimization

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 15 — Designing and Managing Distribution Channels (pp. 329–339)

## Summary

MM 16e frames channel design as architecture of interdependent system whose total-system cost + service output must be optimized simultaneously. No single logistics manager can minimize own cost without creating inefficiencies elsewhere.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> No system can simultaneously maximize customer service and minimize distribution cost. Maximum customer service implies large inventories, premium transportation, and multiple warehouses, all of which raise market-logistics costs.
>
> Given these trade-offs, managers must make decisions on a total-system basis.
>
> This multichannel architecture optimizes coverage, customization, and control while minimizing cost and conflict.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Direct channel (zero-level) means no intermediaries to optimize across — but trade-off shows up internally between AI quality, latency, infra cost, support quality. Total-system thinking applies to Ritsu's stack architecture.
