# Marketing Insights vs Information — The Diagnostic Gap

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 99–100)

## Summary

MM 16e draws a sharp distinction: 'Marketing insights provide diagnostic information about how and why we observe certain effects in the marketplace and what that means to marketers.' Information is not insight. The Star Wars failure case illustrates: the researcher delivered opinion and market data (Vietnam War hangover, sci-fi skepticism) but failed to analyze the script as a fundamentally human story. Insight requires understanding the mechanism, not just the correlation. The Walgreens repositioning case shows insight at work: research revealed it was perceived as a convenience store, which drove a strategic pivot to premium healthcare brand.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Marketing insights provide diagnostic information about how and why we observe certain effects in the marketplace and what that means to marketers.
>
> What this researcher delivered was information and opinion, not insight. He failed to study the script itself, to see that it was a fundamentally human story - of love, conflict, loss, and redemption - that happened to play out against the backdrop of space.
>
> When analyzing the available information and making a decision, it is important to draw a line between market data and market insights. Information is not knowledge, noted Albert Einstein.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's research practice must pursue diagnostic insight, not just NPS scores. The question 'why do users churn after 3 sessions?' requires a mechanism answer (no perceived progress, wrong activity type for their study goal), not just a correlation metric.
