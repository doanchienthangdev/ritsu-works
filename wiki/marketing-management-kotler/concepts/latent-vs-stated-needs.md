# Latent and Unconscious Needs — The Gap Principle

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 3 — Analyzing Consumer Markets (pp. 59–60)

## Summary

Customers frequently cannot articulate their real need. 'Responding only to the stated need may shortchange the customer.' Companies that help consumers discover unarticulated desires ('help customers learn what they want') gain durable advantage. MM 16e uses Dollar Shave Club (realizing you overpay for razors) and Blue Apron (overcoming culinary self-doubt) as canonical examples of new categories built by surfacing latent needs. The implication: product positioning that forces customers to recognize a pre-existing problem is more powerful than feature enumeration.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Some customers have needs that they are not fully conscious of or cannot articulate.
>
> Responding only to the stated need may shortchange the customer.
>
> To gain an edge, companies must help customers learn what they want — and make it convenient for them to obtain it.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's wedge is exactly this: most learners think re-reading works. They have a latent need for active learning that they cannot name. Ritsu's homepage copy ('Stop re-reading. Start mastering.') is a direct application of the gap principle.
