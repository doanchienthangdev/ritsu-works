# Brand Elements: Selection Criteria (Memorable, Meaningful, Likable, Transferable, Adaptable, Protectable)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 216–217)

## Summary

Brand elements are devices that identify and differentiate the brand — names, logos, slogans, characters, jingles, packaging. Selection criteria fall into two groups: brand-building (memorable, meaningful, likable) and defensive (transferable, adaptable, protectable). Memorable elements aid recall at purchase and consumption. Meaningful elements signal product category and benefits inherently. Likable elements are aesthetically appealing. Transferable elements work across categories and geographies. Adaptable elements can be updated over time. Protectable elements can be trademarked and defended against competitive imitation.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> There are several factors to consider when choosing brand elements. They should be memorable, meaningful, and likable, as well as transferable, adaptable, and protectable. The first three characteristics are brand-building. The latter three are defensive; they help maintain leverage and preserve brand equity against challenges.
>
> A recent trend is toward playful names that also offer a readily available URL, especially for online brands like Flickr, Instagram, Pinterest, Tumblr, and Dropbox.
>
> When names are in danger of becoming synonymous with product categories — as has happened to Kleenex, Kitty Litter, Jell-O, Scotch Tape, Xerox, and Fiberglass — their makers should retain their trademark rights and not allow the brand to become generic.
>
