# The Four Distinctive Characteristics of Services (4 I's)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 9 — Designing and Managing Services (pp. 185–188)

## Summary

Services differ from physical products along four dimensions: (1) Intangibility — cannot be seen, tasted, or felt before purchase; providers must 'manage the evidence' to tangibilize the intangible. (2) Inseparability — typically produced and consumed simultaneously; the provider is part of the service. (3) Variability — quality depends on who, when, where, and to whom the service is delivered. (4) Perishability — services cannot be stored; unmatched supply and demand causes revenue loss.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Researchers delineate four key characteristics that distinguish services from products: intangibility, inseparability, variability, and perishability.
>
> Unlike physical products, services cannot be seen, tasted, felt, heard, or smelled before they are bought. Therefore, the service provider's task is to 'manage the evidence' — to 'tangibilize the intangible.'
>
> Services cannot be stored, so their perishability can be a problem when demand fluctuates.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu is a pure service. Key implications: Intangibility means the first onboarding session (the AI tutor interaction) is the product trial — Ritsu must make AI quality immediately tangible. Variability: AI output quality varies by model version, document type, and subject — this is Ritsu's variability problem, requiring quality guardrails. Perishability: unused free trial capacity = lost revenue.
