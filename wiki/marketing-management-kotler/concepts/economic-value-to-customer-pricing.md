# Economic-Value-to-Customer (EVC) Pricing

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 11 — Managing Pricing and Sales Promotions (pp. 248–250)

## Summary

EVC pricing sets price based on the total economic value the customer receives, not on cost plus margin. It incorporates product performance, channel deliverables, warranty quality, customer support, supplier reputation, trustworthiness, and esteem. The method requires understanding customer decision-making processes fully and demonstrating total cost of ownership advantages versus alternatives. The Caterpillar example: a $100K tractor vs. a $90K competitor's tractor is justified by $7K superior durability + $6K superior reliability + $5K superior service + $2K longer warranty = $20K extra value delivered against $10K price premium. The key: deliver more unique value than competitors and demonstrate this to prospective buyers.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Economic-value-to-customer pricing takes into account a host of inputs, such as the buyer's image of the product performance, the channel deliverables, the warranty quality, customer support, and softer attributes such as the supplier's reputation, trustworthiness, and esteem.
>
> A seller can successfully charge a higher price than competitors if it can convince customers that it offers the lowest total cost of ownership.
>
> The Caterpillar dealer is able to show that although customers are asked to pay a $10,000 premium for this tractor, they are actually getting $20,000 extra value! The customer chooses the Caterpillar tractor because he or she is convinced that its lifetime operating costs will be lower.
>
> The key to economic-value-to-customer pricing is to deliver more unique value than competitors and to demonstrate this to prospective buyers.
>
