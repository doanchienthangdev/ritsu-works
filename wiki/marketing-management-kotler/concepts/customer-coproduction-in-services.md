# Customer Coproduction and Self-Service in Services

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 9 — Designing and Managing Services (pp. 190–191)

## Summary

Customers actively participate in service delivery — their words and actions affect service quality for themselves and others. One-third of all service problems are caused by the customer. Strategies to manage coproduction: redesign processes to simplify customer roles, use technology to guide customer behavior, create high-performance customers by clarifying their roles and motivations, and encourage 'customer citizenship' (customers helping each other). Self-service technologies extend coproduction but require customers to have clear role clarity, perceived benefit, and confidence in their ability to use the technology.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The reality is that customers do not merely purchase and use a service; they play an active role in its delivery. Their words and actions affect the quality of their service experiences and those of others, as well as the productivity of frontline employees.
>
> One study estimated that one-third of all service problems are caused by the customer.
>
> Create high-performance customers by enhancing the clarity of their role, their motivation, and their ability to perform their role.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's service is heavily coproduced: the user must upload a good document, choose appropriate activities, and actively engage (not just passively watch). Learners who upload low-quality or irrelevant documents produce poor AI output and blame the product. Ritsu must design onboarding to clarify the user's role (good input = good output), set expectations, and provide document quality feedback — making the customer a 'high-performance' coproducer.
