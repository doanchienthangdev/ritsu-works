# Product-Mix Pricing: Bundling, Captive Pricing, Two-Part Pricing

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 11 — Managing Pricing and Sales Promotions (pp. 252–253)

## Summary

When a product is part of a broader mix, price-setting logic must account for demand and cost interdependencies across the portfolio. Six key situations: (1) Loss-leader pricing — one product priced low to drive overall store traffic and cross-sell. (2) Optional-feature pricing — deciding what to include standard vs. charge extra for. (3) Captive pricing — low price on the main product, high markup on required consumables (razors and blades model). (4) Two-part pricing — fixed fee plus variable usage fee (fixed base + usage overage). (5) By-product pricing — charging for production waste to enable lower main-product prices. (6) Bundling — pure (only as bundle) or mixed (individually plus bundle at a discount).

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> In product-mix pricing, the firm searches for a set of prices that maximize profits on the total mix.
>
> Captive-product pricing: Manufacturers of razors often price them low and set high markups on razor blades.
>
> Two-part pricing: Service firms engage in two-part pricing, consisting of a fixed fee plus a variable usage fee. The fixed fee should be low enough to induce purchase; profit can then come from the usage fees.
>
> In mixed bundling, the seller offers goods both individually and in bundles, normally charging less for the bundle than if the items were purchased separately.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's freemium is a form of captive pricing: the free plan creates habit and data (the razor), the paid plan unlocks full power (the blade revenue). Two-part pricing applies to future B2B or educator plans: base fee (platform access) + usage fee (number of student accounts or document pages processed). Bundling opportunity: 'Ritsu + Notion integration' or 'Ritsu + YouTube learning' as bundles targeting self-learner and engineer ICPs.
