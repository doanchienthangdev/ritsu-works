# Why New Products Fail: The Value-Delivery Gap

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 18 — Developing New Market Offerings (pp. 399–399)

## Summary

New products fail at rates up to 95%. Root causes collapse to a single explanation: the offering fails to create superior value for target customers in a way that sufficiently benefits the company and collaborators. Proximate causes include ignored market research, overestimated market size, poor performance, inadequate pricing, and insufficient distribution.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Successful new-product launches are exceptions rather than the rule. New products fail at rates estimated to be as high as 95 percent. The most common reasons include ignored or misinterpreted market research, overestimates of market size, high development costs, poor product performance, inadequate pricing, ineffective communication, insufficient distribution support, proactive competitive response, lack of organizational support for the new offering, and inadequate return on the company's investment.
>
> The different reasons for failure can be summed up in one sentence: The new offering fails to create superior value for its target customers in a way that sufficiently benefits the company and its collaborators.
>
