# Customer Lifetime Value (CLV): Net Present Value of Future Profit Stream

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 19 — Building Customer Loyalty (pp. 918–929)

## Summary

CLV is the net present value of the expected revenue stream minus costs of acquisition, retention, and cross-selling, discounted at 10-20% depending on cost of capital. It captures not just direct purchase revenue but also referral and word-of-mouth value. The 80/20 rule applies: top 20% of customers may generate over 100% of profits. CLV is the primary driver of shareholder value; managing customer equity means optimizing the aggregate CLV of all customers.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Customer lifetime value (CLV) reflects the monetary equivalent of the value that customers will create for the company during their tenure with the company.
>
> Customer lifetime value is typically calculated as the net present value of the stream of future profits expected over the customer's lifetime purchases. The company must subtract from its expected revenues the expected costs of attracting, selling, and servicing the account of that customer, applying the appropriate discount rate (say, between 10 and 20 percent, depending on cost of capital and risk attitudes).
>
> The well-known 80/20 rule suggests that 80 percent or more of the company's profits come from the top 20 percent of its customers. Some cases may be more extreme: The most profitable 20 percent of customers (on a per capita basis) may account for over 100 percent of the company's profits.
>
