# Stage-Gate Framework for New-Offering Development (5 Stages)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 18 — Developing New Market Offerings (pp. 400–401)

## Summary

A sequential process with decision gates between: (1) idea generation — uncovering unmet needs; (2) concept development — prototype embodying the idea; (3) business-model design — target market, value proposition, offering attributes; (4) offering implementation — resource development and market testing; (5) commercial deployment — selective then full-market launch. Gates screen out unviable projects early when costs are low.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The stage-gate approach divides the innovation process into stages, with a gate or checkpoint at the end of each stage. The ultimate goal of using a stage-gate approach to new-product development is to ensure market success in a way that minimizes risk and optimizes allocation of the company's resources.
>
> This framework presents a streamlined version of the stage-gate approach, which comprises five key stages—idea generation, concept development, business-model design, offering development, and commercial deployment—separated by hurdles that aim to validate the actions taken in the previous step.
>
> At the beginning of the process, the company might consider a large number of ideas at a relatively low cost. Many of these ideas are screened out; only a few proceed to the concept-development stage, which requires a relatively larger investment in prototyping and testing.
>
