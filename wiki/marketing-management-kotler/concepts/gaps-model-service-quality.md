# GAPS Model of Service Quality (Five Gaps)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 9 — Designing and Managing Services (pp. 200–201)

## Summary

The service-quality model identifies five gaps that prevent excellent service delivery. Gap 1: Management does not correctly perceive what customers want. Gap 2: Management correctly perceives wants but does not set a uniform performance standard. Gap 3: Performance standards exist but employees cannot or will not meet them. Gap 4: Service delivery does not match external communications (advertising creates expectations the service fails to meet). Gap 5: Consumer misperceives the quality of service received (perceived < expected service). Gap 5 is the summary gap caused by one or more of Gaps 1-4.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The service-quality model in Figure 9.2 highlights the main requirements for delivering high service quality. It also identifies five gaps that prevent successful delivery.
>
> Gap between consumer expectation and management perception — Management does not always correctly perceive what customers want.
>
> Gap between service delivery and external communications — Consumer expectations are affected by statements made by company representatives and ads. If a hospital brochure shows a beautiful room but the patient finds it cheap and tacky-looking, external communications have distorted the customer's expectations.
>
> Gap between perceived and expected service — The consumer may misperceive the service quality.
>
