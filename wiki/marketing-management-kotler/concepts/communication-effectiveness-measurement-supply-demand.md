# Measuring Communication Effectiveness: Supply-Side vs. Demand-Side

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 12 — Managing Marketing Communications (pp. 924–980)

## Summary

Supply-side measurement assesses media coverage volume (seconds of TV visibility, column inches of press). Demand-side measurement assesses audience effect: recognition, recall, message point retention, attitude change, purchase intent, behavioral response. The share-of-expenditures → share-of-voice → share-of-mind/heart → share-of-market chain provides a composite effectiveness model. Pre-tests refine creative; post-tests evaluate campaign impact. The most actionable metric is behavior (purchase, trial, word-of-mouth), not just awareness.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Research on the impact of communication seeks to determine the degree to which a company's communication has been effective in achieving its goals. Communication effectiveness can be measured from two perspectives: supply side and demand side.
>
> On the demand side, measuring communication effectiveness aims to assess the effect a company's campaign has on the target audience. Members of the target audience are asked whether they recognize or recall the message, how many times they saw it, what points they recall, how they felt about the message, what their previous attitudes toward the product and the company were, and what their current attitudes are.
>
> A company's share of advertising expenditures produces a share of voice that earns a share of consumers' minds and hearts and, ultimately, a share of market.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu should track demand-side metrics: unaided recall among target student segments, NPS as a proxy for share-of-heart, viral coefficient as share-of-voice surrogate. Avoid the vanity trap of supply-side (impression counts) without demand-side (awareness lift, trial conversion).
