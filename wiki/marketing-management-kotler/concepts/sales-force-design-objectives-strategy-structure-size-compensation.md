# Sales Force Design Framework: Objectives, Strategy, Structure, Size, Compensation

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 14 — Personal Selling and Direct Marketing (pp. 270–315)

## Summary

A sales force requires explicit design across five dimensions: (1) Objectives — what specific tasks reps do (prospecting, targeting, communicating, selling, servicing, allocating); (2) Strategy — direct company force vs. contractual manufacturer's reps, with a cost cross-over point (SB) where company force becomes more economical; (3) Structure — geographic, product-based, customer-based, or leveraged hybrid; (4) Size — workload method (accounts x call frequency / rep capacity); (5) Compensation — fixed salary (stability, non-selling duties) vs. variable commission (individual initiative, cyclical sales), with three plan types: straight salary, straight commission, combination.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> In designing the sales force, the company must develop sales force objectives, strategy, structure, size, and compensation.
>
> The days when all the sales force did was sell, sell, and sell are long gone. Sales reps need to know how to diagnose a customer's problem and propose a solution that can help improve the customer's profitability.
>
> A direct (company) sales force consists of full- or part-time paid employees who work exclusively for the company. A contractual sales force consists of manufacturers' reps, sales agents, and brokers who earn a commission based on sales.
>
> U.S. firms spend more than a trillion dollars annually on sales forces and sales force materials—more than on any other promotional method.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu has no sales force today. When institutional/educator sales begins, starting with a contractual model (commission-only education consultants or affiliate-style partnerships with study influencers) is more capital-efficient than a direct force below the SB threshold. The educator segment should use an inside sales model initially — lower cost per contact.
