# Marketing Metrics — Short-Term Results and Brand Equity Scorecard

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 112–113)

## Summary

MM 16e frames marketing metrics evaluation as a two-part system (per London Business School's Tim Ambler): (1) short-term results - sales turnover, shareholder value; (2) brand equity changes - customer awareness, attitudes, behaviors, market share, relative price premium, number of complaints, distribution, total customers, perceived quality, loyalty and retention. The chapter provides three concrete metric taxonomies: Mary Kay's four long-term brand strength metrics plus short-term program metrics; Virgin America's full online cost-per-acquisition/CPM stack; and Mindbody's weekly analytics set (website analytics, social media presence, permission marketing statistics).

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Marketing metrics is the set of measures that help marketers quantify, compare, and interpret their performance.
>
> London Business School's Tim Ambler believes that firms can split evaluation of marketing performance into two parts: short-term results and changes in brand equity. Short-term results often reflect profit-and-loss concerns as shown by sales turnover, shareholder value, or some combination of the two.
>
> Brand-equity measures could include customer awareness, attitudes, and behaviors; market share; relative price premium; number of complaints; distribution and availability; total number of customers; perceived quality, and loyalty and retention.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's early KPI registry should implement the Ambler two-part structure: short-term (signups, conversion, MRR, churn) and brand equity (NPS, awareness in ICP segments, relative price premium vs Quizlet, perceived quality from user reviews). Both parts feed the 10-metrics pillar.
