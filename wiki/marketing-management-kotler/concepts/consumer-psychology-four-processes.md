# Four Psychological Processes Shaping Purchase (Motivation, Perception, Emotions, Memory)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 3 — Analyzing Consumer Markets (pp. 58–59)

## Summary

MM 16e explicitly names emotions alongside motivation, perception, and memory as the four key psychological processes - a subtle but meaningful upgrade from Principles, which uses 'learning' in the fourth slot. The emotions slot reflects behavioral decision theory influence: purchases respond to feelings and anticipated regret, not just rational evaluation. Need becomes motivation only when 'aroused to a sufficient level of intensity to drive us to act.' Marketers must intervene at the right psychological process, not just the right stage of the buying journey.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Four key psychological processes - motivation, perception, learning, and memory - fundamentally influence consumer responses.
>
> A need becomes a motivation when aroused to a sufficient level of intensity to drive us to act. Motivation has both direction (we select one goal over another) and intensity (we pursue the chosen goal with more or less vigor).
>
> Some customers have needs that they are not fully conscious of or cannot articulate.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu users often have a latent need - they sense they are not learning effectively but cannot name the problem. Activation messaging should arouse motivation by surfacing the pain ('you re-read but nothing sticks') rather than leading with features.
