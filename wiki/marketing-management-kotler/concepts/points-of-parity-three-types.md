# POPs: Category, Correlational, Competitive

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 7 — Crafting a Customer Value Proposition and Positioning (pp. 147–148)

## Summary

POPs = shared with others, 'good enough' to stay competitive. 3 types: category (table stakes — 'greens fees'), correlational (negative from positive — cheap=low quality, must neutralize), competitive (neutralize competitor POD). Visa/Amex copy each other's PODs for POPs.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Points of parity (POPs), on the other hand, are attribute or benefit associations that are not necessarily unique to the brand but may in fact be shared with other brands. These types of associations come in three basic forms: category, correlational, and competitive.
>
> Category points of parity are attributes or benefits that consumers view as essential to a legitimate and credible offering within a certain product or service category. They are the 'greens fees' necessary to play the marketing game.
>
> Often, the key to positioning is not so much achieving a point of difference as achieving points of parity!
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu category POPs: (1) accurate AI output — frequent wrong quizzes = legitimacy fail; (2) clean UX. Correlational POP threat: AI-native triggers 'unreliable/hallucination' concern — neutralize via accuracy messaging + transparency. Competitive POP vs ChatGPT: match breadth-of-topic POP while winning structured-learning-output POD.
