# Product Life Cycle: Four Stages and Marketing Strategy Imperatives

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 17 — Driving Growth in Competitive Markets (pp. 384–389)

## Summary

Products pass through introduction (high cost/low profit, build awareness), growth (rapid acceptance, maximize market share), maturity (peak sales, defend share), and decline (harvest/divest). Each stage demands different marketing objectives, pricing, distribution intensity, and communication emphasis.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The growth stage is marked by a rapid climb in sales. Early adopters like the product, and additional consumers start buying it. New competitors enter, attracted by the opportunities.
>
> At some point, the rate of sales growth will slow, and the product will enter a stage of relative maturity. Most products are in this stage of the life cycle, which normally lasts longer than the preceding ones.
>
> To sustain rapid growth in market share now, a firm can adopt a variety of strategies: It can improve product quality and add new features and improved styling; it can add new models and flanker products... it can shift from awareness and trial communication to preference and loyalty communication; and it can lower prices to attract the next layer of price-sensitive buyers.
>
