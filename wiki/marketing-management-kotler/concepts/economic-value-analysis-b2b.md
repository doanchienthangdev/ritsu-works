# Economic Value Analysis (EVA) — Monetizing Functional Benefits to Overcome Price Pressure

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 89–90)

## Summary

EVA is MM 16e's primary tool for B2B price-pressure situations. It monetizes all functional, service, people, and image benefits of an offering to compute a total customer benefit, then compares this to total customer cost (monetary plus time, energy, and psychological costs). The Caterpillar vs Komatsu example illustrates: the buyer computes perceived value across four benefit dimensions, then adjusts for nonmonetary acquisition costs. Marketers can improve perceived value three ways: increase total customer benefit, reduce nonmonetary costs, or reduce monetary price. This framework is more rigorous than the Principles treatment and is directly usable in B2B sales.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Economic value analysis (EVA) is a tool that helps monetize the functional benefits of a company's offering, such as performance, reliability, and warranty.
>
> He adds up all the economic, functional, and psychological benefits from these four sources - product, services, people, and image - and decides he believes that Caterpillar delivers greater customer benefits.
>
> Total customer cost also includes the buyer's time, energy, and psychological costs expended in product acquisition, usage, maintenance, ownership, and disposal.
>
> Caterpillar can improve its offer in three ways. First, it can increase total customer benefit... Second, it can reduce the buyer's nonmonetary costs... Third, it can reduce its product's monetary cost.
>

Ritsu's institutional pricing must clear EVA: total student learning hours saved + quiz-prep automation value must exceed subscription cost + adoption friction (onboarding time, IT approval). Mapping this explicitly strengthens the educator value proposition.
