# Strategic vs Tactical Targeting

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 6 — Identifying Market Segments and Target Customers (pp. 121–122)

## Summary

MM 16e's binary: strategic targeting = WHO (which segment creates mutual value); tactical = HOW (reach cost-efficiently). Strategic targets by deliberately excluding; tactical strives to reach every selected customer.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Strategic targeting focuses on customers whose needs the company can fulfill by ensuring that its offerings are customized to their needs. Tactical targeting identifies the ways in which the company can reach these strategically important customers.
>
> Rather than trying to reach the target audience with one offering that endeavors to lure a wide range of customers with diverse needs, strategic targeting is based on the deliberate choice to ignore some customers to better serve other customers with an offering that matches their specific needs.
>
> Together, strategic and tactical targeting seek to answer two questions: Who are the customers that the company can establish a mutually beneficial relationship with? and How can the company reach these customers most effectively and efficiently?
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu conflates strategic + tactical. Separating: strategic = 'which ICP segment creates mutual value?' (student with exam in 3 days vs professional learner); tactical = 'where do they live online?' (TikTok, Reddit). Currently answers neither rigorously.
