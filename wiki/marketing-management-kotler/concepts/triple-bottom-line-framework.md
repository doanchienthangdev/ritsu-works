# Triple Bottom Line: People, Planet, Profit as the Three Accountability Dimensions

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 21 — Socially Responsible Marketing (pp. 466–466)

## Summary

Socially responsible companies account for performance on three dimensions: people (social/community component), planet (sustainability/environmental component), and profit (monetary component). This reframes stakeholders — beyond shareholders — to include employees, customers, suppliers, communities, and the environment. Companies that balance all three build more durable competitive advantage and customer loyalty than profit-only firms.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Many companies focus on the so-called triple bottom line—people (social component), planet (sustainability component), and profit (monetary component).
>
> The concept of a triple bottom line implies that a company's responsibility lies with stakeholders, all entities directly or indirectly influenced by the actions of the company, rather than just with shareholders, who actually own the firm.
>
> Raising the level of socially responsible marketing calls for a three-pronged approach that focuses on the community, the environment, and the marketplace.
>
