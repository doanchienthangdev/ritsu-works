# Market Demand Hierarchy — Six Nested Concepts for Demand Measurement

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 110–111)

## Summary

MM 16e provides a rigorous definitional hierarchy: (1) Market Demand - total volume buyable by a defined group in a defined context under a defined program; (2) Company Demand - company's estimated share given its marketing effort; (3) Market Forecast - demand at actual industry marketing expenditure; (4) Company Sales Forecast - expected company sales under a chosen plan; (5) Market Potential - theoretical maximum as industry spend approaches infinity (for a given environment); (6) Company Sales Potential - company's limit as its spend rises. The phrase 'for a given marketing environment' is crucial - market potential is environment-contingent, not absolute. Sales quota is set above forecast; sales budget below forecast.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Market demand for an offering is the total volume that could be bought by a defined customer group in a defined geographic area in a defined time period in a defined marketing environment under a defined marketing program.
>
> The phrase 'for a given market environment' is crucial. Consider the market potential for automobiles. It's higher during prosperity than during a recession.
>
> A sales quota is the sales goal set for a product line, company division, or sales representative. It is primarily a managerial device for defining and stimulating sales effort, and it is often set slightly higher than estimated sales to stretch the sales force's effort.
>
