# Buying Center Dynamics — Dual Motivation: Organizational and Personal

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 83–84)

## Summary

MM 16e makes a distinctive analytical move: 'Purchasing decisions are ultimately made by individuals, not organizations.' Individuals are motivated by personal needs (pay, advancement, recognition, achievement) alongside organizational needs. Industrial buying is simultaneously 'rational' (solving the firm's economic problem) and 'emotional' (serving the individual's need for achievement). This dual-motivation thesis has direct selling implications: effective B2B value propositions must address the personal reward calculus of deciders, not just the organization's ROI. MM 16e illustrates with a case where a manufacturer retooled its pitch to address the subconscious insecurity of SME executives.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Purchasing decisions are ultimately made by individuals, not organizations. Individuals are motivated by their own needs and perceptions in attempting to maximize the organizational rewards they earn (pay, advancement, recognition, and feelings of achievement).
>
> Businesspeople are not buying just 'products.' They are buying solutions to two problems: the organization's economic and strategic problem and their own personal need for achievement and reward. In this sense, industrial buying decisions are both 'rational' and 'emotional.'
>
> The manufacturer retooled its selling approach to emphasize a more emotional appeal and the way its product line enabled the customer's employees to improve their performance, relieving management of the perceived complications and stress.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Educator personas buying Ritsu for their students have personal stakes: professional reputation for running an effective course, reduced prep burden, and student satisfaction scores. Ritsu's educator pitch must address these personal rewards, not just curriculum outcomes.
