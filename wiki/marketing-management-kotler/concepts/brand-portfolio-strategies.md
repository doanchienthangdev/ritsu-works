# Brand Portfolio Strategies: House-of-Brands, Branded-House, Sub-Brand

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 219–221)

## Summary

Companies managing multiple products choose among three brand portfolio strategies. House-of-brands (General Mills, P&G) uses individual brand names for each product — failures don't contaminate siblings, but each brand requires its own equity investment. Branded-house (GE, Heinz) uses one corporate umbrella brand — lower launch costs, instant recognition, but a scandal or failure affects all products. Sub-brand (Kellogg's Rice Krispies, Sony VAIO) combines corporate and individual names — the parent legitimizes the product, the sub-brand individualizes it. An optimal brand portfolio maximizes equity in combination — no two brands should compete for the same customer approval.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> A house-of-brands strategy involves individual or separate family brand names. A major advantage of separate family brand names is that if a product fails or appears to be of low quality, the company has not tied the reputation of its other offerings to that product.
>
> A branded-house strategy involves a corporate umbrella or company brand name. Development costs are lower with umbrella names, because there's no need to research a name or spend heavily on advertising to build recognition.
>
> A sub-brand strategy combines two or more corporate, family, or individual product brand names. The corporate or company name legitimizes the new product, and the sub-brand name individualizes it.
>
> The hallmark of an optimal brand portfolio is the ability of each brand it contains to maximize equity in combination with all the other brands.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu currently operates as a branded-house: everything is 'Ritsu.' As product lines expand (e.g., Ritsu for Students vs. Ritsu for Educators), a sub-brand strategy may emerge. The educator product may need its own name to signal different value proposition without diluting the core student brand.
