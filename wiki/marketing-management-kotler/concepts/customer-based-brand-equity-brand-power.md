# Customer-Based Brand Equity (Brand Power)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 213–214)

## Summary

Brand power (also called customer-based brand equity, CBBE) is the ancillary value contributed by the brand beyond the product itself. It reflects the differential effect that brand knowledge has on consumer response to marketing. A brand has positive power when consumers react more favorably to a product when the brand is identified versus when it is not. Brand power arises from consumer brand knowledge — all thoughts, feelings, images, experiences, and beliefs associated with the brand. Stronger brands earn greater revenue, command price premiums of 20-25%, are more resilient to competitive attacks, and enjoy greater trade support.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Brand power, also referred to as customer-based brand equity, is the ancillary value contributed by the brand to a product or a service. Brand power reflects the degree to which the brand influences the way consumers think, feel, and act with respect to the brand.
>
> Brand power arises from differences in consumer response that a brand evokes. If no differences occur, the brand-name product is essentially a commodity, and competition will probably be based on price.
>
> The key benefits of brand power include improved perception of product performance, greater loyalty, less vulnerability to competitive marketing actions and marketing crises, larger margins, more inelastic consumer response to price increases and more elastic consumer response to price decreases.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's brand power is embryonic. At freemium stage, the product IS the brand — users have no pre-existing Ritsu associations. Every interaction builds or erodes CBBE. The goal for the '100 paying who love' milestone is to accumulate enough positive experiences that users react more favorably to Ritsu's features than to an unbranded equivalent — that gap is brand power.
