# Native Advertising: Three Formats for Non-Disruptive Paid Promotion

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 13 — Designing an Integrated Marketing Campaign in the Digital Age (pp. 376–390)

## Summary

Native advertising consists of paid commercial messages that match the look and feel of the publication in which they appear. Three formats: (1) Content recommendations — suggested articles appearing below editorial content; (2) In-feed advertisements — appearing in social network news feeds; (3) Search/promoted listings — appearing above organic search results. Unlike traditional advertising, native is designed to be non-disruptive and integrated into editorial flow. Growing worldwide, applicable across digital and traditional media formats.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Native advertising involves materials resembling the publication's editorial content but is intended to promote the advertiser's product. Native ads are paid commercial messages that match the look and feel of the publication in which they appear. Unlike traditional advertising, native advertising is designed to be non-disruptive and to look like part of the editorial flow of the publication.
>
> Native ads come in three common formats: (1) content recommendations, such as suggested articles that appear below an article featuring editorial content, (2) in-feed advertisements that appear in the news feed on social networks, and (3) search listings and promoted listings that appear above the results of an organic Google search.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Native content is Ritsu's best-fit paid format for early-stage awareness: a 'how I aced my exam' article co-created with a student blogger, or a TikTok in-feed ad that looks like organic study advice, is consistent with how Ritsu's value is discovered organically (via recommendation, not interruption).
