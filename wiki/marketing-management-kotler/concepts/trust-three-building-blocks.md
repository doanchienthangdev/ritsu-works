# Trust Architecture: Competence, Honesty, Benevolence as Independent Building Blocks

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 19 — Building Customer Loyalty (pp. 435–436)

## Summary

Customer trust rests on three independently variable building blocks: competence (skills meet expectations), honesty (statements match actions), and benevolence (genuine concern for customer interests/goals). Competence violations are more forgivable than honesty or benevolence violations — the negative asymmetry is stronger for honesty and benevolence. Trust is most salient early in a relationship; established trust can tempt companies to cut corners.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Trust is established and maintained via three important building blocks. One building block is competence. Companies and brands build competence trust when its managers have the skills required to do the job effectively, and when they meet or exceed expectations for those skills. Another building block is honesty. Firms build honesty trust by consistently telling the truth and keeping promises. A third building block is benevolence. Companies and brands build benevolence trust when they demonstrate genuine concern about the interests and goals of customers and employees.
>
> Competence is more strongly affected by positive information than by negative information. If a brand or company fails to deliver, and if the reason for the failure is related to competence, customers are often willing to forgive the behavior... In contrast, both honesty and benevolence are more strongly affected by negative information than by positive information.
>
