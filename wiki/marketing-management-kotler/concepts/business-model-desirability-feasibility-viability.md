# DFV Triad: Desirability, Feasibility, Viability as New-Offering Validation Criteria

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 18 — Developing New Market Offerings (pp. 354–357)

## Summary

Every new offering must pass three gates simultaneously: desirability (customers find it attractive and it delivers sought benefits at acceptable cost), feasibility (the company can technologically build it), and viability (it creates value for the company — sufficient revenue over cost structure). All three must hold; failure on any one predicts market failure.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The stage-gate approach encompasses three aims. Its goal is to develop: (1) a desirable offering that target customers will find attractive, (2) a technologically feasible offering that the company will find doable, and (3) a viable offering that will create value for the company and its collaborators.
>
> An offering that customers do not find desirable would probably not be viable because it would not create sufficient customer demand to create value for the company.
>
> Viability indicates the extent to which an offering can create value for the company. For most companies, a viable offering is one that is able to generate profits.
>
