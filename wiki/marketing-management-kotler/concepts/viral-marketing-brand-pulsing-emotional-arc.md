# Viral Marketing Design: Brand Pulsing + Emotional Roller Coaster

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 13 — Designing an Integrated Marketing Campaign in the Digital Age (pp. 631–644)

## Summary

Viral ads require specific design principles beyond entertainment value. Harvard's Thales Teixeira framework: (1) Brand pulsing — display brand briefly and non-intrusively so it doesn't overshadow the story; (2) Open with joy or surprise to hook fickle viewers; (3) Build an emotional roller coaster through the ad to sustain engagement; (4) Surprise but do not shock — discomfort prevents sharing. The Blendtec 'Will It Blend?' and Dollar Shave Club cases show that viral success comes from tying creative to current events and a clear product truth, not from production budget.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Harvard Business School viral video expert Thales Teixeira offers the following advice for getting a viral ad shared. Utilize brand pulsing: Display the brand for short periods of time in a way that doesn't make the brand too intrusive within the story, open with joy or surprise to hook those fickle viewers who are easily bored, build an emotional roller coaster within the ad to keep viewers engaged throughout, and surprise but don't shock; if an ad makes viewers too uncomfortable, they are unlikely to share it.
>
> Viral marketing tries to create a splash in the marketplace to showcase a brand and its noteworthy features. Some believe viral marketing efforts are driven more by the rules of entertainment than by the rules of selling.
>
> Ultimately, the success of any viral or word-of-mouth buzz campaign depends on the willingness of consumers to talk to other consumers.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's first viral content should apply brand pulsing: open with a student's pain point ('I read the chapter 3 times and still failed'), introduce Ritsu briefly, then show the emotional payoff of mastering it. The quiz score reveal is inherently shareable — that IS the roller coaster.
