# Brand Personality and Self-Concept Congruence (Actual, Ideal, Others)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 3 — Analyzing Consumer Markets (pp. 57–58)

## Summary

MM 16e distinguishes three self-concept reference points for brand choice: actual self-concept (how we view ourselves), ideal self-concept (how we want to view ourselves), and others' self-concept (how we think others see us). High 'self-monitors' - those sensitive to how others perceive them - are especially likely to choose brands whose personality fits the social consumption context. Publicly consumed products show stronger self-concept matching than private goods. The three-way distinction is more analytically precise than the typical Principles treatment.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Consumers typically choose and use brands with a brand personality consistent with their actual self-concept (how we view ourselves), although the match may instead be based on the consumer's ideal self-concept (how we would like to view ourselves) or even on others' self-concept of us (how we think others see us).
>
> Consumers who are high 'self-monitors' - that is, are sensitive to the way others see them - are more likely to choose brands whose personalities fit the consumption situation.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's 6 ICP personas differ in which self-concept drives adoption. Students may align with ideal self-concept ('become the student who aces it'). Engineers align with actual self-concept ('I learn frameworks efficiently'). Persona-matched messaging should target the operative self-concept.
