# Qualitative Measures and Neuromarketing — Beyond Survey Self-Report

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 105–107)

## Summary

MM 16e presents the laddering technique as a rigorous qualitative instrument for uncovering consumer motivation hierarchies: sequence 'why' questions from attribute (well built) to functional benefit (reliable) to emotional benefit (reachable by family) to core value (being a good neighbor). Separately, neuromarketing (EEG, fMRI, skin sensors) reveals responses that contradict stated preferences: brain wave activity predicted Super Bowl ad effectiveness differently than stated preference scores. Brain waves predicted music purchases better than stated preferences. However, 'given the complexity of the human brain, many researchers caution that it should not form the sole basis for marketing decisions.'

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> A series of increasingly specific 'why' questions can reveal consumer motivation and deeper goals. Ask why someone wants to buy a Nokia cell phone. 'They look well built' (attribute). 'Why is it important that the phone be well built?' 'It suggests Nokia is reliable' (a functional benefit). 'Why is reliability important?' 'Because my colleagues or family can be sure to reach me' (an emotional benefit).
>
> The term neuromarketing describes brain research on the effects of marketing stimuli.
>
> Brain waves predicted music purchases better than stated music preferences did.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Laddering interviews with Ritsu users (why do you care about active learning? → why does mastery matter? → what does passing this exam enable for you?) would surface the core values driving adoption and guide messaging hierarchy: does Ritsu sell confidence, career advancement, or identity as a competent learner?
