# Reverse Innovation: Developing for Emerging Markets, Importing to Developed Markets

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 21 — Socially Responsible Marketing (pp. 366–367)

## Summary

Innovations developed to meet the cost constraints and needs of developing markets (ultra-low-cost, high-functionality) can be successfully re-introduced as affordable alternatives in developed markets. Reverse innovation inverts the traditional flow of product development. Companies like Haier, Nestlé (Maggi), and Harman International have leveraged it to expand product portfolios across income segments simultaneously.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The transfer of innovations from developing to developed markets is what is often referred to as reverse innovation. Reverse innovation adopts successful solutions developed to meet the needs and constraints of a developing market to create an inexpensive product that can be introduced as a cheaper alternative in developed markets.
>
> Among successful reverse innovators, Nestlé repositioned its low-fat Maggi brand dried noodles—a popular, low-priced meal in rural Pakistan and India—as a budget-friendly health food in Australia and New Zealand.
>
