# Country-of-Origin Effects: Mental Associations That Shape Brand Perception Globally

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 20 — Tapping into Global Markets (pp. 455–457)

## Summary

Country of origin triggers mental associations that systematically affect purchase intent — consumers hold distinct attitudes toward brands from different countries (Japan = electronics, France = luxury/wine, USA = high-tech and soft drinks). These perceptions operate in both directions: domestic consumers may favor local brands for patriotic reasons; foreign consumers may use origin as quality heuristic. Marketers can leverage, neutralize, or obscure origin effects depending on the country-category fit.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Country-of-origin perceptions are the mental associations and beliefs triggered by a country.
>
> Research studies have found that certain countries enjoy a reputation for certain goods: Japan for automobiles and consumer electronics; the United States for high-tech innovations, soft drinks, toys, cigarettes, and jeans; France for wine, perfume, and luxury goods.
>
> The mere fact that a brand is perceived as successful on a global stage—whether it sends a quality signal, taps into cultural myths, or reinforces a sense of social responsibility—may lend credibility and respect.
>
