# Brand Repositioning and Revitalization Strategies

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 10 — Building Strong Brands (pp. 224–229)

## Summary

Brands can be repositioned on a continuum from 'back to basics' (return to original sources of equity) to 'reinvention' (entirely new positioning). Back-to-basics applies when the marketing program fails to live up to brand promise but the original positioning remains viable. Reinvention applies when the old positioning is no longer viable. Key step: understand which brand associations were the original sources of equity, which positive associations are losing strength or uniqueness, and which negative associations have formed. Brand crises require swift, sincere response — delay allows negative impressions to solidify and causes permanent switching.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Any new development in the marketing environment can affect a brand's fortunes.
>
> Often, the first thing to do in repositioning a brand is to understand what the sources of brand equity were to begin with. Are positive associations losing their strength or uniqueness? Have negative associations become linked to the brand?
>
> There is obviously a continuum of repositioning strategies, with pure 'back to basics' at one end, pure 'reinvention' at the other, and many combinations in between. The challenge is often to change enough to attract some new customers, but not so much as to alienate old ones.
>
> In general, the stronger the brand and corporate image — especially for credibility and trustworthiness — the more likely the firm can weather the storm.
>
