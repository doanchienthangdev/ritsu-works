# Price Elasticity of Demand: Analytics and Strategic Implications

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 11 — Managing Pricing and Sales Promotions (pp. 244–245)

## Summary

Price elasticity measures how responsive quantity sold is to price changes. Inelastic demand: price increase causes small volume decline; total revenue rises with price increases. Elastic demand: price increase causes large volume decline; lowering price increases total revenue. Average price elasticity across 40 years of research: -2.62 (a 1% price decrease leads to 2.62% increase in sales). Elasticity is low when: product is distinctive with few substitutes, price changes are not readily noticed, buyers change habits slowly, price is a small fraction of total income. Price elasticity is higher for durable goods and products in introduction/growth stages versus mature/decline stages. Promotional price elasticities are higher than actual price elasticities in the short run.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> Price elasticity of demand reflects the degree to which a change in price leads to a change in quantity sold. The lower the price elasticity, the less sensitive consumers are to price increases.
>
> One comprehensive review of a 40-year period of academic research on price elasticity found that the average price elasticity across all products, markets, and time periods studied was -2.62. In other words, a 1 percent decrease in prices led to a 2.62 percent increase in sales.
>
> Price elasticity was higher for durable goods than for other goods and was higher for products in the introduction/growth stages of the product life cycle than for products in the mature/decline stages.
>
> Promotional price elasticities were higher than actual price elasticities in the short run (although the reverse was true in the long run).
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu is in the growth stage of its product life cycle — expect high price elasticity. This means small price increases on paid plans could significantly reduce conversion from free-to-paid. Current priority: build enough CBBE (brand power) and perceived value to reduce elasticity before raising prices. Test promotional pricing (limited-time upgrade discounts) which research shows has higher short-run elasticity — useful for conversion campaigns.
