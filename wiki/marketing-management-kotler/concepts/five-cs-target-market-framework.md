# 5-C Target Market Framework (Chernev's Customer-Centered)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 2 — Marketing Planning and Management (pp. 33–35)

## Summary

MM 16e's alternative to Porter's 5 Forces: Customers (center), Collaborators, Company, Competitors, Context (outer ring). Defines market by customer NEEDS, not industry — substitutes become subset of cross-category competitors.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> These five market factors — the Five Cs — are visually represented in the 5-C framework as a set of concentric ellipses: Target customers are in the center, with collaborators, competitors, and the company in the middle and the context on the outside.
>
> The 5-C framework, on the other hand, defines the market based on customer needs rather than on the industry in which the company competes. Accordingly, it defines competitors in terms of their ability to fulfill customer needs and create market value.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's real competitors are not 'AI quiz apps' (industry view) but 'anything a student uses to understand content before exam' — ChatGPT, YouTube, tutors, rereading. 5-C lens exposes the broader threat.
