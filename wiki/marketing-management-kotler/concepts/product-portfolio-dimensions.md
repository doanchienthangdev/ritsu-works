# Product Portfolio Dimensions: Width, Length, Depth, Consistency

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 8 — Designing and Managing Products (pp. 166–167)

## Summary

A product portfolio is characterized by four dimensions. Width is the number of distinct product lines. Length is the total number of items across all lines. Depth is the number of variants within each product item. Consistency is how closely related the lines are across end use, production, or distribution. These four dimensions provide tools for expanding or rationalizing the business.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> A company's product portfolio has a certain width, length, depth, and consistency.
>
> These four product mix dimensions permit a company to expand its business in four ways. It can add new product lines, thus widening its product mix. It can lengthen each product line. It can add more product variants to each product and thereby deepen its product mix. Finally, a company can pursue more product line consistency.
>

Ritsu's product portfolio: width = learning plans + individual activities. Depth = variants of quiz (adaptive, timed, Feynman-style). Depth decisions directly affect development prioritization — should Ritsu deepen quiz variants or widen to add a new line like group study mode?
