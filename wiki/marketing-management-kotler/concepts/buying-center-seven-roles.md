# The Buying Center — Seven-Role Decision Unit

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 4 — Analyzing Business Markets (pp. 82–83)

## Summary

The buying center is 'all those individuals and groups who participate in the purchasing decision-making process, who share some common goals and the risks arising from the decisions.' It comprises seven distinct roles: Initiators, Users, Influencers, Deciders, Approvers, Buyers, and Gatekeepers. A single person can occupy multiple roles simultaneously; a purchasing manager often serves as buyer, influencer, and gatekeeper at once. Buying centers typically have 5-6 members but can number in the dozens, including external parties (consultants, government officials). This is MM 16e's most analytically precise B2B structure — markedly more granular than Principles.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The buying center includes all members of the organization who play one or more of seven roles in the purchase decision process.
>
> A purchasing manager, for example, is often buyer, influencer, and gatekeeper simultaneously. She can decide which sales reps can call on other people in the organization, what budget and other constraints to place on the purchase, and which firm will actually get the business.
>
> Gatekeepers - People who have the power to prevent sellers or information from reaching members of the buying center.
>

## Ritsu relevance

When Ritsu sells to Educators or enterprises (B2B channel), a buying center exists: the teacher is Initiator and User, department head is Approver, IT is Gatekeeper. Growth must map this center before pricing or access-model decisions.
