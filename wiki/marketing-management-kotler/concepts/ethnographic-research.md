# Ethnographic Research — Immersive Method for Unarticulated Desires

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Marketing Management* (Kotler/Keller/Chernev 16e SE), Ch 5 — Conducting Marketing Research (pp. 103–104)

## Summary

Ethnographic research 'uses concepts and tools from anthropology and other social sciences to provide deep cultural understanding of how people live and work.' Its goal is to 'immerse the researcher into consumers' lives to uncover unarticulated desires that might not surface in any other form of research.' MM 16e provides multiple cases: Orville Redenbacher (popcorn as interaction facilitator, discovered via 9-month home observation), Thomson Reuters (three-minutes-before-and-after end-user research that revealed growth opportunities), IDEO (children grip toothbrushes differently than adults). This method is superior to surveys when the behavior of interest is habitual or unconsciously motivated.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

> The goal is to immerse the researcher into consumers' lives to uncover unarticulated desires that might not surface in any other form of research.
>
> Researchers found a key insight: The essence of popcorn is that it is a 'facilitator of interaction.'
>
> Thomson then conducted surveys and 'day in the life' ethnographic research on how end users did their jobs. Using an approach called 'three minutes,' researchers combined observation with detailed interviews to understand what end users were doing three minutes before and after they used one of Thomson's products.
>

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's most valuable unresearched question is 'what does a student actually do in the 30 minutes before and after a Ritsu session?' A three-minute ethnographic protocol (screen recording + think-aloud) would surface the study context that shapes feature design.
