# CX Maturity Model

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A 5-level diagnostic model that scores an organization's customer experience capability across six dimensions, establishing the current maturity baseline and the target state for the CX strategy.

**Origin:** Synthesized from Forrester Research's Customer Experience Index and Temkin Group's CX Maturity Model (2010s); widely adopted by McKinsey, Bain, and Deloitte CX practices.

## Why it works
Organizations progress through predictable CX maturity stages — from reactive firefighting (Level 1) to proactively designing differentiated experiences (Level 5). Diagnosing current maturity prevents organizations from investing in sophisticated CX programs before foundational capabilities (measurement, governance, data) exist. The six-dimension structure ensures balanced assessment across people, process, and technology.

## When to use
Use at the start of any CX strategy engagement to baseline current capability, set a realistic target, and direct investment to the highest-leverage gaps. Also use annually to track progress.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Define the six assessment dimensions: customer understanding, strategy & governance, design & delivery, culture & talent, measurement, and technology.
2. 2. Design a 40-question scorecard (6–7 questions per dimension), each rated 1–5 by respondents.
3. 3. Recruit 10–15 cross-functional leaders spanning CX, operations, technology, HR, and finance.
4. 4. Administer structured interviews (45–60 minutes each), probing for evidence of each practice, not just awareness.
5. 5. Average scores per dimension; identify the two dimensions with the largest gap between current score and the Level 4 target.
6. 6. Map results onto a radar chart; overlay the target state (typically Level 4 in 3 years).
7. 7. In a leadership workshop, agree on the maturity level that is 'good enough' for the business model — not every organization needs Level 5.

## Real-life example — British Airways
Following a series of service failures in 2017, British Airways commissioned a CX Maturity Assessment. Results showed Level 3 on customer understanding (they had data but not empathy-based insights), Level 2 on culture & talent (frontline training was compliance-based, not customer-values-based). The assessment directed investment toward culture and talent programs rather than technology — a finding that contradicted the initial technology-heavy plan.

**So what:** The maturity model prevents organizations from investing in the wrong layer — technology won't fix a culture problem, and culture programs won't fix a measurement gap.

## Pitfalls
- Self-scoring bias: leaders consistently rate their own area 0.5–1 point higher than external assessors. Counter: use a facilitator, require evidence for each score, and triangulate with customer data.
- Treating all dimensions equally: in most organizations, measurement and customer understanding are the highest-leverage gaps. Prioritize those first.
- Setting Level 5 as the universal target: Level 5 requires significant cultural transformation and is not the right target for every business model (e.g., low-cost airlines may rationally target Level 3).
