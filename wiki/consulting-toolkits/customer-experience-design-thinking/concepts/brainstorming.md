# Structured Brainstorming

*Category: ideation · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A facilitated group ideation technique that generates a high volume of diverse solution ideas in a short time through rapid, non-evaluative idea sharing — using the rule 'quantity before quality' to move the team past obvious ideas to more creative territory.

**Origin:** Developed by advertising executive Alex Osborn in 'Applied Imagination' (1953); subsequently refined by IDEO, the Stanford d.school, and design thinking practitioners to include more structured variations (Brainwriting, Round Robin, 635 Method).

## Why it works
Premature evaluation kills creativity. When team members self-censor ideas for fear of judgment, the creative ceiling is set by the most conservative person in the room. By deferring all evaluation until after idea generation is complete, structured brainstorming enables the 'wild idea' that, once transformed in evaluation, becomes the breakthrough solution.

## When to use
Use as the core divergent ideation method in Phase 7 (Ideate), after HMW questions are defined and the Crazy 8s visual warm-up is complete.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Assign a facilitator whose role is to enforce the four rules: (1) Defer judgment — no evaluation during generation; (2) Aim for quantity — target 50+ ideas from a group of 8; (3) Encourage wild ideas — the more extreme, the more likely it seeds a useful adaptation; (4) Build on others' ideas — say 'yes, and' to extend ideas you hear.
2. 2. Anchor the session to the top-voted HMW question. Write it large on the wall.
3. 3. Start with a 5-minute silent individual generation (each person writes ideas on sticky notes independently). This prevents anchoring on the first person to speak.
4. 4. Transition to group sharing: each person reads one idea per turn, Round Robin. As ideas are shared, other participants can add related ideas to new sticky notes.
5. 5. Continue for 20 minutes or until the flow of new ideas slows significantly.
6. 6. Count the ideas: if fewer than 50, run a 5-minute 'what would be crazy?' stimulus to push past the obvious.
7. 7. After generation is complete, begin evaluation (dot voting → NABC).

## Real-life example — IDEO / Oral-B
When IDEO helped Oral-B brainstorm ideas for a better children's toothbrush, the team applied structured brainstorming rules: defer judgment, go for volume. The wild idea that emerged: 'What if the toothbrush were so fat that a child could hold it with their whole fist instead of pinching it?' This idea would have been self-censored without the 'wild ideas encouraged' rule — it seemed absurd. Instead, it became the basis for the Fat Handle Toothbrush, one of the best-selling products in Oral-B's history and an industry standard.

**So what:** The idea that breaks the rules often breaks the market.

## Pitfalls
- Group think: teams without the silent generation phase anchor on the first idea shared. Counter: always begin with 5 minutes of individual silent generation before group sharing.
- Facilitator evaluating ideas: the facilitator says 'great idea!' or 'we've tried that' during generation. Counter: the facilitator's only words during generation are 'write it down' and 'who else has an idea?'
