# Design Sprint

*Category: ideation · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A 5-day structured process (Monday: Map, Tuesday: Sketch, Wednesday: Decide, Thursday: Prototype, Friday: Test) developed by Google Ventures that compresses the full Design Thinking cycle into one intensive week — producing a validated, user-tested prototype from a single design challenge.

**Origin:** Developed by Jake Knapp at Google (2010); refined with Google Ventures portfolio companies and documented in 'Sprint' (Knapp, Zeratsky & Kowitz, 2016). Adopted by thousands of companies as a standard innovation methodology.

## Why it works
The Design Sprint solves the most common innovation failure mode: spending months building products that users don't want. By compressing the cycle into 5 days and validating with users on Day 5 (before any real development investment), the Sprint generates learning at 100x the speed of a traditional project.

## When to use
Use when a team needs to validate a high-stakes design decision in 5 days rather than 5 months. Ideal for: new product launches, major feature redesigns, and innovation challenges with a clear user population.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Assemble a 5–7 person cross-functional sprint team plus a 'Decider' (executive with authority to make decisions).
2. 2. Monday: Define the long-term goal, the sprint question, and the target moment on the customer journey. Draw an experience map and vote on where to focus.
3. 3. Tuesday: Run Lightning Demos (each person presents 3 inspiring examples from different industries). Then each person sketches 3 solution concepts individually.
4. 4. Wednesday: Each person presents their sketches (silently — via 'art museum' walk). Vote on the best concepts. The Decider makes the final selection. Build a storyboard (8-panel) from the selected concept.
5. 5. Thursday: Build a 'Goldilocks' prototype — realistic enough to generate authentic user reactions, no more. Use Keynote, Figma, or even printed paper prototypes.
6. 6. Friday: Run 5 user sessions (60 min each). Observe as a team, taking notes on a shared grid. After all sessions, synthesize findings into a list of patterns and a Go/No-Go decision.

## Real-life example — Slack
Before Slack's major notification system redesign, the team ran a 5-day Design Sprint. Tuesday's Lightning Demo phase introduced the team to how Basecamp, Asana, and a hospital paging system handled notification priority. The hospital paging analogy — urgent pages interrupt; non-urgent messages queue — directly inspired Slack's notification hierarchy (critical DMs interrupt; channel messages batch). The prototype was built in one day and tested Friday. User testing showed 4/5 users immediately understood the hierarchy without explanation. Go decision: develop the full notification system.

**So what:** The Design Sprint's value is not the 5-day structure — it is the discipline of validating before building.

## Pitfalls
- No Decider in the room: sprints without a decision-maker stall at Wednesday's selection. Counter: the Decider must be present for at least Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.
- Prototype over-engineering: Thursday teams spend 8 hours building a fully coded prototype. Counter: a Keynote or Figma prototype with realistic visuals but no real functionality is sufficient for Friday testing.
