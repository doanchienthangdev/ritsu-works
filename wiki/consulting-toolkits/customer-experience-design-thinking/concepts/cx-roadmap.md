# CX Implementation Roadmap

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A sequenced, time-phased plan that organizes approved CX initiatives into implementation waves (typically 3 waves over 24 months), each with assigned owners, budget allocations, KPI targets, and dependencies — translating the initiative portfolio into an executable program of work.

**Origin:** Standard management consulting deliverable; the CX roadmap follows the same structure as technology transformation roadmaps (Gartner, Forrester) adapted for customer experience transformation programs.

## Why it works
A list of initiatives is not a plan. The roadmap converts initiative selection into delivery commitment by assigning ownership, sequencing dependencies, and setting milestone dates. It also communicates the CX strategy to the broader organization in an accessible format.

## When to use
Use in Phase 4 (Initiatives & Roadmap) as the culminating output of the CX Strategy track. Present to the Steering Committee for budget approval. Refresh quarterly.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Start from the prioritized initiative list (Impact vs. Effort matrix). Quick Wins go in Wave 1; Major Projects in Waves 2–3.
2. 2. For each initiative, assign: Initiative Owner (accountable leader), Core Team (3–5 FTEs), Budget (OpEx + CapEx), and KPI Target (the specific KPI that this initiative moves and by how much).
3. 3. Identify dependencies: which Wave 2 initiatives require Wave 1 outputs? Which initiatives require a technology platform that must be in place first?
4. 4. Build the Gantt: plot each initiative as a bar on a monthly timeline, respecting dependencies. Mark key milestones (prototype completion, launch, evaluation).
5. 5. Calculate the total resource requirement per quarter: ensure no quarter is over-resourced relative to organizational capacity.
6. 6. Conduct a 'risk review' of the roadmap: which initiatives have the highest execution risk? Add buffer time and mitigation actions.
7. 7. Present the roadmap to the Executive Steering Committee for approval and budget allocation. Establish a quarterly governance cadence to track progress.

## Real-life example — T-Mobile US
T-Mobile's 'Un-carrier' CX transformation was executed in 13 publicly announced waves over 5 years (2013–2018). Wave 1 (Quick Wins): eliminate annual contracts and overage fees. Wave 2: introduce Music Freedom (unlimited music streaming). Wave 3: introduce Binge On (video streaming). Each wave built brand momentum from the prior wave's customer response data before committing the next investment. This wave-based roadmap model prevented T-Mobile from overcommitting before validating customer response.

**So what:** The wave structure creates learning loops — each wave's results inform the scope and design of the next.

## Pitfalls
- Over-loading Wave 1: teams try to start everything simultaneously. Counter: limit Wave 1 to a maximum of 5 initiatives — the organization cannot absorb more without quality suffering.
- Roadmap without owners: initiatives without a named, accountable owner are intentions, not commitments. Counter: do not add an initiative to the roadmap unless a specific person has accepted ownership.
