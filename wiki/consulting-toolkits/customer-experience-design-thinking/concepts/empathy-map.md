# Empathy Map

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A structured visual tool that organizes what a team knows about a specific user into four quadrants — what they Say, Think, Do, and Feel — to build a shared, nuanced understanding of the user's inner world that goes beyond surface-level behavior.

**Origin:** Developed by Scott Matthews at XPLANE (now Dachis Group); popularized by Dave Gray and widely adopted by the Stanford d.school and design thinking practitioners worldwide.

## Why it works
Most teams know what users do (observable behavior) but have shallow understanding of what they think and feel (the mental and emotional layer). The Empathy Map forces teams to distinguish these layers explicitly and to surface contradictions — the gap between what users say and what they actually do is often where the design opportunity lies.

## When to use
Use after completing user research and Affinity Mapping, as the final synthesis step before building User Personas or crafting the Problem Statement. Use whenever the team has lost sight of the user's perspective during design debates.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Place the user persona at the center of a large whiteboard or Miro canvas. Write the persona's name and their 'job to be done' in the center.
2. 2. Working as a team, populate the SAY quadrant with direct quotes from user research — only use verbatim or lightly edited quotes, never paraphrases.
3. 3. Populate the DO quadrant with observed behaviors from DILO, shadowing, or contextual inquiry sessions — what did users actually do, not what they said they do.
4. 4. Populate the THINK quadrant with inferred beliefs and concerns — what is this user worrying about that they might not say out loud? Use post-its labeled 'I believe...' or 'I wonder if...'
5. 5. Populate the FEEL quadrant using Emotional Response Cards: lay out the deck, have each team member select 5 cards that best describe the user's emotional state during the journey, then converge on the top 8–10 emotions.
6. 6. Below the map, document PAINS (what frustrates them, risks they face, barriers they encounter) and GAINS (outcomes they want, metrics they use to measure success).
7. 7. Identify the most significant contradictions: where does what users SAY differ from what they DO? These contradictions are often the most valuable design insights.
8. 8. Use the completed Empathy Map to validate your Problem Statement: does the problem statement address what the user genuinely THINKs and FEELs, not just what they SAY?

## Real-life example — IDEO / Kaiser Permanente
When IDEO helped Kaiser Permanente redesign its hospital nursing handoff process, the Empathy Map for nurses revealed a critical contradiction: nurses SAY 'we always have the information we need for handoffs' but DO the opposite — observed nurses were frequently asking the same questions twice because the written handoff was incomplete. The THINK quadrant revealed 'I don't want to look incompetent by admitting I didn't read the previous nurse's notes.' The FEEL quadrant: anxious, rushed, responsible. This insight led to the design of a structured handoff script that normalized the act of asking questions, reducing handoff errors by 80%.

**So what:** The SAY-DO contradiction is the design opportunity. Empathy maps make it visible.

## Template
Complete each quadrant based only on research data (quotes, observations, inferences). Do not use assumptions. Write each item as a sticky note — one observation per note.

- [ ] User persona: ___ | Context being mapped: ___
- [ ] SAY quadrant (5–10 direct verbatim quotes): ___
- [ ] THINK quadrant (5–10 inferred beliefs/concerns labeled 'I believe... / I wonder if...'): ___
- [ ] DO quadrant (5–10 observed behaviors from research): ___
- [ ] FEEL quadrant (8–10 emotions from Emotional Response Cards): ___
- [ ] PAINS (top 5 frustrations, barriers, risks): ___
- [ ] GAINS (top 5 desired outcomes, success metrics): ___
- [ ] Key SAY-DO contradiction identified: ___
- [ ] Design implication of contradiction: ___

## Pitfalls
- Confusing SAY and THINK: teams often populate THINK with what users say rather than what they believe internally. Counter: require that everything in THINK is prefaced with 'I believe...' or 'I worry that...' — if you can quote it directly, it belongs in SAY.
- Skipping FEEL: teams uncomfortable with emotional language skip the FEEL quadrant. Counter: use Emotional Response Cards (a deck of 50 emotion descriptors) to make emotional labeling feel structured and safe.
- Building the map without research: teams build Empathy Maps from internal assumptions rather than user data. Counter: require each item in each quadrant to be traceable to at least one research source (interview, observation, or diary study).
