# Landscape Review

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A structured competitive and analogous benchmarking exercise that surveys how competitors and companies in adjacent industries have addressed a similar problem — generating 'inspiration from unexpected places' for the Ideate phase and preventing the team from reinventing solutions that already exist.

**Origin:** Adapted from McKinsey's 'best practice benchmarking' methodology and IDEO's 'analogous inspiration' technique for design thinking.

## Why it works
Design teams without external reference points default to incremental improvements of the current experience. The Landscape Review deliberately imports solutions from different industries and geographies that have solved analogous problems — creating the 'cross-pollination of ideas' that drives breakthrough innovation.

## When to use
Use at the end of the Define phase / start of the Ideate phase, as the final input before the Ideation workshop. Also use when an ideation session is producing only incremental ideas — a mid-session Landscape Review resets creative ambition.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Define the 'landscape' scope: 2–3 direct competitors, 2–3 analogous companies (different industry, similar user problem), and 1–2 'best in class' benchmarks (companies known for CX excellence in any industry).
2. 2. For each company, research how they address the user problem identified in the Problem Statement. Use publicly available information: their website, app store reviews, case studies, and customer testimonials.
3. 3. Document for each company: their approach, its strengths, its weaknesses, and the inspiration it could provide for your design.
4. 4. Present the Landscape Review at the start of the Ideation workshop as a 10-minute 'inspiration tour.' Use screenshots, videos, and direct user quotes about each company's approach.
5. 5. After the presentation, ask team members: 'What element of any of these approaches could we adapt or improve for our users?' These adapted elements seed the ideation.

## Real-life example — ING Bank
When ING Bank redesigned its mortgage application experience, the Landscape Review included not just competitor banks, but Airbnb's onboarding flow (analogous: a complex, high-trust, multi-step registration process) and Tesla's car configurator (analogous: a major purchase decision guided step-by-step online). The Airbnb analogy inspired a 'save and continue later' feature for the mortgage application; the Tesla analogy inspired a real-time quote simulator that showed monthly payments as users changed their inputs. Neither feature would have emerged from benchmarking banks alone.

**So what:** The most valuable inspiration for CX innovation comes from companies solving similar user problems in completely different industries.

## Pitfalls
- Only reviewing direct competitors: benchmarking only competitors produces incremental improvements within the existing paradigm. Counter: mandate at least 2 analogous companies from completely different industries.
- Review without presentation: teams complete the Landscape Review but never share it with the ideation group. Counter: the Landscape Review must be presented at the start of the Ideation workshop — it is an input to ideation, not a standalone deliverable.
