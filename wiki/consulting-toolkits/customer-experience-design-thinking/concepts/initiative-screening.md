# Initiative Screening (3-Criteria Quick Screen)

*Category: portfolio · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A rapid three-criteria filter applied to a long-list of CX initiative ideas to eliminate non-viable options quickly — evaluating each idea against Moment of Truth relevance, technical feasibility, and strategic alignment — reducing a brainstormed list of 30–60 ideas to a workable portfolio of 15–25 candidates.

**Origin:** Adapted from the McKinsey and BCG 'hypothesis screening' methodology used in strategy engagements to prioritize analytical focus; applied to CX initiative evaluation in CX transformation programs.

## Why it works
A complete initiative list from a CX ideation workshop typically contains 30–60 ideas. Without a fast screening mechanism, teams either build business cases for all of them (expensive and slow) or rely on gut-feel to reduce the list (biased). The 3-criteria quick screen provides a principled, fast filter that preserves analytical rigor while eliminating clearly non-viable options.

## When to use
Use in Phase 4 (Initiatives & Roadmap), immediately after the CX ideation workshop, to reduce the long-list before business case investment. Run in a 2-hour group session.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. List all 30–60 ideas from the CX ideation workshop, one per row of the screening table.
2. 2. Apply Criterion 1 (Moment of Truth Relevance): Does this initiative directly address at least one identified Moment of Truth from the customer journey map? If no, mark N.
3. 3. Apply Criterion 2 (Technical Feasibility): Can this initiative be implemented within 18 months given the current technology stack and budget? If the team cannot answer 'yes' with reasonable confidence, mark N.
4. 4. Apply Criterion 3 (CX Vision Alignment): Does this initiative contribute to achieving the approved CX Vision? If it optimizes for a metric not connected to the vision (e.g., operational efficiency at the expense of customer experience), mark N.
5. 5. Eliminate all initiatives that fail 2 or more criteria.
6. 6. Review borderline cases (fail 1 criterion): discuss as a team whether the failed criterion can be addressed. If yes, keep. If not, eliminate.
7. 7. The resulting list (typically 15–25 initiatives) advances to business case development.

## Real-life example — Marriott International
Marriott's CX team used a 3-criteria screen when prioritizing 44 post-merger (Marriott + Starwood) CX harmonization initiatives. The screen eliminated 21 ideas in one session: 12 failed Moment of Truth relevance (they optimized back-office processes with no direct customer impact), 6 failed technical feasibility (required platform migration beyond an 18-month horizon), and 3 failed vision alignment (they optimized revenue at the expense of guest experience quality). The resulting 23-initiative portfolio was prioritized using Impact vs. Effort analysis.

**So what:** A 3-criteria quick screen in one session does the work of 2 weeks of initial business case analysis — at 1% of the cost.

## Template
Apply all 3 criteria to every initiative. Eliminate initiatives failing 2+ criteria. Document the rationale for borderline decisions.

## Pitfalls
- Voting rather than screening: teams that apply the screen by majority vote introduce politics. Counter: the criteria are binary facts, not opinions — a facilitator applies them, not a vote.
- Too-generous feasibility scoring: teams keep ideas that require 3+ years to implement because they 'might be feasible with the right resources.' Counter: the feasibility criterion is '18 months with current resources' — no assumptions of future budget or capability.
