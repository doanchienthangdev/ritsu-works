# CX Organization Design

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A structured approach to designing the organizational model, governance mechanisms, and RACI framework that will deliver the CX strategy — covering the choice between centralized, federated, or hybrid CX structures and the roles required.

**Origin:** Based on McKinsey and Bain organizational design methodologies adapted for CX; informed by Forrester Research's CX organizational design best practice series.

## Why it works
The best CX strategy fails without the right organizational model. The structural choice (centralized CoE, federated chapters, or hybrid) determines how consistently standards are applied, how quickly the organization can adapt, and how accountability is distributed.

## When to use
Use in Phase 2 (Objectives & Organization) after the CX KPI Hierarchy is established. The org design enables the governance structure that will deliver the KPI targets.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Map current CX-relevant roles across all functions: customer service, UX, marketing, operations, digital, analytics.
2. 2. Identify ownership gaps: which customer journey stages have no clear internal owner?
3. 3. Evaluate three organizational models (Centralized CoE, Federated, Hybrid) against the company's size, culture, and maturity level.
4. 4. Select the model most aligned with the maturity level (Level 1–2: centralized; Level 3–4: hybrid; Level 5: federated with strong standards).
5. 5. Define RACI for key CX decisions: who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for journey design, KPI setting, technology selection, and budget allocation.
6. 6. Estimate headcount and roles required for the target model. Build the budget request.
7. 7. Present to the executive team for approval.

## Real-life example — USAA
USAA, consistently rated #1 in customer experience for financial services, operates a hybrid CX model: a central CX CoE owns member experience standards, journey mapping, and measurement; every business unit (insurance, banking, investing) has dedicated CX liaisons who implement those standards locally. This hybrid model prevents the inconsistency of pure federation while avoiding the bureaucracy of pure centralization.

**So what:** The hybrid CX org model is the most common answer for mid-to-large organizations because it balances standard-setting with local accountability.

## Pitfalls
- Choosing the model before the analysis: many organizations default to the centralized CoE because it's the most visible choice, regardless of whether the culture supports it. Counter: run the maturity assessment first — the model should match the maturity level.
- RACI with multiple 'A' owners: a decision with two Accountable parties has no accountable party. Counter: enforce a single 'A' for every key decision in the RACI.
