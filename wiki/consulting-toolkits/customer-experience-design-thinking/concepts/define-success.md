# Define Success Statement

*Category: ideation · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A structured pre-ideation alignment technique that produces a written, agreed description of what the world looks like when the problem is solved — including which KPI moves by how much, for whom, and by when — giving the ideation team a vivid, concrete goal to aim for rather than an abstract problem to escape from.

**Origin:** Adapted from Outcome-Driven Innovation (Anthony Ulwick, 1999) and the 'definition of done' concept in Agile methodology; used by Google Ventures and IDEO to anchor design sprints.

## Why it works
Teams that ideate without a clear picture of success generate solutions that 'address the problem' without knowing what 'solved' looks like. The Define Success Statement converts the Problem Statement's measurable success criterion into a vivid, motivating description that guides idea evaluation.

## When to use
Use at the start of Phase 7 (Ideate), before any idea generation begins. Post it visibly throughout the ideation session and use it as a filter during idea evaluation.

## Visual
`none`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Starting from the Problem Statement's success criterion, write a 1-paragraph 'future state' narrative: describe what the user's experience looks like once the problem is solved. Use present tense as if the solution is already in place.
2. 2. Add 3 specific KPIs with targets: e.g., 'NPS +12 points, checkout completion rate +25%, customer support contacts -30%.'
3. 3. Review the Define Success Statement against the CX KPI Hierarchy to ensure the targets are consistent with the strategic objectives.
4. 4. Post the Define Success Statement visibly in the ideation room. Begin every ideation session by reading it aloud.
5. 5. After ideas are generated, use the Define Success Statement as the first evaluation filter: 'Does this idea plausibly achieve the stated KPI targets?'

## Real-life example — Monzo Bank
Monzo's design team for their overdraft feature wrote a Define Success Statement: 'Our customers feel in control of their finances, not surprised by overdraft charges. They can see exactly what will happen to their account before it happens, and they choose to use overdraft because it's the right tool for them — not because they didn't notice a charge. Success: overdraft surprise complaints -80%, overdraft opt-in rate +40%, overdraft-related churn -25%.' This statement guided ideation toward transparency features (real-time account projections, pre-charge warnings) and away from feature-reduction ideas that might reduce complaints by reducing access.

**So what:** A vivid Define Success Statement prevents the team from optimizing for the wrong metric.

## Template
Write the future-state narrative in present tense, as if the solution is already in place. Include 3 measurable KPI targets.

## Pitfalls
- Defining success as 'users are happy': happiness is not measurable. Counter: every Define Success Statement must include at least 3 specific, measurable KPI targets.
- Success defined in terms of solution delivery: 'Success is when we launch the new app' is not user-centered success. Counter: success must be defined in terms of user outcomes and business KPIs, not internal milestones.
