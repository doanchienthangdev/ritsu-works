# 5W+H Questions (Who, What, When, Where, Why, How)

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A structured problem-characterization framework that applies six fundamental interrogatives — Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How — to a pain point or problem situation to build a comprehensive factual picture before attempting to solve it.

**Origin:** Rooted in Rudyard Kipling's 'six honest serving men' poem (1902); formalized as a journalism and investigative methodology; adopted into design thinking and consulting problem-framing practice.

## Why it works
Many problem-solving sessions jump to solutions before the problem has been fully characterized. Applying all six interrogatives forces the team to map the complete context of the problem: who is affected, what exactly happens, when and where it occurs, why it persists, and how it manifests — preventing solutions that address only one dimension of a multi-dimensional problem.

## When to use
Use in the Define phase after the Affinity Map and Empathy Map are complete, as a structured bridge between synthesis and the Problem Statement. Best used in a 45-minute team workshop.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. State the pain point or problem observation (from the journey map or affinity map).
2. 2. Apply each interrogative systematically: WHO is affected? (which segment, role, or persona); WHAT exactly happens? (the observable symptom); WHEN does it occur? (triggers, frequency, timing); WHERE does it occur? (which channel, location, or step in the journey); WHY does it persist? (the root cause hypothesis — validate with Five Whys); HOW does it manifest? (the specific mechanism and its scale/frequency).
3. 3. Fill the 'Current state' column for all 6 rows.
4. 4. Fill the 'Ideal state' column: for each W+H, what would be true in the ideal experience?
5. 5. Use the completed 5W+H table as a structured input to the Problem Statement template.

## Real-life example — Uber
Uber applied 5W+H to characterize the 'driver cancellation' problem: WHO — high-demand drivers with high ratings; WHAT — cancelling rides within 2 minutes of acceptance; WHEN — peak hours (7–9am, 5–7pm weekdays); WHERE — city center pickup zones; WHY — the pickup location required navigating a one-way system that added 5–8 minutes; HOW — at a rate of 12% of peak-hour requests. This characterization revealed that the problem was a routing issue, not a driver behavior issue — leading to a pickup location optimization feature (riders directed to the nearest navigable pickup point) that reduced cancellations by 35%.

**So what:** 5W+H reveals the dimensions of a problem that a single 'Why?' misses.

## Template
Apply all 6 interrogatives to the same pain point. Both columns must be completed before moving to the Problem Statement.

- [ ] Pain point being characterized: ___
- [ ] WHO is affected: Current state: ___ | Ideal state: ___
- [ ] WHAT exactly happens: Current: ___ | Ideal: ___
- [ ] WHEN does it occur: Current: ___ | Ideal: ___
- [ ] WHERE does it occur: Current: ___ | Ideal: ___
- [ ] WHY does it persist: Current: ___ | Ideal: ___
- [ ] HOW does it manifest (scale, frequency): Current: ___ | Ideal: ___
- [ ] Problem Statement derived from this analysis: ___

## Pitfalls
- Skipping WHEN and WHERE: teams focus on WHO, WHAT, and WHY while treating WHEN and WHERE as obvious. Counter: WHEN and WHERE frequently contain the most actionable insight (e.g., the problem only occurs on mobile, not desktop — a WHERE finding that changes the entire solution space).
- Treating WHY as the same as Five Whys: 5W+H WHY is a first-level hypothesis, not a root-cause analysis. Counter: use 5W+H to characterize the problem in all dimensions, then use Five Whys separately to trace the WHY hypothesis to its root cause.
