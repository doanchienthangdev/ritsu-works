# CX Vision, Mission & Values

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A three-part aspirational framework that defines where the customer experience is headed (Vision), what the CX organization does and for whom (Mission), and the behaviors and principles that guide every CX decision (Values) — creating the emotional and cultural anchor for the CX transformation.

**Origin:** Adapted from corporate vision-mission-values methodology (Collis & Rukstad, Harvard Business Review, 2008); applied to CX by McKinsey, Forrester, and Bain CX practices as a standard Phase 1 deliverable.

## Why it works
Without a clear vision, every CX investment decision becomes a debate without a reference point. The Vision anchors what 'excellent' looks like; the Mission provides operational clarity; the Values prevent the CX culture from regressing to the mean during organizational pressure.

## When to use
Use in Phase 1 after the Strategic Context Brief is approved. The Vision/Mission/Values become the permanent anchor for all subsequent CX strategy and governance decisions.

## Visual
`none`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Facilitate a half-day executive workshop using the 'Future Press Release' technique: ask leaders to write the WSJ headline about the company's customer experience in 5 years.
2. 2. Synthesize the themes across all leaders' press releases into a single 1-sentence Vision statement: aspirational, time-bound, and distinctive (not generic like 'We will provide excellent customer service').
3. 3. Write the Mission in 2 sentences: what the CX organization does (the activities), for whom (the customer segments), and to what end (the outcome).
4. 4. Define 3–5 CX Values: behaviors that distinguish your CX culture from the average. Each value should be phrased as a behavior ('We listen before we act') not an adjective ('Empathetic').
5. 5. Validate the trio against the Strategic Context Brief: is the Vision consistent with the business strategy? If not, revise.
6. 6. Ratify with the full Executive Leadership Team. Post visibly in all CX team spaces.

## Real-life example — Zappos
Zappos CX Vision: 'Deliver happiness through the world's best customer service.' This single sentence eliminated millions of dollars of cost-reduction initiatives that would have compromised call center quality. When any team proposed a change that reduced human interaction, the Vision served as the veto — 'Does this deliver happiness?' became the standard question in every CX design review.

**So what:** A strong CX Vision is a decision-making shortcut for every team that touches the customer.

## Pitfalls
- Generic vision statements: 'Deliver exceptional customer experiences' is so generic it could belong to any company. Counter: the Vision must be distinctive enough that competitors could not credibly claim it without changing their strategy.
- Values that describe aspirations not behaviors: 'Integrity' is not a CX value — it is an aspiration. Counter: every Value must describe what someone does, not who they are.
