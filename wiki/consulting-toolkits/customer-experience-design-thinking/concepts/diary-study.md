# Diary Study

*Category: research · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A longitudinal research method in which participants self-document their experiences, behaviors, and emotions related to a specific activity over a period of 7–21 days using a structured journal format — capturing context, frequency, and temporal patterns that single-session research cannot reach.

**Origin:** Adapted from clinical psychology and social science diary methods; formalized for UX research by Larson and Csikszentmihalyi's Experience Sampling Method (1983) and IDEO's HCD toolkit.

## Why it works
Many important user behaviors are infrequent, situational, or context-dependent — they happen once a week, at specific times of day, or in specific environmental conditions. Single-session interviews or observations miss these behaviors entirely. A diary study captures the full pattern over time, revealing frequency, triggers, and contextual factors that shape the experience.

## When to use
Use in the Empathize phase for design challenges involving infrequent, situational, or temporally distributed behaviors. Especially valuable for mobile app, financial services, and healthcare design challenges.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Define the target behavior to study (e.g., 'every time you need to contact customer support') and the study duration (7–21 days).
2. 2. Recruit 10–15 participants who exhibit the target behavior regularly enough to generate useful entries.
3. 3. Create the diary protocol: a set of 5–7 prompts per entry (trigger, actions taken, tools used, emotion score, open reflection). Make each entry completable in under 3 minutes.
4. 4. Brief participants thoroughly: explain when to make an entry (every time the target behavior occurs), how to make it, and what level of detail is expected.
5. 5. Send daily check-in reminders. Check submissions on day 3 — if entries are too short or too infrequent, coach participants before the study drifts.
6. 6. After the study, analyze entries chronologically and by participant. Look for patterns in timing, triggers, emotional progression, and workarounds.
7. 7. Use diary findings as input to the Empathy Map and Affinity Mapping session.

## Real-life example — Microsoft (Xbox)
Microsoft used a 14-day diary study with 15 Xbox users to understand gaming session patterns. The diary revealed that 80% of gaming sessions were initiated by social triggers (a friend came online or texted) rather than by the participant's independent decision to play. This contextual insight was invisible in usability tests and interviews — participants would say 'I play when I feel like it.' The finding shaped Xbox's social notification system design, prioritizing 'friend is online' alerts over content recommendation alerts.

**So what:** Diary studies reveal the triggers and social context of behavior that participants cannot accurately recall in a single interview.

## Template
Give one copy of this log to each participant. Remind them to complete an entry every time the target behavior occurs.

- [ ] Participant ID: ___ | Study dates: ___ | Target behavior: ___
- [ ] Entry 1: Date/time: ___ | Trigger: ___ | Actions: ___ | Tools used: ___ | Emotion (1-5): ___ | Reflection: ___
- [ ] Entry 2: [repeat]
- [ ] [Daily entries for study duration]
- [ ] Researcher analysis: Most frequent trigger: ___ | Highest-emotion moment: ___ | Most common workaround: ___

## Pitfalls
- Participant fatigue: participants who must complete complex diary entries lose motivation by day 5. Counter: design entries to take under 3 minutes. Compensate participants for their time.
- Sparse data: some behaviors occur less frequently than expected during the study period. Counter: run the study for longer (14–21 days) and over-recruit (aim for 15 participants to end with 10 consistent contributors).
