# Ideation Constraints

*Category: ideation · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A pre-ideation alignment exercise that explicitly identifies and documents the 3–5 non-negotiable constraints (regulatory, budget, technology, timeline) that all solution ideas must respect — preventing the common failure of generating ideas that cannot be implemented and the opposite failure of self-censoring creative ideas unnecessarily.

**Origin:** Adapted from IDEO's 'constraints as creative fuel' design philosophy and McKinsey's 'degrees of freedom' strategic analysis framework.

## Why it works
Constraints serve two functions in ideation: (1) they prevent the team from spending time on ideas that are not executable (a budget constraint eliminates ideas requiring $10M when $200K is available); (2) when made explicit, they reveal which constraints are real (regulatory, physical) and which are assumed (organizational, habitual) — and assumed constraints are often the best source of creative breakthroughs.

## When to use
Use at the start of Phase 7 (Ideate), immediately before the Ideation Workshop. Post the Constraint Charter alongside the Define Success Statement for the duration of the session.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Before the ideation session, convene a 30-minute constraint mapping exercise with the Sponsor and technical lead.
2. 2. List all constraints that team members believe apply to this design challenge.
3. 3. For each constraint, classify: Type (regulatory, budget, technology, timeline, or organizational culture); Source (is this a hard fact, a policy, or an assumption?)
4. 4. Explicitly test each constraint: 'Is this non-negotiable, or is it assumed to be?' Organizational and habitual constraints are frequently reclassified as assumptions at this step.
5. 5. Reduce to the 3–5 true non-negotiable constraints. Remove all assumed constraints — they will be reconsidered during idea evaluation rather than blocking ideation.
6. 6. Write a 'Constraint Charter': the list of 3–5 non-negotiables with their type, source, and implication for ideation. Post it visibly in the ideation room.
7. 7. At the start of ideation, announce: 'These 3–5 constraints are the boundaries. Everything else is fair game.'

## Real-life example — LEGO
When LEGO redesigned its digital fan community experience in 2010, the Ideation Constraints exercise revealed a critical difference: the team had assumed 'we cannot allow user-generated content because of child safety regulations' was a non-negotiable. Constraint mapping revealed it was an organizational assumption, not a regulatory fact — the regulation required moderation, not prohibition. Removing the assumed constraint from the non-negotiable list opened the LEGO Ideas platform (user-generated set designs that have generated $100M+ in revenue), which would not have been ideated under the original assumed constraint.

**So what:** The most valuable output of Ideation Constraints is discovering which constraints are assumed — because those are the creative opportunities.

## Pitfalls
- Too many constraints: more than 5 non-negotiable constraints reduces the solution space to near zero. Counter: if you identify more than 5 non-negotiable constraints, force a prioritization — which 3 would the Sponsor be willing to die on a hill for?
- Accepting all constraints as given: organizational and cultural constraints feel as solid as regulatory ones but are not. Counter: require every constraint to cite its source. If the source is 'we've always done it this way,' reclassify as assumed.
