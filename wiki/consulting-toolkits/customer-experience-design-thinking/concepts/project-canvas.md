# Project Canvas

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A single-page alignment tool used at the start of a Design Thinking initiative that defines and agrees on the design challenge, the target users, the scope, the team, the methods, and the success criteria before any research or design work begins.

**Origin:** Adapted from Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas (2010) and Ash Maurya's Lean Canvas (2012); formalized for design thinking by the Stanford d.school and IDEO.

## Why it works
Most design projects fail or drift because teams start executing without agreeing on the fundamental question: 'What problem are we solving, for whom, and how will we know when we've solved it?' The Project Canvas forces this alignment into a one-page artifact that can be reviewed and updated at each phase gate.

## When to use
Use at the start of every Design Thinking initiative or design sprint, before any research or ideation begins. Also use when a project is drifting — return to the canvas to re-anchor the team.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Call a 90-minute kick-off session with all stakeholders (sponsor, design team, technical lead, business lead).
2. 2. Start with the Design Challenge: write a 1-sentence description of the problem you're trying to solve. Use the format 'How might we [verb] [user] [achieve goal] in a way that [constraint or context]?' Do not jump to solutions.
3. 3. Define Target Users: name the 1–3 primary user segments affected by this challenge. If personas already exist, reference them here.
4. 4. Define the Scope: what is explicitly in scope (features, channels, user types) and explicitly out of scope?
5. 5. Define the Team: list each team member, their role, and their time commitment (% allocation).
6. 6. Select 3–4 Research Methods to answer the key research questions — map each method to a specific question it answers.
7. 7. Set Success Metrics: 2–3 measurable indicators that will tell you the design solution worked. These should map to the KPIs in the CX Balanced Scorecard.
8. 8. List Constraints: regulatory, budget, technology, or timeline constraints that are non-negotiable.
9. 9. Identify the Sponsor and key stakeholders who must be informed or consulted at each gate.
10. 10. Get all stakeholders to sign the canvas — this creates accountability.

## Real-life example — Procter & Gamble (P&G)
P&G's innovation team used a version of the Project Canvas at the start of every consumer design sprint. For the redesign of a household cleaning product, the canvas forced a critical scope decision: the team wanted to redesign the product, the packaging, and the marketing simultaneously. The canvas revealed that the user research methods they had budgeted could not address all three questions adequately. By constraining the scope to packaging only in the first sprint, the team completed usability testing 6 weeks faster and identified the packaging insight that drove a 22% sales increase.

**So what:** The Project Canvas prevents scope creep before a single dollar is spent.

## Template
Complete all 9 blocks in the kick-off session. Every block should be filled before research begins. The canvas is a living document — update it at each phase gate.

- [ ] Design Challenge (1 sentence, HMW format): ___
- [ ] Target Users (1–3 segments or persona names): ___
- [ ] In Scope: ___ | Out of Scope: ___
- [ ] Team members + roles + % allocation: ___
- [ ] Research methods (3–4) + what question each answers: ___
- [ ] Timeline: Start date: ___ | Phase gates: ___ | End date: ___
- [ ] Success Metrics (2–3 measurable KPIs): ___
- [ ] Constraints (non-negotiables): ___
- [ ] Sponsor: ___ | Stakeholders to inform: ___ | Stakeholders to consult: ___

## Pitfalls
- Writing a solution in the Design Challenge field: teams often write 'Redesign the mobile app' instead of 'How might we help users complete their order in under 2 minutes?' Counter: reject any Design Challenge that specifies a solution rather than a problem.
- Skipping the kick-off session: distributed teams often complete the canvas asynchronously, resulting in misaligned assumptions. Counter: require a synchronous 90-minute session with all stakeholders present.
- Never updating the canvas: teams often complete the canvas and never revisit it. Counter: make reviewing and updating the canvas a mandatory agenda item at every phase gate.
