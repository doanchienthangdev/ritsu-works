# Feedback Capture Grid

*Category: testing · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A structured four-quadrant synthesis tool used immediately after each user testing session to organize observations into Positives (what worked), Negatives/Pain Points (what didn't), Questions (things users were confused by or that the team doesn't yet understand), and Ideas (suggestions from users or team) — creating a consistent, comparable record across multiple testing sessions.

**Origin:** Developed at the Hasso Plattner Institute of Design (Stanford d.school); widely adopted as the standard post-testing synthesis format in design thinking practice.

## Why it works
Without a structured capture format, testing observations are typically remembered selectively — teams remember what confirms their prior beliefs (confirmation bias) and forget the rest. The four-quadrant format forces explicit capture of contradictory information (both Positives and Negatives), prevents the common failure of only recording problems while ignoring what worked, and captures speculative next steps (Ideas and Questions) while they are fresh.

## When to use
Use immediately after every user testing session in the Test phase. Use also after co-creation workshops, expert interviews, and any session where team members observe users interacting with a prototype or concept.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Print or create a Feedback Capture Grid template for each testing session — one grid per session, not one grid for all sessions combined.
2. 2. Immediately after each session (within 15 minutes), all observers complete the grid together: designate one person per quadrant to lead the capture, but all team members contribute.
3. 3. In the + POSITIVES quadrant: record what the user found intuitive, what they completed without hesitation, and any positive verbal feedback. This is not optional — teams that skip positives lose the signal of what they should NOT change.
4. 4. In the - NEGATIVES quadrant: record every point of hesitation (>5 seconds on one element), every error, every negative verbal expression, and every task failure. Rate each with a severity tag: Critical / Major / Minor.
5. 5. In the ? QUESTIONS quadrant: record any question the user asked during the session, any moment where they were visibly confused, and any research question the team still needs to answer after this session.
6. 6. In the ! IDEAS quadrant: record any feature suggestion from the user, any improvement idea generated by the team during the session, and any analogy from a different product that the user referenced.
7. 7. After all sessions are complete, do a cross-session synthesis: overlay all grids and identify patterns (issues appearing in ≥3 of 5 sessions are systemic; issues in 1 session are edge cases).
8. 8. Convert the synthesized NEGATIVES into a prioritized issue list. Severity-rate across all sessions. Generate one design fix per Critical and Major issue.

## Real-life example — IDEO / Bank of America
During the design of Bank of America's 'Keep the Change' program, IDEO used Feedback Capture Grids after each of 8 user sessions testing a prototype of the savings feature. Cross-session synthesis of the QUESTIONS quadrant revealed that 6 of 8 users asked the same question: 'Where does the rounded-up money actually go?' This question was not in the prototype — it was a mental model gap. The team had assumed users would understand that the change went to their own savings account. The ? finding led to the addition of a real-time 'Your savings jar' visualization to the design — a feature that became one of the most-loved aspects of the final product.

**So what:** The Questions quadrant captures the invisible assumption gaps that no one on the design team thought to test.

## Template
Complete one grid per testing session within 15 minutes of the session ending. Use severity tags (C/M/m) for negative items. Synthesize across all session grids at the end of the testing round.

- [ ] Session #: ___ | Participant: ___ | Date: ___ | Facilitator: ___
- [ ] + POSITIVES: (list observations) ___
- [ ] - NEGATIVES: (observation | severity C/M/m) ___
- [ ] ? QUESTIONS: (observation or gap) ___
- [ ] ! IDEAS: (from user or team) ___
- [ ] CROSS-SESSION SYNTHESIS (complete after all sessions):
- [ ] Critical issues appearing in ≥2 sessions: ___
- [ ] Major issues appearing in ≥2 sessions: ___
- [ ] Recurring questions (appearing in ≥3 sessions): ___
- [ ] Top 3 design fixes to prioritize: ___
- [ ] Overall task completion rate this round: ___% | Go/Iterate/No-Go: ___

## Pitfalls
- Completing the grid at the end of the day (not immediately): memories fade, and the most subtle observations (a moment of hesitation, a micro-expression of confusion) are lost within an hour. Counter: complete the grid within 15 minutes of the session ending — treat it as mandatory, not optional.
- Skipping the + POSITIVES quadrant: teams focused on finding problems skip Positives entirely. Counter: require the Positives quadrant to have at least 3 items before any other quadrant is considered complete.
- Conflating the grid with the final issue list: the Feedback Capture Grid is raw data, not a synthesized report. Counter: always run a cross-session synthesis step to distinguish systemic issues from edge cases.
