# Customer Segmentation Matrix

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A 2x2 matrix that plots customer segments on two axes — Current Value (revenue, margin, or CLV today) and Strategic Potential (growth opportunity, loyalty probability, or strategic fit) — to identify which segments deserve the most CX investment.

**Origin:** A variant of the classic GE-McKinsey Industry Attractiveness / Business Strength matrix applied to customer portfolio management; used in Bain, McKinsey, and BCG CX practices.

## Why it works
Not all customers are equally valuable or equally reachable. Investing heavily in CX for a segment with low strategic potential wastes resources. The 2x2 forces explicit debate about segment priority and prevents the common failure of defaulting to 'all customers matter equally.'

## When to use
Use in Phase 3 (Customer Segments & Journey Mapping) to determine which segments receive full journey-mapping investment. Revisit annually as segment dynamics evolve.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Identify all current customer segments using available transactional data (RFM: Recency, Frequency, Monetary).
2. 2. Score each segment on Current Value: combine revenue, margin, and retention rate into a composite score (1–10).
3. 3. Score each segment on Strategic Potential: combine growth rate of the segment, competitive win rate, strategic alignment, and likelihood of CLV expansion (1–10).
4. 4. Plot each segment on the 2x2. Bubble size = number of customers.
5. 5. Select 2–4 priority segments for CX investment. Core Segments receive the highest investment; Emerging Stars receive targeted acquisition investment; Harvest segments receive efficient, cost-optimized service.
6. 6. For each priority segment, commission a full User Persona and Customer Journey Map.

## Real-life example — American Express
American Express's customer portfolio management is built on explicit segment prioritization. The 'Centurion' (black card) segment is a 'Core Segment' (high current value, high strategic potential through word-of-mouth among high-net-worth peers). AmEx invests disproportionately in this segment's CX — dedicated 24/7 concierge, proactive fraud alerts, airport lounge access — because the strategic return per dollar of CX investment is highest here.

**So what:** Explicit segment prioritization is the foundation of CX ROI — you cannot invest equally in all segments and expect equal returns.

## Pitfalls
- Scoring on gut feel: teams score segments based on intuition rather than data. Counter: require each score to be backed by a specific data point (average revenue, retention rate, or growth rate).
- Selecting too many priority segments: more than 4 priority segments means no prioritization has actually occurred. Counter: force a rank-ordering of the top 4 and explicitly deprioritize the rest.
