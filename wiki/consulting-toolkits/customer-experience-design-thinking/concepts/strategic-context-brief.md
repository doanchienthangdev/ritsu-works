# Strategic Context Brief

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Customer Experience Strategy & Design Thinking*

## What it is
A single-page document that synthesizes the corporate strategy, business-unit strategy, and customer value proposition into the non-negotiable boundary conditions for the CX strategy — ensuring CX investments remain aligned with what the business is actually trying to achieve.

**Origin:** A McKinsey and Bain standard deliverable used at the start of any strategy engagement to align stakeholders on context before generating options.

## Why it works
CX investments made without a clear understanding of the business strategy often optimize for the wrong outcome (e.g., reducing call volume when the growth strategy is high-touch relationship-building). The one-page format forces brevity and prioritization — if a strategic priority cannot be stated in one sentence, it is not clear enough to guide investment decisions.

## When to use
Use at the very start of Phase 1 (CX Strategic Foundation), before any maturity assessment or vision work. Revisit at the start of each annual CX planning cycle.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Review all available strategic documents: annual report, investor presentations, board strategy deck, recent CEO communications.
2. 2. Synthesize into three layers: corporate (where the company competes), business unit (how each unit wins), and value proposition (what benefit customers receive and why they choose us).
3. 3. List the top 3 strategic priorities for the next 12–24 months that have a direct bearing on customer experience.
4. 4. List the top 3 constraints (budget ceiling, regulatory environment, technology debt, union agreements) that limit CX options.
5. 5. Draft the brief in one page. Present to the executive sponsor and iterate until they confirm it accurately represents the strategic reality.
6. 6. Sign the brief and use it as the first filter for all subsequent CX decisions.

## Real-life example — Singapore Airlines
Singapore Airlines begins every customer experience initiative with a strategic brief that explicitly states: 'Our strategy is premium differentiation — we compete on experience, not price.' This one statement eliminates entire categories of CX initiatives (reducing staff-to-customer ratios, moving to self-service check-in) that would be viable for low-cost carriers but contradict the strategic position. The brief prevents misaligned CX investments before they are proposed.

**So what:** The Strategic Context Brief is a filter, not a plan — it eliminates bad options before they waste resources.

## Template
Complete each section in bullet points. No section should exceed 3 bullets. Get executive sponsor sign-off before proceeding.

- [ ] Corporate strategy (markets, geographies, M&A): ___
- [ ] Business unit strategy (cost leadership / differentiation / focus): ___
- [ ] Customer value proposition: ___
- [ ] Strategic priority 1 relevant to CX: ___
- [ ] Strategic priority 2: ___
- [ ] Strategic priority 3: ___
- [ ] Constraint 1: ___
- [ ] Constraint 2: ___
- [ ] Constraint 3: ___
- [ ] Signed by: ___ Date: ___

## Pitfalls
- Copying the annual report verbatim: a brief that merely quotes the annual report is not synthesized and will not guide decisions. Counter: require the team to write it in their own words, tested by the question 'Could a new hire use this to make a CX investment decision?'
- Too many priorities: listing 10 priorities is no different from listing none. Counter: enforce a hard cap of 3 strategic priorities per the template.
