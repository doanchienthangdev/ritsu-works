# Data Literacy Programme (Three-Tier Curriculum)

*Category: organisation · Toolkit: Data Analytics & AI Strategy*

## What it is
A structured, role-differentiated learning programme that builds the data skills the organisation needs to make data-driven decisions — segmented into three tiers based on how deeply each role needs to engage with data.

**Origin:** Synthesised from Gartner's Data Literacy Framework (2019), Qlik 'Data Literacy Index' (2020), and Accenture 'The Human Impact of Data Literacy' (2020). The three-tier model reflects the empirical observation that one-size-fits-all data training produces universal mediocrity rather than targeted capability.

## Why it works
A data strategy without a data literacy programme is a car without a driver. The technology can be perfect, but if business decision-makers cannot read a dashboard, interrogate an analyst's assumptions, or commission an analysis, the data investment is wasted. The three-tier structure acknowledges that a CFO, a marketing analyst, and a software engineer need radically different data skills — and that confusing them with the same training destroys ROI and credibility.

## When to use
In Phase 2 (building Pillar 4: Data-Driven Organisation), concurrent with the platform build. Tier 1 should launch alongside the first live dashboard, so employees encounter the training at the moment of first contact with the product.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the target population for each tier using the org chart: Tier 1 = all office-based employees; Tier 2 = all managers, directors, and business analysts; Tier 3 = all data analysts, product managers, and finance team members.
2. Design Tier 1 content around the company's actual KPIs and dashboards — not generic data concepts. A 2-hour module that teaches an employee how to read the company's own revenue dashboard creates 10× more behaviour change than a generic 'what is data?' module.
3. For Tier 2, include a practical exercise: each participant must complete one real analysis using the company's BI tool during the training. The analysis should address a question from their own domain — this ensures the learning is immediately applicable.
4. For Tier 3, run as a cohort-based programme over 8–12 weeks, with weekly 2-hour sessions and a capstone project — a real business analysis presented to a business sponsor at the end.
5. Measure the programme: literacy score pre/post (use a standardised 20-question assessment tool), BI tool adoption rate (% of Tier 2 participants actively using the platform monthly), and data-driven decisions per team per month (track through management reviews).
6. Run Tier 1 annually for all new joiners and when the company's data platform or KPI definitions change significantly.

## Real-life example — Airbnb
Airbnb's data literacy programme ('Data University') trained all 4,000+ employees through three tiers: a foundation course on how Airbnb uses data and what its core metrics mean (Tier 1), an 8-course intermediate curriculum covering SQL, A/B testing, and data visualisation for all business functions (Tier 2), and an advanced track for data scientists and analysts (Tier 3). Within 2 years, 90% of business decisions at Airbnb were supported by data queries or A/B tests — up from ~50% before the programme.

**So what:** Data literacy creates the human demand for data products. Without it, you can build a perfect data platform and find nobody uses it. With it, the business pulls the analytics team forward — rather than the analytics team pushing insights nobody asked for.

## Pitfalls
- Generic content not grounded in company data: counter: always build Tier 1 around the company's actual dashboards, KPIs, and data sources — abstract data concepts produce zero behaviour change.
- Treating literacy as a one-time event: counter: run Tier 1 annually; refresh Tier 2 when the BI platform or analytical methods change; Tier 3 needs continuous learning through reading groups, hackathons, and conference participation.
- Measuring completion, not behaviour change: counter: the programme's KPI is the % of business reviews that include a data exhibit — not the % of employees who completed the e-learning module.
