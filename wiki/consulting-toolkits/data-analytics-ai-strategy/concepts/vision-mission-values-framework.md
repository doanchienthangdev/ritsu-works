# Data & AI Vision, Mission and Values Framework

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Data Analytics & AI Strategy*

## What it is
A structured facilitation approach for articulating a data and AI function's Vision (where it is going), Mission (what it exists to do), and Values (the non-negotiable principles that govern how it operates) — creating the north star that guides every subsequent investment and trade-off decision.

**Origin:** Adapted from Collins and Porras 'Built to Last' (1994) vision-mission framework, applied to data strategy contexts by McKinsey Digital and Deloitte's CDO programme practice. The distinction between Vision (aspirational, future-oriented) and Mission (operational, present-oriented) is a cornerstone of organisational purpose theory.

## Why it works
A data strategy without a clear Vision and Mission produces investment decisions that optimise locally but conflict globally. When the CDO says 'we will build a data lake' without a Vision of why, every subsequent trade-off (cost vs capability, speed vs quality) is decided on individual preference rather than strategic principle. The Values component is often skipped but is the most operationally important — when teams disagree about whether to deploy a model with 85% accuracy or wait for 92%, the Values ('quality before speed') resolve the disagreement without escalation.

## When to use
In Phase 1 (Step 3: define the vision, mission and values) before setting strategic objectives and KPIs. Revisit annually at the strategy review or when the corporate strategy changes materially.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Facilitate a half-day workshop with 8–12 senior leaders (CDO, CEO, CFO, CMO, COO, plus 3–4 business unit heads). The format: 45 minutes per element (Vision, Mission, Values), using structured prompts and iterative drafting.
2. For Vision: ask 'If our data and AI strategy succeeds completely, what will the company look like in 5 years? What will customers experience differently?' Collect 10–15 responses; cluster them; draft a single Vision statement that the group votes on and refines. Criteria: inspiring, specific enough to guide decisions, no longer than 2 sentences.
3. For Mission: ask 'What does the data and analytics function need to do every day for the Vision to become reality? What is our core accountability?' Criteria: present-tense, action-oriented, measurable in principle ('give every decision-maker a trusted single view of the data they need').
4. For Values: ask 'What are the principles we will not compromise on, even when under pressure? Where have we made bad data decisions in the past because we didn't have a principle to guide us?' Collect principles; consolidate to 5–8; for each, write the principle and a one-sentence rationale ('why this matters to us').
5. Pressure-test the draft Vision/Mission/Values against three real trade-off scenarios from the past 12 months: 'Would this Vision/Mission/Values have helped us make the right decision in scenario X?' If not, refine.
6. Validate the final version with the CEO: the data Vision must be consistent with the company Vision — if the CEO cannot articulate the connection, the data Vision is not strategic enough.

## Real-life example — Google
Google's data and AI Vision ('To organise the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful') is inseparable from the company Vision — the data function does not have a separate Vision but is the embodiment of the corporate Vision. The Mission of Google's data teams ('Give every product team the infrastructure and insights to understand their users and improve their product') drives hundreds of analytics investments. The Value 'data quality is a first-class product requirement' prevents the most common data failure: shipping features that produce bad data.

**So what:** The most durable data Vision statements are expressions of how data enables the company to achieve its corporate ambition — not separate 'data department' missions that sound like IT project charters.

## Pitfalls
- Generic Vision statements ('to be a data-driven company'): counter: the Vision must be specific enough that two reasonable people could disagree about whether the organisation has achieved it. 'Data-driven' is a label, not a direction.
- Values that are aspirational rather than operational: counter: Values are only useful if they resolve real trade-offs. 'Integrity' is a character trait, not a principle. 'Data quality is a prerequisite for AI deployment' is a principle that resolves the specific trade-off between 'ship fast' and 'ship right'.
- Facilitating the workshop without real decision-making authority in the room: counter: if the CEO is not in the room and does not validate the output, the Vision has no authority. The CDO's Vision that the CEO does not know about is a document, not a direction.
