# Operating Model & Organization Design

**Turn strategy into execution by designing the operating model and organization that makes it happen.**

---

## What You Are Really Buying

An operating model is not an org chart. It is the answer to: *How do we actually do our work?* It is the complete set of decisions an organization makes about capabilities, structure, culture, talent, processes, and technology—and how those decisions fit together to deliver the strategy.

Most strategies fail not because they are wrong but because the organization was never redesigned to execute them. BCG research found that fewer than half of all reorganization efforts reach their intended value creation objectives. The consistent reason: leaders redesign one or two elements (usually structure) while leaving the others unchanged. A new org chart with old processes, old culture, and misaligned talent is guaranteed to underperform.

This toolkit provides the structured 7-phase process to redesign all six operating model pillars simultaneously and implement the resulting changes in a sequenced, governed, financially tracked way.

---

## When to Use This Toolkit

Trigger this process when:
- **A new strategy requires different organizational capabilities** (the classic 'structure follows strategy' trigger: Chandler, 1962)
- **A merger or acquisition** requires integrating two operating models into one coherent system
- **Chronic performance problems** persist despite repeated tactical fixes—suggesting a structural or cultural root cause
- **Competitive disruption** demands a step-change in speed, innovation, or cost structure that the current model cannot deliver
- **Leadership transition**: a new CEO who wants to ensure the organization is configured to deliver their strategic agenda
- **Accountability and decision-making are chronically broken**—decisions are slow, escalation is excessive, and no one is clear on who owns outcomes

This toolkit is for: CEOs, COOs, CHROs, Strategy Directors, Management Consultants, and Transformation Leads managing an organization-wide change program.

## Phase I: Business Case for Change — Build the Mandate Before You Design

**Goal:** Secure stakeholder-approved authorization to proceed, with financial justification.

**Key Question:** Why must we act now, what will we achieve, and is the investment justified?

**Duration:** 2–3 weeks

### Step 1: Frame the situation using the SCR narrative

Conduct 10–15 structured interviews with senior leaders and front-line managers. Synthesize findings into a Situation–Complication–Resolution (SCR) narrative: one crisp problem statement, three root-cause hypotheses validated by data, and a proposed solution direction. This is the foundation everything else rests on—if the problem is poorly framed, every subsequent phase solves the wrong problem.

Input: Interview transcripts, recent strategy documents, performance data. Output: One-page situation brief.

### Step 2: Define objectives and KPIs using the Balanced Scorecard lens

Run a half-day workshop with the steering committee. Use the Balanced Scorecard's four perspectives—Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth—to draft 6–10 SMART objectives. For each, define one lead KPI (a leading indicator you can influence) and one lag KPI (the outcome you want to achieve), with current baselines and 3-year targets.

Input: Corporate strategy, situation brief. Output: Objectives & KPI register.

### Step 3: Define scope, approach, and team using the RACI matrix

Create a RACI matrix covering all six operating model pillars. Draft the project charter specifying in-scope and out-of-scope boundaries, the 7-phase timeline, governance rhythm (weekly steering, bi-weekly progress), and resource allocation by role.

Input: Objectives register, org chart, resource availability. Output: Project charter with RACI.

### Step 4: Build a rigorous financial business case

Model costs (project investment, implementation) and benefits (revenue uplift, cost savings) over a 3–5 year horizon. Calculate NPV, IRR, and payback period. Sensitivity-test the two most volatile assumptions. Present three scenarios (base, upside, downside). No initiative should proceed without a positive NPV under base-case assumptions.

Input: Cost estimates, revenue/savings hypotheses, discount rate. Output: Financial model with NPV/IRR.

**Deliverable:** Approved Business Case for Change (8 sections: situation, objectives, scope/team, activities, risks, strategic alignment, financial value, implementation ease).

**Gate:** Steering committee formally approves and authorizes Phase 2 resources.

## Phase II: Define the 6-Pillar Operating Model — Design What 'Good' Looks Like

**Goal:** Design the target-state operating model across all six interdependent pillars.

**Key Question:** What does the organization need to look like—capabilities, structure, people, processes, and systems—to deliver the strategy?

**Duration:** 4–6 weeks

The operating model is not one decision but six interconnected decisions. Change one pillar without the others and the system resists. The six pillars are:

### Pillar 1: Capability Management — Start here, everything else follows

Define 8–12 strategic capabilities required by the strategy. For each, score current state (1–5) and required state (1–5) on the Capability Heat Map. Capabilities with a gap ≥ 3 are urgent priorities. For each priority gap, classify the sourcing strategy using the Build-Buy-Borrow-Bot framework:

- **Build:** Develop the capability organically through hiring and training (best for proprietary advantages)
- **Buy:** Acquire or merge with an entity that has the capability (best for speed)
- **Borrow:** Partner with or license from an entity that has the capability (best for cost-efficiency)
- **Bot:** Automate the capability (best for repetitive, rules-based activities)

Input: Strategy, leadership interviews. Output: Capability heat map with gap scores and sourcing strategy per gap.

### Pillar 2: Structure & Governance — Choose the archetype that fits the strategy

Evaluate four structural archetypes (Functional, Divisional, Matrix, Network/Team-of-Teams) against your strategy's key capability requirements using the Organizational Design Archetypes framework. Select the primary archetype. Design the first three organizational levels: each unit's mandate, decision rights (using RAPID), reporting lines, and spans of control (target: 6–10 direct reports per manager). Design the governance calendar: what decisions are made in which governance bodies, on what cycle.

Input: Strategy, capability gaps, current org chart. Output: Target org chart + governance model + RAPID decision-rights register.

### Pillar 3: Culture — Measure the gap, then design the bridge

Administer the OCAI diagnostic (Competing Values Framework) to ≥100 employees. Map current vs. desired culture profile. Identify 3–5 culture design principles that describe the behavioral shifts required. Map each principle to a culture lever: symbols (what leaders do visibly), systems (HR processes that reinforce the behavior), skills (training), and stories (narratives). Note: culture changes in years, not months. Prioritize the two highest-leverage levers.

Input: OCAI survey results, strategy, leadership vision. Output: Culture diagnostic report, design principles, action plan.

### Pillar 4: Talent Development — Map who you have against what you need

Plot the workforce on the 9-Box Talent Grid (performance × potential). Identify critical roles and succession gaps. Define competency profiles per organizational level. Use the Build-Buy-Borrow-Bot logic to determine how each capability gap will be staffed. Build individual development plans for all high-potential employees (Boxes 8 and 9).

Input: Performance data, capability gaps, succession plan. Output: 9-box map, competency framework, talent sourcing strategy.

### Pillar 5: Processes — Redesign what creates and destroys value

Identify the 10–15 most strategically important processes using Business Process Management (BPM) methodology. Map each as-is using swimlane diagrams. Apply Lean waste analysis (eliminate overproduction, waiting, unnecessary transport, overprocessing, inventory, motion, defects, underutilized talent). Design the to-be process. Define process KPIs (cycle time, error rate, cost per transaction).

Input: Process inventory, current process maps, performance benchmarks. Output: To-be process maps, process KPI register.

### Pillar 6: Technology — Audit, rationalize, and roadmap

Conduct an IT landscape audit: list all current systems, their age, integration points, and fitness against the new operating model. Define 4–6 technology design principles. Classify each system as: rationalize, upgrade, replace, or add-new. Produce a 3-year technology roadmap with investment estimates.

Input: IT system inventory, capability gaps, process redesigns. Output: Technology gap analysis, 3-year IT roadmap.

**Deliverable:** Target Operating Model document covering all 6 pillars with gap analysis, design decisions, and action plans.

**Gate:** Executive committee approves the target operating model across all 6 pillars.

## Phase III: Generate Initiative List — Leave No Gap Uncovered

**Goal:** Produce an exhaustive, structured list of improvement initiatives—one per gap—across all six pillars.

**Duration:** 1–2 weeks

Run a 2-hour 'How Might We' (HMW) ideation session for each pillar with the relevant functional owner. Use silent brainstorming (5 minutes), group sharing, and affinity clustering to generate 8–15 initiative ideas per pillar (48–90 total). Capture each in a standard initiative card: title, problem it solves, pillar, estimated effort, estimated value.

Combine with affinity mapping to cluster and deduplicate. Produce a consolidated initiative register in spreadsheet form with unique IDs, pillar tags, and owner assignments.

**Deliverable:** Initiative register—exhaustive, deduplicated, with at least one initiative per identified gap.

**Gate:** Functional leads confirm completeness—no major gap left uncovered.

## Phase IV: Business Cases & Financial Models — Only Fund What's Justified

**Goal:** Quantify the value and cost of each shortlisted initiative so leadership can make informed investment decisions.

**Duration:** 2–3 weeks

Apply a 3-criteria screening gate to the initiative register: (1) strategic alignment (traces to an approved objective), (2) minimum value threshold, (3) implementation feasibility. Carry forward the top 20–30 initiatives. Build a one-page business case for each shortlisted initiative. For the top 10–15 by expected value, build a full 3-year DCF model (NPV, IRR, payback period), sensitivity-tested on ±20% in the key value driver.

**Deliverable:** Business case package: one-pager + financial model per shortlisted initiative.

**Gate:** CFO and steering committee approve; confirm which initiatives proceed.

## Phase V: Prioritize, Plan & Implement — Sequence for Momentum

**Goal:** Execute approved initiatives through a structured program with continuous improvement built in.

**Duration:** 6–24 months (ongoing)

Plot approved initiatives on the Value vs. Complexity Matrix. Classify: Quick Wins (implement Months 1–3), Strategic Investments (Months 3–18), Fill-Ins (opportunistic), Deprioritize (formally out of scope).

Build a multi-year transformation roadmap with swimlanes per pillar. Establish a Program Management Office (PMO) with a governance rhythm: weekly team standups, bi-weekly PMO review, monthly steering committee, quarterly board update. Use a RAG (Red-Amber-Green) dashboard for status reporting.

Implementation methodology by initiative type:
- **Agile/Scrum** for digital and iterative initiatives (2-week sprints)
- **Design Thinking** for human-centered experience initiatives
- **Waterfall/Prince2** for compliance and infrastructure initiatives

Embed a monthly PDCA cycle for all initiatives in flight. Conduct post-implementation reviews 30 days after each go-live.

**Deliverable:** Transformation roadmap, PMO charter, implementation playbook, first-wave initiatives in flight.

**Gate:** Steering committee confirms first-wave delivery; authorizes next-wave resourcing.

## Phase VI: Change Management — Win Hearts and Minds Before Go-Live

**Goal:** Ensure the human side of the transformation succeeds.

**Duration:** Runs concurrently with Phases 2–5 (12–18 months)

Apply the ADKAR model (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) to diagnose where each stakeholder group sits in the change journey. The model is sequential—identify the 'barrier point' (the first stage where average score drops below 3) and target all interventions at that stage. Jumping to Knowledge (training) without addressing Awareness or Desire is the single most common and costly change management mistake.

Design the internal communication architecture: key messages per audience, channels, frequency, owners. Apply the WIIFM ('What's In It For Me?') test to every message. Create a communications calendar aligned to the transformation roadmap milestones.

Measure adoption with monthly pulse surveys (10 questions, >70% response rate). Track ADKAR stage distribution across the organization. Report on the steering committee RAG dashboard.

**Deliverable:** Change management strategy, master change plan, communications calendar, monthly adoption tracker.

**Gate:** Adoption metrics meet minimum thresholds before each major go-live milestone.

---

## Phase VII: Strategic Dashboards — Sustain What You've Built

**Goal:** Provide real-time visibility into transformation performance and ongoing operating model health.

**Duration:** Ongoing from Month 1; full suite live by Month 3

Design three dashboard tiers using the Balanced Scorecard structure:
1. **Board-level (annual/quarterly):** 6–8 headline KPIs tracking transformation value delivery
2. **ExCo-level (monthly):** 15–20 KPIs covering all six pillars plus financial performance
3. **Operational (weekly):** Initiative RAG status, PDCA flags, change adoption

For each KPI, specify: owner, data source, refresh frequency, threshold (green/amber/red). Build a monthly financial performance dashboard (revenue, profit, cost, savings vs. business case). Maintain a live 3-statement financial model that integrates transformation investment and benefit tracking. Re-project NPV/IRR quarterly.

**Deliverable:** Three-tier dashboard suite with financial model integration.

**Gate (Program Close):** Final NPV delivered ≥ 80% of business case; operating model KPIs trending green for 3 consecutive months.

## The Frameworks & Tools

### McKinsey 7S Framework — Diagnose organizational coherence

The 7S model maps seven interdependent organizational elements: Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Skills, Style, and Staff. Shared Values sit at the center because all six other elements must align to them. Use at the start of Phase 2 to identify misalignments, and again after implementation to verify that all seven elements have been realigned to the new operating model.

**How to use:** Score each S on alignment between current and target state (1–5). Address the three lowest-scoring Ss first. The most common error is ignoring the 'soft Ss' (Shared Values, Style, Staff) in favor of the 'hard Ss' (Strategy, Structure, Systems)—the soft Ss are exactly where implementation typically fails.

### Competing Values Framework — Measure culture scientifically

Maps culture on two axes: internal vs. external focus, and stability vs. flexibility. Four quadrants: Clan (collaborate), Adhocracy (create), Market (compete), Hierarchy (control). Administer the OCAI diagnostic to ≥100 employees. Design culture interventions using four levers: symbols, systems, skills, stories.

**Amazon example:** Amazon's deliberate Adhocracy + Market culture profile is operationalized through two-pizza teams, PRFAQ process, and data-driven leadership principles—not posters.

### 9-Box Talent Grid — Calibrate talent across the organization

Plots employees on performance (x-axis) × potential (y-axis). Boxes 8 and 9 are the high-potential pipeline. The calibration conversation is more valuable than the classification—it forces a relative talent comparison that prevents grade inflation. Run annually.

**GE example:** Welch's Session C process used the 9-box to calibrate 600+ senior leaders annually, enabling GE to redeploy talent rapidly across its diverse business portfolio.

### ADKAR Model — Diagnose and close change resistance

Five sequential building blocks for individual change adoption. The key insight: identify the barrier point and target all interventions there. Most change programs fail by jumping to Knowledge without building Awareness and Desire first.

**Ford example:** Mulally's 2006–2009 turnaround followed ADKAR logic—building Awareness through real financial data, Desire through the 'One Ford' vision, and Reinforcement through the weekly Business Plan Review ritual.

### Organizational Design Archetypes — Choose the right structure

Four archetypes: Functional (scale/efficiency), Divisional (product-market focus), Matrix (dual accountability), Network/Team-of-Teams (speed and adaptability). Select by scoring against your strategy's capability requirements. Design coordination mechanisms to mitigate the selected archetype's inherent weakness.

**Spotify example:** The Squad Model (Network archetype) with Tribes, Chapters, and Guilds enabled 200→5,000+ employee scaling while maintaining startup speed.

### RAPID Decision-Rights Framework — Eliminate decision ambiguity

Five roles: Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide. One person holds 'D' only. Use sparingly for Agree—every A is a veto. Target: 30% reduction in decision cycle times for the top 10 decisions.

### Value vs. Complexity Matrix — Sequence the portfolio rationally

Four quadrants: Quick Wins, Strategic Investments, Fill-Ins, Deprioritize. The most valuable output is often the bottom-right quadrant—the explicit decision to stop doing something.

### Balanced Scorecard — Translate strategy into measurable objectives

Four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth. Build a Strategy Map showing the causal chain from Learning to Financial. Cap at 20 objectives and 25 KPIs. Drive the monthly operational rhythm.

### Capability Heat Map — Make capability gaps visible and actionable

Scores 8–12 strategic capabilities on current vs. required state. Gap ≥3 = urgent priority. For each priority gap, classify sourcing: build, buy, borrow, or bot.

### PDCA Cycle — Embed continuous improvement permanently

Four steps: Plan (hypothesis), Do (pilot), Check (compare actual to predicted), Act (standardize or adjust). Run monthly cycles for all initiatives. Toyota runs 1 million+ PDCA cycles per year.

## A Worked End-to-End Example: RetailCo's Omnichannel Transformation

RetailCo is a mid-size European specialty retailer (€2B revenue, 8,000 employees) whose market share is eroding as customers shift to online purchasing. The CEO and Board have approved an omnichannel strategy. Here is how they ran the Operating Model & Organization Design process.

**Phase I:** The project team conducted 20 interviews and framed the situation: 'RetailCo's store-centric operating model cannot support the omnichannel experience customers now expect.' The objectives were: (1) 20% of revenue from online channels within 3 years (vs. 4% current); (2) NPS from 32 to 50; (3) operating cost ratio flat despite investment. NPV of the transformation program: €45M over 5 years. Steering committee approved.

**Phase II — Six Pillars:**
- *Capability:* The capability heat map showed critical gaps in digital commerce (current: 1/5, required: 5/5), data analytics (2/5 → 5/5), and supply chain agility (2/5 → 4/5). Decision: buy digital commerce via acquisition of a 50-person e-commerce startup; build analytics internally; borrow supply chain agility via a 3PL partnership.
- *Structure:* RetailCo selected a modified functional structure with a new 'Digital Commerce' unit reporting to the COO. RAPID clarified that pricing decisions were 'Decided' by the CMO within a band; the CFO had 'Agree' rights for decisions above 15% discount.
- *Culture:* The OCAI diagnostic showed strong Clan culture (47/100) with weak Adhocracy (18/100). The required profile shifted Adhocracy to 30, Market to 35. Three culture design principles: 'test and learn beats perfect plan', 'customer obsession overrides store P&L', 'data-driven decisions'.
- *Talent:* The 9-box showed 12% of employees in Boxes 8/9. Critical gaps in digital product management and data science. A hiring plan targeted 40 external digital hires in Year 1.
- *Processes:* Key process redesigns: order management (as-is: 6-step, 72-hour; to-be: 3-step, 4-hour), returns (omnichannel returns accepted in-store and online), and inventory visibility (real-time across all channels).
- *Technology:* The IT landscape audit identified 3 legacy systems to retire, 1 ERP upgrade, and 2 new platforms to add (e-commerce platform, customer data platform).

**Phase III:** 73 initiatives generated across the 6 pillars.

**Phase IV:** 25 shortlisted, 12 with full DCF models. The top initiative (unified customer data platform) showed an NPV of €18M standalone.

**Phase V:** Quick Wins (8 initiatives, Months 1–3): store staff omnichannel training, click-and-collect rollout, digital weekly reporting. Strategic Investments (15 initiatives, Months 3–18): e-commerce platform build, supply chain redesign, data analytics capability.

**Phase VI:** ADKAR assessment showed the store manager population stuck at Desire (score: 2.1/5)—they saw omnichannel as threatening their store P&L bonus. Intervention: redesigned bonus metrics to include online sales originating from their store. Desire score rose to 4.1 within 6 months.

**Phase VII:** Three-tier dashboard live by Month 2. At Month 18: online revenue at 12% (vs. 20% target—on track), NPS at 44 (vs. 50 target—on track), cost ratio flat (on target).

## Templates — What to Use and When

1. **Business Case Template (Phase 1):** 8-section structured document: situation, objectives, scope, activities, risks, strategic alignment, financial model, ease of implementation.
2. **Capability Heat Map Template (Phase 2):** Spreadsheet with capability rows, current/target/gap columns, color-coding, sourcing decision.
3. **RAPID Decision-Rights Register (Phase 2):** Decision table with RAPID role assignments per major cross-functional decision.
4. **Initiative Register Template (Phase 3):** Spreadsheet with initiative ID, title, pillar, gap it closes, owner, effort score, value score.
5. **ADKAR Assessment Template (Phase 6):** Per-stakeholder-group scoring table with barrier point identification and intervention plan.
6. **Transformation Roadmap Template (Phase 5):** Multi-year Gantt with pillar swimlanes, milestones, dependencies, RAG status.

## Pitfalls & Best Practices

**Pitfall 1: Redesigning structure without redesigning the other five pillars.** A new org chart with old processes, culture, and talent is a guaranteed failure. Treat the six pillars as a system.

**Pitfall 2: Skipping the business case.** Without an approved business case, the initiative lacks authorization, financial targets, and a mandate to make hard decisions. Every transformation initiative needs an NPV.

**Pitfall 3: Underinvesting in change management.** BCG research shows that change management is the most underfunded workstream in most transformation programs, yet it is the one most correlated with success. Budget 10–15% of total program cost for change management.

**Pitfall 4: Implementing everything at once.** The Value vs. Complexity Matrix exists precisely to prevent this. Sequence for momentum: Quick Wins first to build credibility and fund further investment.

**Pitfall 5: Misidentifying the ADKAR barrier point.** Most programs fail at Desire, not Knowledge. Employees often know what to do but don't want to. Fix Desire before investing in training.

**Pitfall 6: Treating culture change as a communications exercise.** Culture changes through systems (what gets measured and rewarded), not through posters. Redesign the HR processes that reinforce the old culture.

**Best Practice 1:** Anchor every design decision in the strategy. If a proposed change cannot be traced to a strategic objective, it is out of scope.

**Best Practice 2:** Run the 7S alignment check at the start of Phase 2 and again after implementation. Every change to one S should trigger a check of how it affects the other six.

**Best Practice 3:** Make the program governance public and consistent. A steering committee that cancels meetings signals to the organization that the transformation is not a priority.

**Best Practice 4:** Celebrate Quick Wins loudly and publicly. They demonstrate that change is happening, build Desire in skeptical stakeholder groups, and fund the credibility needed for harder Strategic Investment decisions.
