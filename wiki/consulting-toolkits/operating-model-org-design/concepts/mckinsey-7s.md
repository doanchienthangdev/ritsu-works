# McKinsey 7S Framework

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Operating Model & Organization Design*

## What it is
A diagnostic and alignment framework that maps an organization across seven interdependent elements—Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Skills, Style, and Staff—to identify misalignments that impede performance.

**Origin:** Tom Peters and Robert Waterman at McKinsey & Company, 1980, popularized in 'In Search of Excellence' (1982).

## Why it works
The 7S model asserts that organizational effectiveness depends not on any single element but on the coherence among all seven. When one S changes, all others must adapt. The three 'hard Ss' (Strategy, Structure, Systems) are easy to define; the four 'soft Ss' (Shared Values, Skills, Style, Staff) are harder to change and are often the true source of misalignment.

## When to use
Use at the start of Phase 2 to diagnose organizational coherence, and again at the end of implementation to verify that all seven elements have been realigned to the new operating model.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List all seven Ss and define the current state of each in 3–5 bullet points based on document review and leadership interviews.
2. Define the target state for each S implied by the new strategy.
3. Score alignment between current and target state for each S on a 1–5 scale (1=severely misaligned, 5=fully aligned).
4. Identify the three Ss with the lowest alignment scores—these are the highest-priority redesign areas.
5. For each low-alignment S, define 2–3 specific changes required and assign an owner and timeline.
6. Re-score after implementation to measure alignment improvement.

## Real-life example — Unilever
During its 2010–2015 Sustainable Living Plan transformation, Unilever used a 7S-style alignment review to ensure that Shared Values (sustainability as a business driver, not just CSR) were coherent with Structure (new sustainability function embedded in business units), Systems (sustainability KPIs in performance management), Skills (training for 170,000 employees), Style (leadership model emphasizing long-term thinking), Staff (CEO Paul Polman's explicit mandate), and Strategy (doubling revenues while halving environmental footprint).

**So what:** The 7S lens prevented the classic failure mode of updating Strategy and Structure without adapting the soft Ss—particularly Shared Values and Style—which had been the undoing of previous sustainability initiatives.

## Pitfalls
- Treating the 7S as a one-time snapshot rather than a continuous alignment check—re-run it every 6 months during a transformation.
- Spending all time on the hard Ss (Strategy, Structure, Systems) and underinvesting in soft Ss—the soft Ss are the ones most likely to derail implementation.
- Conflating 'Staff' with headcount rather than using it to mean competencies and capabilities of the people.
