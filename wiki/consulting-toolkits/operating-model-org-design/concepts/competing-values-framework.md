# Competing Values Framework (CVF)

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Operating Model & Organization Design*

## What it is
A culture measurement model that maps organizational culture on two axes—internal vs. external focus, and stability vs. flexibility—producing four culture quadrants: Clan, Adhocracy, Market, and Hierarchy.

**Origin:** Robert Quinn and John Rohrbaugh, University of Michigan, 1983. Expanded in Quinn & Cameron's 'Diagnosing and Changing Organizational Culture' (2006).

## Why it works
No culture type is universally superior; effectiveness requires the right culture for the strategic context. High-innovation strategies require Adhocracy; efficiency-focused strategies need Hierarchy; customer-intimacy strategies suit Clan; results-driven strategies suit Market. The CVF quantifies the current culture profile and the desired profile, surfacing the delta that change management must close.

## When to use
Use in Phase 2, Step 3 (Culture pillar design) and re-administer annually as the operating model is implemented.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Administer the OCAI (Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument) survey—36 questions across 6 dimensions—to a representative sample of >100 employees spanning all levels and functions.
2. Average scores for each of the four quadrants (Clan, Adhocracy, Market, Hierarchy) to produce the current culture profile. Plot as a diamond/radar chart.
3. Ask the same respondents to complete the survey again describing their preferred or ideal culture in 5 years. Plot the preferred profile on the same diagram.
4. Identify the two or three largest gaps between current and preferred (gaps > 10 points are significant).
5. Design 3–5 culture design principles that describe the behaviors needed to close the key gaps.
6. Map each principle to a culture lever: symbols (what leaders do visibly), systems (HR processes that reward the behavior), skills (training programs), and stories (narratives that exemplify the desired culture).
7. Execute the levers, remeasure with the OCAI at 12-month intervals.

## Real-life example — Amazon
Amazon's leadership principles—'Customer Obsession', 'Bias for Action', 'Invent and Simplify'—reflect a deliberate CVF design that emphasizes Adhocracy (innovation, experiment culture) and Market (customer results, data-driven accountability) over Clan and Hierarchy. The PRFAQ (press release/FAQ) process and two-pizza team structure are systems that reinforce these quadrants at scale.

**So what:** Culture is not aspirational poster words; it is operationalized through HR systems, decision protocols, and rituals that make the desired CVF quadrant behaviors the path of least resistance for employees.

## Pitfalls
- Administering the survey to senior leaders only—their culture perception systematically differs from front-line reality. Survey all levels.
- Declaring a desired culture that is more than 20 points different from current on multiple quadrants simultaneously—culture shifts of this magnitude take 5–7 years; be realistic.
- Treating culture change as a communications exercise rather than a systems redesign—only changing symbols without changing systems produces cynicism.
