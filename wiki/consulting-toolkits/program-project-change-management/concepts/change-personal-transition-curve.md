# Change Personal Transition Curve

*Category: change · Toolkit: Program, Project & Change Management*

## What it is
A model of the emotional journey individuals experience during unwanted or disruptive change — from shock through denial, frustration, depression, and ultimately to acceptance and integration — based on Kübler-Ross's stages of grief adapted for the workplace.

**Origin:** Derived from Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's 'On Death and Dying' (1969); adapted for organisational change by John Adams, John Hayes, and Barrie Hopson (1976), 'Transition: Understanding and Managing Personal Change,' and further developed by William Bridges in 'Managing Transitions' (1991).

## Why it works
Business change is experienced as personal loss (of familiar routines, identity, status, or certainty). Leaders who ignore the emotional dimension of change and focus only on process compliance will achieve shallow, fragile adoption. By naming and normalising the emotional curve, managers can validate employees' experiences, reduce shame around expressing distress, and accelerate the journey to acceptance and capability.

## When to use
In manager coaching kits; when adoption is stalling in specific teams despite training completion; when a change involves significant loss of identity, status, or familiar ways of working.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Share the curve visually with managers 4 weeks before go-live and explain: 'Your team will go through these stages — this is normal. Your job is to support them, not force them to skip stages.'
2. Train managers to identify each stage by observable behaviours: Denial (business as usual conversations, refusing to attend training), Frustration (voicing complaints, cynical humour), Depression (disengagement, absence).
3. For each stage, provide managers with a 2–3 sentence coaching prompt to use in 1:1 conversations. Example for Denial stage: 'I notice you haven't attended the training sessions. Help me understand what is getting in the way for you.'
4. Identify the fastest and slowest movers in each team. Pair high performers who have reached 'Experiment' with those still in 'Frustration' — peer modelling is more effective than manager direction at this stage.
5. Measure the aggregate 'morale stage' of the organisation via a monthly pulse survey (1 question: 'Where are you on the change journey right now?'). Plot as a distribution across the curve to spot stuck groups.
6. Never pressure someone out of the Depression stage — it is a necessary letting-go stage. Acknowledge the loss explicitly ('I know this change means losing [X], and that is genuinely hard').

## Real-life example — Nokia (shift from hardware to software services, 2011–2013)
When Nokia's engineering teams were told to abandon Symbian development — the OS they had built for 15 years — the majority entered the Frustration and Depression stages simultaneously. The change team that worked well was Nokia's Maps division, which explicitly used the Transition Curve: managers held individual conversations naming the loss of the Symbian identity, hosted 'farewell ceremonies' for the old platform, and provided engineers with early exposure to Windows Phone development (Experiment stage) before the official switch. That division transitioned 40% faster than peer groups who received only communications and training.

**So what:** The 'farewell ceremony' — formally acknowledging the loss — was the key intervention that unlocked movement from Depression to Experiment. It was a 2-hour event that saved weeks of emotional stagnation.

## Template
Use in manager briefing kits. For each stage, document the observable signs and the recommended manager response.

- [ ] Stage 1 — Shock: Observable signs in team? Recommended manager response?
- [ ] Stage 2 — Denial: Observable signs? Recommended response?
- [ ] Stage 3 — Frustration: Observable signs? Recommended response?
- [ ] Stage 4 — Depression: Observable signs? Recommended response? (Note: Do NOT push; validate the loss)
- [ ] Stage 5 — Experiment: Observable signs? How to support experimentation safely?
- [ ] Stage 6 — Decision: Observable signs? How to celebrate commitment?
- [ ] Stage 7 — Integration: Observable signs? How to recognise and share success stories?

## Pitfalls
- Expecting the curve to complete in weeks: significant organisational changes take 3–18 months for the full population to move through the curve — plan sustainment activities across this full horizon.
- Managers skipping the Depression stage in their teams: discomfort with emotional conversations leads managers to rush employees to 'Decision' before they have processed the loss — this creates fragile compliance, not genuine adoption.
