# McKinsey 7S Framework

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Program, Project & Change Management*

## What it is
A holistic organisational diagnosis model that maps seven interdependent elements — Strategy, Structure, Systems, Staff, Skills, Style, and Shared Values — to identify misalignment and guide transformation.

**Origin:** Tom Peters, Robert Waterman, and Julien Philips, McKinsey & Company, 1980 — published in 'The Art of Japanese Management' (1981) and popularised in 'In Search of Excellence' (1982).

## Why it works
Organisations are complex adaptive systems where changing one element without adjusting the others creates new problems. The 7S model forces a systemic view: the 'hard' Ss (Strategy, Structure, Systems) and the 'soft' Ss (Staff, Skills, Style, Shared Values) are equally important, and Shared Values is the central hub that influences all others. Misalignment between any two elements is a root cause of transformation failure.

## When to use
Before designing a transformation programme; after a merger or acquisition to identify integration risk; when a strategy is not translating into operational results.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the target state: what is the organisation trying to achieve? Use the company strategy as the anchor for the entire 7S analysis.
2. For each of the 7 elements, write a current-state description (2–4 bullet points): Strategy (how do we compete?), Structure (org chart and reporting lines), Systems (key processes and IT), Staff (headcount, roles, demographics), Skills (core competencies), Style (leadership and management behaviour), Shared Values (culture, mission, norms).
3. For each element, write the target-state description: what needs to be true for the strategy to succeed?
4. Identify the delta (current → target) for each element. Rate the size of the gap: Large (requires significant investment/change), Medium, Small.
5. Map interdependencies: draw lines between the elements where a change in one affects another. Prioritise the most highly-connected elements first.
6. Build a change priority matrix: which elements are misaligned AND have the highest impact on strategy delivery? These become the focus of the transformation programme.
7. Validate the analysis with 5–10 senior leaders using a structured interview protocol. Their perspective will surface gaps your desk analysis missed.

## Real-life example — Microsoft (2014 Satya Nadella transformation)
When Nadella became CEO, the 7S diagnosis revealed: Strategy had to shift from 'Windows first' to 'mobile-first, cloud-first'; Structure was siloed by product line (must shift to platform teams); Systems were Windows-centric (must embrace open-source); Staff had too few cloud engineers; Skills gap in Azure and AI; Style was competitive internally (stack-ranking culture needed to change to growth mindset); Shared Values had to shift from 'know-it-all' to 'learn-it-all.' Addressing all 7S simultaneously — not just the strategy — is what enabled the turnaround.

**So what:** The 7S analysis revealed that the Shared Values gap (stack ranking and internal competition) was the most critical barrier; fixing strategy and structure without changing values would have failed.

## Pitfalls
- Treating it as a static one-time audit: the 7S must be re-assessed every 6 months during a transformation — elements shift as the programme proceeds.
- Underweighting the soft Ss: leaders routinely fix Strategy and Structure but ignore Skills, Style, and Shared Values — these soft elements are where most transformations stall.
- Using it as a label, not a diagnostic: 'we have a culture issue' is not an insight — the 7S must name the specific misalignment and the causal chain.
