# Prosci 3-Phase Change Management Process

*Category: change · Toolkit: Program, Project & Change Management*

## What it is
A structured three-phase process for professional change management: Phase 1 — Preparing for Change (building the foundation), Phase 2 — Managing Change (executing the plans), Phase 3 — Reinforcing Change (embedding and sustaining).

**Origin:** Prosci Research (Jeff Hiatt and Tim Creasy), published as the 'Prosci Change Management Methodology' in the 1990s and continuously updated; current version known as the PCT Model (Project Change Triangle). The methodology is backed by 20+ years of benchmarking research across thousands of organisations.

## Why it works
Prosci separates the 'what are we changing' (project management) from the 'how do we help people change' (change management). The three-phase structure mirrors a project lifecycle: prepare before acting, execute with discipline, and reinforce after going live. The PCT Model shows that project success requires all three vertices: effective change management, strong sponsorship, and sound project management.

## When to use
Any change initiative that impacts people's daily work. Most powerful for large-scale, multi-year programmes where the risk of people not adopting is the primary delivery risk.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Phase 1 — Assess the change: Complete the Change Characteristics Assessment (size, scope, duration) and the Organisational Attributes Assessment (history of change, culture, capacity). These two assessments size the change management effort needed.
2. Phase 1 — Build the change management strategy: using ADKAR targets and the readiness assessments, define the overall approach, team structure, sponsor model, and special tactics for high-resistance groups.
3. Phase 1 — Secure an active and visible sponsor: brief the primary sponsor on their specific role (communicate the 'why', build the coalition, remove barriers) and get their written commitment to the time required.
4. Phase 2 — Build and execute the master change management plan: integrate all 5 sub-plans (communication, sponsorship, coaching, training, resistance management) into a single Gantt.
5. Phase 2 — Deploy the communication plan, then the coaching plan, then training — in that order. Awareness before Knowledge, Desire before Ability.
6. Phase 3 — Conduct a reinforcement survey 30 days post go-live: measure each ADKAR dimension by role group. Identify gaps.
7. Phase 3 — For each gap, design a corrective action: if Ability is low, provide additional coaching; if Reinforcement is low, implement a recognition programme and remove the old process.
8. Phase 3 — Conduct a lessons-learned session and archive all change management artefacts in the PMO library for reuse.

## Real-life example — Siemens (SAP S/4HANA global rollout, 2018–2022)
Siemens applied the Prosci methodology across a multi-country SAP S/4HANA implementation covering 300,000+ users. Phase 1 identified that 4 of 12 business divisions had historically resisted IT-led change (low Desire scores) and required dedicated executive sponsors in each division rather than a central sponsor. Phase 2 deployed region-specific communications in 30 languages with local manager coaching kits. Phase 3 measured adoption 60 days post go-live and found two divisions with low Ability scores, triggering a targeted 4-week super-user coaching programme.

**So what:** Segmenting the sponsor model by business division in Phase 1 — rather than assuming one central sponsor sufficed — was the single most impactful adaptation.

## Pitfalls
- Treating Phase 3 as 'done': organisations close the change workstream at go-live, but sustainment is where 80% of change failures occur — schedule a formal Phase 3 review at T+30, T+60, and T+90.
- Skipping the Change Characteristics Assessment in Phase 1: under-resourcing the change team because the change was assessed as 'not that complex' is one of the most common failures — always quantify the scope formally.
