# Kotter's 8-Step Model of Change

*Category: change · Toolkit: Program, Project & Change Management*

## What it is
A sequential eight-step process for leading large-scale organisational change, designed to build momentum, overcome resistance, and anchor new behaviours permanently.

**Origin:** John P. Kotter, Harvard Business School, 1995 — published in 'Leading Change' (Harvard Business Review Press, 1996) after analysing 100+ transformation efforts.

## Why it works
Kotter observed that most transformations fail because leaders skip steps or move too fast. The model addresses the root causes: insufficient urgency, weak guiding coalition, no vision, under-communication, uncleared obstacles, no short-term wins, declaring victory too soon, and failing to anchor change in culture. Each step builds on the prior one; skipping any step creates a fatal flaw.

## When to use
Any large-scale organisational transformation (M&A, ERP, restructuring, culture shift) where the primary risk is human resistance rather than technical execution.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Map current performance data and burning platform evidence; prepare 5 facts that show the status quo is unsustainable. Share these in executive leadership meetings to build the felt need for change.
2. Identify 8–12 individuals from across the hierarchy and functions who have formal authority, informal influence, and high credibility. Hold a half-day coalition meeting; align on the burning platform and mandate.
3. Write a change vision in plain language (fewer than 150 words). Test it: can a bystander understand and repeat it in 5 minutes? If not, simplify until they can.
4. Brief the coalition; cascade the vision through a structured communication cascade (executives → managers → employees). Use every channel and repeat the message at least 7 times in 6 months.
5. Run a 'barrier audit': interview 20 frontline employees and 10 middle managers. List every structural, system, process, or managerial barrier to working in the new way. Assign an owner and deadline to remove each one.
6. Identify 3 quick wins achievable within 90 days that are unambiguously related to the change. Execute them. Publicly celebrate the individuals who delivered them.
7. After the first wins, restock the change team, increase the pace, and tackle the harder systemic issues (culture, incentives, structure) that the early wins revealed.
8. Explicitly link the new behaviours to business results in every leadership communication. Update job descriptions, performance reviews, and hiring criteria to reflect the new culture. Never stop until old habits are completely replaced.

## Real-life example — Ford Motor Company (2006–2009 turnaround)
CEO Alan Mulally applied Kotter-like urgency by mortgaging Ford's assets before the financial crisis (Step 1: burning platform), built a unified leadership team across previously siloed brands (Step 2), communicated a single 'One Ford' vision globally (Steps 3–4), simplified the product portfolio from 97 models to 20 to remove complexity (Step 5), and celebrated early wins including the first profitable quarter in 2009 (Step 6), ultimately anchoring the culture by tying executive bonuses to shared company metrics.

**So what:** Urgency and coalition are prerequisites — without Mulally's credible burning platform and unified leadership, the operational changes would have stalled in middle management.

## Pitfalls
- Declaring victory too early: announcing 'we've changed' after one successful quarter causes regression — sustain pressure through Step 8.
- Coalition without real authority: a coalition of middle managers with no executive backing cannot clear structural barriers — ensure C-level members are active, not ceremonial.
- Communicating the vision only once: Kotter's research shows the vision needs to be repeated 7+ times through multiple channels before it is internalised.
