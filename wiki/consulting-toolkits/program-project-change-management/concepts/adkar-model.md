# ADKAR Change Management Model

*Category: change · Toolkit: Program, Project & Change Management*

## What it is
An outcome-oriented change framework that tracks an individual's progression through five sequential building blocks: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement — identifying exactly where a change effort is stalling at the individual level.

**Origin:** Jeff Hiatt, Prosci Research, 1998 — published as 'ADKAR: A Model for Change in Business, Government and our Community' (2006). Derived from research across 700+ organisations.

## Why it works
All organisational change happens through individuals. A collective change effort stalls because specific individuals are blocked at a specific ADKAR dimension. By measuring each dimension separately, a Change Manager can diagnose root causes precisely: if training fails, it may be because Desire was never built (the person doesn't want to change), making the training effort pointless. ADKAR is the diagnostic complement to Kotter's prescriptive sequence.

## When to use
Any change that requires individuals to adopt new behaviours — ERP implementations, process redesigns, restructurings, mergers. Most valuable as a diagnostic tool when adoption is lagging.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the change from each impacted role group's perspective — not the project's perspective. A finance analyst and a field technician experience the same ERP implementation completely differently.
2. Build an ADKAR assessment survey: 5 questions (one per dimension), scored 1–5. Deploy via anonymous survey to a representative sample 8 weeks before go-live.
3. Identify the 'barrier point' — the lowest-scoring dimension for each role group. The barrier point is the first dimension scoring below 3/5; no subsequent dimension can be stronger than the barrier point.
4. Design interventions for the barrier point only. Do not run training (Knowledge) for a group whose Desire scores below 3/5 — it will not work.
5. Map each change management activity to the ADKAR dimension it builds: communications = Awareness; leadership endorsement + manager conversations = Desire; training + job aids = Knowledge; on-the-job coaching = Ability; recognition + accountability = Reinforcement.
6. Re-survey at T-4 weeks, T-2 weeks, and T+4 weeks versus go-live. Plot ADKAR scores over time as a trend chart. Target all dimensions ≥4/5 at T+60 days.

## Real-life example — Kaiser Permanente (EHR implementation, 2000s)
During a large-scale Electronic Health Records rollout, Prosci's ADKAR assessment found that physician groups had high Awareness (they understood WHY the system was changing) but very low Desire (they did not want to change their documentation habits). Training completion was high, but adoption was low. The intervention shifted from more training to peer-led physician champions (desire-building), resulting in a 40-point improvement in physician adoption rates within 90 days.

**So what:** Desire was the barrier point, not Knowledge — diagnosing the right dimension saved months of ineffective training spend.

## Template
For each role group, score each ADKAR dimension and note the primary intervention. The barrier point is the first dimension below the threshold (3/5).

- [ ] Role group: [name]
- [ ] A — Awareness score (1–5): [score] | Current state: [describe] | Intervention: [comms/briefings/data]
- [ ] D — Desire score (1–5): [score] | Current state: [describe] | Intervention: [leadership/peer/WIIFM]
- [ ] K — Knowledge score (1–5): [score] | Current state: [describe] | Intervention: [training/job aid/SOP]
- [ ] A — Ability score (1–5): [score] | Current state: [describe] | Intervention: [coaching/practice/sandbox]
- [ ] R — Reinforcement score (1–5): [score] | Current state: [describe] | Intervention: [recognition/measurement/remove old way]
- [ ] Barrier point dimension: [A/D/K/A/R]
- [ ] Primary intervention: [specific action + owner + date]

## Pitfalls
- Running training before Desire is built: the highest-cost intervention (training) is wasted if employees don't yet want to change — build Desire first through manager conversations and visible leadership endorsement.
- Declaring success at Knowledge: completion certificates measure K, not Ability or Reinforcement — measure actual behaviour change (system usage, process adherence) 30–90 days post go-live.
- Generic Awareness communications: a one-size-fits-all email builds minimal Desire — segment by role and make the message personal ('what does this mean for YOUR job?').
