# Income-Expense-Savings Dashboard

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Personal Finance & Warren Buffett Investing*

## What it is
A monthly personal profit-and-loss statement that categorises all income and expenses, calculates free cash flow (investable surplus), and tracks the savings rate as the primary wealth-building ratio.

**Origin:** Household budget frameworks date to 19th-century household management manuals; formalised in personal finance practice through the work of Elizabeth Warren (the 50/30/20 budget rule, 'All Your Worth', 2005) and the broader CFP financial planning standards. The concept of the savings rate as a wealth-building multiplier was quantified by Mr. Money Mustache (Pete Adeney) in blog work circa 2011–2012 showing the mathematical relationship between savings rate and years to financial independence.

## Why it works
Free cash flow = total income − total expenses. The savings rate = (income − total expenses) / income. At a 10% savings rate, reaching financial independence takes ~40 years. At 50%, it takes ~17 years. Every percentage point increase in savings rate materially compresses the wealth-building timeline. The dashboard forces practitioners to see the ratio, not just the absolute numbers.

## When to use
Monthly, always. This is the operational engine of Phase 1 and the primary control lever for increasing investable capital over time.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Pull 3 months of bank statements and credit card statements. Do not rely on memory.
2. 2. Create two columns: Income and Expenses. Under Income, list every source: salary (net of taxes), side income, rental income, investment dividends.
3. 3. Under Expenses, create sub-buckets: Fixed (rent, insurance, loan payments, subscriptions — amounts that do not change month to month) and Variable (groceries, dining, transport, clothing, entertainment, travel — amounts that vary).
4. 4. Categorise every line item from statements into one of the expense sub-buckets. Uncategorised spending is a red flag — investigate.
5. 5. Sum each column. Calculate: Free Cash Flow = Total Income − Total Expenses.
6. 6. Calculate Savings Rate = Free Cash Flow / Total Income × 100. Record this as your primary wealth-building KPI.
7. 7. Identify the top 3 expense categories by dollar amount. These are your levers. A 10% reduction in each of the top 3 categories produces the most savings improvement per unit of behaviour change.
8. 8. Set a target savings rate (minimum 20%) and a timeline to reach it. Review monthly.

## Real-life example — Vanguard's retirement research (individual investor households)
Vanguard's 'How America Saves' annual report (2022 edition) found that households with auto-escalating 401(k) contribution rates — a mechanism that automatically increases the savings rate each year — accumulated 30–50% more retirement assets over a 10-year period than comparable households without auto-escalation, even when the initial contribution rates were identical. The savings rate improvement was the single strongest predictor of retirement wealth accumulation, outperforming investment return selection.

**So what:** The savings rate, not the investment return, is the variable most in the individual's control in the early wealth-building years. A 5% higher savings rate has more impact than a 2% higher investment return when the portfolio is small.

## Pitfalls
- Using net income as income base but gross income as the savings-rate denominator — be consistent; use net-of-tax income throughout.
- Excluding irregular expenses (annual insurance premiums, car maintenance) — average them to monthly by dividing annual total by 12.
- Conflating the 'savings rate' with the '401k contribution rate' — savings rate includes all savings (401k, IRA, brokerage, emergency fund additions); 401k rate is a subset.
- Reviewing quarterly instead of monthly — by the time you see a quarterly overspend, three months of inefficient behaviour have compounded.
