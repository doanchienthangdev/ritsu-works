# Buffett Philosophy Canvas

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Personal Finance & Warren Buffett Investing*

## What it is
A structured framework for extracting, cataloguing, and internalising the core investment principles from the six canonical Buffett texts, then mapping each principle against the practitioner's existing beliefs to identify conflicts — producing a Personal Investment Manifesto that governs all subsequent decisions.

**Origin:** Synthesised from the collective body of six primary sources: The Intelligent Investor (Graham, 1949), Security Analysis (Graham & Dodd, 1934), Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Fisher, 1958), One Up On Wall Street (Lynch, 1989), The Warren Buffett Way (Hagstrom, 1994/2013), and The Essays of Warren Buffett (compiled by Cunningham, multiple editions). The canvas structure is an original synthesis tool; the principles themselves are drawn from Buffett's own letters and the cited texts.

## Why it works
Buffett's consistent outperformance over 60+ years is not the result of a single technique but of a coherent, internally consistent system of first principles. Practitioners who read about Buffett without explicitly extracting and committing to these principles tend to apply them selectively — using value investing language while maintaining speculative behaviours. The canvas forces explicit articulation and commitment, making implicit beliefs visible and testable.

## When to use

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Gather (or access summaries of) all six canonical texts: The Intelligent Investor, Security Analysis, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits, One Up On Wall Street, The Warren Buffett Way, and The Essays of Warren Buffett.
2. 2. Work through each text sequentially. For each major concept, extract it as a single-sentence principle in your own words. Aim for 4–6 principles per book.
3. 3. For each extracted principle, write an operational rule: a concrete, first-person, action-specific statement of how you will apply this principle in your investment process. 'Mr. Market provides prices, not advice' becomes: 'I will never make a sell decision based on a price decline alone; only on fundamental deterioration.'
4. 4. For each principle, ask: 'Does this conflict with a belief I currently hold?' If yes, write down the current belief you are agreeing to discard.
5. 5. Compile all principles into a personal manifesto document (20–30 principles). Number them. Sign and date it.
6. 6. Review the manifesto before every investment decision in Phase 5 as a checklist — do my four-gate answers reflect these principles or contradict them?
7. 7. Annually review the manifesto and add principles you discovered through experience.

## Real-life example — Charlie Munger / Berkshire Hathaway
Charlie Munger, Buffett's business partner, describes his own investment philosophy as a 'latticework of mental models' — a deliberate, curated set of frameworks from multiple disciplines that he applies to every decision. Munger's '100 mental models' approach (described in 'Poor Charlie's Almanack') is the practitioner-level example of the philosophy-extraction process. The key is not reading books but explicitly extracting operable principles and committing to them — Munger has described specific investment failures that occurred when he violated a principle he knew but did not enforce.

**So what:** A philosophy that exists in your head but is not written down is not an operating system — it is a set of nice ideas that evaporates under emotional pressure. The manifesto is the practitioner's commitment device.

## Pitfalls
- Extracting principles at face value without translating them into operational rules — 'Buy great companies at fair prices' is a principle, not an operating instruction; it must become a checklist item with specific criteria.
- Skipping the conflict-identification step — practitioners who do not explicitly identify and discard conflicting beliefs retain them; the speculative behaviour persists beneath Buffett vocabulary.
- Creating a manifesto and never consulting it — the document is only useful if reviewed before every Phase 5 decision.
