# Net Worth Statement

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Personal Finance & Warren Buffett Investing*

## What it is
A point-in-time personal balance sheet that lists all assets at current market value and all liabilities at outstanding balance, producing a single net worth figure and a liquid net worth figure that defines the true investable base.

**Origin:** Double-entry bookkeeping principles dating to Luca Pacioli (1494); applied to personal finance by financial planning practitioners and codified in standards such as the CFP Board's financial planning process. The format mirrors corporate balance sheets but scoped to individual or household.

## Why it works
Net worth = total assets − total liabilities. The power is in the trending over time: a rising net worth confirms that wealth-building behaviours are working. Separating liquid assets from illiquid assets reveals how much of the wealth is actually deployable for investment versus trapped in real estate or retirement accounts.

## When to use
At the start of any personal financial planning process; monthly thereafter as a progress metric; immediately after any major financial event (home purchase, job change, large debt payoff).

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Open the Net Worth Statement template. Date it as of today.
2. 2. List every asset in the appropriate category. For liquid assets (cash, savings, money market), use exact balances from today's statements. For investable assets (brokerage, 401k, IRA), use today's closing values. For real estate, use a conservative market estimate (Zillow or a recent comparable sale). For vehicles, use Kelley Blue Book private-party value.
3. 3. List every liability at its current outstanding balance from the most recent statement. Do not net or estimate — use exact figures.
4. 4. Calculate Total Assets, Total Liabilities, and Net Worth. Separately calculate Liquid Net Worth = Liquid + Investable assets minus Total Liabilities.
5. 5. Record the Debt-to-Asset Ratio = Total Liabilities / Total Assets. A ratio above 0.50 signals significant leverage.
6. 6. Save this snapshot. Set a calendar reminder to repeat this exercise monthly on the same day of the month.
7. 7. After 3 months, plot the trend line. Net worth should be rising. If not, the root cause is in Step 1.2 (income vs expense mismatch).

## Real-life example — Dave Ramsey's Baby Steps Framework (individual finance)
Ramsey's 'Financial Peace' programme begins with an identical net-worth snapshot as step zero. Participants who complete this step report that seeing the total liability number in a single figure — rather than fragmented across multiple statements — provides the psychological shock necessary to commit to behavioural change. Investors who skip this step and go straight to stock picking routinely face forced liquidation during market drawdowns because they did not know their true cash buffer.

**So what:** The net worth statement converts vague financial anxiety into a precise, actionable number. You cannot improve what you have not measured.

## Pitfalls
- Using estimated or rounded numbers for liquid assets — always use exact statement balances; estimation introduces false precision.
- Including future expected income (bonus, inheritance) as an asset — only current market values count.
- Ignoring illiquid assets entirely — real estate equity IS wealth even if not investable today; omitting it understates net worth.
- Updating quarterly instead of monthly — a 90-day lag obscures the trend signal; monthly cadence catches deterioration early.
