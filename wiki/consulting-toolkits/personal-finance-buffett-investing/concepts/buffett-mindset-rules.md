# Buffett Mindset Rules

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Personal Finance & Warren Buffett Investing*

## What it is
A set of five pre-committed behavioural rules drawn directly from Buffett's writings and the canonical texts, designed to neutralise the most common emotional and cognitive failure modes that cause individual investors to underperform: reactive selling, FOMO buying, overconfidence, and benchmark envy.

**Origin:** Derived from Buffett's annual shareholder letters (1977–2023), particularly the 1987, 1992, 1996, and 2008 letters, supplemented by Daniel Kahneman's behavioural finance research ('Thinking, Fast and Slow', 2011) and Robert Cialdini's work on influence and commitment devices. The Mr. Market metaphor originates with Benjamin Graham in The Intelligent Investor (1949). The Fear/Greed aphorism is one of Buffett's most cited quotes from his 1986 shareholder letter.

## Why it works
Behavioural finance research (Kahneman, Thaler, Shefrin) consistently finds that individual investor returns are 1.5–2% per year lower than the funds they invest in, entirely due to timing decisions — buying after peaks and selling after troughs. Pre-committing to mindset rules before facing emotional situations creates a friction device that forces a pause. The pause reduces the probability of emotional override. Written, signed rules work better than mental resolutions because of the commitment-consistency principle (Cialdini).

## When to use
Phase 3 (written), Phase 5 (reviewed before each investment decision), and any time market conditions are creating emotional pressure.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Open the Mindset Rules Card template. Write each of the five rules in the first person, in your own words.
2. 2. For the Mr. Market Rule: define in writing what constitutes a 'quality business' for you (companies that passed Gates 2 and 3). Commit: 'I will not sell [any company that passed Gates 2 and 3] based on price decline alone. A price drop increases my interest, not my fear.'
3. 3. For the Fear/Greed Rule: define what 'widespread fear' looks like (VIX > 30, market down > 20% YTD, financial media using the word 'crash' in >3 headlines per day). Commit to reviewing your watchlist's margin-of-safety gap whenever these signals appear.
4. 4. For the Circle of Competence Rule: list your 3–5 industries explicitly by name. Sign the statement: 'I will not invest in any company outside these industries: [list].' If you cannot describe a business's competitive dynamics in 5 minutes, it is outside your circle.
5. 5. For the News Blackout Rule: decide your trigger threshold (e.g., portfolio down > 15% from peak). Write: 'If my portfolio declines > [threshold]%, I will close all portfolio tracking for [30] days and focus only on quarterly business reviews.'
6. 6. For the Do-Nothing Default: write: 'If my watchlist contains no companies at ≥ 20% margin of safety today, the correct action is to hold cash. An idle portfolio is not a problem; it is discipline.'
7. 7. Print or save the Mindset Rules Card. Review it at the start of every Phase 5 analysis session and every time you feel compelled to make a rapid trade.

## Real-life example — Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway during the 2008–2009 financial crisis
In September 2008, when Lehman Brothers collapsed and the S&P 500 began a 57% peak-to-trough decline, Buffett published a New York Times op-ed titled 'Buy American. I Am.' He deployed $5B into Goldman Sachs preferred shares at 10% yield, $3B into GE preferred, and later $2.4B into Swiss Re. Berkshire's stock fell 48% from peak to trough — but Buffett added to positions rather than selling, directly applying the Fear/Greed Rule and the Mr. Market Rule. By the 2013 recovery, all these investments had generated exceptional returns. This behaviour was only possible because the principles were pre-committed and internalised, not adopted post-crisis.

**So what:** The mindset rules are only valuable if they are pre-committed in writing BEFORE the emotional situation arises. Deciding 'I will be greedy when others are fearful' while reading about the Buffett op-ed in hindsight is not a commitment — it is an observation. The rules must be written, signed, and reviewed before the next 2008 arrives.

## Pitfalls
- Writing the rules but not reviewing them before Phase 5 decisions — the rules only work if consulted; they are a checklist, not a poster.
- Defining the circle of competence as a broad sector ('technology') rather than a specific business model type — broad definitions permit anything, which defeats the purpose.
- Abandoning the do-nothing default because 'missing opportunities' feels uncomfortable — Buffett explicitly endorses missing opportunities outside the circle of competence as a feature, not a bug.
