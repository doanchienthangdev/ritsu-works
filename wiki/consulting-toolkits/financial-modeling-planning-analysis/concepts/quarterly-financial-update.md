# Quarterly Financial Plan Update

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Financial Modeling, Planning & Analysis*

## What it is
A structured quarterly process that locks period-end actuals, conducts a comprehensive variance analysis (actuals vs. plan and prior year), reforecasts the remainder of the year and the following year, updates initiative business cases with actual performance, and presents an updated financial plan to the Board.

**Origin:** Derived from standard quarterly earnings reporting requirements (SEC Form 10-Q for US public companies) and best practices in corporate FP&A governance. The quarterly reforecast model was popularized by rolling-forecast pioneers including Unilever and IKEA.

## Why it works
Annual plans become obsolete as market conditions change. The quarterly update is the mechanism that keeps the financial plan alive and relevant throughout the year. It also closes the governance loop: by comparing actuals to plan every quarter, management creates accountability for the commitments made in the annual budget.

## When to use
Run every quarter as the primary governance mechanism connecting the financial plan to actual performance. This is the 'heartbeat' of the FP&A function.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Close quarterly actuals: ensure books are locked by Day 10 post-quarter-end. Load actuals into the financial model. Run the automatic check: does actuals loading affect the model's balance checks?
2. Build the revenue variance bridge: actual revenue vs. plan, decomposed into volume effect, price/mix effect, new products, lost customers, FX, and one-time items. Every variance >1% of quarterly revenue must have a named root cause.
3. Build the EBITDA variance bridge: actual EBITDA vs. plan, decomposed into revenue drop-through at gross margin rate, COGS over/under, SG&A over/under, and one-time items. A one-page bridge chart communicates this more effectively than a table.
4. Reforecast the remaining quarters: update the rolling forecast model with the latest market intelligence, management calls, and updated macro assumptions. Present the updated full-year forecast with confidence range (High/Base/Low).
5. Update initiative business cases: for each active initiative, compare actual benefits to date against the business case projection. If actual benefits are >20% below plan in the first year, the initiative owner must present a revised recovery plan.
6. Prepare the Board pack: standard format (1 page exec summary, Q actuals vs. plan, updated FY outlook, initiative tracking, top 3 risks with updated mitigation). Target 10–15 slides — not 50.
7. Present to the Board with an honest assessment: present the best and worst-case scenarios, not just the Base case. The Board's job is to make informed decisions, not to approve management's preferred story.

## Real-life example — Amazon Quarterly Earnings and Internal Forecast Process
Amazon's quarterly financial update process is recognized as one of the most disciplined in corporate America. Amazon's finance team presents a GAAP vs. plan variance analysis at the quarterly earnings review, but internally the focus is on 'Input Metrics' (items per order, session conversion rate, AWS revenue growth rate per product) that predict future financial performance. The quarterly update includes: a 'look-back' analysis comparing the forecast made 90 days ago to actuals (to calibrate forecast accuracy), and a 'look-forward' with explicit Base / Upside / Downside ranges. This discipline of tracking forecast accuracy over time is what drives Amazon's consistently high Analyst EPS accuracy (typically within 5% of consensus).

**So what:** The most valuable element of the quarterly financial update is not the variance analysis (explaining what happened) but the forecast accuracy retrospective (was our 90-day-ago forecast good?). Organizations that track and publish their forecast accuracy internally create a culture of honest forecasting.

## Pitfalls
- Presenting only the Base case forecast to the Board — the Board's job is governance and strategic oversight; they need to see the range of outcomes to fulfill that role. Always present a Downside case with specific risk drivers.
- Not tracking forecast accuracy over time — if the finance team never knows how accurate its forecasts are, they cannot improve. Track MAPE quarterly; publish it internally as the finance team's 'accountability metric.'
- Spending the Board meeting on explanation of past results rather than forward-looking decisions — the variance analysis should be distributed in the Board pack before the meeting; the meeting should focus on forward-looking decisions.
