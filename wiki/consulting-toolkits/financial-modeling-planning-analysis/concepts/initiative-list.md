# Initiative Long List Generation

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Financial Modeling, Planning & Analysis*

## What it is
A structured ideation framework for generating a comprehensive long list of potential performance-improvement initiatives — organized across revenue levers (price, volume, mix, new markets), cost levers (procurement, operations, SG&A), and capital levers (working capital, CapEx optimization) — that will close the gap between current financial performance and the strategic financial targets.

**Origin:** The 'growth levers' and 'cost lever' taxonomy is standard in McKinsey and BCG strategy engagements for financial planning. The structured brainstorming approach is rooted in SCAMPER and TRIZ innovation frameworks, adapted for corporate financial planning contexts.

## Why it works
Most organizations approach financial planning with 'what can we do that we haven't done before?' — a question that biases answers toward novelty and pet projects. The long list framework provides a disciplined taxonomy of ALL possible levers (revenue, cost, capital), ensuring no high-impact opportunity is systematically missed. The taxonomy forces completeness; the subsequent prioritization (initiative-prioritization framework) forces selectivity.

## When to use
Use in Phase 3, Step 5 of the financial planning process to generate the raw material for the initiative portfolio. Also use any time the financial plan has a gap between the Base case projection and the strategic financial targets that must be closed by new initiatives.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Start with the Phase 2 financial analysis findings: identify the 3–5 largest performance gaps (e.g., 'EBITDA margin is 6pp below top-quartile peers; DSO is 15 days above median; revenue growth is below market growth rate'). These gaps are the initiative generation brief.
2. Run a structured brainstorming session: 2–3 hours with the CFO, FP&A team, BU heads, and strategy team. Use the Revenue / Cost / Capital lever taxonomy as the agenda. Appoint a facilitator and a scribe. Goal: generate 30–50 initiative ideas — quantity over quality at this stage.
3. For each lever category, generate ideas using the question 'What could we do to improve [metric] by 20% in the next 3 years?' Apply both inside-out (internal capability improvements) and outside-in (what are peers doing that we are not?) perspectives.
4. Document each initiative on a standard template (2-minute description): initiative name, lever category, target metric, rough impact estimate ($M), required resources, and implementation timeline.
5. Apply a quick 3-tier impact screen: High (>$25M NPV), Medium ($5–25M), Low (<$5M). Remove the Low items from the list unless they are very low-effort (Quick Win candidates). This reduces the long list to a manageable short list of 15–20 initiatives for business case development.
6. Validate with subject matter experts: for each short-listed initiative, review the rough impact estimate with the relevant BU head or functional expert. Revise estimates based on their input before investing in full business case development.

## Real-life example — Nestlé 'Nestlé for Healthier Kids' initiative generation (2016)
When Nestlé CFO François-Xavier Roger participated in the 2016 strategic financial planning cycle, the initiative long list for improving Nestlé's 15%+ trading operating profit margin target included: Revenue levers — portfolio premiumization (shift mix to Nespresso, KitKat premium, Gerber organics); Cost levers — zero-based budgeting for indirect spend, Maggi production optimization, procurement consolidation across regions; Capital levers — divestiture of non-core segments (U.S. confectionery divested to Ferrero for $2.8B in 2018, Nestlé Skin Health divested for $10.2B in 2019). The long list generated 60+ ideas; 8 were selected as Major Projects after prioritization. The capital lever initiatives (divestitures) turned out to be the highest-impact, producing $13B+ in proceeds reinvested into margin-accretive categories.

**So what:** Capital lever initiatives (divestitures, asset monetization) are systematically underrepresented in initiative long lists because they require the most organizational courage. Ensure the brainstorming explicitly covers 'what should we stop doing or sell?' — not just 'what should we start doing?'

## Pitfalls
- Generating only cost-cutting initiatives — cost reduction is easier to quantify but may undermine future revenue growth; ensure the long list has a balanced mix of revenue, cost, and capital levers.
- Anchoring on last year's initiatives — 'we ran procurement consolidation last year, so we can run it again' produces diminishing returns. Use the benchmarking analysis to generate outside-in ideas that are new to the organization.
- Assigning initiative generation to the finance team alone — the best ideas come from people closest to customers and operations (BU heads, sales leaders, operations managers); finance's role is to facilitate, not to generate.
