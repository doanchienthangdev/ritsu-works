# Financial Governance Structure

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Financial Modeling, Planning & Analysis*

## What it is
A set of decision rights, governance bodies, meeting cadences, RACI matrices, and close calendars that create explicit financial accountability — defining who decides what, when they report, and how financial performance is reviewed and acted upon.

**Origin:** Financial governance formalized in the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (2002) post-Enron/WorldCom scandals, requiring documented internal controls for financial reporting. COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework (2004) extended governance to risk management. The FP&A governance model (monthly Finance Committee, quarterly Board review) is standard corporate governance practice.

## Why it works
The best financial plan delivers zero value if there is no governance structure to hold the organization accountable to executing it. Financial governance creates the cadence, authority, and consequence structure that turns a financial plan from a document into a commitment.

## When to use
Use at the start of Phase 4 (Implement, Track & Manage) to establish the governance structure before the financial plan goes live. Also use when joining a new organization as CFO or FP&A Director to assess and strengthen the existing governance structure.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the governance bodies: Finance Committee (CFO + BU Heads, monthly), Operating Committee (CFO + CEO + COO, monthly or bi-weekly), Audit Committee (Board sub-committee, quarterly), and Full Board (quarterly).
2. Define the RACI for financial decisions: Who Decides (D), Who is Accountable (A), Who is Consulted (C), Who is Informed (I) for key financial decisions: CapEx approval, budget changes, initiative launch/stop, debt refinancing.
3. Build the FP&A close calendar: a month-by-month schedule showing exactly: which day data is due from BUs, which day consolidation is complete, which day the management pack is distributed, and which day the Finance Committee meets. Every stakeholder must know and commit to this calendar.
4. Define variance escalation protocols: what level of variance triggers which governance action? Suggested: >5% revenue variance vs. plan → Finance Committee discussion; >10% revenue variance → Operating Committee and CEO notification; >20% → Board notification.
5. Define the financial authority matrix: which financial decisions require which approval level? Suggested: CapEx <$100K = FP&A approval; $100K–$1M = CFO approval; $1M–$10M = CEO approval; >$10M = Board approval.
6. Establish the quarterly Board financial pack structure: Executive Summary (1 page) + Actuals vs. Plan + YTD and FY Forecast + Scenario Analysis + Initiative Tracking + Risks and Mitigations. Standardize the format — the Board should see the same layout every quarter.

## Real-life example — General Mills FP&A governance model
General Mills operates a structured FP&A governance model recognized as best-in-class by the Hackett Group. Monthly: each of the 4 operating segments (North America Retail, International, Pet, Foodservice) submits actuals by Day 5; consolidated group actuals distributed Day 8; Finance Committee meets Day 10 with CFO + all 4 Segment CFOs; action items issued by Day 12. Quarterly: full Board financial review with updated FY outlook and 2-year scenarios. Annually: 3-year strategic financial plan updated and approved in November for the following fiscal year. The result: General Mills' forecast accuracy (MAPE) consistently ranks in the top quartile of the Hackett benchmark at <3% annual forecast error.

**So what:** Governance quality is the single most underinvested area in FP&A. Most organizations have the models; few have the meeting cadence, the close calendar discipline, and the variance escalation protocols that make financial accountability real.

## Pitfalls
- Creating governance meetings without authority — if the Finance Committee meets monthly but has no authority to require action from BU heads, it is a reporting exercise, not governance. Every governance body must have explicit decision rights.
- Letting the close calendar slip — if the Finance Committee is on Day 12 but BU data is due Day 8 and actuals are never ready before Day 15, the governance structure is theoretical. Enforce the close calendar with consequences.
- Making financial governance a Finance-only activity — financial performance is owned by BU heads, not the CFO. The governance structure must include BU heads as accountable parties, not just informed participants.
