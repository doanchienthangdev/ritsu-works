# Monthly Budget vs. Actuals Tracker

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Business Plan & Entrepreneurship*

## What it is
A structured monthly financial tracking tool that compares all planned revenue and expense line items against actual results, calculates variances, colour-codes them by severity, and maintains a rolling cash runway projection.

**Origin:** Standard management accounting tool; embedded in every CFO's monthly close process. Operationalises the 'plan vs. actual' discipline from management accounting (variance analysis) in a startup-accessible format.

## Why it works
Without a structured budget vs. actuals process, founders either over-spend (discovering the problem when they run out of cash) or under-invest (hoarding cash and missing growth opportunities). The monthly tracker creates a forcing function: every variance must be explained, and every explanation must point to a corrective action or a model update. The rolling cash runway projection adds the critical temporal dimension — not just 'are we on budget' but 'how long do we have?'

## When to use
Phase 5 (every month from launch onwards) and phase 7 (foundation of the monthly financial analysis). The budget tracker is the most important financial discipline for startup founders who are not finance professionals.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. At the start of each month, populate the Budget column from the financial model. Never modify the Budget column during the month — it is a fixed reference point.
2. At month-end (within 5 business days of month close), populate the Actual column from the accounting system (QuickBooks, Xero, or equivalent). Do not estimate — use actual invoiced/paid figures.
3. Calculate variance: Variance ($) = Actual – Budget. For revenue, a positive variance is favourable (actual > budget); for expenses, a negative variance is favourable (actual < budget). Calculate Variance (%) = Variance / Budget × 100.
4. Apply status codes: 🟢 ≤±5%, 🟡 ±5–15%, 🔴 >±15%. For any 🔴 line, the owner must document the root cause and corrective action within 24 hours.
5. Update the cash position section: Opening Cash + Net Cash from Operations + Net Cash from Investing + Net Cash from Financing = Closing Cash. Calculate burn rate (monthly net cash outflow). Calculate runway = Closing Cash / Burn Rate.
6. Alert the founding team immediately if runway falls below 3 months — this is a cash emergency. Target to always maintain ≥12 months runway and to raise the next round when runway is 12–18 months (not 3–6).
7. Conduct a monthly budget review meeting (60 minutes): review all 🔴 lines, agree corrective actions, update the rolling 3-month forecast. Update the annual financial model if the monthly actuals reveal a structural deviation from the plan.

## Real-life example — Athena Learning Technologies (Month 6)
Athena's Month 6 budget vs. actuals: Revenue budget $37K, actual $28K (🔴 -24%). Root cause: two enterprise deals delayed by 6 weeks due to procurement review — identified via the pipeline tracker. Corrective action: add a 'procurement estimate' step to the sales process to identify deal risk 8 weeks before close. Salary budget $30K, actual $29K (🟢). Marketing budget $10K, actual $14K (🟡 +40%). Root cause: launched a LinkedIn campaign that was performing at 2× the planned CAC. Corrective action: pause LinkedIn campaign, redirect to outbound SDR channel with 40% lower CAC. Burn rate: $85K. Cash balance: $1.96M. Runway: 23 months. Action: no immediate cash concern, but revenue miss must be addressed within 90 days.

**So what:** The budget tracker's value in this example is separating a revenue timing issue (solvable) from a structural miss (alarming). The root cause analysis revealed a manageable problem — procurement delays are predictable and can be planned for. Without the tracker, the founding team might have overreacted (slash marketing, reduce headcount) or underreacted (assume next month will fix itself).

## Pitfalls
- Modifying the Budget column mid-month to make actuals look better. The Budget is fixed at month start — the whole point is to measure actual vs. plan.
- Preparing the tracker more than 5 business days after month-end. Delayed financial information loses its corrective value — the team has already made the next month's decisions without it.
- Treating cash runway as a static number. Runway must be recalculated every month using actual burn rate, not the plan burn rate.
