# Value Proposition Canvas

*Category: business-model · Toolkit: Business Plan & Entrepreneurship*

## What it is
A companion tool to the Business Model Canvas that zooms into the fit between a specific Customer Segment (their jobs, pains, and gains) and a specific Value Proposition (products/services, pain relievers, and gain creators), ensuring design is driven by real customer needs.

**Origin:** Developed by Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Greg Bernarda, and Alan Smith, documented in 'Value Proposition Design' (2014). Extends the BMC by providing a structured process for achieving product-market fit.

## Why it works
Most product failures trace to a mismatch between what the company builds and what the customer actually needs. The VPC prevents this by forcing designers to map the customer profile in detail before designing the solution, then explicitly connecting each product feature to a specific job, pain, or gain — making the design logic visible and testable.

## When to use
Phase 2 (design value proposition), phase 3 (validate customer profile), phase 4 (write problem/solution and marketing plan). Also use for any major feature addition decision.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Select one Customer Segment from the BMC. Work one segment at a time — the canvas is segment-specific.
2. Complete the Customer Profile (right, circle) BEFORE the Value Map. Start with Customer Jobs: list all functional, social, and emotional jobs the customer is trying to do. Be specific.
3. List Customer Pains: frustrations, risks, and obstacles the customer faces when doing those jobs. Rank by severity.
4. List Customer Gains: outcomes and benefits the customer desires. Rank by importance.
5. Complete the Value Map (left, square): list Products and Services offered. Then write Pain Relievers — exactly how your offering reduces the top 3 pains (be specific, not generic). Then write Gain Creators — exactly how your offering produces the top 3 gains.
6. Check for fit: draw arrows from each Pain Reliever to the Pain it addresses and from each Gain Creator to the Gain it produces. Gaps = product opportunities or over-engineering.
7. Write the Value Proposition Statement: 'We help [segment] who want [top job] by [solution] unlike [alternative] because [key differentiator].'

## Real-life example — Slack
Slack's VPC for software engineering teams: Customer Jobs (coordinate distributed work, find information shared last week, reduce email overload). Top Pains (email is asynchronous and noisy, information buried in threads, switching between 10 tools). Top Gains (real-time contextual conversation, searchable history, integrations with existing tools). Slack's Value Map: channel-based conversations (Pain 1), persistent searchable history (Pain 2), 1,500+ integrations (Pain 3). Near-perfect fit — which is why adoption was viral among engineering teams from launch.

**So what:** Slack's success was a function of how precisely the value proposition matched the most painful aspects of the customer job. The VPC — filled with real interview data — would have predicted this fit before a single line of code was written.

## Template
Complete Customer Profile BEFORE Value Map. Base the Customer Profile on real interview data, not assumptions. One canvas per segment.

- [ ] Customer Segment: ___
- [ ] Customer Jobs (rank top 3): Job 1: ___ | Job 2: ___ | Job 3: ___
- [ ] Customer Pains (rank top 3): Pain 1: ___ | Pain 2: ___ | Pain 3: ___
- [ ] Customer Gains (rank top 3): Gain 1: ___ | Gain 2: ___ | Gain 3: ___
- [ ] Products and Services: ___
- [ ] Pain Reliever 1 (→ Pain 1): ___ | Pain Reliever 2: ___ | Pain Reliever 3: ___
- [ ] Gain Creator 1 (→ Gain 1): ___ | Gain Creator 2: ___ | Gain Creator 3: ___
- [ ] Fit Check: Unaddressed Pains: ___ | Unaddressed Gains: ___
- [ ] VP Statement: 'We help [segment] who want [job] by [solution] unlike [alternative] because [differentiator].'

## Pitfalls
- Completing the Value Map before the Customer Profile — leads to rationalising a pre-conceived solution.
- Using assumptions rather than real customer interview data for the Customer Profile.
- Claiming 'fit' with only partial coverage. True fit means top pain relievers address top pains, not minor ones.
