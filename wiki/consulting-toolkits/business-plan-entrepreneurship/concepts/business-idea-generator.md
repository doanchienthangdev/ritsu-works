# Business Idea Generator

*Category: ideation · Toolkit: Business Plan & Entrepreneurship*

## What it is
A structured ideation protocol that uses four strategic lenses — personal experience, market disruption, jobs-to-be-done, and capability adjacency — to systematically generate a large pool of business concepts before any evaluation occurs.

**Origin:** Synthesised from the entrepreneurship literature; popularised in business-school curricula at Harvard, Wharton, and INSEAD in the 2000s and refined in the Domont Consulting entrepreneurship toolkit.

## Why it works
Creativity research (Amabile, 1996; Dyer, Gregersen & Christensen, 2011) shows that the quality of the best idea in a set correlates strongly with the size of the set. The four lenses force the founder to explore idea space from orthogonal starting points, making it harder to anchor on the first concept that comes to mind. SCAMPER prompts (applied within each lens) leverage combinatorial thinking — the insight that most breakthrough ideas are recombinations of existing elements rather than true novelties.

## When to use
At the very start of a venture, before any commitment of capital or time to a specific idea. Also effective for corporate innovators evaluating new business lines, and for entrepreneurs whose current venture is failing and who need to pivot.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Block 2 hours of uninterrupted time. Gather the founding team (2–4 people max for ideation quality).
2. Write down each of the four lenses on separate flip-chart sheets: Personal Experience, Market Disruption, Jobs-to-Be-Done, Capability Adjacency.
3. For each lens, set a 15-minute timer and generate raw ideas silently (no discussion, no judgement). Each person writes ideas on sticky notes — one idea per note. Target ≥5 ideas per person per lens.
4. After all four lenses, apply SCAMPER prompts to the pool: pick an idea and ask Substitute (what could be substituted?), Combine (what if we merged two ideas?), Adapt (what from another industry could be adapted here?), Modify (what could be scaled up or down?), Put to other use (what other problems could this solve?), Eliminate (what if we removed the most costly component?), Reverse (what if we did the opposite of the current product?).
5. Consolidate all ideas into a single idea log spreadsheet. De-duplicate and group similar ideas. Each entry should have: a one-sentence problem statement and a one-sentence proposed solution.
6. Apply the knockout filter: eliminate any idea that fails on capital access, legal/regulatory feasibility, or founder domain knowledge (<30%). This typically reduces the list by 30–50%.
7. Carry the remaining ideas forward to the Business Idea Prioritisation Matrix.

## Real-life example — Airbnb
Brian Chesky and Joe Gebbia generated the 'rent your home as accommodation' idea via the Personal Experience lens — they personally could not afford their San Francisco rent and needed to monetise their spare room. The SCAMPER 'Adapt' prompt (adapting the hotel industry model to peer-to-peer) helped them move from 'charge guests to sleep on an air mattress' to 'a marketplace for short-term accommodation globally.' The Capability Adjacency lens surfaced their design school background as an unfair advantage in creating a trust-building, design-forward platform.

**So what:** The most transformative ideas often start from a very personal experience of a problem, combined with a capability the founders already own. The Business Idea Generator forces exploration of both, reducing the risk of anchoring on an intellectually interesting idea that the founders have no genuine advantage in executing.

## Template
Complete the four lens sections with a minimum of 10 raw ideas each. Apply SCAMPER to the top 5 ideas from each lens. Record every idea in the idea log — no filtering until the knockout step.

- [ ] Lens 1 — Personal Experience: List ≥10 problems you personally experience with no good solution: ___
- [ ] Lens 2 — Market Disruption: List ≥5 industries being disrupted and an idea for each: ___
- [ ] Lens 3 — Jobs-to-Be-Done: List ≥10 'jobs' customers do badly today: ___
- [ ] Lens 4 — Capability Adjacency: List your top 5 unfair advantages and one idea each: ___
- [ ] SCAMPER Output: For each of your top 10 ideas, apply 1–2 SCAMPER prompts: ___
- [ ] Idea Log (after consolidation): Idea | Problem | Proposed Solution | Pass Knockout? (Y/N): ___

## Pitfalls
- Evaluating ideas during generation — the moment someone says 'that won't work,' creative output drops by 50%. Enforce a strict 'no judgement during ideation' rule.
- Stopping too early — founders who generate only 5–10 ideas tend to anchor on the first one that sounds plausible. Target ≥20 ideas before any evaluation.
- Ignoring the personal experience lens — ideas in domains the founder does not personally understand tend to fail more often because the founder cannot distinguish a real customer problem from a perceived one.
