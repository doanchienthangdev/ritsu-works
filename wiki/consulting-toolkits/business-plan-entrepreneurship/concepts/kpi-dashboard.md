# KPI Dashboard (Key Performance Indicators)

*Category: operations · Toolkit: Business Plan & Entrepreneurship*

## What it is
A single-page performance monitoring system that displays the company's most critical metrics — actual vs. target — providing the founding team and board with a real-time health signal and the basis for weekly management decisions.

**Origin:** Adapted from the Balanced Scorecard concept (Kaplan and Norton, 1992) and David Skok's SaaS metrics work (2012, forEntrepreneurs.com), combined with Dave McClure's AARRR framework and Andreessen Horowitz's startup metrics publications.

## Why it works
Startups that track too many metrics lose focus; startups that track too few miss early warning signals. An 8–12 KPI dashboard forces the team to agree on the 'vital few' metrics that most predict future performance, assigns ownership, and surfaces variance before it becomes crisis. The dashboard's value is not the data itself — it is the conversation it forces about what the data means.

## When to use
Phase 5 (begin before launch, so baselines are established at go-live). Maintain throughout the life of the business. Also use as the primary exhibit in monthly investor updates and board packs.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Select 8–12 KPIs covering all five AARRR stages plus financial health. More than 12 KPIs is noise; fewer than 8 leaves blind spots.
2. For each KPI, complete a definition card: (a) Metric name (specific and unambiguous), (b) Definition (exactly how it is calculated), (c) Data source (exactly where the data comes from — named tool or report), (d) Owner (named person, not a team), (e) Target value (from the financial model or industry benchmark), (f) Reporting cadence (weekly, monthly, or real-time).
3. Build the dashboard in a single-page format: the founding team should be able to assess company health in 5 minutes. Use colour coding: 🟢 ≤5% variance from target, 🟡 5–15% variance, 🔴 >15% variance.
4. Review the dashboard in the weekly all-hands (30 minutes max). For every 🔴 KPI, the owner must have a root cause hypothesis and a proposed corrective action ready before the meeting.
5. Review the dashboard with investors/board monthly (include in the monthly investor update). For any 🔴 metric, proactively explain the root cause and corrective action — do not wait for investors to ask.
6. Update the KPIs quarterly: as the business matures, some early-stage metrics (e.g., 'days until first paying customer') become irrelevant; replace with more sophisticated metrics (e.g., NRR, payback period).
7. Automate data collection for all KPIs where possible. Manual data collection introduces delays and errors that make the dashboard less useful.

## Real-life example — HubSpot (early days, 2007–2009)
HubSpot's early KPI dashboard focused on 8 metrics: Monthly Unique Visitors (acquisition), Trial Sign-up Rate (activation), Day-30 Trial Conversion (activation), Monthly Churn Rate (retention), MRR (revenue), CAC (revenue efficiency), LTV (revenue sustainability), and Cash Runway (health). These 8 metrics gave the founding team and investors a complete health signal in one glance. When trial conversion dropped from 12% to 8% in Q3 2008, the dashboard flagged it as 🔴 within 30 days — triggering a product investigation that revealed a UI friction point in the trial onboarding. Fixed in 6 weeks; trial conversion returned to 13%.

**So what:** The KPI dashboard's most important feature is speed of signal: the faster a problem surfaces, the more options the team has to fix it. HubSpot detected and fixed a conversion problem in 6 weeks because the dashboard was reviewed weekly. Without it, the same problem might have gone undetected for a quarter, costing 3× as much in lost MRR.

## Pitfalls
- Tracking vanity metrics (social followers, app downloads, total registered users) instead of engagement and revenue metrics. Vanity metrics look good; actionable metrics drive decisions.
- Having metrics with no named owner — unowned metrics never improve.
- Reviewing the dashboard quarterly instead of weekly. By the time a quarterly review surfaces a problem, 3 months of damage has already occurred.
