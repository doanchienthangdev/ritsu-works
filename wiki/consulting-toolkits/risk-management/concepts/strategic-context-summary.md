# Strategic Context Summary (Strategy-on-a-Page)

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Risk Management*

## What it is
A concise one-page synthesis of the organization's current strategic objectives, key initiatives, competitive context, and major constraints, serving as the foundational document that anchors the risk management function to business value creation rather than compliance.

**Origin:** The 'strategy-on-a-page' format originates in McKinsey's approach to rapid strategic alignment — the discipline of compressing complex strategic plans into a single-page artifact that any manager can reference in any meeting. Applied to risk management, it provides the 'context-setting' step mandated by ISO 31000 as the foundation of the risk management process.

## Why it works
A risk management program that is not explicitly anchored to strategic objectives is a compliance program, not a risk management program. The strategic context summary creates the link: every risk in the register must connect to at least one objective in the summary. This prevents the common failure mode of comprehensive risk registers full of risks that have no relationship to the organization's actual priorities — 'compliance with obscure regulation X' catalogued alongside 'failure to deliver the €500M revenue plan.'

## When to use
Phase 1 (Risk Management Strategy), Step 1. Must be complete before any other Phase 1 activities begin. Also use as a reference document in every risk identification workshop and as the cover page for the Board Risk Management Strategy presentation.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Interview the CEO and 2–3 executive committee members (30 minutes each) using the question: 'What are the top 3 things that could prevent us from achieving our most important strategic objective this year?'
2. Extract the strategic objectives from the most recent strategic plan. If more than 6 objectives exist, apply the prioritization question: 'If you could only achieve 3 of these, which would you choose?' This reveals the real priority order.
3. Populate the competitive context using publicly available analysis (annual reports of top 3 competitors, industry analyst reports) — 2 hours of desk research is sufficient for a strategy-on-a-page.
4. Draft the one-page summary and validate with the CRO and CEO before distribution. The strategic context summary should fit on a single A4 page — if it doesn't, it is not strategic enough.
5. Use the strategic objectives list as the primary input for risk identification workshop facilitation. Every workshop must begin: 'The organization is trying to achieve [objectives]. What could prevent this?'
6. Review and update annually (minimum) and whenever the strategic plan is updated. The risk register and appetite statement must be re-validated against the updated strategic context.

## Real-life example — Tesla, Inc.
Tesla's strategic context for its risk management program centers on 3 objectives: (1) Achieve 2M+ vehicle deliveries per year at >15% automotive gross margin; (2) Scale energy storage to 100+ GWh per year; (3) Deploy FSD robotaxi service at commercial scale. The key constraints include: semiconductor supply chain dependency, regulatory approval timelines for autonomous driving, and production ramp pace at Gigafactories. Tesla's risk register prioritization directly reflects this context: supply chain risks (especially semiconductor), regulatory risks (FSD approval), and production execution risks are all High-priority — aligned to the strategic constraints. Risks not connected to these objectives (e.g., minor marketing compliance issues) remain in the register but receive Amber or Green classification.

**So what:** Tesla's strategic context makes risk prioritization straightforward: any risk that could prevent delivery of 2M vehicles, scale energy storage, or launch FSD is High priority by definition. Without this context, a risk assessor would have to score semiconductor supply risk and marketing compliance risk on the same generic scale — producing misleading prioritization.

## Template
Complete this one-page template as the first deliverable of Phase 1. Validate with the CEO and CRO before using it as the foundation for risk identification workshops.

- [ ] Organization name and reporting date
- [ ] Mission (1 sentence: why we exist)
- [ ] Vision (3-year horizon: 3 bullet points describing success)
- [ ] Strategic Objectives (3–6 objectives, each with: objective statement, measurable target, target year, accountable executive)
- [ ] Key Strategic Initiatives (list of major programs delivering the objectives, with estimated investment and completion date)
- [ ] Competitive Context: top 3 market dynamics; top 2 competitor threats
- [ ] Key Constraints: capital (budget constraints), talent (capability gaps), regulatory (licence or compliance boundaries), technology (platform or infrastructure limits)
- [ ] Risk Implication (for each strategic objective): 'The biggest single risk to this objective is...' — to be validated and elaborated in the risk identification workshops

## Pitfalls
- Creating a strategic context summary that mirrors the full strategic plan in miniature — a 10-page 'one-pager' defeats the purpose. Counter: enforce the one-page constraint ruthlessly. If prioritization is needed, ask 'Which 3 strategic objectives are the Board most worried about?' and anchor the risk program on those.
- Updating the risk register without updating the strategic context — if the strategy changes (new acquisition, new product, new market) but the context summary isn't updated, the risk register will be assessing risks against outdated objectives. Counter: link the strategic context review to the annual strategic planning cycle.
