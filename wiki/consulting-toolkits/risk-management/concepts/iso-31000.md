# ISO 31000:2018 Risk Management Guidelines

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Risk Management*

## What it is
The international standard that defines the principles, framework, and process for managing risk in any organization. It provides a universal, non-prescriptive architecture that can be adapted to any sector, size, or context.

**Origin:** International Organization for Standardization (ISO), first published 2009, updated 2018. Developed by a global committee of risk practitioners from 57 countries.

## Why it works
ISO 31000 is built on the insight that risk management is only effective when it is integrated into the organization's governance, strategy, planning, and operations — not siloed in a compliance function. Its three-tier architecture (Principles → Framework → Process) ensures the 'why' (principles), the 'how to organize' (framework), and the 'what to do operationally' (process) are all addressed. The process component — Context → Identification → Analysis → Evaluation → Treatment → Monitoring → Communication — is the definitive representation of risk management as a cycle, not a one-time exercise.

## When to use
Use as the governing framework for all enterprise risk management work. Reference ISO 31000 when designing the risk management system, when seeking international alignment (especially for multinationals), or when regulatory requirements reference it (e.g., Australian/NZ government procurement, EU risk governance standards).

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Map the organization's strategic objectives to the ISO 31000 'context' step — what are we trying to achieve and what would prevent us?
2. Conduct a structured risk identification exercise (workshops, interviews, PESTLE, process reviews) to populate the raw risk inventory.
3. Analyze each identified risk: determine the likelihood of occurrence and the consequence across all relevant impact dimensions.
4. Evaluate: compare the analysis output against your Risk Appetite Statement to classify risks as tolerable, manageable, or intolerable.
5. Design and implement risk treatment plans (the 4Ts: Tolerate, Treat, Transfer, Terminate) for all risks exceeding appetite.
6. Establish monitoring and review rhythms: weekly for High risks, monthly for Medium, quarterly for Low. Track residual risk score trends.
7. Maintain recording and reporting: update the risk register after every review; report to governance bodies per the stakeholder engagement plan.
8. Run communication and consultation continuously throughout: no stage of ISO 31000 is a closed-room exercise — stakeholders surface the risks you cannot see from the inside.

## Real-life example — Deutsche Telekom AG
Deutsche Telekom adopted ISO 31000 as the governing framework for its Group-wide Integrated Risk Management system. The company maps all risks through the ISO 31000 process cycle — from strategic risks in the Group Executive Committee's risk appetite discussion down to operational risks captured in business unit risk registers — and reports to the Board's Audit Committee using the ISO-aligned heat map and risk report structure. The company uses the ISO 31000 Communication and Consultation principle to mandate that all 220,000 employees complete annual risk awareness training.

**So what:** ISO 31000 gives a multinational the common language and process architecture to aggregate risks from 50+ subsidiaries into a coherent Board-level view without forcing a one-size-fits-all scoring tool.

## Pitfalls
- Treating ISO 31000 as a compliance checkbox rather than an operating discipline — the standard explicitly warns that certification is not its purpose. Counter: embed the process cycle into operational cadences (team meetings, project reviews) not just governance meetings.
- Skipping the 'context' step and jumping straight to identification — without knowing what objectives are at stake, risk identification produces a generic list, not a business-relevant one. Counter: always start with the strategic objectives map.
- Using ISO 31000 as a framework alone without adding the practical tools (heat map, register, dashboard) it deliberately leaves unspecified. Counter: supplement ISO 31000 with the specific instruments in this toolkit.
