# Risk Management KPI Scorecard

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Risk Management*

## What it is
A structured performance measurement system that defines 4–8 measurable strategic objectives and 1–2 KPIs per objective for the risk management function, enabling the CRO to demonstrate that the function is performing effectively and improving over time.

**Origin:** The Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton, 1992) applied to functional performance management. The adaptation for risk management functions was developed in the 2000s by consulting firms and governance professionals, reflecting the recognition that risk management functions were increasingly being asked to demonstrate their value beyond 'we have a risk register.'

## Why it works
Without a KPI scorecard, the risk function's performance is evaluated subjectively ('the Board thinks the risk reporting is good'). With a scorecard, it is evaluated against specific, pre-agreed measures that demonstrate whether the function is achieving its objectives. The most powerful KPIs for risk management are leading indicators (number of risks surfaced proactively before they materialize) rather than lagging indicators (number of incidents occurred) — because leading indicators create an incentive to surface risks early, which is exactly the culture the function is trying to build.

## When to use
Phase 1 (Risk Management Strategy), Step 5. Completed after the Mission/Vision/Values document establishes the aspirational state. Updated monthly (operational) and annually (strategic review).

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Start with the risk management Vision (from the Mission/Vision/Values document). Each vision attribute should have at least one KPI that measures progress toward it.
2. Define 4–8 strategic objectives for the risk function. Use the SMART test: each objective must have a measurable target and a timeline. 'Improve risk culture' is not a strategic objective; 'Achieve Level 4 maturity on the Culture domain by Year 3' is.
3. For each objective, define 1–2 KPIs: one leading indicator (a measure of activity or behavior that predicts future outcomes, e.g., number of risks surfaced proactively) and one lagging indicator (a measure of outcomes, e.g., number of High risks within appetite). Leading indicators are more manageable; lagging indicators are more credible.
4. Set baseline values for each KPI before setting targets. Baseline collection may require a one-time data gathering exercise (e.g., surveying employees on risk reporting behavior).
5. Set targets collaboratively with the CRO and 2–3 business unit leaders. Targets that are set only by the risk function are never owned by the business; targets set collaboratively are more likely to be acted on.
6. Assign an owner for each KPI — the person responsible for collecting the data, reporting it, and taking corrective action when the KPI is off-track.
7. Review the scorecard monthly (operational KPIs), quarterly (strategic KPIs), and annually (maturity KPIs). Present the scorecard to the Board annually as evidence of risk function performance.
8. After 12 months, re-evaluate the KPI set: which KPIs drove the most useful management conversations? Which proved impossible to measure? Replace unmeasurable KPIs with better proxies.

## Real-life example — Standard Chartered Bank
Standard Chartered's Group Risk function presents a Risk Function Performance Scorecard to the Board Risk Committee annually, covering: Risk Capability Maturity (target Level 4 by Year 3; current: 2.8 average); Residual Risk within Appetite (target 100% of Principal Risks; current: 87%); Audit Findings on ERM Design (target 0 major findings; current: 1); Regulatory Relationship Quality (target 'Adequate' rating from all primary regulators; current: 'Adequate' in all 5 major jurisdictions); and Employee Risk Awareness (target 85% training completion; current: 91%). The scorecard is publicly disclosed in the Annual Report as part of the Bank's corporate governance disclosure, creating external accountability.

**So what:** Standard Chartered's public disclosure of its risk function KPI scorecard transforms an internal management tool into an external governance signal. The metric 'Regulatory Relationship Quality: Adequate in all 5 jurisdictions' is a direct measure of the function's effectiveness in the eyes of the regulators who matter most.

## Template
Define 4–8 strategic objectives for the risk management function. For each objective, define 1–2 KPIs with baseline, target, RAG status, frequency, and owner. Review monthly/quarterly/annually.

- [ ] Strategic Objective (SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound)
- [ ] KPI name and definition (how is it measured? what data source?)
- [ ] KPI type: Leading Indicator / Lagging Indicator
- [ ] Baseline value (current state, measurement date)
- [ ] Target value and target date
- [ ] Current value (most recent measurement)
- [ ] RAG status (Green=on/ahead of target, Amber=slightly behind, Red=significantly behind)
- [ ] Reporting frequency (Monthly / Quarterly / Annual)
- [ ] Owner (individual responsible for data collection and corrective action)
- [ ] Trend (improving / stable / deteriorating vs. prior period)

## Pitfalls
- KPIs that measure activity rather than outcomes — 'number of risk reports produced' is an activity KPI; 'Board satisfaction with risk reporting' is an outcome KPI. Counter: for every KPI, ask 'So what?' If the KPI goes up, does the organization become safer or better governed? If the answer requires another step of logic, the KPI is measuring activity, not outcome.
- Too many KPIs obscuring the critical ones — a scorecard with 20 KPIs has no priorities. Counter: a maximum of 8 KPIs on the strategic scorecard. Operational KPIs (e.g., action completion rates) can be tracked separately without appearing on the Board scorecard.
