# 12-Month Stakeholder Engagement Plan

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Risk Management*

## What it is
A detailed 12-month action tracker that translates the Stakeholder Engagement Strategy into specific, dated activities with named owners, enabling the risk function to manage engagement as a planned operational program rather than an ad-hoc communication activity.

**Origin:** 12-month engagement planning as a management discipline derives from project communications management (PMI PMBOK §10) and was adapted for risk management by consulting firms and governance professionals. The format mirrors the corporate communications calendar used by Investor Relations and Public Affairs functions.

## Why it works
The gap between strategy and execution in stakeholder engagement is well-documented: organizations design comprehensive engagement strategies that are never implemented because the individual activities are not calendared, owned, or tracked. The 12-month plan closes this gap by converting strategy decisions ('we will hold quarterly Board risk presentations') into calendar commitments ('Board Risk Committee presentation: 15 March, 15 June, 14 September, 7 December — CRO to prepare draft 10 days in advance'). The plan also serves as a feedback tracker: each engagement is a source of new risk information that must be captured and fed back into the risk register.

## When to use
Phase 7 (Stakeholder Engagement), Step 3. Built after the Stakeholder Engagement Strategy is approved. Refreshed annually, updated quarterly. Also triggers a mid-year review if there is a material change in the stakeholder landscape.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Take the Stakeholder Engagement Strategy as the input. For each stakeholder × channel × frequency combination, create one or more plan entries.
2. Populate specific dates for every planned engagement. For Board-level engagements: align to the Board's published calendar; book preparation time (typically 10–15 days before the meeting) as a milestone in the plan.
3. Assign named owners for each activity. The owner is responsible for: scheduling (confirming the date with the stakeholder's secretariat), preparation (producing the relevant risk artifact), delivery (conducting the engagement), and follow-up (capturing feedback and updating the risk register).
4. Add preparation milestones: for each engagement, add a milestone 10 business days earlier: 'Draft [artifact] complete; circulate for review.' This prevents last-minute preparation failures.
5. Create a feedback capture process: after every engagement in the 'Manage Closely' and 'Keep Informed' categories, the owner completes a 5-minute feedback log: 'What new risks were surfaced? What concerns did the stakeholder raise? What action is required in the risk register?'
6. Review the plan quarterly: mark completed activities, capture reasons for any delays, and adjust forward-looking dates based on calendar changes.
7. At the end of the year, evaluate the plan's effectiveness: what % of planned engagements were completed on schedule? What was the quality of feedback received? What new risks were surfaced via stakeholder engagement? Use these metrics to improve the plan for the next year.

## Real-life example — Royal Dutch Shell
Shell's Group Risk function operates a 12-month Risk Communication Plan that aligns to Shell's annual business planning cycle. The plan includes: quarterly Risk Committee presentations to the Board (preparation begins 15 days before each meeting); monthly Risk Dashboard delivery to the Executive Committee; bi-annual 'risk deep-dive' sessions with business unit leadership (focusing on the top-3 risks per business unit); annual 'Risk Awareness Week' for all 85,000+ employees (online training module + leadership messages); and ad-hoc regulatory engagement protocols triggered by material risk events. The plan is tracked in Shell's GRC platform, with completion rates reported to the CRO monthly. Shell's 2023 Annual Report disclosed that 98% of planned stakeholder engagements were completed on schedule — a metric that signals risk governance discipline to investors.

**So what:** Shell's public disclosure of engagement plan completion rates creates external accountability and signals that risk communication is managed with the same rigor as operational performance. A 98% completion rate is not accidental — it requires the plan structure described above.

## Template
Build the 12-month plan from the Stakeholder Engagement Strategy. Populate specific dates for all Q1 activities before the plan is finalized. Update quarterly. Track feedback in the designated column.

- [ ] Stakeholder name / group
- [ ] Activity description (specific: 'Board Risk Committee quarterly presentation — 15-slide dashboard + 10-min narrative')
- [ ] Named owner (role and name)
- [ ] Planned dates for all 12 months (or quarterly dates for quarterly activities)
- [ ] Preparation milestone date (10 business days before each engagement)
- [ ] Artifact required (Board Paper / Dashboard / Training module / Briefing note)
- [ ] Completion status for each planned date: P=Planned / C=Completed on schedule / L=Late / M=Missed
- [ ] Feedback captured: Y/N for each completed engagement
- [ ] Feedback summary: new risks surfaced / concerns raised / risk register updates triggered
- [ ] Effectiveness rating for each completed engagement (1–5, owner's assessment)

## Pitfalls
- Building a plan without confirming dates with stakeholder secretariats — a plan with dates that don't align to the Board calendar is not a plan, it is a wish list. Counter: before finalizing the plan, confirm all key dates with the relevant secretariats (Board Secretary, CEO's PA, Audit Committee Chair).
- Tracking completion without tracking effectiveness — completing 95% of planned engagements is meaningless if none of them surfaced new risks or improved stakeholder understanding. Counter: always measure engagement effectiveness (feedback quality, new risks surfaced, stakeholder satisfaction) alongside completion rates.
- Annual plan that is never updated — a January plan that reflects the organizational structure and calendar from January is a stale document by June. Counter: schedule a quarterly plan review (30 minutes) as a standing agenda item in the risk function's operational calendar.
