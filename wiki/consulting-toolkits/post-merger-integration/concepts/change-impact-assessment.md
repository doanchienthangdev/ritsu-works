# Change Impact Assessment

*Category: change-management · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A structured analysis of how each major integration initiative impacts each affected stakeholder group — documenting what is changing, who is affected, the magnitude of the change, and what interventions are needed.

**Origin:** Standard change management practice; codified in Prosci's ADKAR methodology, Kotter's 8-Step Process, and used across all major consulting firms' change management practices.

## Why it works
Change management resources are always scarce. A change impact assessment forces a disciplined identification of where change is actually happening (not just where it is announced) and allocates change management effort to the highest-impact populations.

## When to use
Phase V Step 2. Conduct in parallel with Phase III detailed planning so change interventions are built into the timeline.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List all integration initiatives with significant people impact from the initiative portfolio.
2. For each initiative, identify the affected population segments (e.g., 'Target Sales Team', 'Acquirer Finance Department').
3. For each population/initiative pair, document: type of change (role, reporting line, process, system, culture), magnitude (High = major disruption to daily work, Medium = moderate adjustment, Low = minimal impact).
4. Assess the ADKAR current state for each affected population (use the ADKAR diagnostic).
5. Design the change intervention based on the ADKAR barrier point: Awareness interventions, Desire interventions, Knowledge/Ability interventions, or Reinforcement interventions.
6. Consolidate all change impact assessments into a master change calendar to identify communication overlap and resource conflicts.

## Real-life example — Generic enterprise software integration
An ERP system migration affects Finance (high magnitude — entire workflow changes), IT (medium magnitude — system administration changes), and Operations (medium magnitude — reporting and data entry changes). A change impact assessment ensures that Finance receives the most intensive change management (dedicated training, parallel-run period, help-desk support) while Operations and IT receive standard communication and training packages.

**So what:** The change impact assessment prevents change management resources from being spread uniformly across all populations — it concentrates investment where change is greatest.

## Pitfalls
- Conducting the assessment in the change management team without workstream lead input — only functional owners understand the true magnitude of change for their populations.
- Treating all 'High magnitude' changes the same — a role elimination (irreversible) requires very different change management than a reporting line change (manageable).
- Not connecting the change impact assessment to the integration roadmap — change management interventions should be sequenced to precede the integration events they support.
