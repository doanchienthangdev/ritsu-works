# Integration Initiative Ideation Workshop

*Category: portfolio · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A structured functional workshop process for generating a comprehensive long-list of integration and synergy initiatives — covering all six major functional areas — before filtering and prioritizing.

**Origin:** Standard management consulting workshop facilitation practice; applied to PMI initiative identification in McKinsey and BCG integration methodologies.

## Why it works
If you skip the long-list generation phase and go directly to prioritization, you will prioritize among the obvious initiatives and miss the non-obvious high-value ones. A structured workshop with diverse functional teams surfaces initiatives that no single workstream would have identified alone.

## When to use
Phase II Step 1. Run workshops in Weeks 3–5 post-close once the Integration Strategy is approved.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Run parallel workshops for each major functional workstream — 2 hours per workshop, with 6–10 participants from both organizations in each.
2. Use a structured prompt for each synergy type: Revenue (how can we sell more by combining?), Cost (how can we spend less by combining?), Risk (how can we reduce risk by combining?), Capability (what can we now do that neither could do alone?).
3. Capture every idea, no matter how small or speculative — filtering happens in the business-case phase.
4. After all workshops, consolidate the lists and remove duplicates. Target 50–100 unique initiatives before filtering.
5. Distribute the consolidated list to all workstream leads for completeness review — 'are there initiatives in other workstreams that depend on yours, or vice versa?'
6. Archive the full long-list, including all ideas that are later deprioritized — future deal teams may find value in initiatives that are not feasible today.

## Real-life example — Merger of two regional hospitals
A hospital system integration workshop generated 87 initiatives across clinical (shared specialist services, joint oncology program), operational (combined supply chain, shared IT infrastructure), and financial (combined insurance contracting, shared revenue cycle) workstreams. The combined supply-chain initiative — which neither hospital's operations team had proposed individually — turned out to be the largest cost synergy in the deal.

**So what:** Cross-functional workshops consistently surface the highest-value initiatives — the ones that require both organizations' capabilities to realize.

## Template
Run one workshop per functional workstream. Use the prompt structure below. Consolidate after all workshops.

- [ ] Workshop name (e.g., Finance & Procurement Integration Workshop)
- [ ] Date and participants
- [ ] Revenue synergy ideas (list all)
- [ ] Cost synergy ideas (list all)
- [ ] Risk reduction ideas (list all)
- [ ] Capability acquisition ideas (list all)
- [ ] Dependencies on other workstreams (list)
- [ ] Total initiatives generated

## Pitfalls
- Single-workstream workshops without cross-functional perspectives — the most valuable synergies often sit at the intersection of two workstreams.
- Filtering during the workshop — premature filtering kills creative ideation. Capture everything; filter later.
- Not including target-organization employees in the workshops — they know where their organization's costs and capabilities are better than the acquirer's due diligence team.
