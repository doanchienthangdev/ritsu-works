# Day-One Readiness Checklist

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A comprehensive traffic-light checklist of every operational, legal, HR, IT, and customer-facing requirement that must be confirmed complete before close — structured to surface open items early enough to resolve them.

**Origin:** Standard PMI pre-close planning tool used across investment banking and consulting practices. Codified in integration playbooks by firms including Deloitte and PwC.

## Why it works
Day 1 is the most operationally dangerous moment in an integration — both organizations are simultaneously operating and transitioning. A comprehensive checklist prevents the common failure mode: discovering on Day 1 that an employee payroll system wasn't migrated or a regulatory filing wasn't submitted.

## When to use

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Build the checklist from six categories: Legal (entity registrations, regulatory filings, contract novations), HR (employee communications, payroll migration, benefit enrollment), IT (system access, email migration, SSO), Finance (banking access, AP/AR migration), Customer (account notifications, support routing), Communications (Day-1 all-hands, manager briefings).
2. Assign a named owner to every item — 'TBD' is not an owner.
3. Run the first readiness review 4 weeks before Day 1 to surface amber and red items with enough lead time to resolve them.
4. Run the final readiness review 48 hours before Day 1 — any red item at this stage must be escalated to the Steering Committee immediately.
5. Set a resolution deadline for every amber/red item: 24h, 48h, or 'must resolve before Day 1'.
6. Archive the completed checklist as evidence of Day-1 readiness — it is part of the integration governance record.

## Real-life example — Any large financial services merger
In financial services PMI, the Day-1 readiness checklist is typically hundreds of items covering regulatory notifications to dozens of regulators, FDIC/OCC filings, customer communication requirements under consumer protection laws, and system cutover checkpoints. Investment banks running these integrations rehearse the Day-1 checklist review multiple times in the 2 weeks before close, treating it as a war-game exercise.

**So what:** The readiness checklist is the integration team's equivalent of a pre-flight checklist — the value is not the document but the discipline of verifying every item with evidence, not assumption.

## Pitfalls
- Building the checklist too late — a Day-1 readiness checklist built at Week 8 of a 10-week integration has no remediation runway for red items.
- 'Assuming green' without evidence — status must be based on documented confirmation, not verbal assurance.
- Checklist as a paper exercise — the value is in the governance conversation it forces, not the document itself.
