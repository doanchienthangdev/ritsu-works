# 3-Horizon Organization Design

*Category: org-design · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A three-stage approach to post-merger organization design that defines three distinct organizational states: Day-1 (interim structure for continuity), 6-month (converged structure balancing integration and performance), and 18-month (target state — the fully integrated design).

**Origin:** Applied from McKinsey's 3-Horizons framework (Baghai, Coley & White, 1999) to PMI organization design; codified in Deloitte and BCG integration toolkits as a standard approach to phased org design.

## Why it works
Attempting to implement the fully integrated organization design on Day 1 maximizes disruption and customer/service continuity risk. The three-horizon approach separates what must be in place for operational continuity (Day 1) from what will create the most integrated value (18 months) — allowing the organization to remain functional while the integration proceeds.

## When to use
Phase I Step 7 (operating model design). All three horizons must be designed before Day 1.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Design backward: start with the 18-month target state (the optimal integrated organization) and identify what must be in place to get there.
2. For the Day-1 structure: make only the changes that are operationally necessary (executive team confirmation, IMO establishment, legal entity structure). Leave everything else unchanged to protect continuity.
3. For the 6-month structure: make the changes that capture major synergies (eliminating duplicate management layers, merging co-located teams, unifying the sales organization) while the IT and process integration is still in progress.
4. For each transition, document: what changes (role/reporting/location), who is affected, what the change management implications are, and what operational risks are introduced.
5. Present all three horizons to the Steering Committee simultaneously — decisions about the 18-month structure affect Day-1 decisions (e.g., if a function will be outsourced at 18 months, do not invest in rebuilding it at Day 1).
6. Communicate the timeline to employees: 'This is the Day-1 structure, this is the 6-month structure, this is where we are heading' — transparency about the journey reduces anxiety more than the destination alone.

## Real-life example — Merger of two pharmaceutical companies (generic example)
A pharma merger used a 3-horizon approach: Day-1 kept both R&D organizations intact (continuity of drug pipelines), the 6-month structure merged the commercial organizations (largest headcount synergy), and the 18-month target consolidated R&D into one unified organization with shared platform capabilities. This sequencing protected revenue during the first year while capturing the largest cost synergy early.

**So what:** In deals where the target has valuable intellectual property or in-progress projects, the Day-1 structure must preserve those assets intact — do not restructure before you understand what you own.

## Pitfalls
- Collapsing the three horizons into one — trying to implement the 18-month design on Day 1 maximizes disruption and service continuity risk.
- Not communicating all three horizons to employees — people assume the worst about unanticipated future changes; showing the full journey reduces anxiety.
- 18-month design not aligned to the integration archetype — if the archetype is Preservation, the 18-month target structure should still have significant separation, not full merger.
