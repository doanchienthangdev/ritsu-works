# 12-Month Stakeholder Engagement Plan

*Category: stakeholder-management · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A calendar-based plan translating the Stakeholder Engagement Matrix into a 12-month schedule of specific touchpoints, briefing documents, and ownership assignments for every priority stakeholder.

**Origin:** Standard stakeholder management deliverable in large program and project management; codified in PMI PMBOK Guide and applied to M&A integration in McKinsey and Deloitte integration toolkits.

## Why it works
Stakeholder relationships deteriorate in the absence of proactive engagement. Without a scheduled plan, integration teams engage stakeholders reactively (when a problem surfaces) rather than proactively (before problems develop). The 12-month calendar forces proactive relationship investment.

## When to use
Phase VI Step 3. Built in Month 1 of close; updated monthly throughout the 12-month engagement period.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Take the Stakeholder Engagement Matrix and schedule every touchpoint on a 12-month calendar.
2. For each major integration milestone (Day 1, Month 3 health check, Month 6 synergy update), add stakeholder communication events — announce milestones proactively to priority stakeholders before they appear in public filings or press releases.
3. Prepare a briefing document for each touchpoint involving a 'Manage Closely' stakeholder — the brief should include: progress since last meeting, upcoming decisions/changes, requests for input, and any asks.
4. Assign a calendar owner (typically the Communications Lead or IMO Director) who is responsible for ensuring every scheduled touchpoint happens on time.
5. Review the engagement calendar monthly at the IMO meeting: which touchpoints happened, what was the sentiment outcome, what needs follow-up.
6. Update the calendar in response to integration events — a major system migration, a restructuring announcement, or a synergy milestone announcement should trigger additional stakeholder touchpoints.

## Real-life example — United / Continental merger (2010)
United and Continental built a 12-month engagement calendar for their top corporate travel customers — the highest-value customers most at risk of defecting to competitors during the integration uncertainty. Account managers had pre-scheduled quarterly executive business reviews, and a dedicated integration customer-success team handled ad-hoc inquiries. Customer retention in this segment exceeded 95% through Year 1 of integration.

**So what:** Pre-scheduling customer engagement — rather than waiting for customers to raise concerns — is the most effective retention strategy for high-value accounts during integration.

## Pitfalls
- Building the plan and not executing it — a scheduled touchpoint not executed damages the relationship more than no plan at all.
- Touchpoints without preparation — an executive briefing without a prepared brief signals low priority to the stakeholder.
- Not adjusting the calendar when integration news breaks — reactive stakeholder communication is always worse than proactive.
