# Integration Risk Register

*Category: risk · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A structured log tracking all identified integration risks, their likelihood and impact scores, treatment strategies, owners, and monitoring cadence — the operational output of applying the ISO 31000 framework to integration risk management.

**Origin:** Universal project and program management tool; codified in PMI PMBOK Guide and PRINCE2. Applied to M&A integration in all major consulting firm PMI toolkits.

## Why it works
An integration risk that is identified but not tracked and actively managed is functionally equivalent to an unidentified risk — it still materializes. The risk register forces active ownership and monitoring by giving every identified risk a named owner, a treatment plan, and a review date.

## When to use
Phase III Step 5. Maintained throughout Phase IV. Updated monthly; Critical risks escalated immediately.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Populate the register in a risk identification workshop (Phase III Step 5) with all workstream leads.
2. Score each risk on Likelihood (1 = rare, 5 = almost certain) and Impact (1 = negligible, 5 = catastrophic to deal value).
3. Calculate the risk rating (L × I) and classify: 15–25 = Critical, 8–14 = High, 4–7 = Medium, 1–3 = Low.
4. For each Critical and High risk, document the treatment plan: specific actions, owner, and deadline.
5. Assign every risk a review date — Critical risks reviewed at every Steering Committee; High risks reviewed monthly.
6. Update status at each review: 'Monitoring' (treatment in place, risk stable), 'Active' (risk is materializing, treatment being executed), 'Closed' (risk has passed or been eliminated).

## Real-life example — Sprint / T-Mobile merger (2020)
The Sprint/T-Mobile integration risk register included a Critical risk: FCC and DOJ conditional approval with network divestiture requirements. The risk treatment plan included a dedicated regulatory workstream, retained law firm, and pre-agreed remedies with prospective divestiture buyers (DISH Network). The risk was managed to 'Closed' on Day 1 when all conditions were satisfied.

**So what:** Regulatory risks in large deals are almost always Critical — they benefit from having a dedicated workstream treating them as a project-within-the-project, not a checklist item.

## Pitfalls
- Building the register once and never updating it.
- Too many risks diluting focus — aim for 20–40 active risks; if the register has 200 items, no one is managing any of them.
- Risk register ratings that never change — if a Critical risk's likelihood doesn't decrease after treatment actions, the treatment is not working.
