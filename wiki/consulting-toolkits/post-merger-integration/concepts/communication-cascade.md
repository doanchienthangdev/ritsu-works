# Communication Cascade

*Category: communication · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A structured internal communication architecture ensuring integration messages are delivered to every employee through the right messenger, on the right channel, at the right time — from CEO anchor messages to manager-level individual team conversations.

**Origin:** Standard organizational communication practice codified in Kotter's change management work and in McKinsey, Deloitte, and Prosci communication planning frameworks.

## Why it works
Research consistently shows that employees trust their direct manager more than senior leadership for information about how change will affect them personally. The cascade ensures that the CEO sets the strategic narrative but that managers — who have the trust — deliver the personal-impact messages. Without a cascade architecture, the message gets garbled or lost between tiers.

## When to use

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the anchor messages: 3–5 non-negotiable truths from the CEO that will be repeated on every channel throughout the integration (e.g., 'no involuntary layoffs for 90 days', 'DataStar brand is preserved').
2. Build the communication calendar: for each major integration milestone, plan the communication cascade in advance — who communicates what, when, on which channel, to which audience.
3. Apply the Rule of 7: a key message must be communicated through seven distinct touchpoints (CEO video, manager meeting, intranet post, town hall, FAQ document, manager toolkit, weekly email) before it can be considered 'landed'.
4. Design the Day-1 cascade to the minute: CEO message (time), executive sponsor messages (time), manager conversations (within first hour), employee FAQ available (within first hour).
5. Build two-way channels into the architecture — town halls with live Q&A, anonymous survey, manager Q&A toolkit. Employees who have questions answered become advocates; employees with unanswered questions become rumor-spreaders.
6. Track message receipt, not just message delivery — measure the CRI Awareness score as the indicator of whether the cascade is working.

## Real-life example — Microsoft acquisition of LinkedIn (2016)
Microsoft's Day-1 communication cascade began with a joint video from Satya Nadella and Jeff Weiner published at market open — watched by 91% of LinkedIn employees within 24 hours. This was followed by function-level calls from executives, team-level meetings facilitated by managers using a provided script, and an employee FAQ posted on the intranet. The cascade was rehearsed two days before close to ensure every manager knew their role and timing.

**So what:** Rehearsing the Day-1 cascade with managers 48 hours before close is the single best investment in Day-1 communication quality — managers who are surprised on Day 1 give poor-quality team conversations.

## Pitfalls
- Communicating once and assuming 'done' — the Rule of 7 applies especially in uncertain environments where people are anxious and selective in what they hear.
- Leaving managers out of cascade preparation — managers who receive integration news 30 minutes before their teams cannot give credible, calm conversations.
- Over-engineering the cascade at the expense of authenticity — employees can detect scripted messages; the cascade provides structure, but the tone should be human.
