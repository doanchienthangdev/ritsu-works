# Integration Initiative Prioritization Matrix

*Category: portfolio · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A 2x2 matrix plotting integration and synergy initiatives by expected Value (NPV or strategic benefit) on the Y-axis and Feasibility/Speed on the X-axis, producing four action categories: Quick Wins, Strategic Bets, Fill-ins, and Deprioritized.

**Origin:** Derived from standard portfolio prioritization approaches used in McKinsey and BCG strategy practices; the Value vs. Feasibility matrix is a standard consulting tool adapted for PMI initiative selection.

## Why it works
In a large integration there are always more initiatives than capacity to execute them. Without explicit prioritization, teams spread effort thinly across all initiatives and deliver none well. The matrix forces a binary choice: invest fully in high-value initiatives and defer or drop low-value ones.

## When to use
Phase II Step 3 (initiative prioritization), after all business cases are built and before the integration roadmap is finalized.

## Visual

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Score each initiative on Value (1–5, based on NPV of synergy) and Feasibility (1–5, inverse of complexity).
2. Plot all initiatives on the matrix.
3. Categorize: Quick Wins (Value ≥4, Feasibility ≥4), Strategic Bets (Value ≥4, Feasibility <4), Fill-ins (Value <4, Feasibility ≥4), Deprioritized (Value <4, Feasibility <4).
4. For Quick Wins, assign owners and set 90-day execution deadlines — these prove early integration momentum.
5. For Strategic Bets, build full business cases and resource plans before committing — the complexity warrants the planning investment.
6. Present at Steering Committee for sign-off — make visible which initiatives are being deferred or dropped, and why.

## Real-life example — Marriott International acquisition of Starwood Hotels (2016, $13.6B)
The Marriott-Starwood integration faced hundreds of potential initiatives spanning loyalty programs, reservation systems, procurement, and brand portfolios. The integration team explicitly prioritized the loyalty program merger (Marriott Rewards + Starwood Preferred Guest → Marriott Bonvoy) as the highest-value, highest-visibility Quick Win — it directly affected 100M+ members and was the primary revenue synergy driver. IT system migration was a Strategic Bet — high value but deeply complex — and executed over three years rather than rushed.

**So what:** Customer-facing initiatives (especially loyalty programs in hospitality) are almost always Quick Wins — they generate revenue and signal integration progress to the market.

## Pitfalls
- Over-populating Quick Wins — if everything is a Quick Win, nothing is. Cap Quick Wins at 10–15 initiatives to maintain focus.
- Scoring Feasibility without consulting functional owners — theoretical assessments are often wrong; require workstream leads to validate complexity scores.
- Not revisiting the matrix — an initiative's feasibility score changes as integration complexity becomes clearer. Refresh at Month 3.
