# Stakeholder Power/Interest Grid

*Category: stakeholder-management · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A 2x2 matrix mapping stakeholders by their power to influence the integration outcome (Y-axis) and their level of interest in or impact from the integration (X-axis), producing four engagement strategy quadrants: Manage Closely, Keep Satisfied, Keep Informed, Monitor.

**Origin:** Attributed to Aubrey Mendelow (1991), Stakeholder Mapping, Proceedings of the 2nd International Conference on Information Systems. Widely adopted in project management (PMI PMBOK Guide) and strategic management education.

## Why it works
Not all stakeholders require equal investment. The grid forces disciplined allocation of relationship-management resources: high power/high interest stakeholders can veto or champion decisions and require co-creation and frequent bilateral engagement; high power/low interest stakeholders can block if surprised and require proactive briefing.

## When to use
Phase VI (stakeholder engagement) onset; also use in Phase I when designing the communication strategy. Update monthly.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Brainstorm a comprehensive stakeholder list across six categories: employees, customers, investors/analysts, regulators, suppliers, and community/media.
2. Score each stakeholder on Power (1–5) and Interest (1–5).
3. Plot on the 2x2. Identify the top 10–15 priority stakeholders in the 'Manage Closely' and 'Keep Satisfied' quadrants.
4. For each priority stakeholder, document: current stance (supportive/neutral/resistant), key concern, and ideal engagement outcome.
5. Assign a named relationship owner to each priority stakeholder — CEO or IMO Director for regulators and major investors; workstream leads for customers and suppliers.
6. Update the grid monthly — stakeholder stances shift as integration news develops.

## Real-life example — AB InBev acquisition of SABMiller (2016, $107B)
The deal required regulatory approvals from more than 30 countries. Regulatory stakeholders ranged from 'Keep Satisfied' (US DOJ — high power, relatively low operational interest once structural remedies agreed) to 'Manage Closely' (South African Competition Commission — high power AND high interest due to SABMiller's economic importance in South Africa). AB InBev ran bespoke engagement strategies by country, resulting in clearance in all markets — though the South African approval required significant commitments on local employment and supplier development.

**So what:** Regulatory stakeholders must be segmented as carefully as customers — a one-size-fits-all regulatory communication strategy fails in large cross-border deals.

## Pitfalls
- Treating the grid as a one-time exercise — stakeholder stances shift rapidly in a merger, especially after layoff announcements or system-migration news. Update monthly.
- Assigning too many stakeholders to 'Manage Closely' — this quadrant requires intensive relationship management; if you put 30 stakeholders there, nobody gets real attention.
- Forgetting internal stakeholders — employees, particularly influential informal leaders in the target organization, belong on the grid.
