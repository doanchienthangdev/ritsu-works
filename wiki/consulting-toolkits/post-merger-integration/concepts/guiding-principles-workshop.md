# Guiding Principles Workshop

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A structured facilitation session that produces 6–10 decision-useful principles resolving the most common integration trade-offs, each paired with 'we WILL' and 'we will NOT' statements.

**Origin:** Standard organizational design facilitation technique; codified in McKinsey and Deloitte PMI toolkits as a Day-1 governance foundation exercise.

## Why it works
Integration creates hundreds of micro-decisions daily that cannot all go to the Steering Committee. Guiding principles give workstream leads a shared decision filter, enabling decentralized speed without strategic drift.

## When to use
Phase I Step 5, before the operating model design begins. Re-read at every governance meeting as a decision-alignment prompt.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Identify the top 8–10 trade-off scenarios the integration will face (e.g., speed vs. thoroughness in system migration; target culture vs. acquirer culture in new roles).
2. Present each trade-off to the combined leadership team and ask: what is our default position?
3. Draft a principle statement for each: action-oriented, specific, and decision-directing.
4. For each principle, write a 'this means we WILL' and a 'this means we will NOT' test — these make the principle unambiguous.
5. Test for conflicts between principles — if two principles contradict, resolve or establish a priority order.
6. Publish the principles company-wide within 48 hours of finalization — every employee should be able to find and cite them.

## Real-life example — AB InBev integration of Anheuser-Busch (2008, $52B)
AB InBev established explicit guiding principles including 'we operate as one company from Day 1' (speed archetype) and 'local brands retain their local identities' (preservation archetype). These two principles together resolved dozens of brand and operational decisions without escalation.

**So what:** Guiding principles that appear to be in tension (speed vs. preservation) are actually complementary when scoped correctly — the first applies to operations, the second to customer-facing brands.

## Template
Develop one row per guiding principle. Validate each against the 'will/will NOT' test before publishing.

- [ ] Principle statement
- [ ] Scope (operational / cultural / financial / customer-facing)
- [ ] This means we WILL (concrete example)
- [ ] This means we will NOT (concrete example)
- [ ] Applicable decision scenarios
- [ ] Accountable for enforcing

## Pitfalls
- Principles that are decorative rather than decision-directing — 'we value people' is not a guiding principle; 'when organizational restructuring is required, affected employees will be informed 30 days in advance with support for redeployment' is one.
- Too many principles — more than 10 means none are memorable. Force-rank and cut.
- Not publishing the principles — they only create alignment if every workstream lead and manager can recite them.
