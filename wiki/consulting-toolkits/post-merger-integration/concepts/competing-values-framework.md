# Competing Values Framework (CVF / OCAI)

*Category: culture · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A culture diagnostic categorizing organizational culture across two dimensions — internal vs. external orientation and flexibility vs. stability — producing four culture archetypes: Clan (collaborate), Adhocracy (create), Market (compete), and Hierarchy (control). Applied via the Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI).

**Origin:** Developed by Robert Quinn and John Rohrbaugh at the University of Michigan in 1983, 'A Spatial Model of Effectiveness Criteria', Management Science. Operationalized as the OCAI by Quinn in 'Beyond Rational Management' (Jossey-Bass, 1988).

## Why it works
Culture is the most common intangible cause of PMI failure yet is treated as a soft issue. CVF provides a rigorous, measurable diagnostic that quantifies the culture gap between acquirer and target, enabling a fact-based conversation about which cultural attributes to blend, preserve, or change.

## When to use
Phase III Step 4 (culture integration strategy) and Phase V (change management). Baseline diagnostic in Month 1; follow-up at Month 12.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Administer the OCAI questionnaire (6 dimensions × 4 culture types, 100-point allocation per dimension) to representative samples from both organizations — minimum 30 respondents per organization.
2. Calculate the mean culture profile for each organization: a radar chart showing the weight on each of the four archetypes.
3. Overlay the two radar charts to identify: close matches (integration will be easier), complementary orientations (potential blend), and direct conflicts (require explicit resolution).
4. Facilitate a leadership workshop: present the culture profiles, discuss what each attribute produces in terms of behavior, and debate which attributes should define the integrated organization.
5. Define the desired culture profile for the integrated entity — this is the target on the CVF radar — and build the culture integration plan to close the gap.
6. Re-administer the OCAI at Month 12 and Month 24 to track culture convergence.

## Real-life example — Amazon acquisition of Whole Foods (2017, $13.7B)
Amazon (Market/Hierarchy culture — data-driven, efficiency-obsessed) acquired Whole Foods (Clan/Adhocracy culture — values-driven, community-oriented). The culture clash was evident: Amazon's cost-cutting and algorithmic pricing signals clashed with Whole Foods' ethos of local supplier relationships and artisan products. A CVF analysis conducted pre-integration would have mapped the quadrant distance and designed specific cultural bridging mechanisms — rather than the reactive cultural management that followed employee and supplier backlash.

**So what:** When acquirer and target sit in diagonally opposite quadrants (Market vs. Clan; Hierarchy vs. Adhocracy), the integration team must explicitly design cultural bridging — pure Absorption will destroy the target's value.

## Pitfalls
- Administering the OCAI to only one organization — you need both profiles to identify the gap. Common mistake: only surveying the target because 'we know our own culture'.
- Treating the desired culture profile as aspirational rather than operational — culture change requires behavioral reinforcement through performance management and leadership modeling.
- Not revisiting the culture diagnostic — integration leaders often declare cultural alignment after a few all-hands meetings; the data rarely confirms this before Month 12.
