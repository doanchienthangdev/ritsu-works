# Integration Initiative Business Case

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A one-page structured analysis evaluating each integration or synergy initiative on: the problem it solves, the proposed solution, the investment required, the benefits (quantified over 3 years), the risks, and the implementation timeline.

**Origin:** Standard project and investment governance tool; applied to PMI initiative evaluation in McKinsey, BCG, and Deloitte integration methodologies.

## Why it works
Without a business case, initiatives are evaluated on advocacy (who argues loudest for them) rather than value (what they are actually worth). The business case creates a common currency — NPV — that allows the prioritization matrix to function objectively.

## When to use
Phase II Step 2. Build business cases for all initiatives with estimated NPV >$1M before the prioritization matrix is applied.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Build the business case from the initiative owner's functional perspective — they understand the operational assumptions better than finance.
2. Quantify benefits specifically: not 'cost reduction' but 'elimination of 12 duplicate AP FTEs at an average fully-loaded cost of $80K = $960K annual saving'.
3. Include the one-time cost to achieve: restructuring costs, severance, IT migration costs, consulting fees, training. Many integration teams double-count synergies by forgetting the cost to achieve.
4. Calculate NPV using the company's integration discount rate (typically 10–15%) over a 3-year period.
5. Have finance validate the financial assumptions independently — the initiative owner's estimates are often optimistic.
6. Identify the top 3 risks and their likelihood — this inputs to the Risk Register and the Prioritization Matrix feasibility score.

## Real-life example — A typical procurement consolidation initiative
A procurement consolidation business case for a $5B combined entity identifies $12M in addressable spend (vendors where both companies have contracts), models a 15% price reduction through combined volume (industry benchmark), calculates $1.8M annual saving, net of $0.3M one-time renegotiation cost. NPV at 10% over 3 years = $3.9M. This concrete calculation allows the initiative to be ranked objectively against alternatives.

**So what:** Business cases that use industry benchmarks to validate savings assumptions are more credible and more likely to survive CFO scrutiny than those based purely on internal estimates.

## Pitfalls
- Gross synergies without costs to achieve — a $5M saving that costs $4M to achieve is a $1M net synergy, not a $5M one.
- Benefits expressed as percentages rather than dollars — '15% cost reduction' is not actionable; '15% cost reduction = $2.3M saving from IT vendor consolidation' is.
- Business cases built by the initiative champion without independent financial validation — optimism bias inflates benefits and understates costs.
