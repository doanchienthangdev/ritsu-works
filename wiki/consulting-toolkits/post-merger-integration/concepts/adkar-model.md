# ADKAR Change Model

*Category: change-management · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A goal-oriented change management model tracking individual progression through five stages — Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement — and prescribing targeted interventions to move people through each stage.

**Origin:** Developed by Jeff Hiatt at Prosci in 1998, based on research into 700 companies undergoing change. Published as 'ADKAR: A Model for Change in Business, Government and our Community' (Prosci Learning Center, 2006).

## Why it works
Organizational change only succeeds when individuals change. By diagnosing which stage each person or group is stuck at, leaders apply precisely the right intervention — rather than broadcasting more communication when the real problem is lack of training (Knowledge gap) or more training when the real problem is motivation (Desire gap).

## When to use
From Phase V (change management strategy) onset; diagnostic should run monthly through Month 18 of integration.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. For each major population segment, administer a diagnostic (3–5 questions per ADKAR stage, scored 1–5). Use the mean as the segment's ADKAR profile.
2. Identify the barrier point — the first stage where the segment scores below 3. You cannot skip stages: a person without Desire will not benefit from Knowledge interventions.
3. For each barrier point, select the appropriate intervention: Awareness → CEO communication + town halls; Desire → manager conversations + 'what's in it for me' messaging; Knowledge → training + job aids; Ability → coaching + practice + feedback; Reinforcement → recognition + performance management alignment.
4. Run the ADKAR diagnostic monthly and track movement across stages — this is your Change Readiness Index (CRI).
5. Escalate when a population segment is not moving through stages at expected pace — this indicates either the wrong intervention or a leadership barrier.
6. At Month 6, confirm all major segments have reached Ability — integrated processes and systems are being used correctly in daily work.

## Real-life example — Microsoft acquisition of LinkedIn (2016, $26B)
Microsoft's integration of LinkedIn was notably successful — LinkedIn retained its brand, culture, and product identity while benefiting from Microsoft's enterprise relationships. Microsoft's change strategy focused first on building Awareness and Desire: CEO Satya Nadella personally visited LinkedIn's headquarters and held open Q&A sessions. The explicit signal that LinkedIn's culture would be preserved moved a large portion of the workforce from 'resistant' to 'supportive' at the Desire stage before any operational changes were made.

**So what:** In acqui-hire and talent-acquisition deals, Desire is the hardest ADKAR stage — senior leaders personally engaging at the target organization's sites is the most effective Desire intervention.

## Pitfalls
- Treating ADKAR as a communication checklist — sending emails counts as an Awareness intervention but does not guarantee Awareness. Measure reception, not transmission.
- Rushing to Knowledge and Ability interventions before Desire is confirmed — untrained people waste training; unmotivated people waste it more.
- Forgetting Reinforcement — most integrations invest heavily in the first three stages and stop; without reinforcement, employees revert to old behaviours within 6 months.
