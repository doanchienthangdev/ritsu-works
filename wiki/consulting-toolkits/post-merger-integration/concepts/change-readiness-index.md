# Change Readiness Index (CRI)

*Category: change-management · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A monthly pulse survey measuring awareness, understanding, and commitment across workforce segments — producing a composite Change Readiness Index score (0–100%) that tracks the organization's progress through the ADKAR change curve.

**Origin:** Applied from Prosci's change management measurement methodology and widely adopted in McKinsey and Deloitte change management practices as the primary integration change health metric.

## Why it works
Without measurement, change management becomes anecdotal ('people seem engaged' vs. 'people are not engaged'). The CRI provides a quantitative baseline, tracks momentum, and triggers interventions before disengagement becomes attrition.

## When to use
Phase V Step 3. Begin with the Day-30 baseline survey; run monthly through Month 18.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Design the pulse survey: 5–10 questions, 2–3 per ADKAR stage. Keep it short enough to complete in 3 minutes or response rates drop below 40%.
2. Survey the full workforce or a statistically representative sample (minimum 30 respondents per segment for statistical validity).
3. Calculate the CRI composite: weight Awareness 30%, Understanding 30%, Commitment 40% (commitment is the hardest to achieve and most predictive of retention).
4. Segment results by population (Acquirer vs. Target, by function, by geography) — integration anxiety is almost always higher in the target organization.
5. Identify populations below target thresholds — these are intervention priorities for the Change Management Lead.
6. Present CRI results at the bi-weekly Steering Committee — declining CRI is a leading indicator of attrition and productivity loss.

## Real-life example — Generic post-merger integration Month 3
In a typical integration, Month 1 CRI shows high Awareness (communications have landed) but low Commitment (employees are uncertain about their role). By Month 3, Commitment should be rising as individual role clarity improves. A Month 3 Commitment score below 50% despite high Awareness is the classic 'informed but not convinced' profile — indicating that messages have been received but not believed, and that leader credibility-building actions are needed.

**So what:** The gap between Awareness and Commitment scores is the most diagnostic CRI signal — a large gap indicates the communication is landing but the leadership is not.

## Pitfalls
- Asking leading questions — 'Do you support the integration?' will get a higher commitment score than 'How confident are you that the integration will benefit you personally?' Design for honest answers.
- Reporting CRI without acting on it — if the CRI is red and nothing changes, the survey becomes a source of cynicism ('they survey us but don't listen').
- Stopping the CRI at Month 6 — the most dangerous attrition window is Month 6–18 when the 'new normal' has settled but some employees have concluded it's not for them.
