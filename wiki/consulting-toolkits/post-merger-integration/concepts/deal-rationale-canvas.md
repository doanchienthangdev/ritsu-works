# Deal Rationale Canvas

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A one-page structured template that translates an M&A transaction thesis into the operational narrative workstream leads need to make local integration decisions. It maps each financial driver to a specific integration action and identifies what must be preserved from the target.

**Origin:** Synthesized from McKinsey, BCG, and Deloitte PMI methodologies through the 1990s–2000s. Operationalizes the academic work on merger motives by Haspeslagh & Jemison (1991) and Bower (2001).

## Why it works
Most integration failures occur because frontline workstream leads do not know why the deal was done, so they make decisions inconsistent with the deal value. The canvas forces the leadership team to translate financial reasoning into operational language before any workstream begins.

## When to use
At the kick-off of Phase I integration strategy, before any workstream planning begins. Also use whenever workstreams appear to be working at cross-purposes.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List every financial driver cited in the Board approval memo — typically 3–6 drivers covering revenue synergy, cost synergy, market access, or capability acquisition.
2. For each driver, write the specific integration action required — be operational, not abstract (e.g., 'migrate Target's CRM to Acquirer Salesforce by Month 3' not 'integrate systems').
3. For each driver, list what must be preserved from the target (e.g., the target's brand with its customer segment, the CTO and key engineers, the proprietary pricing algorithm).
4. Quantify the value at risk: how much of the deal's NPV depends on this driver being executed correctly?
5. Present the canvas in the kick-off governance session — require every workstream lead to sign off that they understand their driver.
6. Post the canvas on the integration intranet so every team member can trace their work back to the deal rationale.

## Real-life example — Pfizer acquisition of Warner-Lambert (2000, $90B)
Pfizer's deal rationale was overwhelmingly centered on one asset: Lipitor, then the world's best-selling drug. A clear deal rationale canvas would show that the preservation imperative (Warner-Lambert's Lipitor commercial organization and R&D pipeline) vastly outweighed the cost-synergy driver. Pfizer's integration team preserved Warner-Lambert's key sales force and R&D staff — a decision that protected Lipitor's $13B peak annual revenue.

**So what:** When the deal value is concentrated in one or two assets, the Deal Rationale Canvas forces the whole integration team to treat preservation of those assets as the primary integration objective — ahead of cost synergy or standardization.

## Template
Fill one column per major deal driver. Agree with the CFO on NPV attribution before the kick-off session. Revisit at the 90-day integration review.

- [ ] Deal driver name and type (revenue/cost/market/capability)
- [ ] Financial value (NPV, $M) attributed to this driver
- [ ] Specific integration action(s) required
- [ ] What must be preserved from the target (and by whom)
- [ ] Value at risk if this driver is mismanaged
- [ ] Integration milestone confirming this driver is on track

## Pitfalls
- Listing generic drivers without operational specificity — the canvas only works if it is concrete enough for a workstream lead to act on.
- Filling the canvas after workstreams have already started — inconsistent decisions are already made by then.
- Not revisiting the canvas at 90 days — real integration complexity often reveals that some drivers are harder than expected and relative priorities need rebalancing.
