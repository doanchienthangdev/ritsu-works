# ISO 31000 Risk Management Framework

*Category: risk · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
The international standard for risk management providing principles, a framework, and a process for systematically identifying, assessing, treating, monitoring, and reviewing risks. Applied to PMI to create a structured integration risk register.

**Origin:** Published by the International Organization for Standardization in 2009, updated in 2018. ISO 31000:2018 is the current version. Draws on Australian/New Zealand standard AS/NZS 4360 (1995).

## Why it works
Integration risk is uniquely multi-dimensional — operational, financial, regulatory, people, technology, and reputational risks all crystallize simultaneously at close. ISO 31000's principle of treating risk as a central governance responsibility ensures integration risks are managed at the same level of rigor as synergy commitments.

## When to use
Phase III Step 5 (risk management plan) and maintained throughout Phase IV. Update monthly; escalate Critical risks immediately.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Convene a risk identification workshop with all workstream leads and the IMO Director — use a structured prompt list by category: what could prevent Day-1 readiness? what could cause customer defection? what regulatory event could delay the integration?
2. Document each risk: risk name, category, description, trigger event, likelihood (1–5), impact (1–5), inherent risk rating (L×I), current controls.
3. Apply the 5x5 risk matrix: scores 15–25 = Critical (Steering Committee owns), 8–14 = High (IMO Director owns), 4–7 = Medium (workstream lead owns), 1–3 = Low (monitor only).
4. For each Critical and High risk, select a treatment: Avoid (do not pursue the activity creating the risk), Mitigate (reduce likelihood or impact), Transfer (insure or contract out), Accept (acknowledge and build contingency).
5. Assign a named risk owner and a response plan for each Critical/High risk. Include a trigger (early-warning indicator) and escalation action.
6. Review the risk register at every Steering Committee meeting — update likelihood/impact ratings as integration progress changes the risk landscape.

## Real-life example — Kraft / Heinz merger (2015, $46B)
The Kraft-Heinz merger suffered a $15.4B goodwill write-down in 2019 on the Kraft and Oscar Mayer brands. A robust ISO 31000 risk assessment at integration design would have flagged the brand value concentration risk (over-reliance on legacy brands in a shifting consumer market) as Critical, requiring a mitigation plan for sustained brand investment — rather than the cost-cutting approach applied through integration.

**So what:** Financial reporting and goodwill impairment risk is a systematically underweighted category in PMI risk registers — a 5x5 assessment of revenue and brand deterioration risks should be mandatory for deals with significant intangible asset values.

## Pitfalls
- Building the risk register in Phase I and never updating it — the integration risk profile changes dramatically from pre-close to Day 1 to Month 6.
- Risk register as a reporting artifact rather than a management tool — if risks are not discussed and actioned at governance meetings, the register is cosmetic.
- Under-scoring people and culture risks — because they are harder to quantify, teams tend to assess talent attrition and culture clash as 'medium' when evidence from comparable deals would justify 'critical'.
