# Integration Approach Archetypes

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
Four canonical integration models — Preservation, Symbiosis, Absorption, and Transformation — that define how deeply the target organization is merged into the acquirer across all operational dimensions.

**Origin:** Codified by Philippe Haspeslagh and David Jemison in 'Managing Acquisitions: Creating Value Through Corporate Renewal' (Free Press, 1991), based on a study of 20 acquisitions. Taught at Harvard Business School and used across McKinsey, BCG, and Deloitte PMI practices.

## Why it works
The choice of integration archetype is the most consequential PMI decision — it determines the speed, depth, and trauma of the integration. Choosing the wrong archetype relative to the deal rationale is a primary cause of value destruction: Absorption applied to an acqui-hire destroys the talent you paid for; Preservation applied to a cost-reduction deal delays synergy capture indefinitely.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Use the deal rationale canvas to identify the primary value driver: strategic interdependence (shared customers, systems, brand) vs. organizational autonomy (target's culture, people, market position).
2. Rate the integration on two axes (1–5): Need for Strategic Interdependence and Need for Organizational Autonomy.
3. Plot on the 2x2 to identify the primary archetype.
4. Apply the archetype consistently across five functional dimensions: Brand (merge/co-exist/independent), Systems (migrate/interface/independent), Processes (standardize/harmonize/independent), Culture (converge/bridge/independent), Leadership (unified/dual/independent).
5. Document the archetype choice and its functional implications in the Integration Approach document.
6. Communicate the archetype to both workstreams and target-organization employees before any operational changes begin — in the absence of clarity, employees assume worst-case.

## Real-life example — Google acquisition of YouTube (2006, $1.65B)
Google applied a classic Preservation archetype: YouTube's brand, team, and product were maintained as an independent entity. Google provided capital, infrastructure (reducing YouTube's bandwidth costs by ~$1M/day), and advertising technology — but did not absorb YouTube's organization or culture. YouTube still operates as an independent brand today. The Preservation archetype protected the creative community that made YouTube valuable while delivering the strategic interdependence Google needed.

**So what:** Preservation is the right archetype when the deal value is in the target's brand, community, or culture — Absorption destroys exactly what you paid for.

## Pitfalls
- Defaulting to Absorption because 'that's what acquirers do' — without anchoring to the deal rationale, Absorption destroys the target's source of value in capability and talent deals.
- Inconsistency across functions — HR chooses Absorption while IT chooses Preservation. The archetype must be a consistent signal.
- Not communicating the archetype to target employees — in the absence of clarity, employees assume worst-case (full Absorption) and the best ones leave first.
