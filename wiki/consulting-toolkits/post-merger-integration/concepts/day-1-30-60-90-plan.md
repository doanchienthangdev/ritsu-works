# Day 1 / 30 / 60 / 90 Integration Plan

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A milestone-checkpoint planning framework that decomposes the integration roadmap into four defined organizational states at Day 1 (operational continuity), Day 30 (early quick wins), Day 60 (early synergy), and Day 90 (first integration health check).

**Origin:** Standard PMI planning convention used across McKinsey, BCG, and Deloitte integration practices; mirrors the 30-60-90 day plan used in executive onboarding, adapted for integration program management.

## Why it works
Integration timelines feel overwhelming as a 24-month horizon. The 90-day checkpoint structure breaks the work into sprint-sized horizons, creates visible near-term milestones that drive urgency, and provides four early opportunities to course-correct before problems compound.

## When to use
Phase III Step 1 (detailed planning). Day-1 plan must be complete and stress-tested at the Day-1 Readiness Workshop.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. For each checkpoint, list every milestone that must be achieved — be specific (e.g., 'all employees have received Day-1 communication package' not 'communicate to employees').
2. Assign a workstream owner to each milestone and a binary test for completion (it either is or isn't done).
3. Build the Day-1 plan first and work backward — Day 1 is the non-negotiable constraint; if a milestone can't be achieved by Day 1, it moves to Day 30.
4. At each checkpoint, run a formal milestone review: green (complete), amber (in progress, on track), red (at risk or missed).
5. Use missed milestones as input to the risk register and lessons-learned retrospective.
6. Re-baseline the Day-60 and Day-90 plans at Day 30 based on what was learned in the first month.

## Real-life example — United/Continental merger (2010)
United and Continental set an explicit Day-1 milestone of a single combined website and reservation system interface — visible to every customer on close. The Day-90 milestone was achieving a single FAA operating certificate. Each checkpoint created a public commitment that aligned internal execution and signaled integration progress to travelers and investors.

**So what:** Customer-visible Day-1 milestones create external accountability that focuses internal execution far more powerfully than internal milestones alone.

## Pitfalls
- Treating Day 1 as just an internal communications event — Day 1 is the public moment; customer-facing, regulatory, and IT-system milestones are as important as HR communications.
- Not re-baselining — the Day-60 and Day-90 plans built in Phase I will be wrong; update them at Day 30 with what you learned.
- Mixing operational milestones with synergy realization targets — the 90-day plan tracks readiness and execution, not financial synergy (which the Synergy Waterfall Model tracks separately).
