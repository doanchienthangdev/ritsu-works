# Synergy Waterfall Model

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Post Merger Integration*

## What it is
A structured financial model tracking synergy commitments from the deal-thesis baseline through actual realization, displayed as a cascading waterfall showing where value is being captured, deferred, or lost — category by category.

**Origin:** Standard tool in investment banking and management consulting M&A practices, widely used by McKinsey, BCG, and Bain deal teams since the 1990s. The waterfall chart format popularized by consulting firms for variance analysis; applied to synergy tracking in PMI.

## Why it works
The synergy gap between deal model and actual realization is the most common source of M&A disappointment. The waterfall forces a precise, category-by-category accounting — making it impossible for leadership to claim 'synergies on track' while individual categories are falling behind.

## When to use
From Phase I (synergy target setting) through the full integration lifecycle. The model is live from Day 1 of close and updated monthly.

## Visual
`chart`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Start from the deal model synergy schedule: extract total synergy commitment by category (headcount, procurement, real estate, IT, revenue cross-sell etc.) and by year.
2. During Phase I, refine the baseline with bottom-up data — identify specific headcount roles, named procurement contracts, and measurable revenue opportunities.
3. For each synergy category, estimate the one-time cost to achieve (restructuring, IT migration, retention payments) and net it from the gross synergy.
4. Build a realization schedule: what percentage of each category is captured in Year 1, Year 2, Year 3. Validate with functional owners.
5. Each month, update the waterfall with actual realization data from the financial close process. Calculate the run-rate gap vs. committed.
6. Present the waterfall at the monthly Integration Review Board — the visual makes variance undeniable and forces a conversation about corrective action.

## Real-life example — T-Mobile acquisition of Sprint (2020, $26B)
T-Mobile publicly committed to $6B in annual synergies by Year 3, primarily from network consolidation and cost reduction. The company published quarterly synergy tracking updates — functioning as a public waterfall showing network decommissioning progress, headcount reduction, and procurement savings category by category. By 2023, T-Mobile reported exceeding the $6B target, attributing outperformance partly to the discipline of granular, public synergy tracking that held every workstream accountable.

**So what:** Public synergy commitments create powerful internal accountability — workstream leads know their number is in an analyst presentation, not just an internal spreadsheet.

## Template
Build one row per synergy category. Update 'Actual to date' monthly from financial close data. Review with CFO before each Steering Committee.

- [ ] Synergy category
- [ ] Deal model commitment (Year 1 / Year 2 / Year 3, $M)
- [ ] One-time cost to achieve ($M)
- [ ] Net synergy after costs ($M)
- [ ] Realization schedule (% Year 1 / Year 2 / Year 3)
- [ ] Functional owner
- [ ] Current reforecast (latest estimate)
- [ ] Actual realized to date ($M)
- [ ] Gap vs. committed ($M)
- [ ] Root cause of gap and corrective action

## Pitfalls
- Double-counting synergies across categories — headcount savings counted in both HR and IT workstreams inflates the total and creates ownership confusion.
- Excluding costs to achieve — gross synergies look good; net synergies tell the real story.
- Not distinguishing between synergy reforecast (an honest revision) and synergy realization (actual cash benefit) — the two can diverge significantly in Year 1.
