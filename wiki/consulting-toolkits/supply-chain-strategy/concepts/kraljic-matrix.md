# Kraljic Matrix

*Category: procurement · Toolkit: Supply Chain Strategy*

## What it is
A 2×2 portfolio tool that classifies all procurement spend categories by supply risk and profit impact, prescribing a distinct supply strategy for each of four quadrants: Non-Critical, Leverage, Bottleneck, and Strategic.

**Origin:** Peter Kraljic, Harvard Business Review, September–October 1983. Published as 'Purchasing Must Become Supply Management.'

## Why it works
Not all suppliers deserve the same management attention. The matrix allocates relationship effort proportional to the combination of business risk and business value. High-risk/high-impact relationships (Strategic quadrant) justify partnership investment; low-risk/low-impact relationships (Non-Critical) should be automated and commoditized to free bandwidth. By making these trade-offs explicit, organisations shift from uniform vendor management to targeted, resource-efficient supply strategy.

## When to use
Use whenever you need to: (1) allocate procurement management effort across a large supply base, (2) identify supply risk concentrations before a disruption, (3) define differentiated negotiation and relationship strategies by category, or (4) prioritize supplier development investments.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Compile a complete spend-category list: group all procurement spend into logical categories (aim for 50–200 categories depending on company size).
2. 2. Score each category on Supply Risk (1–5): assess number of qualified suppliers (few = high risk), ease of substitution, technical complexity of specification, supplier financial health, and geopolitical exposure.
3. 3. Score each category on Profit Impact (1–5): calculate spend as % of total COGS, assess impact on product quality or performance if supply fails, and estimate cost of a 2-week supply outage.
4. 4. Plot each category on the 2×2 matrix using the scores: risk on horizontal axis, impact on vertical. Use bubble size to represent annual spend volume.
5. 5. Assign each category to one of the four quadrants based on whether it falls above/below the midpoint of each axis (adjust the midpoints to reflect your industry context).
6. 6. Define the supply strategy for each quadrant — Non-Critical: standardize specs and automate purchasing via e-catalogue; Leverage: run competitive tenders with 3+ suppliers, negotiate volume discounts, use total-cost-of-ownership pricing; Bottleneck: qualify a second source, negotiate long-term contracts, build safety stock; Strategic: appoint a dedicated supplier relationship manager, co-develop roadmaps, share forecasts.
7. 7. Prioritize action: start with Strategic and Bottleneck quadrants (they carry the most risk) — agree a 90-day action plan for each category in those quadrants.
8. 8. Review the matrix quarterly (categories migrate as market conditions change — a sole-source Bottleneck supplier may qualify a competitor, dropping it to Non-Critical).

## Real-life example — Toyota
Toyota applies a version of the Kraljic logic through its tiered keiretsu supplier system. Components that are custom-engineered and critical to vehicle performance (e.g., proprietary transmission components, custom semiconductors) are managed as Strategic suppliers through exclusive long-term relationships, joint engineering teams, and shared investment. Standard commodity inputs (e.g., steel, standard fasteners) are managed through Leverage strategies — volume consolidation across plants and competitive annual pricing. When the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake disrupted sole-source suppliers of specialty resin, Toyota learned the cost of Bottleneck mis-classification and subsequently mapped 4,000+ tier-2 and tier-3 suppliers to identify concentration risks before they materialized.

**So what:** The Kraljic Matrix is not a one-time exercise — it is a living risk map. The value comes from acting on the strategies it prescribes, especially securing dual sources for Bottleneck categories before a disruption forces your hand.

## Pitfalls
- Scoring by spend alone (forgetting risk): companies often weight only spend, missing Bottleneck suppliers that are small spend but single-source. Always score risk independently of spend volume.
- Treating the matrix as permanent: markets change — a commodity can become a Bottleneck overnight (COVID PPE, semiconductor shortage). Build a quarterly review cadence.
- Failing to act on Strategic quadrant: the matrix is worthless if Strategic suppliers remain managed transactionally. The strategic classification must trigger a visible relationship upgrade (executive sponsor, joint roadmap, shared data).
- Over-stratifying: classifying every supplier as Strategic to justify relationship investment. The Strategic quadrant should represent ≤10–15% of categories — if it is larger, re-calibrate your scoring thresholds.
