# Leadership Development
## Nine-module system that turns managers into executive-grade leaders

**Core value:** A complete, executable leadership operating system — nine interlocking skill modules spanning problem-solving, communication, negotiation, time mastery, team management, stakeholder influence, facilitation, and personal mission — so the practitioner can lead with measurable impact from the first day of application.

---

## When to Use This Toolkit

This toolkit is your resource when:

- A newly promoted manager or senior individual contributor needs a structured fast-track to executive-level leadership skills.
- An organisation is running a leadership development programme for its top-100 or top-500 managers and needs a proven, modular curriculum.
- A team is underperforming and the root cause traces to leadership capability gaps (trust, clarity, accountability, communication).
- A leader is preparing for a step-change role — country MD, C-suite, or general management — and needs to rapidly build the full leadership skill stack.
- A management consulting team needs to upskill client managers as part of an organisational transformation.

**Who it is for:** Newly promoted managers, directors and VPs preparing for C-suite, HR and L&D professionals designing leadership programmes, management consultants coaching client leadership teams, and entrepreneurs scaling their first management layer.

## Module I — Structure Every Problem to Find the Real Answer

**Goal:** Equip the leader with a rigorous, structured seven-step methodology to move from an ambiguous problem to an evidence-based recommendation, reliably and repeatably.

**Key question:** How do I move from a messy, ill-defined problem to a clear, prioritised, board-ready recommendation?

### The Seven Steps

**Step 1 — Define the problem precisely.** Write a one-sentence problem statement using SMART criteria (Specific, Measurable, Agreed, Realistic, Time-bound). Validate it with the key decision-maker. Capture context: who owns the decision, what success looks like, and what constraints apply.

*Input:* Initial request or trigger event. *Output:* Signed-off one-page problem statement. *Owner:* Project lead.

**Step 2 — Structure the problem as an Issue Tree.** Break the problem into an exhaustive, mutually-exclusive set of sub-questions (MECE: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive). Draw the tree top-down: the overarching question branches into 3–5 hypothesis-led issues, each branching into testable sub-issues.

*Input:* Signed-off problem statement. *Output:* Issue tree (1-page visual). *Owner:* Project lead.

**Step 3 — Prioritise issues by impact and solvability.** Score each branch on (a) impact on the overall answer, and (b) solvability given time and data. Plot on a 2x2 prioritisation matrix. Focus analytical effort on high-impact, high-solvability issues first.

*Input:* Issue tree. *Output:* Prioritised work plan. *Owner:* Project lead.

**Step 4 — Plan analyses with a work plan and ghost deck.** For each priority issue, define the specific analysis: what data, what method, who runs it, by when. Build a ghost slide deck showing the story you intend to tell — so analyses are insight-driven.

*Input:* Prioritised issue list. *Output:* Work plan table + ghost slide deck. *Owner:* Project lead + team.

**Step 5 — Conduct analyses and capture so-whats.** Execute each planned analysis. After each, immediately write the insight ('so-what'), not the data. Flag if the finding invalidates a hypothesis and requires a work-plan revision.

*Input:* Work plan + raw data. *Output:* Analysed insights (one so-what per analysis). *Owner:* Analyst / Manager.

**Step 6 — Synthesise with the Pyramid Principle.** Assemble insights answer-first: overall recommendation → 3 grouped key messages → 3–5 supporting data points per message. Apply the so-what test to every slide: if removing it doesn't change the story, cut it.

*Input:* Analysed insights. *Output:* Recommendation memo in Pyramid structure. *Owner:* Project lead.

**Step 7 — Present with SCR and action titles.** Prepare a maximum of 10 slides for a 30-minute executive meeting. Open with SCR (Situation, Complication, Resolution). Use action titles — declarative sentences stating the takeaway, not labels. Leave 40% of meeting time for discussion.

*Input:* Recommendation memo. *Output:* Executive presentation deck. *Owner:* Project lead.

**KPIs:** Decision-maker approves in first meeting; time from problem receipt to recommendation < 2 weeks; zero 'so what?' questions from the audience.

**Gate:** Decision-maker confirms the problem is solved and gives go-ahead on the recommended action.

## Module II — Command the Room Through Presence and Clarity

**Goal:** Build the leader's ability to project executive presence, and deliver presentations that persuade and motivate action.

### The Four Practices

**1. Cultivate strong personal presence.** Ground yourself physically before any high-stakes presentation: stand tall, breathe slowly, make intentional eye contact with 3–5 individuals for 2–3 seconds each. Speak at 130–150 words per minute. Use 2–3 second pauses after key points.

**2. Use gestures intentionally.** Map key messages to physical gestures in advance. Practise once on video; eliminate self-touching and closed gestures. Keep gestures above the waist.

**3. Adopt a collaborative presenting style.** Shift from broadcast to dialogue. At slide transitions, ask a rhetorical or real question. Invite pushback early: 'Where do you see this differently?'

**4. Apply the 3 Cs — Clear, Concise, Compelling.** Structure every spoken segment: one idea (Clear), under 30 seconds (Concise), opening with a hook (Compelling). Rehearse with a timer.

**KPIs:** Audience recall of top 3 messages at 24 hours > 80%; peer feedback on presence and clarity > 4/5.

## Module III — Build Trust Through Every Conversation

**Goal:** Develop the leader's full communication repertoire — listening, verbal and non-verbal, feedback, questioning, and style-adaptation.

### Seven Practices

**1. Listen with RASA** (Receive, Appreciate, Summarise, Ask). In every 1:1, spend the first 2 minutes listening without interrupting. Wait 3 seconds before responding. Paraphrase before sharing your view.

**2. Replace communication killers with openers.** Audit your language for the seven killers (interrupting, judging, advising prematurely, minimising, distracting, comparing, interrogating). Replace with: validating, curiosity, and appreciation.

**3. Align non-verbal and verbal.** Face the person squarely, lean slightly forward, nod to show comprehension. In video calls, camera at eye level, gaze at the camera.

**4. Prepare before every real conversation.** Spend 5 minutes: What outcome do I want? What does the other person want? My top-3 opening questions. Likely objections and responses.

**5. Give feedback with SBI** (Situation–Behaviour–Impact). Describe the specific Situation, the observable Behaviour, and the concrete Impact. For receiving feedback, use ANNA (Acknowledge, No defend, Note it, Act on it).

**6. Encourage the other person's full story.** Use open questions and silence: 'What happened next?', 'What else?' Resist completing their sentences or jumping to solutions.

**7. Adapt your style to the person using DISC.** Dominants: direct and brief. Influentials: relational warmth. Steadies: slow down, build rapport. Conscientiouses: data and detail.

**KPIs:** 360-feedback score on listening > 4/5; ≥ 2 SBI feedback conversations per direct report per month.

## Module IV — Negotiate to Create Value, Not Just Capture It

**Goal:** A structured, principled three-stage negotiation process that consistently creates value while preserving relationships.

### Three Stages

**Stage 1 — Prepare.** Complete a pre-negotiation prep worksheet: your BATNA, your opening position and walk-away point, the other party's likely interests and BATNA, areas of potential value creation, and your negotiation style.

**Stage 2 — Conduct.** Open by establishing rapport and understanding underlying interests via open questions. Apply principled negotiation (Fisher & Ury, 1981): separate people from the problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options for mutual gain, insist on objective criteria. Use the 'if-then' trade structure.

**Stage 3 — Close.** Summarise agreed points before closing. Address remaining open issues with a specific proposal for each. Secure commitment by setting a next step with a date and owner. Circulate a written summary within 24 hours.

**KPIs:** Deal closed within BATNA boundaries; relationship score from counterpart > 4/5; written summary circulated in 24 hours (100% of negotiations).

## Module V — Protect Your Focus to Do Your Best Work

**Goal:** A personal productivity system that eliminates low-value activity, protects deep-work time, and consistently directs energy to highest-priority tasks.

### Five Practices

**1. Build a disciplined filing system.** Two-level folder structure (domain → project). Apply the 4D rule to email: Delete, Do (< 2 min), Delegate, or Defer. Process email in two daily batches; turn off notifications the rest of the time.

**2. Break procrastination with the 5-minute rule.** Commit to working on an avoided task for only 5 minutes. Identify your procrastination triggers and apply the counter: time-boxing, good-enough standard-setting, or task breakdown.

**3. Protect deep-work windows.** Block 2-hour deep-work windows every morning. During those blocks: email closed, phone silent, 'do not disturb' signal active. Track interruptions for one week; address the top three recurring sources.

**4. Eliminate ineffective meetings.** Meeting audit: Is a decision made? Could this be an email? Is the right attendee list in place? Cancel or halve any meeting failing two of three checks. Mandate an agenda 24 hours in advance and a written summary within 2 hours after.

**5. Prioritise with the Eisenhower Matrix.** Weekly task categorisation on a 2x2: Urgent & Important (Do now), Important & Not Urgent (Schedule), Urgent & Not Important (Delegate), Neither (Eliminate). Target: ≥ 50% of week in quadrant 2 (important, not urgent).

**KPIs:** ≥ 30% of week in deep-work blocks; ≥ 80% of tasks completed on planned day; email response < 24 hours.

## Module VI — Build a Team That Performs Without You Micromanaging

**Goal:** A high-performing, cohesive team with a clear mission, defined ways of working, strong trust, and a culture of coaching and accountability.

### Seven Steps

**Step 1 — Build trust first.** Apply Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions model. Run a 'Personal Histories' exercise. Establish monthly 1:1s with a structured template.

**Step 2 — Define mission, vision, and values together.** 2-hour team workshop. Craft a 1-sentence mission, 2-sentence vision, and 3–5 values with behavioural definitions.

**Step 3 — Set goals with OKRs.** 2–3 Objectives (qualitative, inspiring), 3–5 Key Results per Objective (quantitative). Bi-weekly review; score each KR 0.0–1.0 at end of cycle (target: 0.7).

**Step 4 — Clarify roles with a RACI matrix.** Map every key process and decision to Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. Define 5–7 'ways of working' norms.

**Step 5 — Create the one-page team charter.** Consolidate all of the above into a single A3/A4 poster. Display in team space. Refresh quarterly.

**Step 6 — Coach with GROW.** Monthly 30-minute coaching session per direct report: Goal, Reality, Options, Way Forward. Track commitments in writing.

**Step 7 — Lead change with ADKAR.** Before any change: assess Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement. Address the weakest stage first before moving forward.

**KPIs:** Team trust score (Lencioni survey) > 3.5/5; OKR completion ≥ 70%; team engagement > company average; 100% of direct reports with live GROW coaching plan.

## Module VII — Earn the Support of Every Key Stakeholder

**Goal:** A systematic stakeholder management practice that identifies every influential party and implements tailored engagement plans to secure their support.

### Three Steps

**Step 1 — Map all stakeholders.** Brainstorm every person or group who can influence or be affected by your work. Document each on a stakeholder register.

**Step 2 — Segment by Power and Interest.** Plot each stakeholder on the Power-Interest grid: Manage Closely (high/high), Keep Satisfied (high/low), Keep Informed (low/high), Monitor (low/low).

**Step 3 — Build tailored engagement plans.** For each 'Manage Closely' and 'Keep Satisfied' stakeholder: identify their key interests, your desired outcome, specific engagement actions, frequency, and the message you want them to carry. Review monthly.

**KPIs:** 100% of priority stakeholders with an active engagement plan; satisfaction score > 4/5 for 'Manage Closely' group; zero surprises.

## Module VIII — Facilitate Every Meeting to a Decision

**Goal:** Design and facilitate high-energy, outcome-driven meetings and workshops where every participant contributes and every session produces a clear decision or plan.

### Three Facilitation Moves

**Move 1 — Create the foundation for collaboration.** Open every meeting with: (1) specific purpose, (2) desired output, (3) norms. Post these visibly.

**Move 2 — Keep discussion on track.** Use a visible parking lot for off-topic contributions. Use structured rounds to prevent domination. Summarise progress periodically.

**Move 3 — Get discussion back on track when it derails.** Use: Time-check ('We have 15 minutes left; let's focus on deciding X'), Straw-man ('Let me propose a working answer so we can react to something concrete'), or Vote and move ('Quick show of hands, then minority view, then decide').

**KPIs:** 100% of meetings end with documented decisions and owners; participant satisfaction > 4/5; meeting duration within 10% of plan.

## Module IX — Lead From a Clear Personal Identity

**Goal:** Ground the leader's development in a clear personal identity: a mission statement, long- and short-term goals, and explicit values that guide every day's decisions.

### Four Steps

**Step 1 — Write your personal mission and vision.** Answer: What do I do best? Who do I serve? Why does this matter? Synthesise into a 1–2 sentence mission statement and a 3-year vision.

**Step 2 — Set long- and short-term goals.** 3 SMART long-term goals (3-year horizon) × 3 short-term milestones each (6-month horizon). Weekly 5-minute review of short-term; quarterly review of long-term.

**Step 3 — Define your values with behavioural definitions.** 5–7 values, each with a one-sentence behavioural definition. Identify 2 past situations where you compromised a value — these become 'watch-out' triggers.

**Step 4 — Create your personal development poster.** One A3 poster: photo, mission, vision, values, top-3 development goals. Display where you see it daily. Refresh annually.

**KPIs:** Poster created and displayed; ≥ 60% of weekly actions aligned with long-term goals; annual refresh completed.

## Worked End-to-End Example: Microsoft's Satya Nadella Leadership Transformation

**Situation:** When Satya Nadella became CEO of Microsoft in 2014, the company faced a culture of internal competition ('stack ranking'), unclear strategic direction, and a failing mobile strategy. His leadership transformation programme applied the nine modules sequentially.

**Module I (Problem-solving):** Nadella defined the problem precisely: 'Microsoft is a mobile-first, cloud-first world company that is still operating as a software-licensing world company.' He structured the problem as an issue tree (culture, strategy, portfolio, talent). Prioritised culture as the root-cause issue. The recommendation: shift to a 'growth mindset' culture and pivot to Azure cloud.

**Module III (Communication):** Nadella modelled active listening in town halls — he asked questions and shared vulnerability about his son Zain's disability as a catalyst for empathy. He replaced the communication killer of internal competition with the opener of collaboration ('We are one Microsoft').

**Module VI (Team Management):** He eliminated stack ranking (the structural driver of internal competition — Lencioni's trust deficit), introduced OKRs aligned to Azure growth, defined a new RACI between product groups, and ran GROW-style coaching sessions with his direct reports.

**Module VII (Stakeholders):** Nadella mapped the board (Power: high, Interest: high), enterprise customers (Power: high, Interest: high), and the press (Power: medium, Interest: high). He engaged the board monthly with transparent financial projections and personally called top-20 enterprise CIOs in his first 100 days.

**Module IX (Personal Mission):** Nadella published a clear personal mission: 'Empower every person and every organisation on the planet to achieve more.' He defined three values (growth mindset, empathy, making things happen) with specific behavioural definitions shared company-wide.

**Outcome:** Microsoft's market cap rose from $300B (2014) to $3T (2024). Azure became the #2 cloud platform globally. Employee engagement scores rose from bottom-quartile to top-quartile in the technology sector.

## Top Pitfalls and Best Practices

**Pitfall 1: Solving the wrong problem.** Leaders jump to solutions before confirming the problem is correctly defined. Counter: always validate the problem statement with the decision-maker before structuring the analysis.

**Pitfall 2: Data-driven rather than insight-driven analysis.** Presenting data instead of insights. Counter: write the so-what before building the slide; never put a chart on a slide without an action title.

**Pitfall 3: Confusing position with interest in negotiation.** Fighting over stated positions instead of finding underlying interests. Counter: always ask 'Why is this important to you?' before negotiating on the 'what'.

**Pitfall 4: Treating time management as a tool rather than a system.** Using time-management techniques inconsistently. Counter: build all five practices simultaneously; they reinforce each other.

**Pitfall 5: Building trust after performance problems, not before.** Leaders invest in team trust only when there is a crisis. Counter: run the Lencioni trust-building exercises in the first 30 days of leading any team.

**Pitfall 6: Stakeholder management as a one-time exercise.** Doing the Power-Interest grid once and never updating it. Counter: review the grid quarterly and after every major project event.

**Pitfall 7: Facilitating meetings without a clear output definition.** Starting a meeting without stating what a 'successful outcome' looks like. Counter: always open with 'By the end of this meeting, we will have...'

**Pitfall 8: Personal mission statements that live in a drawer.** Writing a mission but never acting on it. Counter: display the personal development poster where you see it daily and review goals weekly.

**Best practice: Integrate the modules.** The nine modules are designed to reinforce each other. A well-defined personal mission (Module IX) informs how you manage your time (Module V) and coach your team (Module VI). A leader who communicates effectively (Module III) will also negotiate better (Module IV) and facilitate better (Module VIII). Treat the toolkit as a system, not a menu.
