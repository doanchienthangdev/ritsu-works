# ADKAR Change Management Model

*Category: change-management · Toolkit: Leadership Development*

## What it is
A five-stage model for managing organisational change that maps the sequential building blocks required for any individual to change successfully: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.

**Origin:** Developed by Jeff Hiatt, founder of Prosci, published in 'ADKAR: A Model for Change' (2006). Emerged from Prosci's benchmarking research of hundreds of change projects. Now the most widely taught change management framework in corporate training programmes globally.

## Why it works
Change programmes fail not because the strategy is wrong but because individuals resist or are unable to change. ADKAR identifies five reasons an individual does not change: lack of Awareness (why), Desire (wanting to), Knowledge (how), Ability (can they), or Reinforcement (will it stick). By diagnosing the weakest stage, leaders target interventions precisely.

## When to use
At the start of any significant organisational change initiative. Also use to diagnose why a change programme is stalling mid-implementation.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Before communicating the change, conduct an ADKAR assessment: survey/interview a representative sample and score each stage 1–5. Identify the lowest-scoring stage.
2. Address Awareness first if score < 3: develop clear, honest communication about why the change is necessary. Deliver from the most credible leader.
3. Address Desire if Awareness is solid but Desire is low: identify the coalition of the willing; hold listening sessions to address fears.
4. Address Knowledge if Desire is solid but Knowledge is low: design training, job aids, and reference materials.
5. Address Ability if Knowledge is high but Ability is low: remove structural barriers; provide coaching during the transition.
6. Address Reinforcement last but plan it first: before launch, redesign performance management and incentives to reinforce the new behaviour.

## Real-life example — Microsoft — elimination of stack ranking under Satya Nadella (2014)
Stack ranking was eliminated via all five ADKAR stages: Awareness — Nadella communicated how it harmed collaboration in his first all-company email. Desire — personal vulnerability story (son Zain) made the cultural shift resonant. Knowledge — HR redesigned performance management training for growth-mindset behaviours. Ability — managers received coaching to have development conversations without forced distribution. Reinforcement — new system explicitly rewarded collaborative behaviour.

**So what:** Most culture change fails at Reinforcement: the communication and training change but the incentive system doesn't. The old system then quietly reasserts itself within 12 months.

## Pitfalls
- Training as the default response: If the root cause is Desire or Ability, more training won't help. Always diagnose the correct stage before prescribing.
- Planning Reinforcement at the end: By the time the change team gets to it, energy is exhausted. Plan Reinforcement in the design phase, not as an afterthought.
- Treating the population as homogeneous: Different individuals will be at different ADKAR stages simultaneously. Segment your interventions.
