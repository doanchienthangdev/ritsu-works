# BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement)

*Category: negotiation · Toolkit: Leadership Development*

## What it is
The most attractive alternative action available to a negotiating party if the current negotiation fails — the real source of negotiating power and the true determinant of any party's walk-away point.

**Origin:** Coined by Roger Fisher and William Ury in 'Getting to Yes' (1981). BATNA has become the single most important concept in negotiation theory, taught in every major business school and adopted across industries from M&A to labour relations.

## Why it works
Negotiating power does not come from position, authority, or emotional intensity — it comes from the quality of your alternatives. A negotiator with a strong BATNA (an excellent alternative if the deal falls through) can walk away at any moment, which paradoxically makes agreement more likely because the other party knows they cannot exploit them. A negotiator with a weak BATNA is vulnerable to exploitation regardless of their positional strength.

## When to use
In every negotiation, without exception — from salary negotiation to supplier contracts to partnership terms. Also use as a diagnostic when you feel pressured in a negotiation: ask 'what is my real BATNA?' to recalibrate your walk-away point.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Before any negotiation, write down every alternative available to you if no agreement is reached. Be specific — not 'find another supplier' but 'Supplier B has quoted $X for equivalent quality.'
2. Assess each alternative realistically: what would you actually do? What would it cost (time, money, relationship, reputation)? Select the single best alternative — this is your BATNA.
3. Estimate the other party's BATNA using available information: Who else might they sell to? What are their alternatives to reaching an agreement with you? A counterpart with a weak BATNA has more to lose from no deal — this is leverage.
4. Calculate your 'Reservation Point' (walk-away point): this is the minimum outcome that is still better than your BATNA. Any deal above this point is better than no deal; any deal below it should be declined.
5. Consider how to strengthen your BATNA before entering the negotiation: Can you create a competing offer? Can you reduce your dependence on this deal? A stronger BATNA is worth more than any negotiation tactic.
6. During the negotiation, never disclose your walk-away point explicitly — but signal your BATNA's strength through confident body language and willingness to pause or reconvene.

## Real-life example — Amazon — negotiations with Hachette (book publisher) in 2014
In 2014, Amazon negotiated e-book pricing terms with Hachette. Amazon had a strong BATNA: it could de-prioritise Hachette titles in search results (which it did, causing a public dispute) and could self-publish or sign directly with authors. Hachette's BATNA was weaker: Amazon accounted for approximately 30% of its US book sales, and no alternative channel offered equivalent reach. The negotiation lasted months; Amazon's stronger BATNA enabled it to eventually achieve more favourable e-book pricing terms, though Hachette ultimately secured some protections after the public controversy raised reputational costs for Amazon.

**So what:** BATNA determines the real negotiating power in any room. Assess both yours and the counterpart's before starting — the negotiation outcome is largely determined before anyone speaks.

## Pitfalls
- Confusing BATNA with opening position: BATNA is what you do if there is NO deal — not what you open with. Anchoring your opening position to your BATNA leaves value on the table (your opening should be more ambitious).
- Ignoring the counterpart's BATNA: Many negotiators focus only on their own alternatives and fail to assess the other party's. Counterpart's BATNA directly determines their urgency and flexibility.
- Failing to improve the BATNA before negotiating: The best negotiation preparation is often not practising negotiation tactics but strengthening your alternative (e.g., getting a competing offer, reducing dependency on this deal).
