# GROW Coaching Model

*Category: coaching · Toolkit: Leadership Development*

## What it is
A four-stage coaching conversation structure (Goal, Reality, Options, Way Forward) that enables a coach to help a coachee find their own solutions through structured questioning rather than advice-giving.

**Origin:** Developed by Graham Alexander, Alan Fine, and Sir John Whitmore in the 1980s and popularised in Whitmore's 'Coaching for Performance' (1992, 5th edition 2009). The most widely used coaching framework in executive coaching and leadership development globally.

## Why it works
Adults learn and commit to change most durably when they arrive at solutions themselves. The GROW model operationalises this: structured questions in sequence build self-awareness (Reality), generate options the coachee might not have considered (Options), and create commitment to specific actions (Way Forward). The coach's role is not to have answers but to ask questions that unlock the coachee's own thinking.

## When to use
In monthly 1:1 coaching conversations with direct reports. When a team member faces a development challenge or difficult decision requiring behavioural change.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Set the context: 'This is your time — I'm here to help you think through something. What would be most useful to focus on today?'
2. Establish the Goal: 'What do you want to achieve in this conversation?' + 'How does this relate to your broader development goal?' Write both down.
3. Explore Reality with open questions only: 'What is the current situation?' 'What have you tried?' Do not offer your own assessment at this stage. Listen with RASA.
4. Generate Options by inviting divergent thinking: 'What are all the things you could do?' List every option without evaluating. Aim for 5+ options.
5. Move to Way Forward: 'Which option feels right to you and why?' Then: 'What specific action will you take? By when? What support do you need?'
6. Close by summarising commitments out loud: 'So you're going to do X by Y. Is that right?' Check for genuine commitment — energy and body language should match the verbal agreement.

## Real-life example — Unilever — Leadership Development Programme (2018)
Unilever incorporated GROW as a core manager capability. In one example, a Supply Chain Director used GROW to retain a high-performing engineer who had turned down two promotions. GROW session surfaced that the engineer wanted innovation work. The Way Forward: a 20%-time innovation project with clear ownership. The engineer stayed for 22 additional months.

**So what:** GROW shifts the manager from expert-with-answers to thought-partner-with-questions. This produces more durable commitment and builds the coachee's self-efficacy over time.

## Template
Use for every formal coaching session. Duration: 30–60 min. Monthly for each direct report. Review commitments at the start of the next session.

- [ ] Coachee: _________ | Date: _________ | Session #: _________
- [ ] G — Goal for session: _________ | Related development goal: _________
- [ ] R — Reality (key facts from coachee's account): _________ | Obstacles: _________
- [ ] O — Options generated (list all): 1. __ 2. __ 3. __ 4. __ 5. __
- [ ]   Option chosen by coachee: _________
- [ ] W — Way Forward: Action 1: _________ | By: _________ | Support needed: _________
- [ ]   Potential obstacle: _________ | Mitigation: _________
- [ ]   Check-in date: _________

## Pitfalls
- Skipping Goal stage: Jumping straight to Reality leaves the session unfocused. Always agree the session goal first.
- Advice-giving in Options stage: The coach's instinct is to share the right answer. Hold advice unless the coachee genuinely cannot generate options after extended effort.
- Weak Way Forward: 'I'll think about it' is not a commitment. Require specific actions with specific dates.
