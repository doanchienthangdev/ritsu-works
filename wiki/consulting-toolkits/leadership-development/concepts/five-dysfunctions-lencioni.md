# The Five Dysfunctions of a Team (Lencioni Model)

*Category: team-management · Toolkit: Leadership Development*

## What it is
A hierarchical model identifying five root causes of team failure — absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, and inattention to results — and the interventions needed to address each in sequence from the foundation up.

**Origin:** Developed by Patrick Lencioni and published in 'The Five Dysfunctions of a Team' (2002, Jossey-Bass). One of the most widely cited team effectiveness frameworks in organisational development globally.

## Why it works
Dysfunctions are hierarchical: you cannot fix Dysfunction 2 (fear of conflict) without first addressing Dysfunction 1 (absence of trust). The model's power is in its diagnostic sequence: identify the lowest unsatisfied level and address that first.

## When to use
At the start of leading a new team. When team performance is below expectations and the cause traces to dynamic rather than skill. When an existing team needs a reset after a significant event.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Administer the Lencioni Team Assessment (free at tablegroup.com) to the whole team. Score each dysfunction 1–5.
2. Address Dysfunction 1 (Trust) with the Personal Histories exercise: each member shares three personal facts. The leader goes first and models vulnerability.
3. Address Dysfunction 2 (Conflict) by mining for conflict: ask 'What are we not saying?' and 'Who sees this differently?' in team meetings.
4. Address Dysfunction 3 (Commitment) by making decisions clearly in every meeting: 'We have decided X, by Y, owned by Z.'
5. Address Dysfunction 4 (Accountability) by building team behavioural norms and calling each other out respectfully when violated.
6. Address Dysfunction 5 (Results) by making team goals visible and reviewing them regularly — celebrate collective wins, not individual recognition.

## Real-life example — Google — Project Aristotle (2012–2016)
Google's 2-year study (Project Aristotle) found that psychological safety — the team equivalent of Lencioni's Dysfunction 1 (absence of trust) — was the single strongest predictor of team effectiveness. Teams where members felt safe to take interpersonal risks significantly outperformed teams with identical talent but low psychological safety.

**So what:** Building trust is not soft — it is the single highest-leverage team investment. Every other team improvement is limited by the trust level.

## Pitfalls
- Skipping the foundation: Addressing accountability without building trust first. Accountability without trust feels like punishment. Build from the base up.
- One-time intervention: Running the Personal Histories exercise once and declaring trust built. Trust is built through consistent vulnerability over months.
- Confusing harmony with health: A team that never argues is likely suppressing important issues. Artificial harmony (Dysfunction 2) is a warning sign.
