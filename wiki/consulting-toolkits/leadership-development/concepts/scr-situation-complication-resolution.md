# SCR (Situation-Complication-Resolution) Narrative Structure

*Category: communication · Toolkit: Leadership Development*

## What it is
A three-part story structure for opening any presentation or communication: first establish the shared context (Situation), then introduce the tension or problem (Complication), then deliver the recommendation or answer (Resolution) — creating an engaging narrative arc that makes the audience receptive to the content.

**Origin:** The SCR structure is derived from Barbara Minto's narrative framework in 'The Pyramid Principle' (1987), adapted from classical story structure (Gustav Freytag's dramatic arc, 1863) for business communication. It is sometimes called the 'SCQA' structure (Situation-Complication-Question-Answer). McKinsey, BCG, and Bain use it as the standard opening structure for all client presentations.

## Why it works
The human brain is wired for narrative: it engages with stories (which have a complication) far more readily than with information (which does not). By framing a business presentation as a mini-story — we were here (Situation), something changed (Complication), here is what we should do (Resolution) — the presenter creates the conditions for the audience to be receptive to the recommendation before a single data point is shown. The Complication is the most important element: without it, there is no reason to change anything.

## When to use
As the opening structure for every executive presentation, board paper, strategy memo, or stakeholder briefing where a recommendation or decision is being sought. Also use for email communications to senior stakeholders when a specific action or decision is required.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Write the SCR as three sentences — one for each element — before building any slides. The discipline of writing it as three sentences forces clarity.
2. Test the Situation: 'Would everyone in the room agree this is true, right now, without my having to prove it?' If no, it is not the Situation — it is data that needs to be presented. Simplify until it is genuinely shared context.
3. Test the Complication: 'Does this create a genuine tension or problem that requires action?' If the audience could hear the Complication and reasonably say 'so what?', the complication is not strong enough. The complication should make the audience think 'that is a problem — what should we do?'
4. Test the Resolution: 'Is this my main recommendation — the apex of my pyramid?' If the Resolution requires more than two sentences to state, you are not at the right level of abstraction. State the recommendation; the supporting arguments come in the slides that follow.
5. Deliver the SCR as the verbal opening of your presentation — before showing any slides, or as the content of your first slide (titled with an action title such as 'Entry-price competition has eroded 4pp of share and requires an immediate two-part response').
6. Practise the SCR aloud until it flows as a natural 60-second story. If it sounds written (stiff and formal) rather than spoken (natural and direct), revise the language.

## Real-life example — McKinsey & Company — merger integration recommendation to European telecoms CEO (composite, McKinsey Quarterly 2022)
In a 30-minute board meeting on a potential acquisition, the McKinsey team opened with the SCR structure: S — 'The mobile market in Europe has consolidated from 6 to 4 major players over the past decade, and your company has maintained a stable 18% share at above-average margins.' C — 'The proposed acquisition of TelcoX would immediately give you a 26% share and access to their fibre infrastructure — but at a price that assumes 35% cost synergies materialising within 24 months, which our analysis suggests is optimistic by approximately 50%.' R — 'We recommend pursuing the acquisition at a 15% lower price point, contingent on a renegotiated synergy timeline. If the vendor does not accept, our second recommendation is to pursue organic network investment as a superior capital allocation.' The board approved the negotiation mandate in the same meeting.

**So what:** The SCR structure works because it respects the audience's intelligence — it assumes they know the Situation (so you don't waste their time with background they already have) and trusts them to understand the Complication before you pitch the Resolution. This signals that you have done the analytical work and are not here to impress, but to advise.

## Template
Write the SCR for every major presentation before building the slide deck. The three sentences should be the first slide (with an action title summarising the Resolution) or the verbal opening.

- [ ] Presentation: _________ | Date: _________ | Audience: _________
- [ ] S — Situation (agreed, shared context, 1-2 sentences): _________
- [ ] C — Complication (the tension/problem requiring action, 1-2 sentences): _________
- [ ] R — Resolution (the main recommendation, 1-2 sentences): _________
- [ ] Combined SCR (read aloud — should take 60-90 seconds): _________
- [ ] Tests: Situation agreed by audience? [Yes/No] | Complication creates urgency? [Yes/No] | Resolution is the apex of the pyramid? [Yes/No]

## Pitfalls
- Starting with context the audience already knows: A Situation that spends 3 minutes explaining the background the audience already lived wastes their time and creates the impression that you are padding rather than advising. If they know the Situation, state it in one sentence and move to the Complication.
- Weak Complication: The most common SCR failure is a Complication that does not compel action. 'Costs have increased slightly' is not a compelling complication. 'Costs have increased 18% while revenue has grown 3%, creating a structural margin compression that will eliminate profitability within 18 months if not addressed' is compelling.
- Resolution that is too vague: 'We need to do something about our cost structure' is not a Resolution — it is an acknowledgement of the Complication. The Resolution must name a specific action or decision: 'We recommend exiting the distribution business and redirecting $50M in capital to the digital sales channel within 12 months.'
