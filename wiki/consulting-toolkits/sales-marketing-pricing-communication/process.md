# Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication Toolkit

**One-liner:** End-to-end commercial strategy: diagnose the market, set strategy, build five go-to-market pillars, and implement with rigour.

**Core value:** A commercial leader can run the entire go-to-market cycle — from market sizing through brand positioning, pricing architecture, distribution design, digital execution, and change management — using a single sequenced, gate-controlled process rather than a disconnected bag of frameworks.

---

## Use This Toolkit When You Need to Solve a Commercial Problem

This toolkit is the right instrument when your organisation faces any of the following triggers:

- **Launching a product or entering a new market** and needing a validated, evidence-based go-to-market plan before committing capital.
- **Repositioning a brand** facing competitive pressure, declining relevance, or a new target segment.
- **Addressing margin erosion** caused by undifferentiated pricing, excessive discounting, or competitive price wars.
- **Redesigning the sales or marketing function** — headcount, structure, channels, or budget allocation.
- **Planning a commercial transformation** — a multi-year programme to move from operational to strategic commercial capability.

**Primary audience:** Chief Commercial Officer, CMO, VP Sales, Head of Pricing, Strategy Director, Management Consultant, Entrepreneur.

## Phase 1: Build the Commercial Strategy on Evidence

**Goal:** Establish a grounded-in-data strategy by diagnosing the market, competitors, and internal capabilities, then setting a vision, objectives, and resource plan for the Sales and Marketing functions.

**Key question:** Where do we compete, why will we win, and what does the Sales & Marketing function need to look like to get there?

**Duration:** 4–6 weeks.

### Step 1.1 — Market Analysis: Establish the Market Fact-Base

Collect market-size data (TAM/SAM/SOM), segment the market by geography, customer type, and behaviour, and map growth trends using PESTLE and Porter's Five Forces. Synthesise into a one-page market fact-base with a heat-map of attractive segments.

**Input:** Industry reports, syndicated data (Euromonitor, Nielsen), internal CRM exports.
**Output:** Market fact-base: size, growth, segmentation, and attractiveness heat-map.
**Owner:** Strategy / Market-Intelligence team.
**Frameworks used:** Porter's Five Forces, PESTLE Analysis, TAM/SAM/SOM.

### Step 1.2 — Competitor Analysis: Map the Competitive Landscape

Identify the top 5–10 direct and indirect competitors. For each, map positioning, pricing, channels, strengths, and weaknesses using a Competitive Profiling Matrix. Apply the GE-McKinsey Matrix to assess competitive intensity versus market attractiveness per segment.

**Input:** Market fact-base, publicly available competitor financials, mystery-shopping data.
**Output:** Competitive landscape map + GE-McKinsey portfolio plot.
**Owner:** Strategy / Market-Intelligence team.
**Frameworks used:** GE-McKinsey Matrix, Competitive Profiling Matrix.

### Step 1.3 — Sales Analysis: Identify the Revenue Pareto

Pull 3 years of sales data segmented by product, channel, customer, and region. Calculate growth rates, win/loss ratios, and margin by segment. Identify the top 20% of customers and products driving 80% of revenue using Pareto Analysis.

**Input:** CRM data, ERP sales transactions, customer P&L.
**Output:** Sales performance dashboard with Pareto analysis and revenue gap map.
**Owner:** Sales Director / Revenue Operations.
**Frameworks used:** Pareto Analysis, Sales & Marketing Funnel.

### Step 1.4 — Marketing Analysis: Diagnose Channel ROI

Audit all active marketing channels: measure ROI per channel (CAC, CPL, conversion), brand awareness, and NPS. Benchmark spend allocation against industry norms.

**Input:** Marketing spend data, Google Analytics, brand tracking surveys.
**Output:** Marketing channel effectiveness matrix with ROI benchmarks.
**Owner:** CMO / Marketing Director.
**Frameworks used:** Marketing ROI Analysis.

### Step 1.5 — Funnel Process Mapping: Find the Biggest Conversion Gap

Map the current end-to-end customer journey from awareness through purchase and retention. Quantify conversion rates at each stage. Identify the top three drop-off points and root causes.

**Input:** CRM pipeline data, web analytics, call-centre logs.
**Output:** Funnel diagnostic with current vs. target conversion rates per stage.
**Owner:** Revenue Operations / Marketing Ops.
**Frameworks used:** Sales & Marketing Funnel.

### Step 1.6 — Company SWOT Analysis: Generate Strategic Options

Run a structured cross-functional workshop to populate the SWOT grid. Run SO/ST/WO/WT cross-pair analysis to generate concrete strategic options.

**Input:** Market fact-base, competitor landscape, sales analysis.
**Output:** Prioritised SWOT with four strategic option directions.
**Owner:** Cross-functional leadership team, facilitated by Strategy.
**Frameworks used:** SWOT Analysis.

### Step 1.7 — Strategy Framework Selection: Choose the Direction

Select the appropriate growth framework based on strategic context: Ansoff Matrix for growth direction, Blue Ocean Strategy for escaping competitive pressure, STP for clarifying positioning. Apply and stress-test with leadership.

**Input:** SWOT output, market and competitor analysis.
**Output:** Chosen strategic direction with supporting rationale.
**Owner:** CCO / Strategy Director.
**Frameworks used:** Ansoff Matrix, Blue Ocean Strategy, STP Model.

### Step 1.8–1.10 — Vision, Objectives & Budget

Draft Vision, Mission & Values for Sales and Marketing functions. Translate into SMART strategic objectives and KPIs using the Balanced Scorecard. Design the organisational structure and zero-based budget aligned to the strategy.

**Output:** VMV charters, Balanced Scorecard, approved org structure and 3-year budget.

**Phase 1 Deliverable:** Commercial Strategy Document.

**Phase 1 Gate:** Executive leadership approves strategic direction, VMV, KPIs, and budget. Three gate questions: Is the market analysis grounded in data? Is the strategic direction unambiguous? Are KPIs measurable?

## Phase 2: Design the Five Pillars

**Goal:** Translate the approved strategy into five executable go-to-market pillars.

**Duration:** 6–10 weeks.

### Pillar 1 — Product/Service Positioning & Value Proposition

Define the target market using the STP model. Map the offering against the Product Life Cycle. Articulate key attributes, benefits, and the USP. Write the value proposition using the Value Proposition Canvas. Design packaging and bundling options.

**Key outputs:** Positioning document with USP, value proposition statement, packaging and bundling options.

### Pillar 2 — Communication Strategy

Clarify context and objectives. Define 3–5 overarching messages using the Pyramid Principle. Map stakeholders using the Stakeholder Analysis Power-Interest Grid. Select channels by audience. Build a risk-log. Produce high-level and detailed communication plans using the Communication Plan Matrix.

**Key outputs:** Communication Strategy deck + high-level and detailed communication plans (11 elements).

### Pillar 3 — Sales Distribution Strategy

Evaluate eight distribution models against five criteria using the Distribution Channel Matrix. Select the optimal channel mix with supporting economics. Design the sales team structure.

**Key outputs:** Distribution Strategy with selected channel mix, channel economics model, sales team structure.

### Pillar 4 — Pricing Strategy

Assess pricing maturity (Level 1–5 using the Pricing Maturity Model). Select the pricing strategy (penetration, premium, freemium, value-based, competitive, or dynamic) using the Pricing Strategy Selection Ladder. Validate with Van Westendorp PSM survey data. Build the price architecture and discounting policy.

**Key outputs:** Pricing Architecture document: strategy rationale, price points, tiers, discount policy, WTP evidence, monitoring dashboard.

### Pillar 5 — Digital Strategy

Audit current digital presence across SEO, PPC, Social, Email, and Website using the Digital Marketing Mix framework. For each channel, define objectives, audience, content calendar, budget, and KPIs. Ensure brand guidelines govern all digital assets.

**Key outputs:** Digital Strategy with channel-by-channel plans.

**Phase 2 Deliverable:** Five Pillar Strategy Pack.

**Phase 2 Gate:** CCO and executive team sign off all five pillars. Gate check: Are the five pillars internally consistent with Phase 1 strategy and mutually reinforcing?

---

## Phase 3: Generate and Filter Potential Initiatives

**Goal:** Generate a long-list of 75–100 initiatives and filter to the 15–20 highest-value for business-case development.

**Duration:** 2–3 weeks.

**Steps:** (1) Ideation workshops per pillar (15–20 ideas each). (2) Effort-Impact scoring matrix: plot all initiatives, classify into Quick Wins, Strategic Bets, Fill-Ins, Hard Slogs. (3) Ansoff strategic fit test: discard initiatives inconsistent with the chosen direction.

**Phase 3 Deliverable:** Prioritised initiative shortlist with impact scores and strategic fit rationale.

**Phase 3 Gate:** CCO approves shortlist. Gate check: Is there a clear rationale for each included and excluded initiative?

---

## Phase 4: Build the Business Cases

**Goal:** Build rigorous financial models for each shortlisted initiative so investment decisions are evidence-based.

**Duration:** 3–4 weeks.

**Steps:** (1) Design a standard business case template approved by CFO. (2) Build NPV/IRR/payback financial model per initiative with 3 scenarios. (3) Rank all initiatives using a composite score (NPV 40%, Strategic Alignment 30%, Risk 20%, Speed to Value 10%).

**Phase 4 Deliverable:** Business Case Pack with individual models and aggregate portfolio ranking.

**Phase 4 Gate:** CFO and CCO approve the funded programme. Gate check: Are assumptions stress-tested? Is total investment within board-approved budget?

---

## Phase 5: Prioritise, Plan, and Implement

**Goal:** Sequence and execute the funded portfolio with governed, adaptive delivery.

**Duration:** 2 weeks for roadmap; 12–36 months for delivery.

**Steps:** (1) Weighted criteria sequencing → 90-day / 6-month / 12-month horizons. (2) 3-year swimlane roadmap with milestones and critical path. (3) Governance design using RACI (Steering Committee monthly, Programme Board bi-weekly, project stand-ups weekly). (4) Live delivery dashboard tracking RAG status, milestone completion, and benefits realisation. (5) Methodology selection: Agile for digital/marketing initiatives, Design Thinking for new product development, traditional waterfall for infrastructure. (6) Post-project evaluation comparing actual vs. forecast benefits.

**Phase 5 Gate:** Steering Committee confirms 80%+ of milestones on track before releasing next tranche of budget.

---

## Phase 6: Manage Change and Stakeholder Engagement

**Goal:** Ensure the people affected by the commercial transformation understand, accept, and sustain the changes.

**Duration:** 12–24 months, running in parallel with Phases 2–5.

**Steps:** (1) Change Impact Assessment heat-map. (2) Stakeholder Engagement Strategy using the Power-Interest Grid. (3) ADKAR change management plans per impacted population. (4) Internal Communication Plan using the Communication Plan Matrix (message architecture + channel mix + calendar). (5) Adoption tracking dashboard (leading indicators: training completion, survey sentiment, process adherence). (6) Reinforcement: embed changes into performance management, SOPs, and onboarding.

**Phase 6 Gate:** Change readiness score exceeds 70% across all impacted populations before each major go-live milestone.

## The Frameworks & Tools

### Porter's Five Forces
Analyses the competitive structure of an industry across five dimensions: Threat of New Entrants, Supplier Power, Buyer Power, Threat of Substitutes, and Rivalry. Use at the start of any commercial strategy to establish structural context before making choices. The hub-and-spoke visual (Industry Rivalry at centre, four forces as spokes) is the standard output.

### PESTLE Analysis
Macro-environment scan across Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental dimensions. Produces a prioritised table of macro factors feeding into the SWOT's Opportunities and Threats columns. Run with a cross-functional team to prevent single-lens blind spots.

### TAM/SAM/SOM
Three-tier market sizing: Total Addressable Market (global demand), Serviceable Addressable Market (what your model can reach), Serviceable Obtainable Market (realistic 3–5 year capture). The SOM must be bottom-up anchored to be credible — triangulate with top-down estimates.

### GE-McKinsey Matrix
Nine-cell portfolio matrix (3×3) scoring business units or products on Industry Attractiveness (5–7 weighted factors) and Competitive Strength (5–7 weighted factors). Three zones: Invest/Grow, Selectively Invest/Hold, Harvest/Divest. More nuanced than BCG's 2×2 because it uses multi-factor scoring, not proxy metrics alone.

### Competitive Profiling Matrix (CPM)
Structured table benchmarking your organisation against competitors across 8–12 Critical Success Factors with weights summing to 1.0. Weighted scores reveal genuine competitive gaps — the priority areas for investment.

### SWOT Analysis
Internal Strengths & Weaknesses + External Opportunities & Threats. Value is in the cross-pair analysis (SO/ST/WO/WT), not the grid itself — each cross-pair generates a concrete strategic option direction.

### Ansoff Matrix
2×2 growth direction framework: Market Penetration (lowest risk), Market Development, Product Development, Diversification (highest risk). Used both to choose the primary growth direction and to test strategic fit of initiatives in Phase 3.

### Blue Ocean Strategy (ERRC Grid + Strategy Canvas)
Escape competitive pressure by creating uncontested market space using the Four Actions Framework: Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create. The Strategy Canvas plots competing factors on x-axis and performance on y-axis — a blue ocean move creates a new curve that diverges from the competitive pack.

### STP Model
Segment (divide market into homogeneous groups) → Target (select 1–3 priority segments on attractiveness criteria) → Position (write the distinctive positioning statement using Geoffrey Moore's template). The output positioning statement drives all downstream marketing decisions.

### Balanced Scorecard
Translates strategy into objectives and KPIs across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, and Learning & Growth. The strategy map (L&G → Process → Customer → Financial cause-and-effect chain) is the most important output — it shows why L&G investment is a financial decision.

### Value Proposition Canvas
Customer Profile (Jobs, Pains, Gains) × Value Map (Products/Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators). Fit is achieved when Pain Relievers address the top-ranked Pains and Gain Creators address the top-ranked Gains. Complete the Customer Profile before the Value Map to avoid building-first thinking.

### Product Life Cycle (PLC)
Four stages — Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline — each with distinct marketing, pricing, and distribution strategies. The most expensive error is classifying a Declining product as Mature and continuing to invest in share capture. Use 3 years of sales and margin trend data to classify objectively.

### Perceptual Map
Two-dimensional scatter plot mapping brands on the two most important category attributes (from customer research). Identifies white space (unoccupied positioning opportunities) and overcrowding (commodity traps). Use customer survey data for coordinates — avoid using internal assumptions.

### Pricing Maturity Model
Five-level model from Level 1 (cost-plus, reactive) to Level 5 (dynamic, AI-driven). Diagnose current level and define a target level 1–2 levels above current, achievable in 18–24 months. The most common jump that delivers the highest EBITDA improvement is Level 2→Level 4 (competitive to value-based).

### Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM)
Four-question survey producing four cumulative frequency curves. Key outputs: Acceptable Price Range (lower and upper bounds), Indifference Price Point (IPP), and Optimal Price Point (OPP). Use as evidence for any value-based pricing move or new product launch price-setting.

### Pricing Strategy Selection Ladder
Six canonical pricing strategies (Penetration, Competitive, Premium/Skimming, Freemium, Value-Based, Dynamic) with diagnostic questions to select the right one. The key risk: selecting Penetration when you have a differentiated product leaves significant margin on the table.

### Digital Marketing Mix
Five-channel framework (SEO, PPC, Social Media, Email, Website) with channel-specific roles, KPIs, and optimisation levers. Channels must be integrated (SEO drives traffic; email converts; PPC captures high-intent) rather than operated as independent silos.

### Communication Plan Matrix
Structured table organising communications by Message, Audience, Channel, Sender, and Timing. Sequencing rule: managers must be informed before their teams — violating this creates the 'employees know more than their managers' credibility crisis.

### Sales Distribution Channel Selection Matrix
Weighted scoring of eight distribution models (Retail, Wholesale, Online, Own Stores, Franchising, Partnerships, Multi-channel, Direct Sales) against five criteria. Channel economics model (unit contribution by channel) is the essential companion output.

### Stakeholder Analysis (Power-Interest Grid)
Four-quadrant grid: Manage Closely (high power, high interest), Keep Satisfied (high power, low interest), Keep Informed (low power, high interest), Monitor (low power, low interest). Hidden stakeholders (informal power, trusted advisors) are the most commonly missed.

### ADKAR Model
Five-stage individual change model: Awareness → Desire → Knowledge → Ability → Reinforcement. Interventions must address the lowest-scoring (barrier) stage first — you cannot skip stages. Measure adoption with Ability (A4) as the primary outcome metric, not training completion.

### Effort-Impact Matrix
2×2 prioritisation tool: Quick Wins (high impact, low effort — do first), Strategic Bets (high impact, high effort — plan carefully), Fill-Ins (low impact, low effort — backlog), Hard Slogs (low impact, high effort — avoid). Quick Wins build political capital to fund Strategic Bets.

### NPV / IRR / Payback Financial Model
Three financial metrics for investment evaluation. NPV answers 'how much value?' (use for absolute decisions). IRR answers 'what is the return rate?' (use for comparison). Payback answers 'when do we get our money back?' (use for liquidity). Always run sensitivity analysis on the top 2–3 assumptions.

### RACI Model
Responsibility assignment matrix: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome — exactly one per activity), Consulted (provides input), Informed (kept updated). Common failures: multiple A's per row (accountability confusion) and roles with >30% C assignments (consultation fatigue).

### Agile / Scrum
Iterative 2-week sprint delivery framework with Planning, Daily Stand-up, Review, and Retrospective ceremonies. Measure velocity (story points per sprint) over 3–5 sprints to establish a reliable planning baseline. Apply to digital, marketing, and product development initiatives.

### Design Thinking
Five-stage human-centred innovation process: Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test. Non-linear — iterate between stages based on testing insights. Do not skip Empathise: solutions designed without deep customer understanding solve the wrong problem.

## Worked End-to-End Example: Ritsu (AI EdTech, 2026)

**Context:** Ritsu is a B2C AI tutoring platform (ritsu.ai) targeting US-led English-speaking serious learners. It faces increasing competition from generic AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini) as substitutes, and from study apps (Quizlet, Anki) as direct competitors. The founder needs a full commercial strategy to reach 100 paying users who love the product.

**Phase 1 — Commercial Strategy:**

*Market Analysis:* TAM for self-directed digital learning = ~$80bn globally (HolonIQ). SAM (English-language, digital-first, WTP $15–120/year) = ~$12bn. SOM in 3 years (0.1% SAM) = ~$12m ARR. Porter's Five Forces: Buyer Power — High (free alternatives including ChatGPT); Rivalry — Medium (specialised study apps lack AI depth); New Entrants — High (large AI companies could enter). Dominant force: Buyer Power, driven by near-zero switching cost.

*Competitor Analysis:* CPM across 8 CSFs (AI quality, content depth, engagement mechanics, mobile UX, pricing, content breadth, personalisation, brand trust). Ritsu outscores ChatGPT on content-specific depth (+1.5 points) and engagement mechanics (+1.8 points), but underperforms on brand awareness (-2.1 points) and pricing transparency (-1.2 points).

*SWOT and Strategy:* Strength — AI-native architecture enables 17+ activity types from any source. Opportunity — US college exam season creates a recurring, high-WTP trigger. Blue Ocean move: eliminate generic AI-chat format; reduce flashcard commoditisation; raise learning-science rigour (cited sources, Feynman explanations); create 30-second magic moment (from upload to first quiz). Strategic direction: penetrate the US college STEM exam-prep segment with aggressive content marketing and freemium pricing.

*Balanced Scorecard:* Financial — reach $1m ARR by month 18. Customer — NPS >50, D30 retention >25%. Process — time-to-first-quiz <60 seconds. L&G — publish 50 exam-prep SEO articles per month.

**Phase 2 — Five Pillars:**

*Positioning:* Target — US college STEM student with exam in 2–14 days. USP — 'Upload any PDF or video; in 30 seconds get an AI tutor that quizzes you, explains concepts in Feynman style, and helps you truly understand.' Value proposition validated by 12 customer interviews showing 'understanding vs. memorising' as the primary unmet need.

*Pricing:* Pricing Maturity Level 2 → target Level 4 (value-based). PSM survey (n=150 US college students): Acceptable Range $5–$25/month; OPP = $10; IPP = $12. Price architecture: Free (600 credits), Plus $29/month (12,000 credits), Pro $59/month (25,000 credits). Van Westendorp evidence supports $29 Plus tier as within the acceptable range for the target segment.

*Digital Strategy:* SEO — 50 articles/month targeting 'how to ace [subject] exam' and '[textbook name] quiz' keywords. PPC — Google Ads on high-intent terms ('AI study tool', 'exam prep app'), target ROAS 3×. Email — 7-day onboarding sequence from sign-up to first paid conversion.

**Phase 3 — Initiatives:** Long-list of 45 initiatives across five pillars. Quick Wins: 'Share your quiz' viral loop feature (high impact, low engineering effort), SEO article sprint (high impact, low cost). Strategic Bets: knowledge map feature (high impact, high development effort). Hard Slogs dropped: enterprise/school partnerships (low near-term impact, high sales effort at current stage).

**Phase 4 — Business Cases:** Viral loop feature NPV = $180k at 3 years, IRR = 340%, payback 2 months. SEO article sprint NPV = $420k at 3 years, payback 4 months. Both approved by founder (who acts as CFO at this stage).

**Phase 5 — Roadmap:** 90-day horizon: viral loop + SEO sprint. 6-month: PPC launch + email optimisation. 12-month: knowledge map feature + paid retention programme.

**Phase 6 — Change Management:** N/A for a founding-stage company (no large workforce change). Applied to content creators and early customer success processes: ADKAR to onboard first 30 paying users personally (Collison install protocol), identify friction points, and embed solutions into onboarding automation for users 31+.

**Outcome:** Commercial strategy built on evidence (PSM, CPM, funnel data), five pillars designed to reinforce each other, and a sequenced roadmap with clear financial cases. The toolkit prevented the most common early-stage commercial error: pursuing all channels simultaneously before any one is proven.

## Templates

### Template 1: Commercial Strategy One-Page Summary
A one-page synthesis of Phase 1 outputs: strategic context, chosen direction, three strategic objectives per function, and resource plan.

### Template 2: Five Pillar Strategy Pack Template
Five structured templates (one per pillar) with sections, field prompts, and KPI defaults for positioning, communication, distribution, pricing, and digital.

## Pitfalls & Best Practices

**Top failure modes across the process:**

1. **Starting with the answer.** The most common consulting error: selecting the strategy before the analysis. Phases 1 must precede Phase 2. Every 'obvious' strategic choice collapses under data scrutiny more often than not.

2. **Building the Five Pillars in isolation.** Pricing, positioning, communication, distribution, and digital must be designed together — a premium positioning paired with mass-market distribution destroys brand equity. Run a cross-pillar coherence check before Phase 2 gate.

3. **Treating pricing as a cost-plus exercise.** The single highest-ROI improvement for most organisations is moving from Level 1–2 to Level 3–4 pricing maturity. McKinsey data consistently shows that 1% price improvement → 6–8% EBITDA improvement. Invest in pricing capability first.

4. **Skipping the Van Westendorp or conjoint step.** Pricing without WTP data is guessing. Even a 100-respondent PSM survey costs <$5,000 and frequently prevents a $500,000+ pricing mistake.

5. **Communication without sequencing.** Always brief leaders before managers, managers before frontline. Every communication calendar must be reviewed for sequencing, not just content.

6. **Treating change management as a Phase 6 activity.** Change management must start in Phase 1 (stakeholder mapping) and run continuously. The most common reason transformations fail to deliver benefits is under-investment in change management — typically 1–3% of programme budget vs. the recommended 10–15%.

7. **Measuring initiative completion rather than benefits realisation.** Project delivered on time ≠ value created. Post-project evaluation (6–12 weeks post-go-live, actual vs. forecast benefits) is the only honest test of whether the strategy worked.
