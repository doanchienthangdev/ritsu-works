# Design Thinking

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A human-centred innovation process with five stages — Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test — used to solve complex problems by deeply understanding user needs before generating solutions.

**Origin:** Hasso Plattner Institute of Design (d.school) at Stanford, led by David Kelley; popularised by IDEO; formalised in Tim Brown's Change by Design (2009)

## Why it works
Most product failures are failures of empathy: solutions designed without deep customer understanding solve the wrong problem. Design Thinking forces problem definition before solution generation, and testing with real users before full investment.

## When to use
When developing new products, redesigning customer experiences, solving complex organisational problems, or generating innovation for a new market category.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Empathise: conduct 5–8 user interviews (60 minutes each) using open-ended questions. Observe users in their natural context. Document verbatim quotes and direct observations — avoid interpreting during data collection.
2. Define: cluster observations using an affinity diagram. Identify the most significant patterns. Write a Point of View (POV) statement: '[User] needs [need] because [insight].' Then reframe as 3–5 'How Might We' (HMW) questions.
3. Ideate: run a 30-minute brainstorm session targeting 50+ ideas per HMW question. Apply 'Yes, And' rather than 'Yes, But'. Vote on the top 5–10 ideas for prototyping.
4. Prototype: build a paper, cardboard, or click-dummy prototype of the top 3 ideas in 2–4 hours. The prototype must be testable, not perfect — 'fail faster to succeed sooner'.
5. Test: return to 5+ users with the prototype. Observe how they use it. Ask: 'What did you expect to happen? What would you change?' Synthesise feedback and iterate in the next sprint.

## Real-life example — IDEO (shopping cart redesign, ABC News Nightline, 1999)
IDEO's shopping cart redesign (documented live on ABC's Nightline) applied Design Thinking in 5 days: Empathise — observed shoppers in supermarkets, interviewed parents about pain with current carts. Define — POV: 'Parents with young children need a safer, more convenient cart because current carts are theft-prone and unsafe for children.' Ideate — 50+ ideas including folding carts, child-facing seats, modular baskets. Prototype — rapid cardboard models of 4 concepts. Test — in-store testing with real parents. Result: a revolutionary cart design with scannable baskets (reducing checkout time), a child seat, and modular top basket — none of which the supermarket had requested, because they had not observed the user problem.

**So what:** IDEO's team generated a breakthrough product in 5 days by observing the actual user experience rather than starting with design assumptions.

## Pitfalls
- Skipping Empathise: teams that jump to Ideate generate solutions to the wrong problem.
- Falling in love with the first prototype: Design Thinking requires testing multiple solutions before committing.
- Using Design Thinking for problems with a known, correct answer (e.g., tax calculation): it is a divergent, exploratory tool for complex, ill-defined problems.
