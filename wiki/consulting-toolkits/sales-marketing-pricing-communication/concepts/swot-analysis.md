# SWOT Analysis

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A structured framework for identifying internal Strengths and Weaknesses, and external Opportunities and Threats, to generate strategic options through cross-pair analysis (SO, ST, WO, WT).

**Origin:** Albert Humphrey, Stanford Research Institute, 1960s; popularised in corporate planning through the 1970s

## Why it works
Good strategy exploits strengths to capture opportunities while defending against threats that exploit weaknesses. The cross-pair analysis (SO/ST/WO/WT) forces explicit strategic option generation rather than leaving the SWOT grid as a descriptive list.

## When to use
After market and competitor analysis are complete, to synthesise insights into strategic directions before selecting a growth framework.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Run a cross-functional workshop (2–4 hours). Brief participants in advance with the market analysis, competitive landscape, and financial data so contributions are evidence-based.
2. Populate each quadrant with 5–8 specific, non-obvious points. Reject generic entries (e.g., 'strong brand') unless backed by data (e.g., 'NPS of 72, top-quartile in category per Bain benchmark').
3. Prioritise within each quadrant: vote on the top 3–4 items by impact.
4. Run cross-pair analysis: SO pairs (use Strength S to capture Opportunity O), ST pairs, WO pairs, WT pairs. Generate one strategic option per cross-pair.
5. Consolidate the strategic options into 4–6 overarching directions and feed into the strategy framework selection step.
6. Document assumptions and validate with data sources — SWOT is only as good as the underlying evidence.

## Real-life example — Apple (iPhone launch, 2007)
Apple's SWOT at iPhone launch: Strengths — iconic design capability, loyal Mac/iPod ecosystem (50m users), software integration (iTunes). Weaknesses — no carrier relationships, no enterprise sales force, unproven phone hardware. Opportunities — mobile data growth, consumer frustration with 'dumb phones', music-phone convergence. Threats — Nokia's market dominance (40% share), carrier control of handset distribution, BlackBerry's enterprise lock-in. Key SO option: leverage design + iTunes ecosystem to capture the frustrated consumer segment. Key WT defence: exclusive carrier deal (AT&T) to bypass distribution threat. Both options materialised precisely as executed.

**So what:** The cross-pair analysis produced concrete strategic options (exclusive deal, ecosystem leverage), not just a list of facts.

## Pitfalls
- Using SWOT as a dump of opinions rather than evidence — require data citations for every entry.
- Stopping at the grid without running cross-pair analysis — the options are the value.
- Making the SWOT too long: more than 8 items per quadrant means priorities are not set.
