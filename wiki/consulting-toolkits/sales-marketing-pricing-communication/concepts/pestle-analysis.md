# PESTLE Analysis

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A structured scan of the macro-environment across six dimensions: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental factors affecting an organisation.

**Origin:** Francis Aguilar, Harvard, 1967 (ETPS); expanded to PESTLE by various practitioners through the 1980s–90s

## Why it works
Strategy must be grounded in the macro-environment. By forcing analysis across six distinct lenses, PESTLE prevents tunnel vision (e.g., focusing only on economic factors while ignoring regulatory risk).

## When to use
At the start of a strategy review, market-entry assessment, or annual planning cycle to ensure the external macro-context is understood before setting direction.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Gather a cross-functional team (strategy, legal, finance, operations, marketing) to ensure diverse lenses.
2. For each dimension, brainstorm 3–5 specific factors relevant to your industry and geography over the next 3–5 years.
3. For each factor, rate its likely Impact on your business (High / Medium / Low) and its Probability of occurrence.
4. Prioritise the top 5–7 highest-impact factors across all six dimensions.
5. For each prioritised factor, derive a concrete strategic implication: an opportunity to capture or a risk to mitigate.
6. Feed the prioritised factors into the SWOT Analysis as external Opportunities and Threats.

## Real-life example — Uber (global ride-hailing, 2015–2020)
Uber's PESTLE: Political — taxi lobby pressure led to bans in Germany, France, and several Asian markets (High Impact); Economic — price-sensitive users in emerging markets required localised pricing; Social — gig economy acceptance growing but driver welfare debate intensifying; Technological — smartphone penetration and GPS accuracy enabled the core product; Legal — employment classification lawsuits in UK and California (High Impact, High Probability); Environmental — pressure to electrify fleet. The two highest-impact factors (Political bans, Legal classification) forced Uber to invest heavily in regulatory lobbying and create Uber Eats as a revenue hedge against ride-hailing restrictions.

**So what:** PESTLE identified that Legal and Political factors were existential — they drove Uber's diversification into food delivery.

## Pitfalls
- Listing factors without rating impact results in an equal-weight analysis — prioritisation is essential.
- Not updating PESTLE annually: geopolitical and regulatory environments shift rapidly.
- Failing to connect PESTLE outputs to the SWOT — if PESTLE findings don't change the strategy discussion, they were wasted.
