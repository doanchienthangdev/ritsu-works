# Digital Marketing Mix (SEO / PPC / Social / Email / Website)

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A structured framework for planning, executing, and measuring digital marketing across five channels — SEO, PPC/Paid Search, Social Media, Email Marketing, and Website — with channel-specific strategies, KPIs, and budget allocation principles.

**Origin:** Evolved from the traditional 4Ps marketing mix applied to digital channels; channel-specific best practices codified by Google (SEO/PPC), HubSpot (inbound/email), and social platform documentation through the 2010s

## Why it works
Each digital channel has a different role in the customer journey, a different cost structure, and a different optimisation lever. Treating all digital channels identically leads to misallocated budget and mismatched content.

## When to use
When designing or auditing a digital marketing strategy, planning marketing budget allocation, or diagnosing underperforming digital acquisition.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Audit current digital presence: use Google Analytics for website, Ahrefs/SEMrush for SEO, Google Ads for PPC, native analytics for social, and your email platform for email metrics. Benchmark against category average.
2. SEO strategy: identify 50–100 target keywords aligned with ICP search intent (informational, navigational, transactional). Prioritise by search volume × relevance × current rank gap. Build a content calendar targeting top 20 keywords. Ensure technical SEO (site speed, mobile, schema markup) is clean.
3. PPC strategy: define campaign structure (brand, competitor, category). Set target ROAS (Revenue / Ad Spend, target >3×). Build conversion-optimised landing pages per campaign. Run A/B tests on headlines and CTAs monthly.
4. Social media strategy: select 2–3 channels where your ICP is active (not all of them). Define content pillars (3–4 themes). Set a posting frequency consistent with quality (not volume). Use retargeting ads to re-engage website visitors.
5. Email strategy: segment list by lifecycle stage (new leads, active users, lapsed). Design 3 automated flows: onboarding (Day 1, 3, 7 post-signup), nurture (weekly value content), and win-back (post-30-day lapse). Aim for >25% open rate and >3% CTR as baseline benchmarks.
6. Website strategy: ensure the homepage delivers the 'magic moment' in <10 seconds (clear headline, value proposition, primary CTA). Optimise the 3 highest-traffic pages for conversion. Page speed target: <2.5s LCP (Core Web Vitals).

## Real-life example — HubSpot (inbound marketing pioneer, 2006–2014)
HubSpot built its $100m+ ARR largely on an integrated digital marketing mix: SEO (published 1,000+ in-depth blog posts targeting 'marketing + [problem]' keywords; organic became 60% of leads by 2012); PPC (Google Ads for high-intent 'marketing software' terms; ROAS >4× justified ongoing spend); Email (automated nurture sequences segmented by company size and role, achieving 35% open rates vs. 20% industry average); Website (redesigned for conversion in 2011, reducing bounce rate from 72% to 48% and tripling trial sign-up rate). Each channel fed the next: SEO drove organic traffic; email converted traffic to leads; PPC captured high-intent buyers. The result: HubSpot grew from $29m to $115m ARR in 3 years on digital-only acquisition.

**So what:** HubSpot's digital mix worked because channels were integrated and sequenced — SEO for awareness, email for nurture, PPC for intent capture — not operated as independent silos.

## Pitfalls
- Spreading budget equally across all five channels: channel ROI is highly variable; concentrate spend where your ICP actually is.
- Treating social media as a broadcast channel: social requires two-way engagement — respond to comments, participate in conversations.
- Ignoring Core Web Vitals: Google's page experience signals directly affect SEO rankings and paid search Quality Score.
