# RACI Model (Responsibility Assignment Matrix)

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A responsibility assignment matrix that clarifies roles across a process or initiative by defining who is Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome), Consulted (provides input), and Informed (kept updated).

**Origin:** Rooted in project management practice from the 1950s; RACI as a specific acronym attributed to various sources in PM literature from the 1980s onwards

## Why it works
Decision-making confusion and accountability gaps are among the top causes of programme failure. RACI eliminates ambiguity by ensuring every activity has exactly one Accountable person and at least one Responsible person.

## When to use
At the start of any programme, project, or process redesign to establish clarity on roles before execution begins.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List all key activities and decisions in the process or programme (typically 15–30 rows).
2. List all roles involved across the top (not named individuals — roles, as people change).
3. For each activity, assign exactly one 'A' (Accountable — the person who is ultimately responsible for the outcome and has authority to approve it) and at least one 'R' (Responsible — who does the work).
4. Assign 'C' to roles that need to be consulted before decisions are made (two-way communication).
5. Assign 'I' to roles that need to be kept informed after decisions are made (one-way communication).
6. Review for: rows with no 'A' (accountability gap), rows with multiple 'A's (accountability confusion), roles with too many 'C's (consultation fatigue), and activities where 'I' should be 'C' (information asymmetry risk).
7. Validate with all role holders in a 30-minute review session before publishing.

## Real-life example — Procter & Gamble (product launch governance)
P&G uses a RACI-equivalent framework called 'Decision Rights' across its product launch process. For a new product launch, the RACI assigns: Brand Manager = A for the marketing plan; Regional Sales VP = A for the distribution plan; Supply Chain = R for production readiness; Finance = C for the business case; CEO/CCO = I until the launch is within 30 days (when they become A for the go/no-go). This clarity means P&G can execute 30+ launches per year across 10+ categories without executive involvement in routine decisions, reserving senior attention for genuine escalation points.

**So what:** P&G's launch velocity comes directly from clear decision rights — when everyone knows their RACI role, decisions happen without meetings.

## Pitfalls
- Multiple 'A' assignments for a single activity: when two people are accountable, no one is — select one.
- Confusing 'R' and 'A': the person who does the work is R; the person who owns the outcome (and can be fired if it fails) is A. They are often different people.
- Using individual names instead of roles: when people leave, the matrix becomes obsolete immediately.
