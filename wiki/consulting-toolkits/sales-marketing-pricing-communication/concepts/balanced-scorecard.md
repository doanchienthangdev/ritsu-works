# Balanced Scorecard

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A strategic management system that translates vision into objectives, measures, targets, and initiatives across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, and Learning & Growth.

**Origin:** Robert S. Kaplan and David P. Norton, Harvard Business Review, 1992 ('The Balanced Scorecard — Measures That Drive Performance')

## Why it works
Financial metrics are lagging indicators. The Balanced Scorecard adds leading indicators (customer satisfaction, process efficiency, employee capability) that predict future financial performance, creating a balanced view of organisational health.

## When to use
Setting strategic objectives and KPIs for any function or business unit; aligning team performance with strategy; building the commercial performance management system.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Articulate the organisation's (or function's) vision and strategy in one clear statement.
2. For each of the four perspectives, define 2–4 strategic objectives using action-oriented language (e.g., 'Grow recurring revenue from enterprise customers').
3. For each objective, define 1–2 KPIs with a current baseline, a 12-month target, and a data source.
4. Define the strategic initiative(s) that will close the gap between baseline and target for each objective.
5. Map cause-and-effect links: Learning & Growth objectives enable Internal Process improvements, which improve Customer outcomes, which drive Financial results. Draw the strategy map.
6. Review the Scorecard monthly at the leadership level; cascade to team-level scorecards quarterly.

## Real-life example — Hilton Hotels (post-2009 recovery)
Hilton used a Balanced Scorecard to drive its recovery after the 2008 financial crisis. Financial: target EBITDA margin recovery to 30%+. Customer: NPS uplift from 42 to 58 over 24 months, driven by a redesigned check-in experience. Internal Process: reduce average check-in time from 4.5 to 2 minutes through the Digital Key initiative. Learning & Growth: train 100% of front-desk staff on the new process within 6 months. The strategy map made explicit that L&G investment (training) enabled the Internal Process improvement (faster check-in), which drove the Customer outcome (NPS), which drove the Financial outcome (repeat bookings and RevPAR growth). By 2013 Hilton achieved industry-leading RevPAR growth.

**So what:** The Scorecard's value is the cause-and-effect strategy map, not the KPI list — it shows why investing in training is a financial decision.

## Pitfalls
- Creating too many KPIs (>20 total): the Scorecard should focus attention, not capture everything measurable.
- Failing to build the strategy map — without the cause-and-effect logic, the scorecard is just a KPI dashboard.
- Not cascading to team level: the Scorecard only changes behaviour when individuals see their actions linked to the strategic objectives.
