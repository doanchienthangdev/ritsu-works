# ADKAR Change Management Model

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A goal-oriented change management model describing the five building blocks required for successful individual change: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.

**Origin:** Jeff Hiatt, Prosci Research, 1998; published as ADKAR: A Model for Change in Business, Government and our Community (2006)

## Why it works
Change fails at the individual level, not the project level. ADKAR identifies which building block is missing for each person or group, enabling targeted interventions rather than generic communications. An organisation cannot change until a critical mass of individuals have moved through all five stages.

## When to use
For any organisational change initiative — technology implementation, process redesign, strategy execution, cultural transformation — where people must change their behaviours.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Assess the current ADKAR state for each impacted group: run a short (5-question) survey asking one question per ADKAR stage, rated 1–5. A score below 3 on any stage indicates a barrier.
2. Identify the lowest-scoring stage for each group — this is the 'barrier point'. You cannot skip stages; address the barrier point first.
3. Design targeted interventions for each stage: Awareness — leadership town halls, written rationale; Desire — one-to-one conversations with managers, WIIFM messaging; Knowledge — training, job aids; Ability — coaching, practice environments, buddy systems; Reinforcement — performance management, celebration, feedback loops.
4. Execute interventions, then re-assess ADKAR scores at 4–6 week intervals.
5. Track the proportion of the impacted population that has reached Ability (A4) as the primary adoption metric.
6. Build Reinforcement into the permanent operating model: revise job descriptions, KPIs, and SOPs to reflect the new way of working.

## Real-life example — Microsoft (cultural transformation under Satya Nadella, 2014–2018)
Microsoft's shift from a 'know-it-all' to a 'learn-it-all' culture under Nadella followed implicit ADKAR logic: Awareness — Nadella's 'Hit Refresh' narrative explained why growth mindset was existential (cloud competition from AWS). Desire — Nadella personally modelled vulnerability and learning, making it safe to admit failure. Knowledge — Carol Dweck's growth mindset framework was embedded in all-hands and manager training. Ability — 360 feedback was redesigned to reward learning behaviours, not just results. Reinforcement — performance reviews shifted from stack ranking (which penalised collaboration) to a team-results model. Within 4 years, Microsoft's market cap tripled from $300bn to $900bn, partly driven by cultural transformation enabling the Azure cloud strategy.

**So what:** Nadella's transformation worked because it was sequenced correctly: awareness and desire (leadership narrative) before knowledge and ability (training systems) before reinforcement (performance management).

## Pitfalls
- Jumping to Knowledge (training) without addressing Awareness and Desire: training is wasted if people do not understand why the change is happening or do not want it.
- Measuring activity (training completion) rather than outcome (ability to perform the new behaviour) — completion rates are a leading indicator, not an adoption metric.
- Neglecting Reinforcement: without embedding change into performance management and SOPs, people revert to old behaviours within 3–6 months.
