# Ansoff Matrix

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A 2×2 matrix mapping four growth strategies based on product (existing vs. new) and market (existing vs. new): Market Penetration, Product Development, Market Development, and Diversification.

**Origin:** H. Igor Ansoff, Harvard Business Review, 1957 ('Strategies for Diversification')

## Why it works
Growth always involves some combination of changing the product and/or the market. The matrix makes the risk profile of each direction explicit: Market Penetration has the lowest risk (known product, known customer); Diversification has the highest (new product, new customer). This prevents organisations from accidentally taking on diversification risk while calling it 'market penetration'.

## When to use
When deciding the primary direction of growth investment, or when evaluating a portfolio of initiatives to ensure strategic coherence.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define 'existing' clearly: existing product = currently in production; existing market = currently served customer segment.
2. Map your current revenue base: what % comes from each quadrant?
3. List all growth initiatives under consideration and classify each into one of the four quadrants.
4. For each quadrant with initiatives, assess: required capabilities, investment needed, time to value, and risk level.
5. Select the growth direction that best matches your risk appetite, capability base, and strategic objectives from Phase 1.
6. Document the chosen direction and its rationale in the strategy document — the quadrant choice is a board-level conversation.

## Real-life example — Amazon (1994–2023)
Amazon has executed all four quadrants sequentially: Market Penetration (selling more books to US online shoppers, 1994–1997); Market Development (international expansion — UK, Germany, Japan, 1998–2000); Product Development (adding electronics, music, and eventually AWS to existing US customer base, 2000–2010); Diversification (Amazon Go convenience stores, Amazon Pharmacy — new product for a substantially new customer context, 2016–2022). Each quadrant shift was supported by a new capability build, not just a brand extension.

**So what:** Amazon's growth sequence is the textbook Ansoff progression: penetrate, develop markets, develop products, diversify — each step funded by the previous.

## Pitfalls
- Misclassifying product extensions as new products when they are variants of existing ones — a premium tier of the same software is Market Penetration, not Product Development.
- Pursuing Diversification without the capabilities or balance sheet to absorb the higher failure rate.
- Using the matrix only at the corporate level — it is equally powerful at the business unit or product-line level.
