# TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A three-tier market sizing model: Total Addressable Market (TAM — the total global demand if 100% market share), Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM — the segment you can realistically reach), and Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM — the share you can capture given competitive position).

**Origin:** Venture capital due-diligence practice, widely attributed to SOM usage in pitch decks since the 1990s; formalised in Howard, 2005 (The Owner's Manual)

## Why it works
Investors and executives need to know whether a market is worth pursuing before allocating capital. TAM/SAM/SOM grounds the strategy in realisable market potential rather than theoretical maxima.

## When to use
When seeking investment, entering a new market, sizing a new product line, or building the revenue section of a strategic plan.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define TAM using a top-down approach: start with published industry research (e.g., Gartner, IDC, Euromonitor) and state the total global or relevant-geography spend in the category.
2. Refine to SAM: apply filters for your geographic footprint, customer segments you serve, and channels you operate in. SAM = TAM × (% of market your model can reach).
3. Estimate SOM bottom-up: from your current customer acquisition rate, average contract value, and realistic market share growth over 3–5 years. Benchmark against comparable companies at your stage.
4. Triangulate top-down and bottom-up: if they diverge by >2×, investigate the assumptions causing the gap.
5. State explicit assumptions and data sources for each number — investors and executives will probe them.

## Real-life example — Ritsu (AI EdTech, 2026 planning exercise)
TAM: Global self-directed learning market ~$80bn (HolonIQ, 2024). SAM: English-language, digital-first learners with access to PDF/video content and willingness to pay $15–120/year ~$12bn. SOM: With current product-market fit evidence (US-led, exam-prep wedge), a 3-year realistic capture of 0.1% SAM = $12m ARR target, anchored in comparable growth trajectories of Quizlet ($30m ARR at Year 5).

**So what:** TAM justifies the category; SOM anchors the plan. The SOM was grounded in a comparable benchmark, not a percentage guess.

## Pitfalls
- Using only top-down estimates for SOM — the SOM must be bottom-up anchored to be credible to investors.
- Conflating TAM with SAM: a US-only SaaS company cannot claim the global software TAM.
- Not updating the model as distribution channels or competitive share changes.
