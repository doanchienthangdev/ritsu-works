# Value Proposition Canvas

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A tool for designing products and services that customers want, by mapping the fit between a Customer Profile (jobs, pains, gains) and a Value Map (products/services, pain relievers, gain creators).

**Origin:** Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, and Greg Bernarda, Strategyzer, 2014 (Value Proposition Design)

## Why it works
Products fail when they address a problem customers do not prioritise or solve it in a way customers do not value. By explicitly mapping jobs-to-be-done and ranking pains/gains, the canvas forces a customer-first discipline before writing the value proposition.

## When to use
When designing or repositioning a product, writing the value proposition, building the messaging hierarchy, or diagnosing low conversion rates.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Start with the Customer Profile (right side). List the Customer Jobs: what tasks, problems, or needs your customer is trying to address (functional, social, emotional).
2. List Pains: obstacles, risks, and frustrations customers encounter when trying to do those jobs. Rank by severity.
3. List Gains: outcomes and benefits customers desire. Rank by importance.
4. Move to the Value Map (left side). List your Products & Services.
5. For each Pain, write a Pain Reliever: how does your product/service reduce or eliminate that specific pain?
6. For each Gain, write a Gain Creator: how does your product/service produce that desired outcome?
7. Achieve Fit: check that your most important Pain Relievers address the most severe customer Pains, and your Gain Creators address the most important customer Gains. Gaps indicate product-market misalignment.

## Real-life example — Slack (workplace messaging, 2014 launch)
Customer Profile: software teams. Jobs — coordinate work, share files, find past decisions. Pains — email is slow and hard to search, meetings are frequent but undocumented, project context is lost between tools. Gains — faster team decisions, single source of truth, reduced meetings. Slack's Value Map: Products — threaded channels, search, integrations. Pain Relievers — searchable persistent history (eliminates lost context), channels (eliminates reply-all email). Gain Creators — integrations with GitHub, Jira (produces a single workspace). The Fit was high on the top-ranked pains and gains of software teams, explaining rapid early adoption (8,000 signups on day one of launch in August 2013).

**So what:** Slack's product decisions mapped directly to the top-ranked customer pains — it was not a feature list, it was a pain-relief architecture.

## Pitfalls
- Completing the Value Map before the Customer Profile: teams default to what they are building, not what customers need.
- Ranking all pains and gains as equally important: rank explicitly or the canvas will not identify genuine product-market fit gaps.
- Not validating the canvas with real customer interviews — assumptions about pains are frequently wrong.
