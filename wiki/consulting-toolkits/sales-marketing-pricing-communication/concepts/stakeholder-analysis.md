# Stakeholder Analysis & Power-Interest Grid

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A structured process for identifying all parties affected by or able to influence a change, mapping them on a Power-Interest grid, and designing differentiated engagement strategies for each group.

**Origin:** Power-Interest grid attributed to Aubrey Mendelow, 1991 (Stakeholder Analysis); stakeholder theory foundational work by R. Edward Freeman, 1984 (Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach)

## Why it works
Not all stakeholders require the same engagement intensity. The Power-Interest grid prioritises effort: high-power/high-interest stakeholders must be actively managed; low-power/low-interest stakeholders need only monitoring. Mis-allocating engagement resources (e.g., spending time on low-power blockers) is a common programme failure mode.

## When to use
At the start of any change programme, product launch, market entry, or public communication initiative where multiple parties will be affected.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Brainstorm all stakeholders: individuals, groups, and organisations affected by or able to influence the initiative. Cast a wide net initially (internal: staff, management, board; external: customers, regulators, suppliers, investors).
2. For each stakeholder, assess Power (ability to block or accelerate the initiative: High/Medium/Low) and Interest (degree to which they care about the outcome: High/Medium/Low).
3. Plot on the 2×2 Power-Interest grid.
4. For each 'Manage Closely' stakeholder, design a personalised engagement plan: identify their WIIFM (what's in it for them), their likely objections, and the specific ask or decision needed from them.
5. For 'Keep Satisfied' stakeholders (often senior executives with budget control): brief proactively before public announcements; avoid surprises.
6. Review and update the map at each programme milestone — stakeholder positions shift as the initiative progresses.

## Real-life example — Vodafone (network sharing programme, UK, 2012)
Vodafone and O2 developed a network-sharing agreement in the UK. Key stakeholders: Ofcom (High Power, High Interest — regulatory approval required → Manage Closely); O2 joint venture partner (High Power, High Interest → Manage Closely); existing Vodafone network suppliers (High Interest, Medium Power → Keep Informed — contract implications); consumer groups (Low Power, High Interest → Keep Informed). The programme failed early stakeholder mapping for Ofcom's spectrum-sharing concerns, causing a 6-month delay. Post-recovery, a dedicated regulator engagement team was appointed for Manage Closely interactions, and the programme completed.

**So what:** The Ofcom delay resulted directly from under-investing in a High Power/High Interest stakeholder — the grid failure cost 6 months and significant programme cost.

## Pitfalls
- Not identifying 'hidden' stakeholders who have informal power (e.g., influential union reps, trusted senior advisors without formal titles).
- Treating the map as static — a stakeholder who was 'Monitor' at the start can become 'Manage Closely' after a change in scope.
- Using the grid as a communication plan only: engagement must be two-way — stakeholders need to be heard, not just informed.
