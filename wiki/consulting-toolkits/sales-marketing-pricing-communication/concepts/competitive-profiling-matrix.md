# Competitive Profiling Matrix (CPM)

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
A structured table that benchmarks your organisation against 3–5 key competitors across 8–12 Critical Success Factors (CSFs) weighted by importance, producing a total weighted score per competitor.

**Origin:** Strategic management consulting practice; widely taught in business schools as part of competitive analysis frameworks since the 1980s; no single inventor

## Why it works
A direct competitor comparison forces the team to articulate what actually drives success in the category (the CSFs), to weight them by importance (preventing all factors from seeming equal), and to score each competitor honestly — revealing genuine competitive advantages and vulnerability gaps.

## When to use
Competitor analysis phase of any market entry, product launch, or commercial strategy review.

## Visual
`table`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define 8–12 Critical Success Factors: what must an organisation do well to succeed in this industry? Source CSFs from your Porter's Five Forces and market analysis.
2. Assign weights to each CSF summing to 1.0: higher weight for factors that most determine competitive outcomes (e.g., if technology is the primary differentiator, weight it 0.25).
3. Score each company on each CSF on a 1–4 scale: 1 = major weakness, 2 = minor weakness, 3 = minor strength, 4 = major strength. Use data (e.g., customer survey scores, market share data) rather than opinion.
4. Calculate weighted score = weight × rating for each CSF × company combination.
5. Sum the weighted scores for each company. The highest total indicates the strongest competitive position.
6. Diagnose: where is your score significantly below the leading competitor? These gaps are the highest-priority areas for investment.

## Real-life example — Toyota vs. Ford vs. GM (US automotive, 2010)
A CPM in the 2010 US automotive market with CSFs: product quality (weight 0.25), fuel efficiency (0.20), brand reputation (0.15), dealer network (0.15), price competitiveness (0.15), after-sale service (0.10). Toyota scored: quality 4, fuel efficiency 4, brand 3, dealers 3, price 3, service 4 → weighted score 3.55. Ford: quality 3, fuel 3, brand 3, dealers 4, price 3, service 3 → weighted 3.10. GM: quality 2, fuel 2, brand 3, dealers 4, price 3, service 2 → weighted 2.55. Toyota's dominance on the highest-weight CSFs (quality and fuel efficiency) explained its share gains post-financial crisis. Ford's gap was quality; GM's was systemic across quality and fuel efficiency.

**So what:** CPM quantifies intuition: Toyota's advantage was concentrated in the highest-weighted factors, not across all dimensions — this is the precision that drives investment prioritisation.

## Pitfalls
- Using opinion rather than data for ratings — anchor each score to at least one quantitative data point.
- Assigning equal weights to all CSFs — this defeats the purpose of the matrix.
- Updating the CPM less than annually — competitive positions shift faster than annual planning cycles.
