# Agile / Scrum Delivery Methodology

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Sales, Marketing, Pricing & Communication*

## What it is
An iterative project delivery framework that breaks work into 2-week sprint cycles, with daily stand-ups, sprint planning, sprint review, and retrospective ceremonies — enabling fast learning and adaptive delivery.

**Origin:** Agile Manifesto, 2001 (Beck, Beedle, van Bennekum et al.); Scrum framework formalised by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, 1995

## Why it works
Traditional waterfall projects fail because requirements change and problems are discovered late. Agile delivers working output every 2 weeks, creating frequent feedback loops that allow the team to adapt before the full investment is committed.

## When to use
For digital initiative delivery, marketing campaign execution, product development sprints, and any work where requirements are likely to evolve during implementation.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Build the Product Backlog: list all features, tasks, and deliverables as User Stories ('As a [role], I want [feature] so that [benefit]'). The Product Owner prioritises the backlog by value.
2. Run Sprint Planning (2 hours for a 2-week sprint): select the top stories from the backlog that the team can complete in 2 weeks. Break each story into tasks. Define the Sprint Goal.
3. Execute the Sprint: Daily Stand-up (15 min) — each team member answers: What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? What is blocking me?
4. Sprint Review (1 hour): demonstrate the completed increment to stakeholders. Gather feedback. Update the backlog.
5. Sprint Retrospective (45 min): the team reflects — What went well? What should we improve? Define 1–2 action items for the next sprint.
6. Measure velocity (story points completed per sprint) over 3–5 sprints to establish a reliable planning baseline.

## Real-life example — Spotify (squad model, 2012–2018)
Spotify applied Agile at scale through its 'Squad Model': autonomous squads (6–8 people) each owning a feature area, operating in 2-week sprints with their own backlog and Product Owner. Tribes (collections of squads with related missions) aligned on quarterly goals. Chapters (functional communities) shared practices across squads. This enabled Spotify to ship product updates 100+ times per week while coordinating 100+ engineers. The Squad Model became a widely copied organisational design for digital product companies seeking the speed of a startup at enterprise scale.

**So what:** Agile at Spotify was not just a project methodology — it became an organisational design principle, with autonomy and alignment as the two balancing forces.

## Pitfalls
- Installing Scrum ceremonies without empowering the team to make decisions: Agile requires genuine autonomy, not just daily meetings.
- Not having a real Product Owner: if the PO cannot make priority decisions quickly, the team is blocked and sprints extend.
- Applying Agile to everything: some commercial activities (legal approvals, physical infrastructure) do not fit sprint cycles and should use traditional project management.
