# Corporate & Business Strategy Summary (Talent Mapping)

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: HR & Talent Management*

## What it is
A structured one-page document that extracts the organisation's top 3-5 strategic priorities and explicitly maps each to its talent implication — the specific people capability or headcount the strategy requires to succeed.

**Origin:** A practitioner synthesis tool from McKinsey's People & Organizational Performance practice, popularised in the 'War for Talent' research series (1997-2001). The explicit talent-mapping step (business priority → talent implication) was formalised by McKinsey consultants as the mechanism by which HR escapes administrative irrelevance.

## Why it works
HR functions that design people programmes without first understanding business strategy inevitably produce work that business leaders regard as irrelevant. The strategy summary forces an explicit dependency: 'If the business must achieve X, it needs these people capabilities to do so.' This transforms HR from a support function into a strategic necessity — every HR investment can be traced back to a business priority it enables.

## When to use
At the start of every HR strategy cycle (annual), whenever the business strategy changes significantly, and at the start of any post-merger HR integration. This is always the first tool in Phase 1.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Obtain the current corporate strategy document, business unit plans, and board presentation. Read for the three to five most critical priorities — not the full list of objectives, but the non-negotiables that the CEO would stake their tenure on.
2. Conduct 5-10 structured 30-minute interviews with C-suite executives and Business Unit MDs. Ask: 'What are the one or two things that must go right for the business in the next 18-24 months?' and 'Where is your biggest people/capability constraint today?' Record verbatim quotes.
3. For each strategic priority, write one sentence: 'To achieve [priority], the organisation needs [specific talent capability, headcount, or behaviour].' This is the talent implication. Be specific — not 'better leadership' but '30 regional sales managers with APAC market experience within 12 months.'
4. Map each talent implication to the HR pillar(s) that own the solution: Recruitment (for new headcount), Talent Development (for capability building), Performance Management (for behaviour change), etc.
5. Present the one-page summary to the CEO and CHRO and ask: 'Does this accurately represent the strategic bets? Have we missed any critical people dependencies?' Incorporate feedback.
6. Use this summary as the North Star for the entire HR transformation — every Phase 2 pillar design and every Phase 3 initiative should be traceable back to a row in this table.

## Real-life example — Amazon
Amazon's annual 'OP1' (Operating Plan) process explicitly surfaces talent implications for each strategic initiative. When Amazon launched AWS in 2006, the corporate strategy summary for the cloud business identified the talent implication as 'need 500 engineers with distributed-systems expertise within 18 months — a capability that does not exist in sufficient quantity in the market.' This diagnosis drove a differentiated recruitment strategy (hiring from academic research labs), an internal rotational programme (moving 20% of top engineers from retail to cloud), and a talent-development curriculum built around distributed systems. AWS's talent engine was designed before the business launched at scale.

**So what:** The power of the talent mapping step is that it converts business language ('we need to win in cloud') into HR action language ('we need 500 distributed-systems engineers') — creating a precise requirement that the HR function can design to, rather than guessing at business needs.

## Template
Complete one row per strategic priority (limit to the top 5 most important). The talent implication column is the most important — be as specific as possible (number of people, skill type, time horizon). Validate with the CEO before the HR strategy is finalised.

- [ ] Strategic Priority (one sentence statement of the business goal)
- [ ] Business Unit(s) Primarily Responsible
- [ ] Time Horizon (when must this be achieved?)
- [ ] Primary Business Metric (how will success be measured?)
- [ ] Talent Implication (specific people capability or headcount required)
- [ ] Criticality if Talent Gap Unfilled (High/Medium/Low — what happens to the business?)
- [ ] HR Pillar(s) Responsible for Closing the Gap

## Pitfalls
- Writing talent implications at the wrong altitude — 'need strong leadership' is too vague; '30 sales managers with APAC enterprise B2B experience, within 12 months, to enable our Singapore and Tokyo market entry' is actionable.
- Interviewing only HR stakeholders to build the summary — the business strategy inputs must come from the C-suite and BU MDs. HR's view of the business strategy is typically 12 months behind reality.
- Building the summary once and not updating it as the business strategy evolves. Revisit quarterly — strategy assumptions shift, especially in high-growth companies.
