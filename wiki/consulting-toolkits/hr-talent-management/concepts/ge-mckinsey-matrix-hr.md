# GE-McKinsey Matrix (HR Initiative Prioritisation)

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: HR & Talent Management*

## What it is
A 3×3 prioritisation grid that scores each HR initiative on Strategic Value (High/Medium/Low) and Implementation Feasibility (High/Medium/Low), enabling evidence-based selection of the optimal initiative portfolio and sequencing for the 3-year roadmap.

**Origin:** The original GE-McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix was developed by McKinsey & Company and General Electric in the early 1970s as a tool for business unit portfolio planning — helping GE's corporate leadership decide where to invest, hold, or divest across its 150+ business units. In the HR context, the matrix is adapted to evaluate initiative portfolios rather than business units.

## Why it works
The GE-McKinsey Matrix solves the most common HR prioritisation failure: the initiative portfolio is selected based on internal advocacy (who can make the most compelling argument) rather than strategic merit. By making criteria explicit and weighted, the matrix converts political decisions into analytical ones. The two-dimension structure captures both sides of the investment equation: value generated (Strategic Value) and likelihood of delivery (Feasibility) — producing a portfolio view rather than a simple ranking.

## When to use
Phase 5, Step 1 — the primary tool for converting the Business Case Portfolio into a prioritised initiative roadmap. Also use in annual HR planning cycles to review and rebalance the active portfolio.

## Visual

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define and weight the scoring criteria before any scoring begins. For Strategic Value: strategic alignment (does it directly support a named strategic objective? weight: 40%), NPV (is the financial return above the hurdle rate? weight: 40%), risk reduction (does it mitigate a critical talent or compliance risk? weight: 20%). For Feasibility: capability readiness (does HR have the skills to deliver this? 40%), resource availability (are headcount, budget, technology available? 40%), change complexity (how much behaviour change is required across how many people? 20%).
2. Score each initiative independently before the group calibration. Assign each scorer the scoring template and ask them to rate each initiative 1-3 on each criterion without discussion. Collect all scores before beginning any group calibration.
3. Calculate weighted scores for Strategic Value and Feasibility for each initiative. Plot on the 3×3 grid (1.0-1.67 = Low, 1.68-2.34 = Medium, 2.35-3.0 = High on each axis).
4. Facilitate a calibration discussion starting with the initiatives in ambiguous grid positions (Medium/Medium or Medium/High) — those in the extremes (High/High, Low/Low) are generally uncontested. For each ambiguous initiative, ask scorers to cite the evidence driving their assessment.
5. Produce the portfolio view: overlay a budget constraint (total investment capacity for Horizon 1, 2, and 3). Select initiatives for each Horizon starting from the top-right quadrant and moving left and down, stopping when the budget is fully allocated. Ensure coverage across all 10 pillars — if 80% of Horizon 1 budget goes to Recruitment, the other 9 pillars are under-resourced.
6. Present the final prioritised portfolio to the Steering Committee with the grid visual and a clear articulation of what is included, what is deferred, and why.

## Real-life example — IBM
When IBM's HR transformation team faced a portfolio of 200+ candidate initiatives in 2010, they used a value-feasibility matrix equivalent to the GE-McKinsey framework. The scoring exercise revealed that 40% of initiatives — those in the Low Value / High Feasibility quadrant — were high on internal enthusiasm but strategically marginal. Eliminating these from the active portfolio allowed IBM to concentrate resources on the 15 truly high-value initiatives. IBM reported that this portfolio discipline saved an estimated $80M in misallocated implementation investment and significantly accelerated delivery of the strategically critical initiatives.

**So what:** The most valuable output of the GE-McKinsey prioritisation is not the identification of high-priority initiatives (which most teams already suspect) — it is the structured elimination of the Low Value / High Feasibility initiatives that consume resources without transformational return. These 'activity traps' are the most common cause of HR transformation underperformance.

## Template
Score each initiative independently (no group discussion) before aggregating. Calculate weighted scores. Plot on the grid. Identify the portfolio for each Horizon. Validate for cross-pillar balance before presenting to the Steering Committee.

- [ ] Initiative Name and Pillar
- [ ] Strategic Value Score: Strategic Alignment (1-3) / NPV (1-3) / Risk Reduction (1-3) / Weighted Total
- [ ] Feasibility Score: Capability Readiness (1-3) / Resource Availability (1-3) / Change Complexity (1-3) / Weighted Total
- [ ] Grid Position (e.g., High Value / High Feasibility)
- [ ] Recommended Horizon (1/2/3/Park)
- [ ] Estimated Investment (one-time + annual run cost)
- [ ] Expected NPV
- [ ] Key Dependency (what must be true before this initiative can launch?)
- [ ] Owner (who will be accountable for delivery?)

## Pitfalls
- Letting politics override the scoring. The most common failure is when a senior leader overrides the matrix outcome to protect a pet initiative. Make the criteria and weightings explicit and public before scoring — after that, the matrix output is the group's collective judgement, not one person's preference.
- Scoring all criteria equally rather than applying weights. An initiative with a transformational NPV should score higher on Strategic Value than one with a marginal NPV even if both score '2' on strategic alignment. The weighted criteria prevent equal treatment of unequal importance.
- Prioritising without a budget overlay. The grid identifies relative priority but not affordability. Always overlay the budget constraint to produce a feasible portfolio — a grid full of High Value / High Feasibility initiatives that collectively exceed the budget by 3x is not a prioritisation, it is a wish list.
