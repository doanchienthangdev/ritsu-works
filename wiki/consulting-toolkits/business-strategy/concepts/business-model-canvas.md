# Business Model Canvas

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Business Strategy & Strategic Planning*

## What it is
A one-page visual framework that describes, analyzes, and designs business models across nine building blocks: Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partners, and Cost Structure.

**Origin:** Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, 'Business Model Generation', John Wiley & Sons, 2010. Based on Osterwalder's PhD thesis at HEC Lausanne (2004).

## Why it works
A business model is the system of choices about how a company creates, delivers, and captures value. The canvas makes the complete model visible on a single page, enabling teams to discuss the full system rather than optimizing individual components in isolation — the most common source of strategy-execution failure.

## When to use
New business or product launch; strategy refresh; competitive analysis (map competitor's canvas); M&A due diligence (compare business model compatibility); pitch preparation.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Start with Customer Segments: who are your 1–3 most important customer segments? Define each by demographics, psychographics, and jobs-to-be-done.
2. Define the Value Proposition for each segment: what bundle of products/services creates value for this segment? What pain do you relieve? What gain do you create?
3. Map the Channels through which you reach each segment (awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery, after-sales).
4. Define the Customer Relationships model: personal, automated, self-service, community, co-creation?
5. Map Revenue Streams: what are customers paying for? How (one-time, subscription, licensing, usage)? At what price point?
6. Map the Key Resources required to deliver the value proposition (physical, intellectual, human, financial).
7. Map the Key Activities that must be performed (production, problem-solving, platform/network management).
8. Map the Key Partners (who do you depend on that you do not own?).
9. Estimate the Cost Structure: what are the most important costs? Which are fixed vs. variable? Is the model cost-driven or value-driven?

## Real-life example — Airbnb
Airbnb's Business Model Canvas: Customer Segments — Hosts (property owners) and Guests (travelers) — a two-sided platform. Value Proposition to Guests: unique local accommodation at lower cost than hotels; to Hosts: income from spare space with zero marketing effort. Channels: web/app. Revenue Streams: 3% host service fee + 6–12% guest service fee. Key Resources: platform technology, brand, community trust. Key Activities: platform development, trust/safety (verification, insurance). Key Partners: payment processors, professional photography. Cost Structure: platform infrastructure, trust and safety operations, marketing. The canvas reveals the business model's essence: Airbnb earns by enabling trust between strangers, not by owning assets.

**So what:** The Business Model Canvas's power is in making the full system visible — Airbnb's model would be incoherent if you analyzed any single block in isolation; the magic is in the interaction between Key Activities (trust-building) and Revenue Streams (percentage of every transaction).

## Pitfalls
- Filling in the canvas too quickly without sufficient customer and market evidence: the canvas captures hypotheses, not facts — it must be validated with real customer and financial data.
- Treating the canvas as a one-time exercise: as strategy evolves, the business model evolves; the canvas should be reviewed and updated at every major strategic decision point.
- Optimizing individual blocks without considering their interactions: a highly efficient cost structure that requires compromising the value proposition is a strategic failure.
