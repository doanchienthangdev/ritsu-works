# Greiner's Growth Model

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Business Strategy & Strategic Planning*

## What it is
A model that describes the five phases of organizational growth, each ending in a predictable 'crisis' that the organization must resolve to advance to the next phase.

**Origin:** Larry E. Greiner, 'Evolution and Revolution as Organizations Grow', Harvard Business Review, 1972.

## Why it works
Growing organizations face predictable structural crises because management practices that successfully navigate one growth phase become the source of the next crisis. The model gives leaders a map of the crises they will face before they face them — allowing proactive intervention rather than reactive firefighting.

## When to use
Rapid growth phases; post-M&A integration; organizational restructuring; talent retention crises; when execution consistently fails despite capable individuals.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Identify the organization's current Greiner phase by examining the dominant management style, organizational structure, and the primary tension being experienced. Typical tell: what does leadership argue about most (e.g., 'we need more control' = Phase 3 transition; 'the bureaucracy is killing us' = Phase 4 crisis).
2. Identify whether the current phase crisis has been triggered: what are the symptoms (employee frustration, missed targets, coordination failures)?
3. Plan the organizational interventions required to resolve the crisis and advance to the next phase. Phase 1 → 2: bring in professional management; Phase 2 → 3: decentralize and empower BU leaders; Phase 3 → 4: add coordination mechanisms (planning, cross-functional teams); Phase 4 → 5: simplify, build collaboration culture, reduce bureaucracy.
4. Build the organizational change roadmap addressing the crisis with a named leader, milestones, and success criteria.
5. Plan for the crisis that the current resolution will eventually cause — proactive organizations prepare for Phase N+1 challenges while solving the Phase N crisis.

## Real-life example — Google / Alphabet
Google experienced a classic Greiner Phase 2 → Phase 3 crisis in 2014–2015. The Phase 2 'Growth through Direction' (Larry Page as visionary leader with centralized R&D) produced a Crisis of Autonomy as the organization scaled — talented engineers were frustrated by top-down resource allocation. Google's response: the Alphabet restructuring (2015), which created delegated BU structures (Waymo, Verily, DeepMind, etc.) each with its own CEO and P&L — the textbook Phase 3 'Growth through Delegation' response. The Phase 3 crisis (Control) later manifested as unclear resource allocation and accountability across Alphabet's portfolio businesses.

**So what:** Greiner's model predicts that Alphabet's next challenge will be a Crisis of Control — exactly what analysts observed in 2018–2022 as Alphabet's 'Other Bets' consumed cash without clear accountability.

## Pitfalls
- Treating the model as strictly sequential — some organizations oscillate between phases or skip phases due to M&A, dramatic downsizing, or leadership change.
- Confusing the symptom with the cause: the 'red tape' crisis in Phase 4 is not caused by lazy bureaucrats but by the coordination mechanisms that were necessary to solve the Phase 3 crisis — the cure becomes the disease.
- Not acting before the crisis is fully manifested: the best time to plan the Phase transition is when the organization is still successfully in a phase, not when it is in mid-crisis.
