# Strategic Dashboards

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Business Strategy & Strategic Planning*

## What it is
A suite of integrated performance dashboards — Strategic Objectives, Strategic Pillars, and Strategic Initiatives — that provide real-time visibility into whether the strategy is executing as planned at each level of the organization.

**Origin:** Evolved from the Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton, 1992) and management control systems literature. Modern strategic dashboards integrate the BSC framework with initiative tracking and financial reporting in a single system.

## Why it works
Strategy execution fails when there is no single source of truth connecting strategic intent (what we said we would do) to operational reality (what is actually happening). Three-level dashboards create the information architecture that makes execution visible: the Objectives Dashboard tracks outcomes, the Pillars Dashboard tracks transformation progress, and the Initiatives Dashboard tracks delivery.

## When to use
After strategy approval, when translating strategic intent into an operational management system; when execution has repeatedly fallen short despite good strategy; post-merger to align two formerly separate strategy management systems.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Build the Level 1 (Objectives) dashboard from the Balanced Scorecard: one row per strategic objective, with KPI, baseline, current actual, target, variance, and RAG status. Refresh monthly.
2. Build the Level 2 (Pillars) dashboard: for each strategic pillar, define 3–5 transformation milestones for the year and track completion. Show the % of milestones complete vs. planned schedule.
3. Build the Level 3 (Initiatives) dashboard: for each strategic initiative, track: executive sponsor, project owner, RAG status, planned vs. actual spend, next milestone, and open issues/risks.
4. Add financial dashboards: monthly P&L actuals vs. budget (revenue, EBIT, margin), YTD performance, rolling 12-month forecast, and BU/product breakdowns.
5. Establish the review cadence: Initiatives (Level 3) reviewed weekly by initiative owner; Pillars (Level 2) reviewed monthly by pillar owner + strategy lead; Objectives (Level 1) reviewed monthly by CEO + leadership; full three-level review quarterly at strategy review.

## Real-life example — Siemens AG
Siemens implemented a three-level strategic management system under CEO Joe Kaeser's Vision 2020 plan. The strategic objectives dashboard tracked 12 company-level KPIs (revenue growth, profit margin, ROCE, employee engagement) with monthly updates to the Supervisory Board. The pillar dashboards tracked progress on the four strategic pillars (digital transformation, customer intimacy, operational excellence, talent development). The initiatives dashboard tracked 60+ strategic programs with weekly RAG updates. The system's value was in enabling the Supervisory Board to identify the three underperforming strategic initiatives (identified Red in Q1) and reallocate €300M of investment within the fiscal year — a responsiveness that was previously impossible under annual budget cycles.

**So what:** Strategic dashboards create organizational agility by shortening the feedback loop between execution and strategic decision-making — from annual cycles to monthly or weekly.

## Pitfalls
- Building dashboards that are not acted upon: a dashboard that does not trigger decisions or accountability conversations is decoration, not management.
- Creating too many KPIs: more than 25–30 KPIs on a company strategy dashboard produces noise, not signal.
- Separating the financial dashboard from the strategic dashboard: financial and strategic performance must be reviewed together to surface the causal story.
