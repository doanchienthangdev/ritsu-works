# Porter's Five Forces

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Business Strategy & Strategic Planning*

## What it is
A framework that analyzes the structural attractiveness and competitive intensity of an industry by examining five forces that shape profitability.

**Origin:** Michael E. Porter, Harvard Business School, 1979 — first published in 'How Competitive Forces Shape Strategy', Harvard Business Review.

## Why it works
Industry profitability is driven not just by direct competitors but by four additional forces that all compete for a share of the value created. Understanding the strength of each force lets you predict average industry profitability and identify where your business model must be differentiated to earn above-average returns.

## When to use
When entering a new market, evaluating an acquisition target's industry, setting competitive strategy, or deciding whether to invest in or exit a business unit.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the industry boundary precisely — include a geographic scope and product/service scope; a boundary that is too broad or narrow will produce an inaccurate assessment.
2. For each of the five forces, gather evidence: for rivalry, count competitors and assess price competition; for new entrants, assess capital requirements and switching costs; for buyers, assess buyer concentration and price sensitivity; for suppliers, assess supplier concentration and switching costs; for substitutes, identify alternative ways the customer achieves the same outcome.
3. Rate each force as Low, Medium, or High based on evidence, using a structured scoring rubric (e.g., 3–5 criteria per force, each scored 1–3).
4. Synthesize the five scores into an overall industry attractiveness verdict and identify which 1–2 forces are most strategically significant.
5. Update the analysis annually or when a major structural shift occurs (new regulation, new technology, entry of a well-funded competitor).

## Real-life example — Netflix
When Netflix analyzed the streaming industry in 2013, it found: new entrant threat was medium (large capital and content licensing barriers), buyer power was high (low switching costs, many substitutes), supplier power was high (major studios controlled content), substitute threat was medium (live TV, gaming), and rivalry was intensifying. This five-forces reading motivated Netflix's pivot to original content production — to reduce supplier power and create differentiation.

**So what:** Five Forces analysis revealed that Netflix's long-run profitability depended on reducing its dependence on third-party content — leading to the $100B+ investment in originals that redefined its competitive position.

## Pitfalls
- Defining the industry too broadly (e.g., 'consumer goods') makes the analysis meaningless — be specific about the competitive arena.
- Treating the five forces as static: industry structure evolves; a tech disruption can shift the Threat of Substitutes from Low to Existential in 3 years.
- Stopping at assessment without implication: the point is to identify which forces your strategy must address, not to produce a framework slide.
