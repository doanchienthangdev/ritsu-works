# Ansoff Matrix (Product-Market Growth Matrix)

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Mergers & Acquisitions*

## What it is
A 2x2 strategic planning tool that classifies growth options by whether they involve existing or new products, and existing or new markets, producing four strategies: Market Penetration, Market Development, Product Development, and Diversification.

**Origin:** Igor Ansoff, Harvard Business Review, 1957; foundational text 'Corporate Strategy' (1965).

## Why it works
Risk increases as you move away from what you know: selling existing products to existing customers is lowest risk; selling new products to new customers (diversification) is highest. In M&A, the Ansoff quadrant of the target immediately signals integration complexity and strategic risk: a diversification acquisition requires a fundamentally different integration and risk tolerance than a market penetration acquisition.

## When to use
Phase 1 (target screening), Phase 3 (strategic benefit narrative), and as a board communication tool to explain why a particular acquisition fits the strategy.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Plot the acquirer's current position: what products/services does it sell, and to which markets (geographies, customer segments)?
2. 2. For each M&A candidate, identify which Ansoff quadrant the acquisition represents relative to the acquirer's current portfolio.
3. 3. Validate that the Ansoff quadrant is consistent with the board's risk appetite: a diversification play must be explicitly sanctioned; a market penetration play needs less justification.
4. 4. Use the Ansoff classification to set the integration depth: Market Penetration acquisitions typically require deep integration to extract scale synergies; Diversification acquisitions typically require light-touch preservation to avoid destroying the target's unique capabilities.
5. 5. Document the Ansoff rationale in the strategic benefit section of the business case (Phase 3, Step 1).

## Real-life example — Facebook's acquisition of Instagram (2012)
Facebook classified this as a Market Development acquisition: Instagram's core product (photo sharing) served a younger demographic and mobile-first market segment that Facebook was struggling to reach with its existing platform. The Ansoff framing justified a preservation integration model — Instagram kept its brand, management, and product — because Facebook needed Instagram's market position intact, not absorbed.

**So what:** Ansoff quadrant should directly determine integration depth. Market Development and Diversification acquisitions almost always require preservation models; Market Penetration and Product Development acquisitions can support fuller integration.

## Pitfalls
- Misclassifying a diversification as a market development play because there is superficial product overlap — this under-budgets integration complexity and overstates synergies. Mitigate: require business unit leaders to explicitly validate the Ansoff classification.
- Using Ansoff as a post-hoc rationalization rather than a pre-screening filter. Mitigate: apply Ansoff classification in Phase 1 screening criteria.
