# ADKAR Change Management Model

*Category: change-management · Toolkit: Mergers & Acquisitions*

## What it is
A goal-oriented change management model that defines five outcomes an individual must achieve for a change to succeed: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.

**Origin:** Jeff Hiatt, Prosci Research, 1998. Formalized in 'ADKAR: A Model for Change in Business, Government and our Community' (2006).

## Why it works
Change fails at the individual level, not the organizational level. Organizations change only when enough individuals change. ADKAR maps the causal sequence of individual change: you cannot build Desire without Awareness; you cannot build Ability without Knowledge. By diagnosing which ADKAR element is the barrier for each employee group, the change manager can intervene precisely rather than applying generic communications.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. DEFINE THE CHANGE: write a precise one-paragraph description of what is changing, what is staying the same, and why (the business case). This becomes the Awareness input.
2. 2. IDENTIFY IMPACTED EMPLOYEE GROUPS: segment the workforce into groups with materially different experiences of the change (e.g., acquired employees in Sales, acquired employees in Finance, acquiring company IT team). Create a change impact assessment for each group.
3. 3. ASSESS ADKAR STATUS PER GROUP: for each group, run a 5-question survey (one per ADKAR element, rated 1–5). Identify the lowest-scoring element per group — this is the building block barrier.
4. 4. DESIGN INTERVENTIONS for each barrier: Awareness barrier → targeted communications (why change is happening); Desire barrier → manager coaching, incentives, involvement; Knowledge barrier → training programs; Ability barrier → practice, job aids, coaching; Reinforcement barrier → recognition, accountability.
5. 5. IMPLEMENT AND MONITOR: run the interventions, then re-survey at 30, 60, and 90 days. Track ADKAR scores over time. Escalate groups still scoring ≤2 on any element to HR and senior leadership for direct intervention.
6. 6. INTEGRATE WITH THE IMO: report ADKAR status in the weekly IMO report alongside milestone completion. Treat low ADKAR scores as a project risk requiring mitigating action.

## Real-life example — Kraft Heinz merger integration (2015)
The Kraft-Heinz merger involved bringing together two organizations with different cultures (Kraft's established corporate culture vs. Heinz's 3G Capital cost-discipline culture). Employees at Kraft faced a significant Desire barrier — they understood the change (Awareness was high due to media coverage) but were resistant to the new zero-based budgeting culture. The integration team that applied ADKAR-style diagnostics focused not on more communications (Awareness was already sufficient) but on manager coaching and involving Kraft employees in redesigning processes — directly addressing the Desire barrier.

**So what:** The most common mistake in M&A integration communications is producing more Awareness-level messaging (town halls, emails) when the actual barrier is Desire or Ability. ADKAR forces you to diagnose before prescribing.

## Pitfalls
- Treating ADKAR as a communications planning tool rather than a diagnostic tool — producing a communication plan without first assessing which ADKAR element is the actual barrier. Mitigate: run the assessment before designing any intervention.
- Applying ADKAR at organizational level rather than individual/group level. An average score across all employees hides variance between groups. Mitigate: always segment by impacted group.
