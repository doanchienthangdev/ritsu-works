# Stakeholder Power-Interest Mapping

*Category: stakeholder-management · Toolkit: Mergers & Acquisitions*

## What it is
A prioritization tool that plots stakeholders on a 2x2 grid by their level of power (ability to impact deal success) and interest (degree to which they are affected by the transaction) to determine the appropriate engagement strategy for each group.

**Origin:** Based on Mendelow's stakeholder matrix (1991) and Eden & Ackermann's 'Making Strategy: The Journey of Strategic Management' (1998); standard in project management and corporate governance.

## Why it works
Not all stakeholders deserve equal attention — resources for engagement are finite. By mapping power and interest, the team can direct high-effort, personalized engagement to high-power/high-interest stakeholders (where failure to engage creates deal-breaking risk) and efficient, broadcast-style communication to low-power/low-interest audiences.

## When to use
Phase 5 (pre-announcement planning), Phase 6 (integration communication and governance), Phase 7 (sell-side stakeholder management in carve-outs).

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. IDENTIFY ALL STAKEHOLDERS: brainstorm comprehensively — internal (employees, management, board) and external (customers, suppliers, investors, regulators, community, media). Err on the side of inclusion.
2. 2. SCORE POWER (1–5): assess each stakeholder's ability to impact the deal or integration (regulatory approval authority, contract termination rights, media influence, board seat).
3. 3. SCORE INTEREST (1–5): assess each stakeholder's degree of being affected by the transaction (their commercial relationship, employment, investment).
4. 4. PLOT on the 2x2 grid and assign each stakeholder to one of four quadrants with the corresponding engagement strategy.
5. 5. ASSIGN RELATIONSHIP OWNERS: for every 'Manage Closely' stakeholder, assign a named individual from the deal team who owns the relationship. Set a cadence for outreach.
6. 6. BUILD THE ENGAGEMENT PLAN: for each stakeholder group, define the key message, communication channel, frequency, and feedback mechanism. Document in the stakeholder register.
7. 7. UPDATE QUARTERLY: stakeholder power and interest shifts as the integration progresses. Re-plot at 3, 6, and 12 months post-close.

## Real-life example — AB InBev acquisition of SABMiller (2016)
The $107B deal involved managing an extraordinarily complex stakeholder map: regulators in 30+ countries (high power, high interest — 'Manage Closely'); South African government and employees (high interest, medium power — 'Keep Informed'); institutional shareholders of both companies (high power, varying interest — 'Keep Satisfied' for passive investors, 'Manage Closely' for activist holders). AB InBev's IR team personally engaged the top 20 institutional shareholders in the first week of announcement — a 'Manage Closely' execution that secured shareholder support for the premium deal.

**So what:** In cross-border M&A, regulatory bodies and key customers are almost always 'Manage Closely' stakeholders whose approval is a literal condition to closing. Map them first, engage them personally within days of announcement, and never let them learn about developments from public sources before you brief them.

## Template
Complete at announcement and update quarterly. Assign a named owner to every 'Manage Closely' stakeholder.

## Pitfalls
- Building the stakeholder map and never updating it — stakeholders' power and interest change as the deal progresses. Mitigate: schedule formal quarterly reviews.
- Over-communicating to 'Monitor' stakeholders and under-communicating to 'Manage Closely' stakeholders due to volume bias. Mitigate: track engagement hours by quadrant and rebalance if 'Monitor' stakeholders are consuming disproportionate attention.
