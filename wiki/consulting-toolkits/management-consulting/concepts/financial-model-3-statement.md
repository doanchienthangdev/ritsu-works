# 3-Statement Financial Model

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A linked projection of income statement, balance sheet and cash flow used to test the viability and funding need of the practice.

**Origin:** Standard corporate-finance / FP&A practice.

## Why it works
Profit is not cash. A linked model reveals whether the plan is profitable AND solvent, and when it turns cash-flow positive — the two questions that determine survival.

## When to use
When building the practice's business plan and testing viability.

## Visual
`table`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Build the revenue model (engagements × value × ramp) by month/quarter.
2. Add direct costs and overhead to get the income statement.
3. Project the balance sheet (receivables, payables, assets).
4. Derive the cash-flow statement and find the low point of cash.
5. Compute break-even billed-days and the funding/runway need; run a downside case.

## Real-life example — Startup/practice (method)
A new advisory firm models that 8 billed days/month at its day-rate covers fixed costs — its break-even — and that it reaches cash-flow positive in month 7, defining how much runway to hold.

**So what:** A linked model answers both 'is it profitable?' and 'will it run out of cash?'.

## Template
Link the three statements; output break-even and runway.

- [ ] Revenue drivers
- [ ] Cost structure
- [ ] Income statement
- [ ] Balance sheet
- [ ] Cash flow
- [ ] Break-even
- [ ] Funding/runway

## Pitfalls
- Modelling P&L only — counter: cash flow is what kills businesses.
- Single optimistic case — counter: always run a downside.
