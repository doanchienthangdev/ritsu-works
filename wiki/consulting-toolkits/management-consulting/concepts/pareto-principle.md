# Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule)

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
The observation that roughly 80% of effects come from about 20% of causes, used to focus effort on the vital few.

**Origin:** Named after economist Vilfredo Pareto (1896); generalised into a quality/management principle by Joseph Juran (mid-20th century).

## Why it works
Impact is unevenly distributed. Identifying the small set of drivers responsible for most of the outcome concentrates limited resources where they matter most.

## When to use
To prioritise drivers, customers, defects or initiatives by impact.

## Visual
`chart`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List the causes/items and measure each one's contribution to the outcome.
2. Rank them from largest to smallest.
3. Compute the cumulative percentage and find where it crosses ~80%.
4. Identify the vital few causes below that line.
5. Concentrate action on the vital few; deprioritise the trivial many.

## Real-life example — Microsoft
Microsoft found that fixing the ~20% of reported bugs causing ~80% of crashes eliminated the large majority of user-facing errors — focusing engineering on the vital few.

**So what:** Focusing on the vital few yields most of the benefit for a fraction of the effort.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming exactly 80/20 — counter: it's a heuristic; let the data set the cut.
- Ignoring the long tail when it's strategically important — counter: check before discarding.
