# SMART Strategic Objectives

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A method for writing objectives that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.

**Origin:** George T. Doran, 'There's a S.M.A.R.T. way to write management's goals and objectives', Management Review, 1981.

## Why it works
Vague goals can't be managed or judged. Encoding the five attributes makes an objective trackable and accountable, turning ambition into a scorecard.

## When to use
When converting strategy/vision into something you can actually track.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Write each objective as a specific outcome (not an activity).
2. Attach a single metric that proves it.
3. Set a target value that is ambitious but achievable.
4. Confirm relevance to the mission/vision.
5. Add a deadline and an owner; review on a cadence.

## Real-life example — Google
Google runs OKRs (a SMART-adjacent system): objectives paired with measurable key results scored quarterly, e.g. a product team targeting a specific adoption number by quarter-end.

**So what:** Measurable, time-bound objectives create accountability and focus.

## Template
One row per objective; reject any row missing a measure or date.

- [ ] Objective
- [ ] Metric
- [ ] Target
- [ ] Deadline
- [ ] Owner

## Pitfalls
- Confusing activities with outcomes — counter: state the result, not the task.
- Too many objectives — counter: cap at 3-5.
