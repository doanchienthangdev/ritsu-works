# Business Valuation

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
Estimating the economic value of the practice using DCF and/or market multiples.

**Origin:** Standard corporate-finance valuation methods.

## Why it works
Value guides equity, partnership and exit decisions. DCF values future cash flows; comparable multiples cross-check against the market — together they bracket a defensible range.

## When to use
When valuing the practice for equity, partnership or exit.

## Visual
`table`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Project free cash flows from the financial model.
2. Discount them at an appropriate rate and add a terminal value (DCF).
3. Gather comparable-firm revenue/EBITDA multiples.
4. Apply the multiple to your metrics for a market cross-check.
5. Reconcile the two into a defensible valuation range.

## Real-life example — Advisory M&A (method)
Professional-services firms are commonly valued on an EBITDA multiple cross-checked with a DCF; the two methods bracket a range used to price partner buy-ins or a sale.

**So what:** Two independent methods produce a more defensible value than either alone.

## Pitfalls
- DCF hyper-sensitive to assumptions — counter: cross-check with multiples and run sensitivities.
- Cherry-picked comparables — counter: use a representative set.
