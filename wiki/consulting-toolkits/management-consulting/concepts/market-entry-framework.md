# Market-Entry Framework ('should we enter this market?')

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A structured evaluation of whether and how to enter a new market: attractiveness, fit, competition, mode of entry and economics.

**Origin:** Standard consulting case structure combining market-attractiveness and capability-fit logic.

## Why it works
Entry value = market attractiveness × ability to win × economics, net of entry cost and risk. Sequencing these questions prevents entering a big-but-unwinnable market or a winnable-but-tiny one.

## When to use
When the question is 'should we enter market X?'

## Visual

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Size and assess the market (TAM-SAM-SOM + Five Forces + PESTLE).
2. Assess the right to win: capability fit, differentiation, channel access.
3. Evaluate entry modes — organic build, partnership/JV, or acquisition — on speed/cost/control.
4. Model the economics (investment, NPV, payback) under base/upside/downside.
5. Surface key risks and define a go/no-go with exit options.

## Real-life example — IKEA in India
IKEA spent years assessing India's attractiveness, local sourcing rules and consumer fit, then chose a phased owned-store entry tailored to local price points — an explicit attractiveness-then-fit-then-mode sequence.

**So what:** Disciplined market-entry analysis sequences 'is it attractive?' before 'can we win?' before 'how?'.

## Pitfalls
- Sizing the market but skipping 'can we win?' — counter: both are required.
- One entry mode considered — counter: compare build/partner/acquire.
