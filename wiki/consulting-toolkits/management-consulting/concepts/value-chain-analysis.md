# Value Chain Analysis

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A decomposition of a firm's activities into primary and support activities to find where value (and margin) is created and where advantage can be built.

**Origin:** Michael E. Porter, 'Competitive Advantage', 1985.

## Why it works
Advantage is built activity by activity. Separating primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing & sales, service) and support activities (firm infrastructure, HR, technology, procurement) shows where costs and differentiation actually arise.

## When to use
In internal analysis and to find operational margin or differentiation levers.

## Visual
`value-chain`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Map the firm's primary and support activities.
2. Estimate the cost and the value-add of each activity.
3. Identify which activities drive cost advantage and which drive differentiation.
4. Benchmark key activities against rivals.
5. Target the activities with the biggest margin or differentiation opportunity for improvement.

## Real-life example — Walmart
Walmart's cost advantage is built in specific value-chain activities — inbound logistics (cross-docking), procurement scale, and IT-driven inventory systems — that together produce its everyday-low-price position.

**So what:** Advantage is engineered in specific activities, not the firm 'in general'.

## Template
Cost + value-add per activity; mark advantage sources.

- [ ] Primary activities (cost/value)

- [ ] Cost-advantage activities
- [ ] Differentiation activities

## Pitfalls
- Analysing activities in isolation — counter: look at linkages between them.
- Cost-only lens — counter: also map differentiation value.
