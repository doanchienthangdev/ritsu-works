# McKinsey's 7 Degrees of Freedom for Growth

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A framework laying out seven distinct, increasingly bold avenues for growth, used to generate and prioritise growth initiatives.

**Origin:** McKinsey & Company (Baghai, Coley & White, 'The Alchemy of Growth', 1999).

## Why it works
Growth comes from compounding moves of increasing ambition — from selling more to current customers, out to creating new industry structures. Best companies pursue several degrees as a 'staircase' of continuously compounding skills and options, matched to where they are and where the world is heading.

## When to use
When generating and prioritising a growth strategy.

## Visual
`staircase`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Understand the seven degrees of freedom and what each entails.
2. Identify where your business currently stands across the seven, to see which to focus on first.
3. Determine which growth opportunities are available by analysing your current market and where you can expand.
4. Prioritise initiatives by assessing the potential impact of each and which will move the business most.
5. Develop an action plan with specific steps, timelines and success metrics for the chosen degrees.

## Real-life example — The Walt Disney Company
Disney has used all seven: selling more to existing fans (sequels like Toy Story 2), acquiring new customers (expanding theme parks/cruises), new products (Disney+), new value-delivery (personalised park experiences), new geographies (Shanghai & Hong Kong parks), a new industry structure (acquiring Marvel & Lucasfilm), and new competitive arenas (entering streaming with Disney+).

**So what:** Compounding several degrees of freedom drove decades of Disney growth.

## Pitfalls
- Chasing all seven at once — counter: prioritise by impact and where you stand.
- Ignoring sequencing — counter: build the staircase from your current position.
