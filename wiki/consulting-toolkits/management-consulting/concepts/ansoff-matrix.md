# Ansoff Growth Matrix

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A 2x2 that classifies growth strategies by whether products and markets are existing or new: market penetration, product development, market development, diversification.

**Origin:** Igor Ansoff, 'Strategies for Diversification', Harvard Business Review, 1957.

## Why it works
Risk rises as you move away from what you know. Penetration (existing/existing) is lowest risk; diversification (new/new) is highest. The matrix forces an explicit risk-vs-growth choice.

## When to use
When choosing a growth direction and balancing risk vs ambition.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Map current products and markets.
2. Place each candidate growth move into one of the four quadrants.
3. Assess the risk and required capability for each.
4. Prefer lower-risk quadrants first unless the strategic case for diversification is strong.
5. Build the resourcing and risk-mitigation plan for the chosen quadrant(s).

## Real-life example — Coca-Cola
Coca-Cola applies all four: penetration (promotions on Coke in core markets), product development (Coke Zero), market development (entering new countries), and diversification (acquiring Costa Coffee — new product, new market).

**So what:** Naming the quadrant makes the risk of a growth move explicit and fundable.

## Template

- [ ] Growth move

## Pitfalls
- Jumping to diversification for excitement — counter: exhaust lower-risk growth first.
- Ignoring capability gaps — counter: assess the resources each quadrant needs.
