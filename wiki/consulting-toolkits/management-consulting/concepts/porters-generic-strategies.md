# Porter's Generic Strategies

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
Three fundamental ways to achieve competitive advantage: cost leadership, differentiation, and focus (cost or differentiation).

**Origin:** Michael E. Porter, 'Competitive Strategy', 1980.

## Why it works
Advantage comes from either lower cost or higher perceived value, applied broadly or to a narrow segment. Trying to do both broadly leaves a firm 'stuck in the middle' with no clear advantage.

## When to use
When choosing the competitive posture / basis of advantage.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Decide competitive scope: broad market or a narrow focused segment.
2. Choose the basis of advantage: lowest cost or differentiation.
3. Commit to the one strategy and align the whole value chain to it.
4. Check you are not straddling (stuck in the middle).
5. Build the capabilities the chosen strategy requires (e.g. scale for cost, brand/R&D for differentiation).

## Real-life example — IKEA (cost leadership) vs Ferrari (differentiation focus)
IKEA pursues broad cost leadership (flat-pack, self-service, scale) while Ferrari pursues differentiation focus (exclusive, high-performance, narrow segment) — each coherent and each profitable, neither stuck in the middle.

**So what:** A clear, committed generic strategy beats trying to be all things to all buyers.
