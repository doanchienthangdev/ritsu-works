# Strategic Group Mapping

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A 2-axis map that clusters competitors into groups following similar strategies, revealing where a firm really competes and where white space exists.

**Origin:** Concept from Michael Hunt (1972) and developed in Porter's 'Competitive Strategy' (1980).

## Why it works
Not all competitors compete with each other; rivalry is fiercest within a strategic group. Mapping groups on the two dimensions that matter exposes mobility barriers and un-served positions.

## When to use
In competitor analysis and when choosing the practice's or client's positioning.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Choose the two strategic dimensions that most differentiate firms (e.g. price vs specialisation).
2. Plot each competitor; size bubbles by market share/revenue.
3. Draw boundaries around clusters that follow similar strategies.
4. Identify mobility barriers between groups and any empty, attractive positions.
5. Decide which group to own or whether to claim the white space.

## Real-life example — Automotive industry
Mapping price vs breadth places Toyota/VW (broad, mid-price), Ferrari/Lamborghini (narrow, premium) and Tata (broad, low-price) in distinct strategic groups, clarifying who actually competes with whom.

**So what:** Strategy choices are about which group to join — and the white space between groups can be the prize.

## Pitfalls
- Choosing axes that don't differentiate — counter: use dimensions buyers trade off on.
- Ignoring mobility barriers — counter: assess how hard it is to move between groups.
