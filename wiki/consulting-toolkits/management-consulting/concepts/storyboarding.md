# Storyboarding (Ghost Deck)

*Category: communication · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
Laying out a presentation as a sequence of action-titled slide sketches before building any slides, so the argument's logic is fixed first.

**Origin:** Standard consulting deck-development practice (ghost deck / horizontal logic).

## Why it works
Building slides before the storyline wastes effort and buries the argument. A storyboard with action-titles (each a complete 'so what' sentence) makes the whole argument visible and editable on one page before any chart is built.

## When to use
Before building any client deck.

## Visual
`process-flow`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Take the Pyramid argument and assign each key point to a slide.
2. Write each slide's title as a complete sentence stating its conclusion (action-title).
3. Read all titles in order — they must tell the whole story coherently.
4. Sketch the single chart/visual that proves each slide's title.
5. Get sign-off on the storyboard before building slides in software.

## Real-life example — Consulting (method)
Teams routinely pin a 'ghost deck' on a wall — title strips only — and rearrange them until the titles alone make the argument, before a single chart is produced.

**So what:** Fixing the storyline first makes deck production fast and the argument tight.

## Template
One action-title + one chart idea per slide; read titles in sequence.

- [ ] Slide #
- [ ] Action-title (full sentence)
- [ ] Supporting chart/visual
- [ ] Source/analysis

## Pitfalls
- Label-titles ('Revenue') instead of action-titles ('Revenue fell on price, not volume') — counter: every title is a claim.
- Skipping the storyboard — counter: never build slides before the storyline is signed off.
