# GE-McKinsey Nine-Box Matrix

*Category: portfolio · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A 3x3 portfolio matrix plotting business units by industry attractiveness and competitive strength to guide investment priority.

**Origin:** Developed by McKinsey & Company for General Electric in the early 1970s to manage GE's 150+ business units.

## Why it works
BCG's single proxies (growth, share) were too blunt for GE's complexity. GE-McKinsey uses weighted multi-factor scores — nine industry-attractiveness factors and twelve business-strength factors — giving a richer, more defensible capital-allocation guide across nine cells.

## When to use
For nuanced BU portfolio and capital-allocation decisions where single proxies are too crude.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List the business units to assess.
2. Define and weight the industry-attractiveness factors (market size, growth, profitability, intensity of competition, regulation, cyclicality...).
3. Define and weight the business-strength factors (market share, brand, cost position, capabilities, customer loyalty...).
4. Score each BU on both axes (weighted average) and plot into the 3x3 grid, bubble-sized by market size.
5. Apply the investment logic: Grow (invest) in the high-high band, Hold/selectivity on the diagonal, Harvest/divest in the low-low band.

## Real-life example — General Electric
GE used the matrix to triage 150+ units competing for capital — directing investment to units in attractive industries where GE was strong, holding the diagonal, and exiting weak units in unattractive industries. It became the template for multi-business capital allocation.

**So what:** Multi-factor scoring makes capital-allocation calls across a complex portfolio defensible, not arbitrary.

## Template
Weight and score both axes; plot market-size-sized bubbles.

- [ ] BU
- [ ] Attractiveness factors + weights + score
- [ ] Strength factors + weights + score
- [ ] Cell (1-9)
- [ ] Action (grow/hold/harvest)
- [ ] Market size & share

## Pitfalls
- Subjective, un-weighted scoring — counter: agree factors and weights up front with the client.
- Treating it as one-time — counter: re-score as markets shift.
