# SCQ (Situation-Complication-Question)

*Category: communication · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
An introduction structure that frames any communication via the stable Situation, the Complication that disturbs it, and the Question it raises.

**Origin:** Barbara Minto, part of the Pyramid Principle method (McKinsey, 1987).

## Why it works
A reader engages when the problem is framed in their own terms. Situation establishes shared context, Complication creates the tension/need, and Question is exactly what the rest of the document answers — setting up the governing thought.

## When to use
To open any proposal, report or presentation.

## Visual
`process-flow`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Write the Situation: facts the audience already accepts as true.
2. Write the Complication: the change or problem that creates tension.
3. Derive the Question the complication naturally raises.
4. Ensure your governing thought directly answers that Question.
5. Use it to open proposals, memos and decks.

## Real-life example — Consulting practice (proposal)
A turnaround proposal opens: Situation — 'the company has grown revenue 10%/yr'; Complication — 'but margins fell from 18% to 11% over two years'; Question — 'how do we restore margin without sacrificing growth?' — framing the whole engagement.

**So what:** SCQ earns attention by stating the audience's own problem before proposing anything.

## Template
Three sentences; the question must match your answer.

- [ ] Situation
- [ ] Complication
- [ ] Question
- [ ] Answer (governing thought)

## Pitfalls
- A complication the audience doesn't accept — counter: ground it in agreed facts.
- Question that doesn't match the answer — counter: align them tightly.
