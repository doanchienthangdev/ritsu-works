# Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A cause-and-effect diagram that organises the many possible causes of a problem into categories around a central 'spine'.

**Origin:** Kaoru Ishikawa, 1960s, in Japanese quality management.

## Why it works
Complex problems have causes across multiple categories. Grouping candidate causes (e.g. the 6 Ms: Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Mother-nature) ensures a comprehensive search before zeroing in.

## When to use
For multi-cause problems where a broad, categorised search is needed.

## Visual
`process-flow`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Write the problem at the head of the fish.
2. Draw the main bones — typically the 6 Ms (or 4 Ps for service).
3. Brainstorm candidate causes onto each bone with the team.
4. Add sub-causes (ask 'why?' on each) as smaller bones.
5. Identify the most probable causes and test them with data.

## Real-life example — Manufacturing/quality (Ishikawa)
Ishikawa diagrams are used across industries — e.g. a plant analysing a defect spike maps causes across man/machine/method/material/measurement/environment, then runs experiments on the most likely bones.

**So what:** A categorised cause map prevents tunnel vision and surfaces non-obvious causes.

## Template
Use the 6 Ms (or fit categories to context); test top causes.

- [ ] Effect/problem
- [ ] People causes
- [ ] Process causes
- [ ] Machine causes
- [ ] Material causes
- [ ] Measurement causes
- [ ] Environment causes
- [ ] Most probable causes to test

## Pitfalls
- Stopping at brainstorming — counter: validate causes with data.
- Forcing all 6 Ms — counter: keep only relevant categories.
