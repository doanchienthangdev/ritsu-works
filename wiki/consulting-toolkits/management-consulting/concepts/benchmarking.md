# Benchmarking

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
Systematic comparison of a firm's metrics, processes and practices against competitors or best-in-class performers.

**Origin:** Popularised by Xerox in the late 1970s; codified by Robert Camp, 'Benchmarking' (1989).

## Why it works
Performance is relative. Comparing against the best reveals the size of the gap and what 'good' looks like, converting vague underperformance into a quantified target.

## When to use
In internal and competitor analysis to quantify performance gaps.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Select the metrics that matter to the problem (cost per unit, NPS, cycle time, margin).
2. Choose comparators: direct rivals and best-in-class (even cross-industry).
3. Gather data from filings, reports, expert interviews and mystery-shopping.
4. Compute the gap on each metric and normalise for fair comparison.
5. Prioritise the gaps with the largest impact and translate into improvement targets.

## Real-life example — Xerox
Xerox benchmarked its warehousing and order-fulfilment against L.L.Bean (not a competitor) and its manufacturing costs against Japanese rivals, exposing the true scale of its cost gap and driving a turnaround.

**So what:** The best comparator may be outside your industry; the point is to learn what world-class looks like.

## Pitfalls
- Comparing apples to oranges — counter: normalise for scale and scope.
- Copying practices without context — counter: adapt, don't transplant blindly.
