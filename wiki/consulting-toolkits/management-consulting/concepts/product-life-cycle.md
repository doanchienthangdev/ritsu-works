# Product Life Cycle

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A model of the stages a product passes through — Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline — each implying different strategies.

**Origin:** Popularised by Theodore Levitt, 'Exploit the Product Life Cycle', Harvard Business Review, 1965.

## Why it works
Sales and profit follow a predictable arc. Knowing the stage tells you the right marketing, pricing, investment and portfolio move — and warns when to refresh or harvest before decline.

## When to use
To time growth and portfolio moves and to plan product refresh/retire.

## Visual
`chart`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Plot the product's historical sales/profit curve.
2. Identify the current stage from growth rate and competitive intensity.
3. Match the stage to the right play (invest / scale / defend / harvest).
4. Anticipate the next stage and plan the transition (extension or replacement).
5. Tie portfolio decisions (BCG/GE) to the stage.

## Real-life example — Apple iPod
The iPod ran the full cycle — introduction (2001), explosive growth, maturity, then decline as smartphones absorbed its function; Apple harvested it and shifted investment to the iPhone, eventually discontinuing the line.

**So what:** Reading the stage tells you when to invest, defend, or move on.

## Pitfalls
- Treating the curve as inevitable — counter: extensions can reset maturity.
- Mis-reading a temporary dip as decline — counter: confirm with trend + drivers.
