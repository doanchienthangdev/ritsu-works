# Business Model Canvas

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A one-page visual template that describes a business across nine interlocking building blocks, used here to design the consulting practice itself.

**Origin:** Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur, 'Business Model Generation', 2010 (canvas first proposed in Osterwalder's 2004 PhD).

## Why it works
A business is a system: value proposition, customers, channels, relationships, revenue, resources, activities, partners and costs must cohere. Putting all nine on one page exposes inconsistencies (e.g. a premium value prop funded by a low-cost structure) that linear business plans hide.

## When to use
When designing or pivoting a business model and you need a fast, holistic, testable view on one page.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Start on the right: name your Customer Segments (e.g. mid-market manufacturers needing cost transformation).
2. Write the Value Proposition each segment buys — the painful problem you remove.
3. Define Channels (how you reach them) and Customer Relationships (how you keep them).
4. Specify Revenue Streams (fixed fee, retainer, value-based) and their pricing.
5. Move left: list Key Resources, Key Activities and Key Partners required to deliver.
6. Total the Cost Structure and check the model is profitable and internally consistent.

## Real-life example — Stripe
Stripe's early model maps cleanly: segment = developers at online businesses; value prop = 'payments in 7 lines of code'; channel = developer docs/API; revenue = % per transaction; key activity = maintaining the API & compliance; key partners = banks/card networks.

**So what:** A canvas makes a complex business legible in one page and shows why the parts reinforce each other.

## Pitfalls
- Treating it as a static poster — counter: revisit after each of the first 10 client conversations.
- Vague value proposition — counter: state the quantified pain you remove.
