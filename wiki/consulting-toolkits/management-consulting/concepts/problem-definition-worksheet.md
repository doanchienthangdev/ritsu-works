# Problem Definition Worksheet

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A structured one-pager that pins down exactly what the problem is before any solving begins.

**Origin:** A staple of structured problem-solving and Lean/Six Sigma 'problem statement' discipline; popularised in McKinsey's problem-solving method.

## Why it works
Most failed projects solve a fuzzy or wrong problem. Forcing a single sentence with magnitude, location, timing and impact eliminates ambiguity and aligns stakeholders on the same target.

## When to use
At the start of every engagement and before any analysis, to lock the target.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Write the problem as a single, specific sentence stating the gap between current and desired state.
2. Quantify the magnitude (e.g. 'margin fell from 18% to 11% over 8 quarters').
3. Localise it (which units / products / geographies).
4. State the business impact and who feels it.
5. Add an explicit 'out of scope' line to prevent scope creep.
6. Define measurable success criteria, then get the sponsor to sign off.

## Real-life example — Toyota
Toyota's production-system discipline insists a problem is defined as a specific, measurable deviation from a standard before any countermeasure is designed — e.g. 'station 4 stops 3x/shift, costing 40 units/day'.

**So what:** A precise, quantified problem statement is the cheapest leverage point in any engagement.

## Pitfalls
- Embedding the solution in the problem statement — counter: state only the gap, not the fix.
- No quantification — counter: attach a number to magnitude and impact.
