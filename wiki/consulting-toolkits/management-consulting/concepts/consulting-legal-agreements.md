# Consulting Legal Agreements (MSA/SOW, NDA, Non-compete)

*Category: planning · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
The core legal documents that govern a consulting engagement: the consultant/services agreement (with scope, fees, IP, liability), the NDA, and where relevant a non-compete/non-solicit.

**Origin:** Standard professional-services contracting practice.

## Why it works
Engagements involve confidential data, valuable IP and real liability. The right agreements allocate risk, protect both parties and make scope and payment enforceable — preventing disputes that destroy relationships and margins.

## When to use
When closing a proposal and before any engagement begins.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Use a vetted services-agreement template; fill scope, deliverables, fees and payment schedule.
2. Set the IP ownership terms and a liability cap (typically tied to fees) — have counsel review.
3. Execute a mutual NDA before sharing sensitive data.
4. Where appropriate, add a reasonable (scope/geo/time-limited) non-compete or non-solicit.
5. Get both parties to sign before kickoff; store executed copies.

## Real-life example — Professional services (practice)
Boutiques typically run an MSA + per-engagement SOW plus a mutual NDA, capping liability at the fee and assigning deliverable IP to the client on payment — a standard, enforceable structure.

**So what:** Clear, vetted agreements prevent the scope and IP disputes that ruin engagements.

## Template
Use counsel-reviewed templates; never sign without scope, IP and liability defined.

- [ ] Scope & deliverables
- [ ] Fees & payment terms
- [ ] IP ownership
- [ ] Liability cap
- [ ] Confidentiality (NDA)
- [ ] Non-compete terms
- [ ] Termination

## Pitfalls
- Uncapped liability — counter: cap at fees, reviewed by counsel.
- Ambiguous IP ownership — counter: state it explicitly.
- Unreasonable non-compete (unenforceable) — counter: keep scope/geo/time reasonable.
