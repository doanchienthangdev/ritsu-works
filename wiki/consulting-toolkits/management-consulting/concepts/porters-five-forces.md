# Porter's Five Forces

*Category: analysis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A model of the five competitive forces that determine the long-run profitability (attractiveness) of an industry.

**Origin:** Michael E. Porter, Harvard Business Review, 1979 / 'Competitive Strategy', 1980.

## Why it works
Industry structure, not just rivalry, sets the profit pool. The collective strength of five forces — rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, supplier power and buyer power — determines how much value is created and who captures it.

## When to use
When assessing industry attractiveness, market entry, or why margins are structurally constrained.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the industry boundary precisely (product + geography).
2. Assess each of the five forces as High/Medium/Low with evidence.
3. Identify which force most constrains profitability.
4. Conclude on overall industry attractiveness.
5. Derive strategic moves that blunt the strongest adverse force (e.g. raise switching costs to curb buyer power).

## Real-life example — U.S. airline industry
Porter's classic read: intense rivalry, powerful suppliers (aircraft, labour, fuel), powerful buyers (price-transparent, low loyalty) and low entry barriers historically crushed industry returns — explaining chronic low profitability.

**So what:** Even well-run firms struggle in a structurally hostile industry; structure must inform strategy.

- Static snapshot — counter: note how forces are changing.
- Drawing strategy directly from forces without the firm's position — counter: pair with internal analysis.
