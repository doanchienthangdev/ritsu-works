# BCG Growth-Share Matrix

*Category: portfolio · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A 2x2 portfolio tool plotting business units/products by relative market share and market growth into Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks and Dogs.

**Origin:** Bruce Henderson, founder of the Boston Consulting Group, 1968.

## Why it works
Built on the experience curve: market leadership yields a self-reinforcing cost advantage and superior returns, while growth signals where future potential lies. The matrix guides where to invest, hold, harvest or divest cash.

## When to use
For a fast first cut on BU/product portfolio prioritisation and cash allocation.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Define the BUs/products and the relevant market for each.
2. Compute relative market share (your share ÷ largest competitor's share).
3. Estimate each market's growth rate.
4. Plot each as a bubble sized by revenue into the four quadrants.
5. Assign the strategy per quadrant (invest Stars, milk Cash Cows, decide on Question Marks, divest Dogs) and check the portfolio is balanced (Cash Cows funding Stars).

## Real-life example — The Coca-Cola Company
Coca-Cola's portfolio reads as Cash Cows (carbonated soft drinks — high share, low growth funding the rest), Stars/Question Marks (water, sports and energy drinks in growth segments), and Dogs that get pruned — guiding where cash is reinvested.

**So what:** A balanced portfolio uses Cash-Cow cash to fund tomorrow's Stars.

## Pitfalls
- Market share as the only proxy for competitiveness — counter: use GE-McKinsey for multi-factor nuance.
- Defining the market too broadly/narrowly — counter: be explicit and consistent.
