# New-Product Framework ('should we launch this product?')

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A structured go/no-go for a new product: customer need, market, fit, economics and launch.

**Origin:** Standard product-strategy case structure; aligns with stage-gate product development (Robert Cooper, 1980s).

## Why it works
A launch succeeds only if a real, sized need meets a product the firm can build profitably and take to market. Evaluating need-market-fit-economics in sequence kills weak ideas cheaply.

## When to use
When the question is 'should we introduce a new product?'

## Visual
`process-flow`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Validate the customer need (jobs-to-be-done, evidence of willingness to pay).
2. Size the market and assess growth and competition.
3. Confirm capability and differentiation vs alternatives.
4. Model unit economics: price, cost, contribution, breakeven volume, payback.
5. Plan the launch (channel, positioning, ops) and set a go/no-go with milestones.

## Real-life example — Apple iPhone
Apple validated a real job (one device for phone, music and internet), confirmed it could differentiate via integrated hardware-software, and modelled premium economics before the 2007 launch — need-fit-economics aligned.

**So what:** A launch decision is a sequence of falsifiable gates, not a leap of faith.

## Template
Each gate must pass on evidence; quantify economics.

- [ ] Customer need (evidence)
- [ ] Market size/growth
- [ ] Capability & differentiation
- [ ] Unit economics (margin/payback)
- [ ] Launch plan
- [ ] Go/No-go + milestones

## Pitfalls
- Building before validating the need — counter: evidence of willingness to pay first.
- Ignoring cannibalisation — counter: net the impact on existing products.
