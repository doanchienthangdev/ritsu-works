# Profitability Diagnostic Tree ('why did profit fall?')

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A standard issue tree that decomposes a profit decline into its arithmetic and operational causes to localise the driver.

**Origin:** Canonical McKinsey/BCG case structure (profit = revenue − cost).

## Why it works
Profit = Revenue − Cost; Revenue = Price × Volume. Decomposing this way, then splitting by product/segment/geography, isolates whether the decline is a price, volume, mix or cost-line problem — and internal vs external.

## When to use
When the strategic question is 'what caused profit to decrease?'

## Visual
`tree`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Confirm and quantify the profit decline and the period.
2. Split into revenue-side vs cost-side using the P&L.
3. On revenue, separate price, volume and mix effects; on cost, separate fixed/variable and cost lines.
4. Cut each by product, segment and geography to localise the driver.
5. Determine whether the root cause is internal (execution) or external (market), then prioritise the dominant driver.

## Real-life example — Generic CPG case
A consumer-goods client's profit fall decomposes to flat revenue but rising variable cost in one BU; cutting by product shows a single SKU's input-cost spike — a targeted fix, not a company-wide cost programme.

**So what:** Decomposition converts a scary headline number into a single, fixable driver.

## Template
Quantify each branch; localise to the dominant driver.

- [ ] Profit decline ($/period)
- [ ] Revenue effect (price/volume/mix)
- [ ] Cost effect (fixed/variable/line)
- [ ] Cut by product/segment/geo
- [ ] Internal vs external
- [ ] Dominant driver

## Pitfalls
- Jumping to cost-cutting before localising — counter: complete the decomposition first.
- Ignoring mix effects — counter: separate price, volume AND mix.
