# Blue Ocean Strategy (Strategy Canvas + ERRC / Four Actions)

*Category: growth · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A method to create uncontested market space ('blue oceans') by breaking the value-cost trade-off, using the Strategy Canvas and the Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create (ERRC) grid / Four Actions Framework.

**Origin:** W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne; HBR articles (2004) and the book 'Blue Ocean Strategy' (Harvard Business School Press, 2005).

## Why it works
Competing head-to-head in 'red oceans' erodes profit. By reconstructing buyer value — eliminating and reducing factors the industry over-serves while raising and creating new ones — a firm achieves differentiation AND low cost simultaneously, making competition irrelevant.

## When to use
When head-to-head competition is eroding margins and you need uncontested space.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. Draw the Strategy Canvas: list the factors the industry competes on (X) and plot the value curve of incumbents (Y).
2. Apply the Four Actions: ask what to Eliminate, Reduce, Raise and Create.
3. Fill the ERRC grid with concrete factors under each action.
4. Draw the new value curve and confirm it diverges sharply from rivals AND lowers cost.
5. Test it against the buyer-utility map and pricing; build the blue-ocean offer.

## Real-life example — Cirque du Soleil
Cirque eliminated costly animal acts and star performers, reduced multiple-arena/aisle-concession spend, raised the venue/artistry, and created a theatrical theme and refined ambience — combining circus and theatre to reach adults at premium prices, escaping the declining circus 'red ocean'.

**So what:** Reconstructing buyer value can create a whole new, uncontested market.

## Pitfalls
- Only adding (Raise/Create) and never cutting — counter: Eliminate/Reduce is what keeps cost down.
- A curve that mirrors rivals — counter: it must diverge clearly.
