# SWOT / TOWS Analysis

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Management Consulting*

## What it is
A summary of internal Strengths & Weaknesses and external Opportunities & Threats, converted via TOWS into strategic options.

**Origin:** SWOT from Stanford Research Institute work (Albert Humphrey, 1960s); TOWS matrix from Heinz Weihrich (1982).

## Why it works
Strategy is fit between internal capability and external environment. SWOT inventories both; the TOWS matrix then pairs them (SO/ST/WO/WT) to generate actions, not just a description.

## When to use
As the synthesis that bridges diagnosis to solution design.

## Visual
`matrix-2x2`

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. List internal Strengths and Weaknesses (from internal analysis).
2. List external Opportunities and Threats (from PESTLE / 5 Forces).
3. Prioritise to the few that matter in each box.
4. Cross them in the TOWS matrix to generate SO/ST/WO/WT options.
5. Carry the strongest options into solution design.

## Real-life example — Tesla
A Tesla SWOT pairs strengths (brand, battery tech) with the EV-adoption opportunity (SO: expand model range) while addressing weaknesses (production scaling) against threats (incumbents entering) — turning the inventory into moves.

**So what:** SWOT only earns its keep when TOWS converts it into specific strategies.

## Template
Keep each box to the vital few; then complete the TOWS pairings.

- [ ] Strengths
- [ ] Weaknesses
- [ ] Opportunities
- [ ] Threats
- [ ] SO options
- [ ] ST options
- [ ] WO options
- [ ] WT options

## Pitfalls
- A laundry list with no prioritisation — counter: keep 3-5 per box.
- Stopping at the four boxes — counter: always do the TOWS cross.
