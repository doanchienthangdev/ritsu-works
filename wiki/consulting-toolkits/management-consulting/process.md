# Management Consulting Toolkit — The Handbook

## What you're really buying

**One-liner:** A five-phase operating system to launch a consulting practice and run a Fortune-100-grade engagement from sale to recommendation.

This handbook is not a glossary of frameworks you can already name. It is the **executable process** behind a tier-1 management-consulting engagement: the concrete how-to that lets a practitioner *run* the work — stand up a practice, win the client, diagnose the true problem, solve it with the right tools, and land the recommendation in the room. Where most 'framework libraries' stop at recognition, this stops only at *execution*: every step names its goal, the tool that does the work, the concrete how, the input it consumes, the output it produces, the owner, and the gate that tells you it's done.

## When to use this toolkit

Use it when you are **launching or running an independent consulting practice**, **leading a strategy or transformation engagement** (as an external advisor or an internal team), **preparing a client proposal**, or **facing a board-level strategic question** — *why did profit fall? should we enter this market? launch this product? acquire this company?* — and you need a structured, defensible path from blank page to decision.

**Who it's for:** independent and boutique consultants; strategy and transformation teams; in-house strategy, corp-dev and PMO leaders; founders and executives preparing board recommendations; and aspiring consultants preparing for tier-1 casework.

## The process at a glance

The spine is a **sequence with gates**, not a bag of modules. Phases I–II are the commercial wrapper (fund a practice, sign a deal); Phases III–V are the classic problem-solving arc — *define the problem before solving it, solve it with fit-for-purpose tools, then communicate top-down*. Each gate prevents wasted work on an unbought, ill-defined, or unstructured problem.

| Phase | Goal | Key question | Headline deliverable | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I. Practice business plan** | A fundable, differentiated practice | Is there a profitable business here, and what do I sell to whom? | Consulting-practice business plan | SOM big enough + positive unit economics? |
| **II. Proposal & legal** | A signed, de-risked engagement | How do we win this client and protect both sides? | Signed proposal + MSA/NDA/non-compete | Signed with scope, fees, IP, liability? |
| **III. Identify the problem** | The true, structured problem | What's the real problem, broken into MECE pieces? | Diagnosis pack + problem statement + SWOT/TOWS | Sponsor agrees this is the real problem? |
| **IV. Find the solution** | The best-supported recommendation | What's the recommendation, and why this option? | Quantified, prioritised recommendation + roadmap | Best option, quantified, feasible on 7S? |
| **V. Present effectively** | A decision and action | How do we land it so they decide and act? | Board-ready deck + decision + action plan | Client decided with owners/dates? |

## Phase I — Build a fundable consulting practice

**Goal:** stand up a fundable, legally-sound, differentiated practice. **Key question:** is there a profitable, defensible business here — and what exactly will I sell, to whom, and how will I make money? **Duration:** 2–4 weeks.

### The model comes before the plan
Start by drafting the whole business on a **Business Model Canvas** in one session — right to left, from customer segments and value proposition to revenue and cost — forcing every block to be specific to a consulting practice. *Input:* your hypotheses and any prior client conversations. *Output:* a one-page canvas. *Owner:* founder. Then write the **problem-to-solve / solution statement** for the practice itself (the painful, recurring client problem you remove, quantified), set **mission, vision and decision-useful values**, and translate the vision into 3–5 **SMART objectives** that become your scorecard.

### Size the prize and find the white space
Run the **market analysis**: estimate the opportunity with **TAM-SAM-SOM**, computed both top-down (industry consulting spend) and bottom-up (target accounts × average value × win rate), and reconcile the two — a gap larger than ~2× means an assumption is wrong. Layer **PESTLE** for macro tailwinds and headwinds. Then run the **competitor analysis**: a **strategic group map** on the two axes that matter (e.g. price vs specialisation) to find the white space you'll own, with a **benchmarking** table on positioning, price and proof.

### Engine, structure, economics, launch
Design the **marketing & BD funnel** (positioning, 2–3 priority channels, weekly cadence, target CAC). Choose the **operating and legal structure** (entity, talent model, delivery model). Build the **3-statement financial model** to find break-even billed-days and when the practice turns cash-flow positive, and estimate an **indicative valuation** as a north-star. Close with a **90-day action plan** with owners and dates.

**KPIs:** TAM/SAM/SOM reconciled (<2× gap); break-even billed-days computed; white-space chosen vs ≥5 competitors; 90-day plan owned and dated. **Gate:** is the SOM large enough and are unit economics positive at achievable utilisation? If yes, incorporate and start BD; if no, re-segment or kill.

## Phase II — Win the client with a proposal that proves you understand them

**Goal:** convert a qualified opportunity into a signed, scoped, de-risked engagement. **Key question:** how do we win *this* client — with a proposal that proves we understand the problem and a contract that protects both sides? **Duration:** 3–10 business days per opportunity.

Open the proposal by mirroring the client's world with **SCQ** — the stable **Situation**, the **Complication** that disturbs it (quantify the cost of inaction), and the **Question** it raises. State the **proposed solution and a phased approach** whose workstreams map to named methods from Phases III–IV, so the client sees rigor, not hand-waving. Build the **business case & ROI** — value created (incremental profit, cost saved, risk avoided) against the fee, with payback — because a credible ROI makes the fee a rounding error and is the single biggest driver of win rate. Articulate the **value proposition, named team and fees** (fixed / T&M / value-based, matched to the client's risk appetite). Close with a low-friction **next step** (decision date + kickoff date), and on yes, paper it with the three **legal agreements**: a Consultant/Services Agreement (scope, fees, IP, liability cap, termination), a mutual **NDA**, and where relevant a reasonable **non-compete/non-solicit**.

**KPIs:** win rate; business-case ROI ≥ 3–5× the fee; all three agreements executed before kickoff; scope/fees/liability explicitly defined. **Gate:** is the engagement signed with scope, fees, IP and liability defined? No work begins until yes.

## Phase III — Replace the symptom with the real problem

**Goal:** replace the symptom the client presented with the true, structured, evidence-based problem — framed so it can be solved. **Key question:** what is the real problem (not the symptom), and how do we break it into MECE pieces? **Duration:** 2–4 weeks.

### Look inside, then outside, then at rivals
Run the **internal analysis** across five lenses — financial, HR/org, marketing, customers, suppliers — pulling the P&L apart with a **value-driver tree** and grounding the numbers via **financial (ratio & trend) analysis** and interviews. Run the **market analysis** — **TAM-SAM-SOM** to size, **Porter's Five Forces** to judge industry attractiveness, **PESTLE** for the macro picture — which tells you whether the problem is internal execution or a hostile industry. Run the **competitor analysis** — **strategic group mapping**, **benchmarking**, and **competitor profiling** of the 2–3 most threatening rivals — to isolate the competitive gap.

### Drive to root cause, then synthesize
Decompose the symptom into a MECE **issue tree**, drive to root cause with **Five Whys** and a **Fishbone (Ishikawa)** diagram across the relevant cause categories, and converge on a single sharp statement in a **problem-definition worksheet**. This is the hinge of the engagement: *solving the wrong problem perfectly is the most expensive failure mode in consulting.* Finally, roll internal and external findings into a **SWOT**, and convert it via **TOWS** (SO/ST/WO/WT) so it yields strategic options, not just a description — the bridge into Phase IV.

**KPIs:** problem statement validated with the sponsor; issue tree MECE; root cause evidenced not asserted; SWOT converted to ≥3 options. **Gate:** does the sponsor agree this is the real problem? Re-frame until yes before any solutioning.

## Phase IV — Solve it with the right framework for the question

**Goal:** generate, evaluate and select the recommendation using fit-for-purpose strategy frameworks, with quantified impact. **Key question:** given the validated problem, what is the best-supported recommendation — and why this option over the alternatives? **Duration:** 3–6 weeks.

### Match the framework to the question
Don't force a favourite tool — route by the question. Use the **profit-decline diagnostic tree** for *'why did profit fall?'* (Profit = Revenue − Cost; Revenue = Price × Volume; then cut by product/segment/geography to localise the driver). Use the **market-entry framework** for *'should we enter market X?'* (attractive? → can we win? → how to enter? → do the economics work?). Use the **new-product framework** for *'should we launch product Y?'* (real need → market → fit → unit economics → launch). Use the **M&A acquisition screen / deal funnel** for *'should we acquire company Z?'* (rationale → valuation → synergies net of cost-to-achieve → diligence → integration), remembering that *value is created by synergies realised in integration, not by signing the deal.*

### Portfolio, growth, posture, and the dollar case
Where the question is *'where do we invest?'*, plot the portfolio on the **BCG Growth-Share Matrix** (relative share × growth) for a fast first cut and the richer **GE-McKinsey nine-box** (industry attractiveness × business strength, multi-factor) for the actual capital-allocation call. Generate growth options with the **7 Degrees of Freedom** (a staircase of compounding moves), classify them on the **Ansoff Matrix** (product × market risk), test for uncontested space with **Blue Ocean / ERRC**, and time the moves with the **Product Life Cycle**. Commit to a clear basis of advantage with **Porter's Generic Strategies** (avoid 'stuck in the middle') and confirm the organisation can execute it by checking **McKinsey's 7S** alignment. Finally, where the lever is operational, map the **Value Chain** to find margin levers, size each in dollars with the **Value Driver Tree**, and apply the **Pareto Principle** to focus on the ~20% of drivers delivering ~80% of the impact. Converge on a single recommendation with a quantified value case and an implementation roadmap.

**KPIs:** recommendation tied to the validated problem; ≥2 options evaluated on explicit criteria; impact quantified in $/%; Pareto focus applied. **Gate:** is the recommended option the best-supported, quantified and feasible on the 7S? If not, iterate.

## Phase V — Land the recommendation so they decide and act

**Goal:** land the recommendation in the room so the client decides and acts — a top-down, evidence-backed story, not a data tour. **Key question:** how do we structure and deliver the message so the decision-maker says yes and the organisation moves? **Duration:** 1–2 weeks (overlaps Phase IV).

Structure the message with **SCQ + the Pyramid Principle**: open with Situation-Complication-Question, then **answer the question first** (governing thought on top), supported by 3–4 MECE arguments each backed by data — built so an executive who reads only the first line of every slide still gets the whole argument. *Communication runs top-down, the inverse of how the analysis was built.* **Storyboard** the deck as a flow of **action-titled** slides (each title a complete 'so what' sentence; the titles read in sequence as the whole argument) before building anything in software. **Build slides** by choosing the **right chart for each message** (waterfall for bridges, funnel for conversion, matrix for positioning, Gantt for plans, speedometer for KPIs) — one message per slide, title states the conclusion, chart is the evidence. Then **rehearse, deliver and drive to a decision**: pre-empt the killer questions, lead with the recommendation, handle objections with data, and close on owners and dates so the work becomes action, not a filed PDF.

**KPIs:** governing thought on slide 1; action-titles read as a coherent argument; decision secured in the room; next steps owned and dated. **Gate:** did the client decide and commit owners/dates? If deferred, find and close the open objection.

## The frameworks & tools (executability units)

Every framework below follows the same six-part anatomy — *description + origin + logic · visual · numbered tutorial · real-named example · fill-in template · pitfalls/when-to-use* — because the tutorial and template are what make a framework *do-able* rather than merely *known*.

### Portfolio
- **BCG Growth-Share Matrix** (Henderson, BCG, 1968): relative share × market growth → Stars / Cash Cows / Question Marks / Dogs. *Coca-Cola* milks soft-drink Cash Cows to fund water/energy Stars. Fast first cut on cash allocation.
- **GE-McKinsey Nine-Box** (McKinsey for GE, early 1970s): weighted industry attractiveness × business strength on a 3×3 → grow / hold / harvest. *GE* used it to triage 150+ units' capital. The nuanced successor to BCG.

### Growth
- **7 Degrees of Freedom** (McKinsey, *Alchemy of Growth*, 1999): a staircase from selling more to existing customers up to opening new competitive arenas. *Disney* used all seven (sequels → parks → Disney+ → Marvel/Lucasfilm → streaming).
- **Ansoff Matrix** (Ansoff, HBR, 1957): product × market → penetration / product dev / market dev / diversification, by rising risk. *Coca-Cola* spans all four (Coke Zero, new geographies, Costa).
- **Blue Ocean Strategy / ERRC** (Kim & Mauborgne, 2005): Strategy Canvas + Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create to break the value-cost trade-off. *Cirque du Soleil* eliminated animals/stars, created theatrical artistry.
- **Product Life Cycle** (Levitt, HBR, 1965): introduction → growth → maturity → decline, each with its own play. *Apple's iPod* ran the full arc into managed decline.
- **Porter's Generic Strategies** (Porter, 1980): cost leadership / differentiation / focus — never 'stuck in the middle'. *IKEA* (cost) vs *Ferrari* (differentiation focus).
- **Market-entry / New-product / M&A** frameworks: question-routed decision sequences (*IKEA in India*; *Apple iPhone*; *Disney + Pixar*).

### Diagnosis
- **Issue Tree (MECE)** & **Profit-decline tree** (McKinsey method): decompose questions/problems into testable, non-overlapping branches.
- **Five Whys** (Toyoda, TPS): iterate 'why?' to root cause — the *Toyota* strainer example.
- **Fishbone / Ishikawa** (Ishikawa, 1960s): categorise candidate causes (6 Ms) before testing.
- **Problem Definition Worksheet** & **SWOT/TOWS** (Humphrey 1960s; Weihrich 1982): pin the problem; turn the inventory into options.

### Analysis & financial
- **Porter's Five Forces** (Porter, 1979/80): industry attractiveness — the *US airline* hostile-structure classic.
- **PESTLE** (from Aguilar, 1967): macro scan — *Netflix* reading broadband + on-demand.
- **Strategic Group Mapping** (Hunt 1972/Porter 1980), **Benchmarking** (Xerox/Camp 1989 — the *L.L.Bean* comparator), **Competitor Profiling** (cola wars).
- **Value Chain** (Porter, 1985): primary + support activities → margin — *Walmart's* logistics/IT.
- **Value Driver Tree** (DuPont lineage) & **Financial Analysis** (DuPont decomposition): size value in dollars.
- **McKinsey 7S** (Peters/Waterman/Pascale, ~1980): align strategy/structure/systems/shared-values/style/staff/skills — *Apple's* reinforcing system.
- **Pareto Principle** (Pareto 1896/Juran): the vital few — *Microsoft's* 20%-of-bugs/80%-of-crashes.

### Communication
- **Pyramid Principle** & **SCQ** (Minto, 1987): answer-first, MECE-supported — the McKinsey house style.
- **Storyboarding** (ghost deck) & **Chart Selection** (Zelazny, *Say It With Charts*): fix the storyline in action-titles first; match chart to message type.
- **Executive Storyline Delivery**: rehearse, pre-empt objections, close on owners/dates.

### Practice-building
- **Business Model Canvas** (Osterwalder, 2010 — *Stripe*), **Value Proposition Canvas** (2014 — *Uber*), **Mission/Vision/Values** (*Patagonia*), **SMART Objectives** (Doran, 1981 — *Google* OKRs), **Marketing Funnel** (AIDA lineage — *HubSpot*), **Operating-model design**, **3-Statement Model**, **Business Valuation**, **Action Plan**, **Engagement Approach Design**, **Business Case & ROI**, **Consulting Legal Agreements**.

## How to choose the right framework (the routing logic)

The single most common amateur error is reaching for a favourite framework before knowing the question. Route by the *type of question* on the table:

- **'What is happening / why?' (diagnosis)** → start with an **issue tree** to structure, then **Five Whys** or **Fishbone** to reach root cause; for the specific 'why did profit fall?' question use the **profit-decline tree**. Ground every branch with **financial-analysis** and the **value-driver tree**.
- **'Is the environment for/against us?' (external)** → **Porter's Five Forces** (industry attractiveness), **PESTLE** (macro), and **strategic group mapping / competitor profiling** (rivals).
- **'Where do we invest across the portfolio?'** → **BCG** for a fast cut, **GE-McKinsey** for the multi-factor capital call.
- **'How do we grow?'** → generate with **7 Degrees of Freedom**, classify risk with **Ansoff**, escape competition with **Blue Ocean / ERRC**, time it with the **Product Life Cycle**.
- **'Should we enter / launch / acquire?'** → the **market-entry**, **new-product**, and **M&A** decision sequences respectively — each a gated chain ending in economics.
- **'What is our basis of advantage, and can we deliver it?'** → **Porter's Generic Strategies** + **McKinsey 7S** alignment.
- **'Where is value made and which levers matter most?'** → **Value Chain** + **Value Driver Tree** + **Pareto**.
- **'How do we say it?'** → **SCQ + Pyramid Principle**, then **storyboarding** and **chart selection**.

Two cross-cutting disciplines run through all of them. First, **MECE** — every decomposition (issue tree, SWOT, pyramid) must be mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive; run the two-test check (do any items overlap? is anything missing?) at every level. Second, **hypothesis-driven analysis** — once the tree is built, lead with the branch you believe matters most and test it first, rather than analysing every branch to equal depth; this is what lets a small team produce a tier-1 answer in weeks, not months. The frameworks are interchangeable lenses; the *routing* and these two disciplines are the durable skill.

## A worked end-to-end example — the 'NovaParts' profit-decline engagement

**Situation.** NovaParts (a disguised composite of a mid-market industrial-components maker) grew revenue ~10%/yr for five years across four business units. **Complication.** Group operating margin slid from 17% to 11% over eight quarters. **Question (Phase II SCQ):** how do we restore margin without sacrificing growth? The proposal quantified ~$6M of recoverable annual margin against a $450K fee (>13× first-year ROI), proposing a four-phase approach; it was signed with an NDA and a fee-capped MSA.

**Phase III — diagnosis.** Internal analysis built a value-driver tree showing revenue flat-to-up but variable cost rising fastest in BU2 and BU4. Financial-ratio trends confirmed a gross-margin (not opex) problem. Porter's Five Forces showed a structurally tough industry (powerful steel suppliers, price-transparent buyers), and benchmarking put NovaParts' conversion cost ~15% above best-in-class. A MECE issue tree split 'margin ↓' into price / volume / mix / cost; Five Whys on the dominant branch traced rising variable cost to un-renegotiated, single-source raw-material contracts and an unfavourable product mix shift toward low-margin SKUs. A fishbone confirmed causes across *materials* (sourcing) and *method* (pricing discipline). The validated problem statement: *'Group margin fell 6pts primarily because (a) raw-material costs rose ~12% under single-source contracts and (b) mix shifted to low-margin SKUs in BU2/BU4 — not because of revenue or overhead.'* The sponsor signed off, passing the problem-definition gate. SWOT/TOWS produced three options: renegotiate sourcing, reprice/re-mix, and rationalise the portfolio.

**Phase IV — solution.** A BCG/GE-McKinsey portfolio view showed BU2 as a Question Mark and BU4 as a near-Dog, while BU1 was a Cash Cow. Value-chain analysis localised the cost levers to procurement and operations; the value-driver tree sized them: multi-sourcing + renegotiation ≈ $3.2M, mix/price discipline ≈ $2.1M, SKU rationalisation of the BU4 tail ≈ $0.9M. Pareto confirmed three levers delivered ~85% of the prize. Porter's Generic Strategies clarified NovaParts should commit to *cost leadership in BU1/BU3 and differentiation focus in BU2*, and a 7S check flagged that procurement *skills* and incentive *systems* needed strengthening to sustain the savings. The recommendation: a sourcing-and-pricing programme plus BU4 tail rationalisation, ~$6.2M annual margin, 14-month implementation, passing the recommendation gate.

**Phase V — present.** The team structured the deck on the Pyramid Principle (governing thought slide 1: *'Restore 6pts of margin via sourcing and mix discipline — without cutting growth — worth ~$6M/yr'*), storyboarded it in action-titles, and used a **waterfall** to bridge old-to-new margin by lever, a **2×2** for the portfolio, and a **Gantt** for the roadmap. In the room they led with the recommendation, pre-empted the 'will suppliers walk?' objection with the multi-sourcing analysis, secured the decision, and left with named owners and dates. *Takeaway:* the same arc — define → solve → communicate — converts a frightening headline number into a funded, owned action plan.

## Templates — index & how to use

1. **Engagement Workplan (CSV)** — the phase × step × framework × input × output × owner × gate spine; use it to run any engagement and track gates. Fill one row per step; never skip the gate column.
2. **Problem Definition Worksheet (MD)** — pin the problem before analysing; if any field is blank you are not ready. Get sponsor sign-off.
3. **Framework Application Worksheet (MD)** — the universal six-part anatomy applied to whichever framework you're using on the live situation; forces a tutorial-followed, example-checked, template-filled application.

Each per-framework template (in `frameworks[]`) is the fill-in-the-blank version a practitioner uses on their own situation — the difference between *knowing* a framework and *running* it.

## Pitfalls & best practices (the top failure modes)

- **Solving the wrong problem.** The most expensive failure in consulting. Counter: never leave Phase III without a sponsor-validated, quantified problem statement and a MECE issue tree.
- **Framework-first instead of question-first.** Tools applied for show. Counter: route the framework by the question (Phase IV step 1); evaluate ≥2 options on explicit criteria.
- **Un-quantified recommendations.** 'Improve efficiency' funds nothing. Counter: size every lever in dollars with the value-driver tree; apply Pareto to focus.
- **MECE in name only.** Overlapping or gappy trees. Counter: run the two-test MECE check at every level.
- **Building slides before the storyline.** Counter: storyboard in action-titles and get sign-off first; one message per slide; lead with the answer.
- **Presenting instead of deciding.** A great deck with no decision is a filed PDF. Counter: close every readout on owners and dates.
- **Skipping the commercial gates.** Working unbought or unprotected. Counter: enforce the signature gate (Phase II) — scope, fees, IP and liability defined before kickoff.
- **Top-down-only market sizing / single-case financials.** Counter: triangulate TAM/SAM/SOM bottom-up too; always run a downside in the model.
