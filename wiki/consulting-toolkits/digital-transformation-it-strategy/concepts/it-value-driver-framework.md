# IT Value Driver Framework

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A structured framework that identifies the 4–6 mechanisms through which IT creates measurable value for the organisation (Revenue Enablement, Cost Efficiency, Risk Reduction, Business Agility, Employee Productivity, Customer Experience) and quantifies the current and target IT contribution for each.

**Origin:** Derived from MIT Sloan CISR research on IT business value (Weill, Broadbent, and colleagues, 1990s–2000s) and McKinsey's IT value delivery framework. Widely used in IT strategy and investment governance contexts.

## Why it works
IT investment is often justified by cost comparison ('on-premise vs. cloud') rather than business value creation. The IT Value Driver Framework forces the CIO to articulate how IT creates business value across the full spectrum of mechanisms — not just cost — enabling a richer investment justification and a more strategic IT-business conversation.

## When to use
Use in Phase IT-I Step 5 (IT Value Drivers) to define the investment justification framework for the IT Strategy. Also use at annual IT budget reviews to update the value narrative.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Confirm the 4–6 most relevant value drivers for the organisation based on the business strategy.
2. 2. Define the measurement metric for each driver: the metric must be quantifiable and attributable to IT.
3. 3. Establish the current IT contribution baseline for each driver: what is the current metric value, and how much of it is attributable to IT investment?
4. 4. Set the target IT contribution: based on the IT Strategy investment level, what improvement in each metric is expected by Year 3?
5. 5. Calculate the financial value of improvement: for Revenue Enablement and Cost Efficiency, translate the metric improvement to £/$M. For other drivers, estimate the financial proxy.
6. 6. Use the framework to justify IT investment: the IT Value Driver Framework is the narrative for the IT budget. It answers: 'Why should the business invest in IT?' with a portfolio of value creation mechanisms.
7. 7. Track quarterly: include IT Value Driver metrics in the IT Balanced Scorecard and the IT Steering Committee dashboard.
8. 8. Refine annually: as measurement capability matures, refine the attribution methodology (e.g., move from estimated to measured revenue uplift).

## Real-life example — Walmart
Walmart's CIO explicitly frames IT investment through 4 value drivers: (1) Revenue Enablement — Walmart.com and mobile app revenue as % of total; (2) Cost Efficiency — supply chain automation savings (>$1B annually from AI-powered demand forecasting); (3) Business Agility — time to deploy a new promotion from 3 days to 4 hours through the Walmart media network; (4) Customer Experience — NPS for digital channels vs. in-store. The four-driver framework allows the Board to evaluate IT investment across all value creation mechanisms, not just cost reduction.

**So what:** The IT Value Driver Framework prevents the 'IT as cost centre' framing that dominates many Board discussions. By quantifying IT's contribution to revenue, agility, and customer experience — not just cost efficiency — the CIO can make the case for strategic IT investment rather than defending against cost reduction pressure.

## Pitfalls
- Focusing only on cost efficiency — IT's most strategic value creation is in Revenue Enablement and Business Agility; an IT Value Driver Framework dominated by cost efficiency signals an IT function operating below its strategic potential.
- Metrics without attribution methodology — claiming revenue uplift without a credible attribution model (controlled experiment, multi-touch attribution) will be challenged by Finance.
