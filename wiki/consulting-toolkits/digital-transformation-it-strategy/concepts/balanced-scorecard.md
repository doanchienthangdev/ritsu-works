# Balanced Scorecard

*Category: performance · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A strategic performance management framework that translates vision and strategy into a balanced set of KPIs across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Business Process, and Learning & Growth — ensuring no single dimension dominates and lagging metrics are paired with leading indicators.

**Origin:** Robert Kaplan (Harvard Business School) and David Norton, 'The Balanced Scorecard — Measures That Drive Performance', Harvard Business Review, January 1992. Expanded in their 1996 book and further developed with strategy maps in 2004.

## Why it works
Traditional management systems over-index on financial metrics, which are lagging indicators. The BSC adds three leading-indicator perspectives: Customer (drives financial results), Internal Process (drives customer value), and Learning & Growth (enables process excellence). The causal chain is: invest in people and culture → improve processes → deliver customer value → generate financial results. Strategy maps make these linkages explicit and testable.

## When to use
Use in Phase II to define Digital Transformation strategic objectives; in Phase IT-I for IT Strategic Objectives; in Phase V to design the KPI architecture for the performance dashboard.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Confirm the transformation vision and the 3–5 strategic themes that must be advanced.
2. 2. Define 2–3 Financial objectives: what financial outcomes must the programme deliver? Link each to the ROI model (e.g., 'Achieve £180M NPV over 5 years').
3. 3. Define 2–3 Customer objectives: what must improve in the customer experience to drive the financial results? (e.g., 'Achieve e-commerce conversion of 4.0%'; 'Deliver NPS >65').
4. 4. Define 2–3 Internal Process objectives: what processes must be excellent to deliver the customer outcomes? (e.g., 'Automate 70% of order processing'; 'Reduce time-to-market for digital features to 4 weeks').
5. 5. Define 2–3 Learning & Growth objectives: what human and organisational capabilities must be built? (e.g., 'Achieve Digital Maturity Score 3.5/5'; '80% of managers digitally upskilled').
6. 6. Build the strategy map: draw causal arrows between objectives across perspectives. Verify the logic reads as a coherent story: L&G enables Process, Process enables Customer, Customer enables Financial.
7. 7. Define KPIs, baselines, and targets for each objective — each needs a number, a current value, a target, and a deadline.
8. 8. Assign owners: each KPI must have a named individual accountable for performance.
9. 9. Review monthly: update actuals vs. targets; investigate RED KPIs using the Issue Tree; adjust the programme as needed.

## Real-life example — Hilton Hotels & Resorts
Hilton's digital transformation (Connected Room, Hilton Honours app, Digital Key) used a BSC approach. Financial: incremental revenue per digital member vs. traditional. Customer: app adoption rate, digital key usage, digital NPS. Internal Process: app uptime, check-in time, digital feature release velocity. Learning & Growth: hotel staff training completion, innovation pipeline. The causal chain: better-trained staff (L&G) → smoother digital check-in (Process) → higher digital NPS (Customer) → higher repeat bookings from digital members (Financial). By 2022, Hilton Honours had 129 million members with digital members generating measurably higher RevPAR.

**So what:** The BSC's power is the causal chain. Each perspective's objectives exist to enable the next layer up. Without the strategy map, the BSC becomes four independent lists rather than a coherent theory of how the transformation creates value.

## Pitfalls
- Too many KPIs — limit to 12–15 total; more dilutes focus without improving management.
- KPIs without owners — anonymous KPIs are never managed; assign a named individual to each.
- Ignoring the causal chain — the BSC is a system, not four lists; the strategy map is mandatory.
- Outdated BSC — review objectives quarterly; update when strategy evolves.
