# Communication Plan (Cascade Model)

*Category: change-management · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A structured matrix defining all programme communications: message, audience, channel, frequency, owner, and success metric — organised around a cascade model (CEO → ExCo → Line Managers → Teams) to ensure consistent, timely, multi-channel messaging throughout the transformation.

**Origin:** Standard programme management tool; formalised in OGC MSP and PMI PMBOK communication management. The cascade model draws on internal communications best practice from the 1990s–2000s.

## Why it works
In large organisations, communications that go from Programme Director to all-staff (bypassing line managers) are ignored or misunderstood because they lack local context. The cascade model leverages managers as trusted communication intermediaries who translate corporate messages into team-specific meaning. Each level of the cascade must understand and be equipped to deliver the message before communicating to the next level.

## When to use
Use in Phase IV Step 4. Launch the communication plan before any digital change is visible to end-users.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Map the cascade: define 3–4 cascade levels (CEO → ExCo → Line Managers → Teams). Each level receives the message before passing it down.
2. 2. Identify key messages by programme phase: Phase I (why we are transforming), Phase II (what the plan is), Phase III (how delivery is progressing), Phase IV (what is changing for you).
3. 3. Select channels appropriate to the audience: town halls for all-staff (low frequency, high importance messages), manager briefing packs (monthly, before team meetings), intranet updates (weekly, for detailed updates), short video messages (CEO, quarterly milestones).
4. 4. Define success metrics per communication: open rate (email), attendance rate (town halls), awareness score (pulse survey), manager cascade completion rate.
5. 5. Build the content calendar: schedule all communication events for the first 6 months. Assign owners for each.
6. 6. Create manager briefing packs: for each key message, produce a 1-page briefing for line managers that includes: the message, the key talking points, likely questions and suggested answers, and the call to action for their team.
7. 7. Measure and adapt: monthly measure the awareness score in the change readiness pulse survey. If scores are not improving, adjust the channel, message, or messenger.
8. 8. Maintain a FAQ: publish and update a programme FAQ. Directing people to the FAQ reduces the volume of repeat questions to the programme team.

## Real-life example — Barclays UK
Barclays' branch digital transformation (2017–2019) used a 4-level cascade: Group CEO communication (video, all-staff, quarterly), UK Retail MD communication (monthly briefing email), Area Manager briefing (face-to-face monthly, with manager pack), and Branch Manager team talk (weekly, using the manager pack). The cascade ensured that the 24,000 branch staff received consistent messaging adapted to their context. Manager cascade completion rate was measured monthly and became a KPI for Area Directors.

**So what:** The cascade model ensures that communications are contextualised at each level. A global 'we are transforming our technology' message is meaningless to a branch cashier; 'here is how the new system will change your Monday morning cash balancing procedure' is actionable.

## Pitfalls
- All-staff communications that bypass managers — managers who are surprised by news from their team members lose credibility and become change resistors.
- Measuring communications activity (events held) rather than communications effectiveness (awareness scores).
