# IT Capability Maturity Framework (IT-CMF)

*Category: diagnosis · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A comprehensive framework developed by the Innovation Value Institute that assesses an IT organisation's maturity across 35 IT management capabilities grouped into four macro-capabilities, producing a diagnostic that drives the IT strategic improvement roadmap.

**Origin:** Developed by the Innovation Value Institute (IVI) at Maynooth University, Ireland, from 2006. Published as an open standard in 2012. Built on the Software CMM/CMMI tradition (Watts Humphrey, Carnegie Mellon, 1987) applied to IT management broadly. Now used by 200+ organisations globally.

## Why it works
IT organisations face competing demands: run the business efficiently (keep the lights on), change the business (deliver transformation), and enable future capabilities. The IT-CMF provides a structured way to assess all 35 dimensions of IT management capability — not just technical — including IT financial management, service management, IT governance, and talent development. The 5-level maturity scale (Initial → Managed → Defined → Quantitatively Managed → Optimising) provides a common language for the gap discussion and a sequenced improvement path.

## When to use
Use in Phase IT-I Step 3 (Assess Current and Target IT Capability Maturity) to baseline the IT organisation and set 3-year targets. The assessment output directly drives the IT initiative pipeline in Phase IT-III.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Prepare the assessment: select 2–3 assessors per capability domain from the IT leadership team. Brief assessors on the IT-CMF maturity descriptors (available from IVI's published framework).
2. 2. Score each of the 35 capabilities: for each capability, assessors independently score the current maturity level (1–5) using the maturity level descriptors. Discuss divergent scores to reach consensus.
3. 3. Aggregate to macro-capability scores: calculate the average score per macro-capability (Managing IT like a Business, Managing the IT Budget, Managing the IT Capability, Managing IT for Business Value).
4. 4. Plot the radar chart: visualise the current-state profile across all four macro-capabilities. Identify the most significant gaps relative to industry benchmark (available from IVI research or equivalent Gartner/Forrester data).
5. 5. Set target maturity levels: based on the business demand signal (IT capability gaps identified in the Business Capability Map) and the investment level available, set target scores at 12-month and 3-year horizons for each capability.
6. 6. Prioritise improvement initiatives: rank capabilities by (Target – Current) × Strategic Importance. The highest-ranked capabilities with the largest gap and highest strategic importance drive the IT initiative pipeline.
7. 7. Build the IT Improvement Roadmap: sequence the capability improvement initiatives over 3 years. Ensure dependencies are respected (e.g., improving 'IT Financial Management' typically must precede 'IT Investment Governance').
8. 8. Re-assess annually: run the IT-CMF assessment annually to measure progress. Report improvement in the IT Balanced Scorecard as a 'Learning & Growth' metric.

## Real-life example — Vodafone Group
Vodafone used an IT capability maturity assessment (aligned to IT-CMF principles) as part of its 'TechX' digital transformation programme (2020–2022). The assessment revealed that despite Vodafone being a technology company, its IT organisation scored only 2.1/5 on IT Financial Management (costs were opaque — no clear unit cost for IT services) and 1.8/5 on Benefits Realisation Management (projects delivered but benefits were not systematically tracked). These two gaps were designated as critical blockers: without knowing the cost of IT services, Vodafone could not make rational sourcing decisions; without tracking benefits, the transformation board could not demonstrate ROI. Targeted improvements in these two capabilities — an IT unit cost model and a benefits realisation office — were prioritised in Year 1 and unlocked the broader transformation investment case.

**So what:** The IT-CMF assessment often reveals gaps in the management capabilities that are prerequisites for successful technology delivery — financial transparency and benefits tracking being the most common. Organisations that skip these foundation management capability improvements find their technology investments are technically successful but organisationally unable to demonstrate their value.

## Pitfalls
- Self-assessment bias — IT leaders systematically score their own capabilities higher than external assessors would; build in a validation step with an external peer or consultant.
- Improving for the score — the maturity score is a means, not an end; avoid the trap of documenting processes to score at Level 3 without actually changing how the work is done.
- Improving all capabilities simultaneously — with limited IT resources, attempting to close every gap at once achieves nothing; use the prioritisation framework to focus on the 5 most impactful gaps.
