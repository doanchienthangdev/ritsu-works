# Critical Success Factor Analysis

*Category: strategy · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A structured workshop and register tool that identifies the 5–7 conditions that must be true for the digital transformation programme to succeed, weighted by impact and failure risk, reviewed monthly throughout delivery.

**Origin:** John Rockart, MIT Sloan, 'Chief Executives Define Their Own Data Needs', Harvard Business Review, 1979. Applied to programme management and IT strategy from the 1980s onwards. Standard tool in McKinsey programme design.

## Why it works
Not all success conditions are equally important or equally at risk. CSF Analysis forces the programme team to identify the handful of conditions whose absence would guarantee failure — and to monitor them throughout delivery. The list is deliberately short (5–7) to force prioritisation.

## When to use
Use in Phase II Step 1 to establish the CSF register at programme launch. Review monthly throughout all phases.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Run a half-day CSF workshop with programme leadership.
2. 2. Use a 'keep/start/stop' prompt: what conditions must we maintain? What must we start doing? What must we stop doing for the programme to succeed?
3. 3. Generate 15–20 candidate CSFs. Prioritise to the 5–7 most critical using impact × risk of failure scoring.
4. 4. For each CSF: define what 'success' looks like (measurable), assign an owner, and rate the current risk of failure.
5. 5. Publish as a CSF register. Review monthly in the programme steering committee.
6. 6. Escalate CSFs rated High risk of failure to the Programme Board immediately.
7. 7. Retire a CSF only when its conditions are irreversibly met.
8. 8. Add new CSFs if the programme context changes (new executive sponsor, budget cut, market shift).

## Real-life example — General Electric (GE) Digital
GE's Predix digital platform programme (2014–2017) had 'CEO championship' as its #1 CSF. When Jeff Immelt stepped down and John Flannery deprioritised Predix, the programme's #1 CSF was immediately at risk. A CSF review at this transition point should have triggered a programme reset. The failure to act on the CSF status change contributed to the programme's eventual wind-down.

**So what:** CSFs are most valuable at transition points (new executive, budget change, market shift) where hidden programme risks become visible. Monthly CSF reviews create the early warning mechanism.

## Pitfalls
- Too many CSFs — more than 7 dilutes focus; all conditions become equally 'critical'.
- CSFs set once and never reviewed — CSFs must be live management tools, not workshop outputs.
