# Programme Governance Model

*Category: governance · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A structured framework that defines the governance structure for a digital transformation programme — the committees, decision rights, escalation paths, meeting cadences, and reporting requirements that ensure the programme is managed with appropriate oversight and accountability.

**Origin:** Derived from OGC Managing Successful Programmes (MSP) programme governance framework, PRINCE2 Project Board principles, and McKinsey's large-scale transformation governance methodology. The 3-tier governance model (Board, Programme, Project) has been standard in complex programme management since the 1990s.

## Why it works
Large digital transformation programmes involve dozens of projects, multiple workstreams, and hundreds of stakeholders. Without a defined governance model, decision-making is ad hoc, escalation paths are unclear, and accountability is diffuse. The governance model creates clarity: who makes which decisions at which level, when, with what information, and with what consequences.

## When to use
Use in Phase II Step 2 (Assemble Programme Team and Governance) and Phase III Step 1 (Establish Programme Governance).

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Define the governance tiers: typically 3 tiers — Programme Board (ExCo/Board level), Programme Steering Committee (programme leadership), and Workstream Reviews (delivery level).
2. 2. Define each committee's composition, frequency, decision rights, and quorum requirements.
3. 3. Define the escalation path: any issue or risk that cannot be resolved at Workstream level escalates to Steering Committee within 2 working days; any issue that cannot be resolved at Steering level escalates to the Programme Board.
4. 4. Define the meeting calendar: schedule all governance meetings for the programme duration at the outset. Governance meetings that are not pre-scheduled do not happen.
5. 5. Define the reporting pack for each tier: Tier 3 — workstream status update (1 page); Tier 2 — programme dashboard + risk register + milestone tracker (4–5 pages); Tier 1 — executive summary + benefits profile + budget actuals (2 pages).
6. 6. Define decision-making process: all significant decisions are documented in a decision log with: decision made, rationale, decision-maker, date, and alternatives considered.
7. 7. Publish the governance charter: a one-page document describing all governance tiers, their composition, frequency, and decision rights. Obtain sponsor sign-off before the programme starts.
8. 8. Review governance effectiveness at 3 months: governance that is not working (low attendance, decisions not made, escalations not acted upon) must be redesigned.

## Real-life example — HSBC (digital transformation programme)
HSBC's digital transformation programme (2018–2022) established a 3-tier governance model with strict decision authority limits. The Programme Board (chaired by Group CEO) met quarterly and held authority only for decisions >$50M or significant scope changes. The Digital Transformation Steering Committee (chaired by CDO) met monthly and resolved all cross-workstream issues within programme tolerance. Workstream Leads met weekly. A key governance innovation: any escalation not resolved within 5 working days at the escalation tier automatically went to the next tier up — eliminating the 'stuck decisions' that plague large programmes.

**So what:** Governance effectiveness is determined by the escalation response time, not the governance structure. HSBC's 5-day automatic escalation timer is the mechanism that prevented decision gridlock. Without it, even well-designed governance structures develop decision backlogs.

## Pitfalls
- Governance by committee — too many people in every meeting delays all decisions; define clear attendance requirements (right people for right decisions, not everyone).
- Governance without authority — committees that can only recommend but not decide add bureaucracy without governance value; define real decision rights.
