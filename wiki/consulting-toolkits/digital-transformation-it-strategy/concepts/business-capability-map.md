# Business Capability Map

*Category: architecture · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A structured inventory of what an organisation does (capabilities) — independent of how, who, or what technology — used to connect business strategy objectives to the IT investments that enable them.

**Origin:** Enterprise architecture practice; popularised by Gartner and The Open Group; standard tool in McKinsey, BCG, and Accenture enterprise architecture engagements from 2005 onwards.

## Why it works
Capabilities are stable over time (unlike processes or org structures), making them a reliable bridge between changing strategy and changing IT. A 'Customer Acquisition' capability exists regardless of whether marketing channels are digital or physical.

## When to use
Use in Phase IT-I Step 2 to create the IT demand signal from the business strategy.

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. Define capabilities (what the business does, not how): aim for 50–80 for a mid-size organisation.
2. 2. Group into 6–10 domains.
3. 3. Score each on Strategic Importance (1–3) and IT Maturity (1–3).
4. 4. Calculate Investment Priority = Strategic Importance minus IT Maturity.
5. 5. Colour-code: Red (SI=3, Mat=1), Amber (medium gap), Green (adequate), Grey (non-strategic, low maturity).
6. 6. Map Red/Amber capabilities to the IT initiative pipeline.
7. 7. Validate with business leaders.
8. 8. Refresh when strategy changes.

## Real-life example — ASOS
ASOS classified 'Personalised Product Recommendation' as Red (strategic importance 3, IT maturity 1), directly justifying investment in an AI recommendation engine that contributed approximately 30% of revenue by 2022.

**So what:** The capability map converts political IT budget debates into evidence-based conversations about which capabilities are strategic and which are adequately served.

## Template

- [ ] Top Red capabilities driving IT initiative pipeline: [List]

## Pitfalls
- Capabilities defined at wrong granularity — too broad (loses actionability) or too fine (unmanageable).
- IT team defines capabilities without business input.
- One-time exercise — refresh when strategy changes.
