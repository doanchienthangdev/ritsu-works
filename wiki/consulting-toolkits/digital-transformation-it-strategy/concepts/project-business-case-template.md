# Individual Project Business Case (1-Pager)

*Category: financial · Toolkit: Digital Transformation & IT Strategy*

## What it is
A standardised one-page template for individual digital transformation project business cases — covering problem, solution, investment, quantified benefits, risks, and NPV — ensuring all projects are evaluated on a comparable basis and the total programme NPV is the verifiable sum of individual project cases.

**Origin:** Standard consulting and programme management tool; derived from the broader business case methodology in OGC's Managing Successful Projects. The one-page format is a McKinsey discipline — if a business case cannot be stated on one page, the thinking is not clear enough.

## Why it works
Individual project business cases serve two functions: (1) they justify each project's investment on its own merits; (2) they allow the portfolio prioritisation to be grounded in comparable, standardised financial evidence rather than qualitative advocacy. Without standardised templates, each business unit produces business cases in different formats with different assumptions, making comparison impossible and prioritisation political.

## When to use
Use in Phase II Step 7 (Build Individual Project Business Cases) and Phase IT-III Step 2 (Build IT Initiative Business Cases).

## Step-by-step tutorial
1. 1. State the problem in 1 sentence: be specific (avoid 'our digital capability is insufficient').
2. 2. Describe the solution in 2 sentences: what will be built/bought, at what scale.
3. 3. Itemise the investment: capex (Year 1, Year 2, Year 3), opex (Year 1 ongoing), change management. Total investment.
4. 4. Quantify each benefit: list each benefit, the mechanism, the annual £/$ value, and the first year of realisation. Do not include 'strategic' or 'unquantifiable' benefits — if it cannot be measured, it cannot be claimed.
5. 5. Calculate NPV and payback: sum discounted net cash flows over 5 years. State payback period.
6. 6. Identify the top 3 risks with probability, impact, and mitigation.
7. 7. State the strategic alignment: which strategic objective does this project advance (from the Strategy Alignment Matrix)?
8. 8. Obtain sign-off from Finance (confirms financial assumptions) and the project sponsor (commits to benefit realisation).
9. 9. Use for portfolio comparison: all project business cases use the same template and the same discount rate, enabling like-for-like comparison.

## Real-life example — Tesco (UK)
Tesco's digital transformation portfolio management required individual 1-page business cases for each of 22 approved projects. A standardised template (identical format, same 8% discount rate, Finance-validated cost benchmarks) enabled the Digital Transformation Board to compare a £2M mobile app enhancement with a £15M supply chain AI project on a consistent basis. The standardised format revealed that 3 of the 22 projects had negative NPV under the standard assumptions — these were deprioritised despite strong advocacy from their business sponsors. Without the standardised template, the advocacy would likely have won.

**So what:** Standardisation is the key governance property of the project business case. Any format is better than no format, but a standardised format applied consistently is what enables portfolio decisions to be evidence-based rather than advocacy-based.

## Template
Complete all sections. Obtain Finance and Sponsor sign-off before submission to the portfolio prioritisation process.

- [ ] Project name: [Fill in] | Sponsor: [Name] | Date: [Date]
- [ ] PROBLEM (1 sentence): [Fill in]
- [ ] SOLUTION (2 sentences): [Fill in]
- [ ] INVESTMENT: Year 1 capex [£/$M] | Year 2 capex [£/$M] | Year 3 capex [£/$M] | Ongoing opex [£/$M/year] | Change management [£/$M] | Total investment [£/$M]
- [ ] BENEFITS: Benefit 1 [Name] | Mechanism [Fill in] | Annual value [£/$M] | First year of realisation [Year X] | Benefit 2 [Fill in] | Benefit 3 [Fill in]
- [ ] FINANCIAL SUMMARY: 5-year NPV [£/$M] | IRR [%] | Payback [months] | Discount rate [8%]
- [ ] TOP 3 RISKS: Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation
- [ ] STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT: This project advances [Strategic Objective X] from the strategy alignment matrix.
- [ ] APPROVALS: Finance sign-off [Name, Date] | Sponsor sign-off [Name, Date]

## Pitfalls
- Business cases with unquantified 'strategic value' — every claimed benefit must have a £/$ value and a measurement method; vague strategic benefits are not comparable.
- Different discount rates across projects — all projects in the portfolio must use the same discount rate; otherwise NPVs are incomparable.
