# Paid Advertising — Competitor Analysis (Google Ads & Social Ads)

## Part 1: Google Ads / PPC Competitor Analysis

### Overview
- Analyzing competitors' PPC reveals: spending levels, strategic priorities, top products/services, most important USPs, most valuable traffic sources, keyword strategies
- Three core analysis areas: **Targeting**, **Ad Creative**, **Spend Analysis**
- Main tool: **Semrush** (Advertising Research section)
- Also useful: SEMrush keyword tracking, Spyfu, Ahrefs

### Targeting Analysis — Keywords & Intent

#### Finding Competitor Keywords in Semrush
1. Enter competitor domain → navigate to **Advertising Research**
2. View all keywords triggering their ads
3. Note: positions in search results, search volumes, CPC (cost-per-click), landing pages used
4. Build a map of their keyword strategy

#### Identifying Keyword Themes
Competitors typically organize keywords into clear clusters:
- **Branded terms** (their own company name, exact brand)
  - Defensive strategy: bid on their own brand to appear at top
  - Protects from competitor poaching
  - Usually high CTR (people searching for them intentionally)

- **Free tools / Lead generators** (downloadable resources, trials, webinars)
  - Example: HubSpot email signature generator, Hootsuite's social media calendar template
  - Lower CPC (cheap entry point)
  - Used to capture unqualified prospects and nurture them
  - Better for awareness-stage audiences

- **Tripwire features** (individual product capabilities as standalone keywords)
  - Keywords for specific features rather than broad product
  - Example: "email automation", "lead scoring", "social media scheduling"
  - Targets people solving a specific pain point
  - May not know about the full product

- **Commercial / Category terms** (broad category keywords, competitor names)
  - Example: "CRM software", "email marketing tools", "project management"
  - "Salesforce alternative", "HubSpot competitor"
  - Highest intent, highest CPC
  - Competitor brand bidding (bidding on competitor names)
  - Aggressive strategy: very competitive, targets warm audience

#### Real-World Examples

**HubSpot (B2B SaaS):**
- 4 clear keyword groups identified
- Branded: "HubSpot CRM", "HubSpot email marketing"
- Free tools: "free email signature generator" ($1.73 CPC), "content calendar template"
- Tripwire: "email automation", "landing page builder", "social media scheduling"
- Commercial: "CRM software" ($14+ CPC), "Salesforce alternative", "Pipedrive competitor"
- Strategy: Funnel people in at low cost (free tools), then upsell

**Cult Beauty (B2C E-commerce):**
- Extremely aggressive competitor brand bidding
- Bids heavily on: Olaplex, The Ordinary, Charlotte Tilbury, Nars, Sephora
- Beats those brands in position 1 for their own brand keywords
- Tactic: position as discounter — "Find [brand name] cheaper on Cult Beauty"
- Either very profitable (huge margins) or losing money for brand building

#### Mobile vs Desktop Targeting
- Check if competitors bid differently on mobile vs desktop
- Example: Cult Beauty heavier mobile spending (mobile shoppers)
- Different landing pages for mobile
- Mobile CTAs may differ (click-to-call vs click-to-website)
- CPC may vary significantly by device

#### Landing Page Intent Matching
- Note which keywords go to which landing pages
- **High relevance**: "Email automation" → Email automation product page
- **Missed opportunity**: "Zoho CRM" keyword → Generic homepage instead of comparison page
- Study their landing page choices to see what they're NOT optimizing
- Potential competitive advantage: better landing page + same keywords = lower CPC

### Ad Creative Analysis — Messaging & Positioning

#### What to Look For in Ad Copy
- **Unique Selling Propositions (USPs)**: What makes them different?
- **Features vs Benefits**: Do they emphasize what the product does or what it does for you?
- **Objection handling**: "No credit card required", "No contract needed", "Full refund guarantee"
  - Shows what's preventing customers from buying
  - Your ads should address these same objections
- **Pain point callouts**: "Stop switching between tools", "Reduce admin work by 80%"
- **Social proof**: "2M+ users", "Trusted by Fortune 500"
- **Emotional triggers**: Fear, urgency, aspiration, community

#### Disqualification Copy
- Broad keywords targeted → specific copy to disqualify wrong audience
- Example: "Best for teams of 10+" (filters out solopreneurs)
- "For your business" (disqualifies personal use)
- "For your marketing team" (narrows to specific department)
- **Why?** Saves money: expensive clicks filtered out = lower cost per qualified lead
- **Strategy**: Cast wide net with keywords, narrow in ad copy

#### Ad Format Analysis
- **Standard text ads**: Copy-focused, good for educational/comparison
- **Expanded text ads**: More space for messaging
- **Shopping ads**: Product image + price + reviews (e-commerce)
- **Discovery ads**: Image-based, good for visual products
- **Video ads**: Engagement heavy, good for product demos

#### Real-World Examples

**HubSpot Ads:**
- Feature-specific ads (CRM, email, social, landing pages)
- Heavy on free resources: "Download the complete guide to sales"
- Use of numbers: "Save 6+ hours per week", "Manage 10x more leads"
- Lead gen focused: "Get the guide" CTA (not "Try free")
- Value prop: "All-in-one platform" vs "Best CRM"
- Addresses objection: "Easy to use", "Simple setup"

**Cult Beauty Ads:**
- Almost exclusively about value and discounts
- Copy: "Up to 50% off [brand]", "Voucher code: SAVE15"
- Positioning: **Discounter**, not premium retailer
- Uses urgency: "Flash sale", "Limited time"
- Social proof: "Trusted by thousands", "Free shipping"
- Targets impulse buyers, not brand loyalists

#### What Should You Test Against?
- If competitors emphasize discounts → test premium positioning
- If competitors are text-heavy → test minimalist design
- If competitors use video → test static images
- If competitors focus on features → test customer testimonials
- Look for gaps in their approach

### Spend Analysis — Budget, Intensity, Testing

#### Semrush Spend Estimates
- Semrush provides **estimated ad spending** over time (month by month)
- Not exact, but directionally accurate
- Shows budget trends, seasonal patterns, intensity

#### Interpreting Spend Patterns

**Consistently Increasing Spend:**
- PPC working well for them
- Confident in their ROI
- Testing more keywords, scaling what works
- **Your takeaway**: This strategy is profitable

**Big Spike Then Drop:**
- Tested, found it didn't work
- Campaign abandoned
- **Your takeaway**: Learn from their mistake, don't replicate

**Seasonal Spikes:**
- E-commerce: Black Friday, holiday season
- B2B: Q4 (budget flush), Q1 (new budgets)
- **Your takeaway**: Time your campaigns strategically

**Flat/Low Spend:**
- PPC not core strategy
- Testing phase
- Margin constraints
- **Your takeaway**: They're relying on other channels

#### CPC Comparison Across Keywords
- Compare cost-per-click across different keyword groups
- Example: HubSpot email signature ($1.73) vs CRM software ($14+)
- **Why the difference?**
  - Lower intent keywords = lower CPC
  - Free tools = higher volume, lower quality
  - Competitors in category keywords (more competition)
  - Brand keywords = less competition

**Strategic implication:**
- Free tools = customer acquisition funnel bottom
- Expensive keywords = don't waste money here, go for quality
- Look for sweet spot: moderate CPC with high intent

#### Mobile vs Desktop Spend Split
- Compare budget allocation over time
- Mobile usually growing (more app usage)
- May show different year-over-year patterns
- Different seasonality between devices

#### Branded vs Non-Branded Spend
- Calculate % of budget on branded keywords
- High branded % = strong organic search (protecting it)
- High non-branded % = growth/acquisition focus
- Balance between defense and growth

#### Product/Service Priority Revealed by Spend
- Which products get the most ad budget?
- Which are strategic growth areas?
- Which are defensive/maintenance?
- Example: HubSpot prioritizes CRM heavily → core product

### B2B vs B2C Differences in PPC

#### B2B (HubSpot, Salesforce, Mailchimp)
- **Keywords**: Software features, integrations, use cases
- **Ad copy**: ROI, efficiency, team collaboration
- **CPC**: Higher (commercial intent, valuable customers)
- **Free tools**: Very common (lead generation, nurture)
- **Landing pages**: Long-form, product tours, case studies
- **Conversion goal**: Demo request, trial signup
- **Decision makers**: Multiple (buyer, end-user, CFO)

#### B2C (Cult Beauty, Airbnb, Amazon)
- **Keywords**: Product names, categories, deals
- **Ad copy**: Value, lifestyle, convenience
- **CPC**: Lower (high volume, lower AOV)
- **Free tools**: Less common (shipping, tracking instead)
- **Landing pages**: Short-form, product gallery, social proof
- **Conversion goal**: Immediate purchase
- **Decision makers**: Individual buyer

#### Google Shopping Campaigns (E-commerce)
- Product feed-based (less control than text ads)
- Targeting based on product attributes: category, price, brand
- Can see product positions in shopping results
- Bid aggressiveness shows in frequency of appearance
- Review ratings matter (built into Shopping ads)

## Part 2: Facebook & Instagram Ad Analysis

### Overview
- Three core analysis areas: **Targeting deduction**, **Ad Creative**, **Spend patterns**
- Tool: **Facebook Ad Library** (facebook.com/ads/library)
- Note: Facebook doesn't provide detailed targeting data (hidden for privacy)
- Must **deduce targeting** from ad content, frequency, variants, duration

### Finding & Accessing Competitor Ads

#### Facebook Ad Library Access
1. Go to: facebook.com/ads/library
2. Set **Location**: Your country
3. Set **Category**: "All Ads" (or specific: Politics, Finance, Social Issues)
4. Search for business name
5. View **all active ads** they're currently running

#### What You Can See
- **All active ad variations** (they keep refreshing)
- **Ad start date** (how long it's been running)
- **Platform** where ad appears (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Audience Network)
- **Ad creative** (image, video, copy, CTA button)
- **Geographic regions** targeted (sometimes)

#### What You CANNOT See
- **Exact targeting parameters** (interests, behaviors, lookalike audiences)
- **Budget** (unless it's a political ad with transparency data)
- **Performance metrics** (CTR, CPC, conversions)
- **Audience size estimates**

### Deducing Targeting from Ad Content

#### Retargeting vs Prospecting
- **Brand-specific ads** ("Discover HubSpot CRM") → Likely retargeting website visitors
  - People who've been to their site see different message
  - No hard CTA (they already know the product)

- **"Your next haul" language** → Retargeting previous customers (restock/repurchase)
  - Cult Beauty: showing previous shopper that new items arrived
  - Lower CAC (cheaper to resell than acquire new)

- **"15% off your first haul"** → New customer acquisition OR retargeting non-buyers
  - Both audiences respond to incentive
  - Could be VPO (value proposition optimization) test

- **Generic product shots, no branding** → Could be broad prospecting or dynamic retargeting
  - More likely broad prospecting (don't assume they know your brand)

#### Interest-Based Targeting Signals
- **Feature-specific ads** (e.g., "LinkedIn integration for HubSpot") → targeting people with LinkedIn interest
- **Use case ads** (e.g., "Track sales pipeline") → targeting sales professionals
- **Competitor comparison** (e.g., "HubSpot vs Salesforce") → targeting Salesforce users
- **Job title mentions** (e.g., "For marketing managers") → likely using job title targeting
- **Industry/behavior** ("Small business owners", "Ecommerce sellers") → explicit audience signals

#### Real-World Examples

**HubSpot (B2B):**
- **Feature-specific ads**: "LinkedIn integration", "Email tracking" → Interest in those specific tools
- **CRM ads with no CTA**: Just educating, likely retargeting known audience
- **Lead gen campaigns**: "Get our guide to sales", "Download the template" → Broad prospecting net
- **Event ads**: Geographically targeted conferences, summits
- **Webinar ads**: "Join us Tuesday" → Time-sensitive, retargeting interested audience
- **Long-running ads** (13+ months): These are the controls — study them carefully

**Cult Beauty (B2C):**
- **Brand-specific ads**: "Shop [Olaplex] on Cult Beauty" → Retargeting people who viewed those brands
- **First-time buyer discount**: "15% off first order" → New customer acquisition
- **"Flash sale" messaging**: Urgency-based, likely broad prospecting + urgency retargeting
- **Product category ads**: "Summer skincare collection" → Seasonal + season-interested audience
- **Loyalty program ads**: "Join Our VIP Club" → Retargeting high-value customers

### Analyzing Ad Creative

#### Creative Types & Production Value
- **UGC (User-Generated Content)**: Customer photos, testimonials, unpolished
  - Lower production cost
  - Often higher engagement (authentic)
  - Buildable library

- **Product shots**: Professional photography
  - Consistent, branded
  - Requires ongoing photography budget

- **Lifestyle imagery**: Product in context (aspirational)
  - Conveys usage, not just product
  - Higher production value

- **Feature videos**: Product demo, walkthrough
  - Educational, shows functionality
  - Higher cost, higher engagement potential

- **Branded graphics**: Design + text
  - Consistent with brand identity
  - Text-heavy often underperforms

- **Carousel ads**: Multiple images, swipeable
  - Test multiple angles in one ad
  - Higher engagement potential

#### Key Message Analysis
- What's the primary USP in each ad?
- Is it **feature-focused** or **benefit-focused**?
- **Feature**: "AI-powered analytics"
- **Benefit**: "Save 10 hours per week analyzing data"
- How many messages per ad? (Rule: 1 clear message)
- Social proof elements: "Trusted by 10M+", testimonials, reviews
- Urgency/scarcity: "Limited time", "Only X left", "Early bird discount"

#### Copy & Design Combination
- **Text-heavy images**: Large text overlays, minimal white space
  - Works well for cold audiences (stops scroll)
  - Can feel spammy

- **Clean design**: Minimal text, clear focal point
  - Works better for warm audiences
  - Requires strong visual

- **Image text length**: Facebook penalizes text >20% of image
  - Check if they're in organic or using work-arounds

#### Split Testing Signals
- **Same copy, different images**: Testing creative, not message
- **Same image, different copy**: Testing messaging on same creative
- **Same day multiple variants**: Active split testing (heavier spend)
- **Long-running single ad**: Winning control (perform best)
- **Frequency of new ads**: More frequent = more budget for testing

#### Real-World Examples

**HubSpot Ads:**
- Heavy use of branded graphics (HubSpot orange/white)
- Text-heavy images with value props
- Copy: "Save 6+ hours per week", "Manage 10x more leads"
- Lots of downloadable content offers (lead magnets)
- Feature videos showing product in action
- Testimonial-based ads (social proof heavy)
- CTAs: "Get the guide", "See the demo", "Start free trial"

**Cult Beauty Ads:**
- Product shots (high-quality professional photography)
- Almost no UGC (positioning as premium)
- Copy almost entirely about price/discount: "Up to 50% off", "Voucher code SAVE15"
- No lifestyle imagery (product-focused, not lifestyle)
- Carousel ads showing multiple brand options
- Urgency: "Flash sale ends Sunday"
- CTA: "Shop now", "Save today"

#### Competitive Gaps to Test Against
- If competitors use only **stock photos** → Test UGC
- If competitors emphasize **discounts** → Test premium positioning
- If competitors use **busy designs** → Test minimalist
- If competitors are **text-heavy** → Test visual-only
- If competitors use **feature talk** → Test customer stories
- If competitors show **product only** → Test lifestyle context

### Deducing Spend & Budget Intensity

#### Telltale Signs of Heavy Budget

**Volume of ads:**
- Lots of variations running simultaneously
- More variants = more testing = higher budget
- Example: 20+ ad variations in one week = heavy budget

**Creative quality & production:**
- Professional photography / videography
- Custom illustrations, design work
- Multiple language versions
- indicates investment in paid

**Frequency of ad updates:**
- Updating ads weekly = testing, reoptimizing
- Monthly updates = more conservative
- Weekly = likely significant budget

**Longest-running ads = control:**
- If same ad running for 13+ months → It works
- Lowest CAC, highest ROI
- **Study this ad closely** — it's their proven winner
- Budget is likely concentrated on similar variants

#### Telltale Signs of Low/Moderate Budget
- Same 3-5 ads running for months
- Infrequent updates
- Basic creative (no video, single image)
- Suggests testing on lower budget or channel is not priority

#### Meta Ad Library Transparency (Political/Charitable)
- For political ads and charitable organizations: exact spend data visible
- Shows: how much spent, date range, regions
- Useful if competitor is nonprofit

#### Platform Split Signals
- Some ads Facebook-only, some Instagram-only, some both
- If testing different platforms separately → higher budget
- Instagram usually gets bigger budget for B2C
- Facebook often gets bigger budget for B2B

#### Seasonal Spend Patterns (Inferred)
- If seeing heavy ads Nov-Dec → holiday season focus
- If seeing heavy ads Jan → New Year's resolutions focus
- If ads disappear for months → seasonal business or budget constraints
- Heavy back-to-school (Aug-Sep) for education products

### Channels & Placements

#### Where Ads Can Run
- **Facebook feed** (highest volume)
- **Instagram feed** (visual priority)
- **Instagram Stories** (short-form, disappearing)
- **Instagram Reels** (video-first, algorithm favored)
- **Messenger** (personal, high engagement)
- **Audience Network** (off-platform placements)

#### B2B vs B2C Platform Patterns
- **B2B (HubSpot, Salesforce)**: Facebook primary (LinkedIn harder in Ad Library)
- **B2C (Cult Beauty, Airbnb)**: Heavy Instagram focus
- **Product/Content heavy**: Instagram/Reels prioritized
- **Lead gen focus**: Facebook Messenger ads common

#### Stories vs Feed Differences
- **Feed ads**: Longer copy possible, static/carousel works
- **Stories ads**: 3-5 seconds, must be vertical, needs strong visual
- If competitor running both, may use different messages for each

## Part 3: Synthesis & Strategic Insights

### Combining PPC + Social Advertising Intelligence

#### Complete Competitor Profile
1. **PPC keywords** show intent-based priorities
2. **Social ads** show brand/trust-based priorities
3. **Together** = full funnel strategy:
   - PPC: Capture high-intent (bottom funnel)
   - Social: Build awareness, retargeting (top/middle funnel)

#### Budget Allocation Pattern
- What % of budget on PPC vs social?
- What % on acquisition vs retention?
- What products/services get priority?
- Seasonal vs always-on

#### Pricing & Margin Signals
- If bidding aggressively on expensive keywords = high margins
- If leading with free tools/loss leaders = long LTV play
- If heavy discount messaging = margin pressure or commodity positioning

#### Growth Stage Indicators
- Heavy testing = pre-product-market fit or scaling phase
- Conservative approach = mature, optimizing what works
- New keywords = exploring new markets
- Pause in spending = pivoting or trouble

#### Competitive Moat Detection
- If always bidding competitor names = moat is brand trust, not product
- If bidding free tools + converting = moat is conversion optimization
- If heavy content marketing + ads = moat is education/authority
- If pure discount play = moat is scale/procurement, not defensible

### Red Flags & Caution Signs

#### Be Skeptical Of:
- **One-off campaigns**: May have been a test that flopped
- **Inconsistent messaging**: Sign of unclear strategy or rebrand
- **Dated creative**: Ads looking old = running out of budget or failed test
- **Competitor brand bidding alone**: Doesn't work unless you have better offer

#### Signs They're Struggling:
- Spend dropping over time
- Lower quality creative
- Lots of new campaigns starting (exploring)
- Discount-heavy messaging (margin pressure)
- Fewer new ads (not testing)

### Research Checklist for Competitor Analysis

#### Google Ads Analysis Checklist
- [ ] Enter 3 top competitors into Semrush Advertising Research

- [ ] Screenshot landing page for top keywords (is it optimized?)
- [ ] Check mobile vs desktop spend ratio
- [ ] Estimate monthly spend (increasing or flat?)
- [ ] Document 5-10 top ad copy examples
- [ ] Note how they handle objections in ads
- [ ] Map out their disqualification strategy
- [ ] Identify gaps in their keyword strategy

#### Facebook/Instagram Analysis Checklist
- [ ] Search 3 top competitors in Ad Library
- [ ] Screenshot 5-10 current ad variations
- [ ] Note start dates (what's the control?)
- [ ] Analyze targeting signals from each ad
- [ ] Compare UGC vs professional creative ratio
- [ ] Document primary USPs in top ads
- [ ] Note mobile vs desktop messaging differences
- [ ] Identify platforms (Facebook/Instagram/Messenger/Stories)
- [ ] Estimate budget level (low/moderate/high based on signals)
- [ ] Map retargeting vs prospecting ads

#### Synthesis Checklist
- [ ] Compare PPC keywords to social ad messaging (are they aligned?)
- [ ] Estimate total advertising budget (PPC + social)
- [ ] Identify seasonal patterns
- [ ] Rank their top 3 products by ad spend
- [ ] Note what they're NOT advertising (strategic gaps)
- [ ] Identify your potential competitive advantage
- [ ] Document 2-3 strategies worth copying
- [ ] Document 2-3 approaches to avoid
- [ ] List tactical improvements you could make vs their approach
- [ ] Estimate their CAC range based on spend + estimated conversions
