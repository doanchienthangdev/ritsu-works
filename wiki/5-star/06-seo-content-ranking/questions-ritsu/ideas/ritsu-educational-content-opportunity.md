# Ritsu's Asymmetric Educational Content Opportunity

Ritsu has a structural advantage for content-led SEO that competitors lack: the product itself is a study-method engine, so educational content about how to study (spaced repetition, active recall, interleaving, Feynman technique, the testing effect) is on-strategy rather than tangential. Every educational post both ranks for awareness-stage student queries AND demonstrates the principle the product applies. The content IS the demo.

Competitors split into two groups, neither of which can match this: tools like Anki/Quizlet have no marketing-content engine (their growth has historically been word-of-mouth and academic adoption); platforms like Coursera and Khan Academy publish content about specific courses, not the meta-skill of studying itself. The gap — high-quality educational content about *how to study*, targeted at university students, with credible product anchoring — is wide open.

The implied content pyramid for Ritsu (versus the source's general 20/30/50 split): heavier weighting toward educational content (50-60%) in the first year to build topical authority on "active learning", "spaced repetition", "exam preparation"; then shifting to versus-content (Ritsu vs Anki, Ritsu vs Quizlet) and best-X content (Best AI Study Apps for Med Students) once the marketing site has rankable authority. Per-persona variation (med students, law students, engineering students) opens long-tail variations that aggregate into significant traffic.

This is the SEO version of the founder's broader observation about Ritsu's positioning: the product is built on a pedagogical thesis (active > passive learning), which makes the marketing story and the SEO content one and the same.
