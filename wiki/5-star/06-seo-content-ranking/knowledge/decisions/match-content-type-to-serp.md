# Match Content Type to What's Ranking

Before writing for a target keyword, run a SERP analysis in an incognito window and identify the dominant content type in the top 10 results. If the top results are all blog posts, a homepage will not rank for that keyword; if the top results are all homepages or service pages, a blog post will not rank. The decision rule: match the format Google has already selected for that query, then differentiate on depth and quality within the format.

The analysis goes beyond format. For each top-3 result, evaluate: content quality (comprehensive vs thin, examples present, sourcing), keyword optimization (placement, density, variations), UX (mobile, speed, scannability, visuals), structural elements (heading hierarchy, FAQ section, internal/external links, media), and authority signals (author credentials, citations, original research, published/updated dates). The framing question that drives the next step: *"How can I crush this content into the ground? How can I make something SO much better that Google has no choice but to rank me?"*

This decision prevents a common failure mode where writers invest 3,000 words in a blog post for a keyword Google has decided is best served by a product page (or vice versa). For Ritsu specifically: keywords like "spaced repetition" return educational content in the top 10 — a blog post is the right format. Keywords like "AI study app" return product pages and roundups — a subpage or a "Best AI Study Apps" Bucket-4 post is the right format.
