# Rewrite Old Posts Before Writing New Content

When optimizing SEO output, prioritize rewriting existing posts over producing new content. Old posts already have inbound links, established traffic, and Google's accumulated trust signals. They are typically 50-70% faster to update than to write from scratch, and small improvements produce disproportionately large ranking jumps — especially for posts already ranking in positions 5-20 (the "opportunity zone" where modest content depth gains can push the page into the top 3).

The audit process is concrete: pull the top 20 blog posts by traffic in Google Analytics, identify those ranking 5-20, and prioritize by traffic × improvement-opportunity. For each candidate, add 500-1000 words of substantive content, fix outdated statistics/prices/features, add real examples and case studies, upgrade media (original images > stock photos), improve heading hierarchy, refresh CTAs, and update the publication date to signal freshness. Save updates until improvements are substantial — don't republish small tweaks.

The decision rule cascades naturally into a continuous optimization loop: publish → wait 4-6 weeks → measure in Google Search Console → identify low-CTR or position-5-20 keywords → update content → repeat every 2-3 months, with a full refresh every 12-18 months.

For Ritsu, this means once educational content exists, the marketing budget shifts heavily toward updates over new posts: a 20-visit/mo post in position 12 is often higher leverage than a new post that may never rank.
