# Title Tags Are One of the Strongest On-Page Ranking Factors

The HTML title tag (the blue clickable headline in Google search results, also visible in the browser tab) is among the strongest on-page ranking signals Google uses to interpret page topic and relevance. Beyond direct relevance, the title tag's effect compounds through click-through rate: a well-written title increases CTR, higher CTR signals to Google that the page is valuable, and that signal reinforces the ranking position over time. The optimal display length is 50-60 characters; Google truncates beyond ~60 on desktop.

The practical implication is that title tags are the highest-leverage single element on a page to optimize. Treating them as throwaway internal labels ("Home", "Welcome to icaew.com", "Services", "Page 1") wastes the most valuable SEO real estate on the page. The pattern that consistently performs: lead with the keyword, add a benefit or audience qualifier, end with brand if space allows — for example "Outsourced HR Support for Small Businesses - Top Rated" or "How to Fix Overbite: Guide for Dentists & Orthodontists".

A useful diagnostic: study Google Ads headlines for the target keyword. Advertisers A/B test headlines extensively, so high-performing ad copy reveals the phrasing that drives clicks for that intent. Treat the title tag like a search ad headline.

For Ritsu: if and when a marketing site exists, every page needs a unique title tag with the target keyword, an audience qualifier (e.g., "for Med Students", "for University Students"), and a benefit clause where space permits.
