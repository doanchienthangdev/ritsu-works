# Keyword Difficulty Sweet Spot

A keyword is worth targeting only when three metrics align: monthly search volume in the 100-10,000 range (sweet spot for businesses), a keyword difficulty (KD) score achievable for your current domain authority, and a cost-per-click (CPC) high enough to signal real commercial value. The rule of thumb: if KD exceeds your domain authority + 20, the keyword is too hard to target directly; if it sits within ±20 of your DA, ranking requires good content; if KD is 20 below your DA, it is a quick win.

The framework discards two failure modes: chasing high-volume head terms ("HR" at 165,000/mo, KD 89) that exhaust effort with no ranking payoff, and chasing ultra-narrow long-tail ("Outsourced HR support" at 320/mo, KD 32) that ranks easily but generates negligible traffic. The example "Outsourced HR" — 2,400/mo, KD 45, $28 CPC — is the canonical good-target shape: enough volume to matter, achievable difficulty for an established site, and a CPC that proves advertisers see commercial value.

For an early Ritsu marketing site (low domain authority), this implies skipping "Study App" (likely KD 60+) and starting with long-tail like "Spaced repetition for medical students" — low volume but rankable, and aligned to a specific Ritsu persona.
