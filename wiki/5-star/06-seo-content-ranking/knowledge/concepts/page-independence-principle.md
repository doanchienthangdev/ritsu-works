# Page Independence Principle

Every page on a website that wants to rank in Google must stand alone for cold traffic. A visitor arriving from a Google search knows nothing about the business — no homepage navigation, no brand context, no prior copy. Each subpage must therefore answer the question completely, restate the connection between the product/service and the search query, and make sense without any reliance on adjacent pages.

The principle invalidates the assumption that visitors will arrive at the homepage and navigate inward. In practice, organic search delivers cold traffic to whichever URL ranks for a given query — often a subpage or blog post. Headings written for internal navigation ("Overbite", "Payroll", "Features") fail this test because they encode meaning only relative to the parent site structure. Re-cast for cold traffic, they become "How to Fix Overbite: Treatment Options & Costs", "Payroll Services for Tech Startups", "How Our AI Study Assistant Improves Retention Rates".

For Ritsu, this means every subpage and every blog post must include enough context (Ritsu is an AI tutor, students upload material, etc.) that a searcher landing from a long-tail query can orient themselves without leaving.
