# Copywriting & Persuasion: Generic Questions

## Part 1: Before & After Bridge (12 Questions)

1. Describe a customer's painful "before state" for your product in vivid, specific detail. What exactly are they struggling with?

2. What does the "after state" look like for your ideal customer? What becomes possible once they use your solution?

3. How long does it take for customers to move from before to after? Be specific about the timeline.

4. What is the emotional cost of staying in the "before state" for your customer? What do they miss out on?

5. What is the emotional relief or joy they feel when reaching the "after state"? Describe it viscerally.

6. How would you position your product as the "bridge" between before and after? What specifically does it do to create that transformation?

7. Write a before-and-after headline for your product. Show the contrast clearly.

8. Create a short before-and-after testimonial. Have a customer describe their struggle, then their victory.

9. How would a case study tell the before-and-after story of one customer? Outline the structure.

10. What visual elements (images, videos, graphics) could show the before-and-after contrast?

11. In a landing page, where would you place the before-and-after narrative for maximum impact?

12. How could you weave before-and-after language throughout your email sequence without being repetitive?

## Part 2: The "So What?" Test (14 Questions)

13. Take your main product feature. Ask "so what?" five times in a chain until you reach the deepest customer benefit.

14. What is the difference between a feature and a benefit for your product? Give examples of each.

15. For three of your key features, apply the "so what?" test and identify the true emotional/practical benefit.

16. Which benefits resonate most emotionally with your customers? Which are purely practical?

17. What is the deepest "so what?" for your product? The one that changes a customer's life or identity?

18. How would you translate "our product is AI-powered" into a real customer benefit using the "so what?" test?

19. Take a technical feature of your product. How would you explain it to a customer using benefits, not technical language?

20. What would a customer say when asked "why do you actually use this product?" versus what marketing usually claims?

21. For your ideal customer, what is the one benefit that actually drives their decision to buy?

22. How would you use the "so what?" test to go deeper with a customer in an interview or conversation?

23. What are the 5 levels of benefits for your product? Start with surface-level benefits, end with identity/life transformation.

24. How would you know if you've reached the deepest benefit? What does that sound like?

25. Which benefits are you currently leading with in your marketing? Should you be leading with deeper ones?

26. How could you rewrite three pieces of your current copy using deeper benefits instead of features?

## Part 3: Specificity Sells (15 Questions)

27. What are the specific, measurable results your customers have achieved? Get exact numbers, not rounded.

28. How many customers do you have? What percentage are satisfied? Get specific numbers for both.

29. What specific metrics show your product's effectiveness? Can you break these down by customer type?

30. If you had to make a claim about your product's impact, what exact numbers would you use?

31. Do you have data on how long it takes customers to see results? What are the specific timeframes?

32. What before-and-after metrics can you point to from your best customers?

33. How would you rewrite "our product is fast" using specific numbers and timeframes?

34. Can you collect specific customer stories with exact results? What data would you need to track?

35. What would a specific social proof claim look like for your product versus a vague one?

36. If a competitor claims "better results," how would you counter with specific evidence?

37. What specific details could you add to your testimonials to increase credibility? (Name, title, school, exact results, etc.)

38. How would you implement tracking to gather the specificity data you need for marketing?

39. What specific comparisons to competitors or alternatives could you make with data?

40. Can you find any case studies in your industry that use specific metrics? How could you match that specificity?

41. If you had to prove your biggest claim with a single number, what would it be?

## Part 4: The Power of "You" (12 Questions)

42. Rewrite three sentences from your current marketing, changing "we" to "you." How does it feel?

43. What does your customer want most from your product? Write it from their perspective using "you" language.

44. Take your main value proposition. Rewrite it starting with "you" instead of starting with your company.

45. In your current copy, where are you talking about your company and should be talking about the customer?

46. What would it sound like if your customer was telling someone else about your product? Capture that language.

47. Write a headline using "you" language. Then write one without "you." Which is more compelling?

48. How would you make your CTA more customer-centric? Rewrite three CTA buttons with "you" language.

49. What are the aspirations of your ideal customer? How would you speak to those using "you" language?

50. How would you describe your product from the customer's perspective versus your company's perspective?

51. What would change in your landing page if you led with what customers want instead of what you offer?

52. Write a mission statement for your company from the customer's perspective. What would it be?

53. How would your customer describe the transformation they experience? Can you capture that in copy?

54. What's a surprising fact about your industry that most people don't know? How would you use it as a headline hook?

55. What misconception does your target customer have about your problem? How could you address it with an open loop?

56. If you started a headline with "Everyone thinks [X], but actually [?]..." what would fill those blanks?

57. What's a counterintuitive benefit of your product that would surprise your audience?

58. Create three different open-loop headlines for your product. Which one would you test first?

59. How could you create curiosity in your email subject line without being clickbait?

60. What story about a customer breakthrough could you tell as an open loop?

61. If a customer asked "How does this actually work?" how would you answer it as a story with an open loop?

62. What question does your target customer have that your product answers? How would you tease the answer?

63. How would you structure a sales page using open loops to guide readers through the copy?

64. What's the most common objection or doubt your customers have? How could you address it with an open loop?

65. Create a series of subject lines for an email sequence, each creating an open loop that leads to the next email.

66. How would you know if your open loop is compelling versus boring? What's the test?

68. Where in your sales copy could you integrate social proof to support specific claims?

69. Write three testimonials that feel authentic and specific, without sounding generic praise.

70. How would you place a testimonial right before a CTA to remove the last objection before conversion?

71. What is your strongest social proof (most specific, most credible, most relevant)? Where should it appear first?

72. How could you gather more social proof systematically? What would you need to ask customers?

73. Create a case study outline: problem → solution → implementation → results → customer quote.

74. Which customer results are most persuasive to your ideal audience? Why those specifically?

75. How would you integrate social proof throughout a landing page without feeling repetitive or sale-sy?

76. What proof would best address each major objection a customer might have?

77. If you had no social proof yet, how would you gather enough to be compelling before launch?

## Part 7-8: John Carlton - Know Your Customer (11 Questions)

78. What's one thing your ideal customer believes about your problem that might be wrong? How would you address it?

79. What language does your customer use when talking about their struggle? Capture three phrases they'd actually say.

80. If you sat at a bar with your ideal customer, what would they tell you about why they need your solution?

81. What are your customer's three biggest fears about your problem or your solution?

82. What would your customer be most skeptical about regarding your product? How do you overcome that skepticism?

83. Who influences your customer's decision? What would convince those influencers?

84. What does your ideal customer watch, listen to, and read? Where do they hang out online?

85. What's the biggest misconception your target customer has about solving their problem?

86. How would your marketing change if you spent time in the communities where your customers hang out?

87. What's one customer story you've heard that reveals something important about motivation or buying behavior?

88. If you could only make one claim about your product to your customers, what would it be based on what you know about them?

89. What's the single most important action you want your audience to take? Be specific.

90. How would you simplify your sign-up process to remove friction? What's the minimum information needed?

91. What should a customer expect immediately after clicking your CTA? Be clear about next steps.

92. If you could only have one CTA on your page, what would it be and where would it go?

93. How would your CTA change for cold traffic versus warm traffic?

94. What's currently confusing about your conversion process? How would you clarify it?

## Part 10: Write Like You Talk (9 Questions)

97. Read a paragraph of your current marketing copy aloud. Does it sound like you talking? What feels off?

98. Rewrite that paragraph as if you were explaining it to a friend. What changes?

99. What words or phrases in your marketing feel "corporate" or "marketing-y" instead of conversational?

100. How would your ideal customer describe your product to a friend? Capture that language.

101. What contractions (we're, you'll, it's, don't) could you add to make your copy more conversational?

102. Take a feature-heavy sentence and rewrite it conversationally.

103. What's a phrase you often use that your customers might not? How would you translate it?

104. How would you adjust your tone for different audiences? (College students vs. professionals vs. parents)

105. What's your natural voice as a writer? How can you bring more of that into your marketing copy?

107. For each headline, what promise are you making? Could you deliver on it?

108. Which of your five headlines would you A/B test first and why?

109. Take your current headline. Rewrite it to be more specific and benefit-focused.

110. What's the #1 benefit of your product that would grab your ideal customer's attention?

111. What's a surprising fact about your industry that would make a compelling headline?

112. How would you test headlines? Set up a simple A/B test framework.

113. What's the risk of using a weak headline? How much traffic/revenue might it cost you?

114. For each stage of awareness (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware), write a headline.

115. What makes a headline memorable versus forgettable? Test yours.

116. How long should your headline be? What's the sweet spot for your audience?

117. What emotional response do you want your headline to trigger? Write a headline that does that.

118. How would you differentiate your headline from competitors'?

119. What's the "one legged golfer" principle for your product? How could you apply it to your headline?

## Part 12: Story-Based Selling (13 Questions)

120. Think of a customer who achieved great results with your product. Can you tell their story?

121. What was their main struggle before finding you? Be specific about the emotional impact.

122. What was the moment they realized your product might work for them?

123. How did they implement your solution? What was the process?

124. What specific results did they achieve? Include metrics and timeline.

125. How did achieving those results change them? What became possible?

126. What would that customer say to someone else considering your product?

127. Outline a full case study: Hero → Challenge → Discovery → Implementation → Results → Transformation → Quote

128. How would you structure a customer success story on your landing page?

129. What's one customer story that reveals something important about your product's value that facts alone wouldn't convey?

130. How could you gather more customer stories systematically?

131. What's the hero's journey structure applied to your product? (Hero = customer, Challenge = problem, Mentor = your product)

132. How would you tell your own story as the founder in a way that supports the product story?

135. What information do cold prospects need to believe in your solution? How much is that?

136. How would you write long copy that reads like short copy? What techniques would you use?

137. If you had to write long-form sales copy, what sections would you include in what order?
