# Ritsu Students Believe "I'm Not Smart Enough" — Reality Is Wrong Method

The Ritsu copywriting workbook surfaces a central reframe for the target audience: struggling college and university students explain their failure to themselves as "I'm just not smart enough" or "I'm not a [science/math/engineering] person." The workbook asserts this belief is wrong — the actual cause is that the student is using a learning method (re-reading, passive highlighting, last-minute cramming) that does not match how memory and retention work. The implication for Ritsu's copy is that the strongest emotional move is not "make you smarter" but "you were never the problem — your method was."

This reframe drives downstream copy decisions. Customer-language phrases in the source ("I thought I wasn't smart enough for engineering. Turns out I just needed a better way to learn"; "I spent two years thinking I was too dumb for chemistry") are the raw quotes that should appear in testimonials and case studies. Headlines that lean into the reframe ("Stop blaming yourself — your study method is wrong, and here's the fix") align with the deepest fear in the audience: that they are fundamentally incapable. Removing that fear is more persuasive than promising better grades.

This is observation, not yet validated decision: the framing is implied by the workbook authors as a hypothesis about what resonates with Ritsu students. Validate via interviews and A/B headline tests before betting the landing page on it.
