# Specificity Outperforms Vague Claims in Credibility

Vague claims are ignored and disbelieved; specific claims are noticed, remembered, and believed. "347 customers in 14 countries" beats "many customers"; "42.7% revenue increase within 90 days" beats "significant growth"; "Sarah Chen, VP of Marketing at TechCorp" beats "a marketing manager." Unrounded numbers (42.7% vs 42%) signal that actual measurement happened rather than estimation.

Specificity stacks multiplicatively. The phrase "42.7% of the 347 college students who used Ritsu for 8 weeks increased their exam scores by an average of 8.3 points" combines four layers (population %, sample size, duration, magnitude of improvement) and each layer reinforces the others. The opposite — "Ritsu helps students improve their scores" — has no credibility surface.

The discipline this implies: track real metrics from real customers, run formal studies with disclosed sample sizes, interview customers for exact results, and disaggregate stats by segment. Without that infrastructure, copy defaults to vague claims that get scrolled past.
