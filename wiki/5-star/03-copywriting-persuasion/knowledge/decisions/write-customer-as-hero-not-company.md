# Write the Customer as the Hero, Not the Company

Replace "we" and company-centric language with "you" and customer-centric language at every level of copy: headlines, subject lines, body copy, CTAs, and post-purchase. The customer is the hero of the story; the product is the mentor or weapon they use to win. "We are an innovative platform revolutionizing education" → "You're about to revolutionize how you learn."

This is more than pronoun substitution — it is a structural rule that determines what goes in the copy. Replace "We've served 10,000+ companies" with "You're about to join 10,000+ companies who…"; replace "Our software is easy to use" with "You'll have it running in 5 minutes." The rewrite forces every claim to clear the test of "what does this give the customer?" before it ships.

Carlton's Hero's Journey frame makes the same point at story level: customer = hero, problem = challenge, product = mentor/tool, outcome = victory. Company-centric story arcs ("we built something amazing") consistently underperform customer-centric arcs ("you faced a challenge and won") because customers care about their own narrative, not yours.
