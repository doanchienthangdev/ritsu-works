# Integrate Social Proof at Decision Points, Not in Silos

Do not isolate testimonials, reviews, and case studies into a dedicated "Testimonials" page or end-of-page section. Weave proof through copy at the exact decision points where doubt arises — every major claim is followed immediately by the proof that supports that specific claim. Generic "Here's what customers say" silos force skeptics to hunt for reassurance and feel like an afterthought; integrated proof feels seamless and converts.

Concrete pattern: claim → immediate proof. Claim "You'll understand complex concepts faster" → proof "Students report understanding difficult material 40% faster (avg of 200 users)." Claim "Save hours on studying" → proof Michael Chen testimonial: "Went from 3 hours studying daily to 45 minutes." Strongest proof goes right before the CTA, where final hesitations get resolved. Place proof *before* CTAs, never after — proof after a CTA is ineffective.

Mix proof types (testimonials, case studies, metrics, logos, expert endorsements, media mentions, user-generated content, reviews) so different doubts get addressed by appropriate forms. Two or three strong, attributed, specific proofs always beat ten generic ones; anonymous testimonials are roughly 80% less effective than named ones.
