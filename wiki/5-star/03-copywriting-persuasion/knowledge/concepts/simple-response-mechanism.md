# Simple Response Mechanism (SRM)

John Carlton's Simple Response Mechanism is the conversion-point doctrine: remove every form of friction from the act of responding. Single clear CTA per page (not multiple competing buttons). Specific button text ("Try Free for 2 Weeks" not "Click Here" or "Submit"). Minimum viable form (email beats email+password+school+major+subjects at top of funnel). Clear next-step expectation ("You'll receive instant access to your first lesson"). Each extra click, each additional decision, each ambiguity is conversion lost.

The SRM principle extends to traffic temperature: cold traffic gets the lowest-commitment ask ("Try Free for 2 Weeks (No Credit Card)"); warm traffic gets the next step ("Get Started"); hot traffic gets the close ("Sign Me Up"). Mismatching offer friction to audience temperature — pushing "Buy Now" at cold traffic or "Learn More" at hot traffic — drops conversion.

For Ritsu specifically: the SRM is "Try Ritsu Free for 2 Weeks" with email-only signup and instant first-lesson access. Anything more elaborate (long form, plan picker upfront, credit-card requirement) violates the principle.
