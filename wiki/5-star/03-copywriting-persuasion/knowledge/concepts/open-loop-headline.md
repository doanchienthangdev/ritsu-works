# Open Loop Headline

An open loop is a headline or copy structure that creates curiosity by making an intriguing statement without immediately delivering the payoff, exploiting the Zeigarnik Effect (humans are compelled to complete unfinished tasks/stories). The reader's brain demands closure, which pulls them through the copy until the answer arrives.

Open loop varieties include the curious fact ("Most people don't know this about learning…"), the counter-intuitive statement ("Everything you've been told about studying is wrong"), the bold claim, the story hook, the question, the list tease ("#3 will shock you"), and the before-and-after hint ("I was a C student until I discovered this one thing…"). Each works by surfacing a question the reader cannot leave unanswered.

The discipline that separates open loops from clickbait: the payoff must be more valuable than the curiosity. Real loops deliver useful information that justifies the wait; clickbait disappoints. The headline "The one study technique that actually works (most people never try it)" sets up a loop; the body must then actually teach that technique. Sales pages can run multiple concurrent loops to deepen engagement, but every loop must close with substance.
