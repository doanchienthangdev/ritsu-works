# Bar Stool Test (Bài Kiểm Tra Quán Bar)

John Carlton's Bar Stool Test asks: would your customer actually say this line while sitting at a bar with a friend? If the copy fails — if it sounds like a corporate press release or contains "leverage," "synergy," "paradigm," "cutting-edge" — rewrite until it sounds natural. The test catches the most common copywriting failure: marketing-speak that looks polished but does not persuade because no human actually talks that way.

Worked example from the source: the line "Nền tảng này sử dụng các mô hình dựa trên AI để tối ưu hóa số liệu giữ thông tin nhận thức" (This platform uses AI-driven models to optimize cognitive retention metrics) fails the test; "Cuối cùng hiểu những gì bạn thực sự đang học, không chỉ ghi nhớ nó" (Finally understand what you're actually learning, not just memorize it) passes. The second sounds like a student talking to a roommate; the first does not.

For Ritsu, the test means every line on the landing page, in emails, and in ads should be readable aloud without stumbling. If a Vietnamese-speaking college student would not say it to a friend, it is not Ritsu copy yet.
