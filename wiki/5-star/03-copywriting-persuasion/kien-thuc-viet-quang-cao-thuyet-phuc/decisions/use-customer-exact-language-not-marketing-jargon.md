# Use Customer's Exact Language, Not Marketing Jargon

The Vietnamese rendering of Carlton's "Know Your Customer" principle codifies a concrete discipline: collect real customer phrases (record interviews with permission, harvest exact quotes from reviews and forums, build a swipe file of customer language), then use those phrases verbatim in copy. Avoid jargon unless the customer uses the jargon. Match their metaphors, their tone, their vocabulary, and their emotional words rather than yours.

The source gives two segment-specific examples. For Vietnamese college students, the customer-language phrases are short and direct: "Cuối cùng thực sự hiểu cách này" (finally actually understand this), "Dừng lãng phí thời gian học vô ích" (stop wasting time on useless studying), "Vượt qua kỳ thi của bạn" (ace your exam — not "cải thiện hiệu suất kỳ thi" / improve exam performance), "Lấy lại cuộc sống của bạn" (get your life back). For busy professionals the register shifts: "Vừa vào lịch trình của bạn" (fits your schedule), "5 phút thiết lập" (5-min setup), "Không BS, chỉ những gì hoạt động" (no BS, just what works).

This is the operational consequence of the Bar Stool Test: when Ritsu writes for Vietnamese students, the segment-appropriate phrases above are the raw material. Avoid translating English marketing copy directly into Vietnamese — translate the underlying customer pain into the way Vietnamese students actually voice it.
