# A-pile vs B-pile (Gary Halbert)

Gary Halbert's direct-mail mental model adapted for email subject lines. Mail (or email) falls into two piles when a recipient sorts: the **A-pile** is handwritten-envelope-style — curious, mysterious, personal-feeling — that MUST be opened; the **B-pile** is corporate letterhead, clearly marketing, safe to ignore and discard over the waste paper basket. The same sorting happens in the inbox in roughly two seconds per email.

The operational implication is that subject lines should be A-pile shaped: curiosity gaps ("An idea", "Regarding video"), ego appeals ("Asking for your advice"), personality hooks ("Please don't lie to my wife"), and fear-of-incompetence prompts ("Are you doing this wrong?"). B-pile failures are corporate ("We are award winners"), generic ("August Newsletter"), or pushy/spammy ("LIMITED TIME OFFER", "Last chance" cry-wolf). Algorithm penalty compounds the human one — if recipients consistently don't open, ESP filters move future sends to promotions/spam.

The frame matters beyond subject lines: it's a general posture of "would a friend send this?" If yes, A-pile. If it looks like a broadcast, B-pile.
