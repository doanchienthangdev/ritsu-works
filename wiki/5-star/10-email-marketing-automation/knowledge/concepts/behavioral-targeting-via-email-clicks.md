# Behavioral targeting via email clicks

A segmentation technique that uses link clicks within emails as revealed-preference signals, then tags contacts and routes them into interest-specific follow-up sequences. Each link in an email is unique per content type; when a recipient clicks, the email tool fires an automation rule that tags them ("men's interested", "beach_wedding_interested", "automation feature"). Subsequent emails to that contact draw from the tag set.

The mechanic flips email from broadcast to dialogue: the recipient isn't surveyed explicitly, but every click expresses interest. A clothing brand sending a single "summer arrivals" email with three section links (men's / women's / unisex) ends up with three downstream segments that each receive different follow-up content, producing 40%+ higher engagement on subsequent sends. An event-planning business asks "what's your ideal event?" with four options; a B2B SaaS asks "which feature interests you?" with four feature links — both produce a tag inventory that drives feature-specific case studies, pricing, and demo offers.

Benefits stack: higher engagement (content matches actual interest), better conversion (tailored messaging), lower unsubscribe (people get what they asked for), and a continuous signal stream so founders see which features/products are driving market pull.
