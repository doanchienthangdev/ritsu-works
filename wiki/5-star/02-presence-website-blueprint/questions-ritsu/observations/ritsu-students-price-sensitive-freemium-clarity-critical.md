# Ritsu Students are Price-Sensitive; Freemium Clarity is Critical

Ritsu's audience is described as "price-sensitive, freemium model planned." The Ritsu-specific question deck flags this as a recurring congruence-and-content concern: students are broke, and the landing page must make the freemium nature **immediately obvious** — not buried, not a surprise at signup.

Practical implications for the landing page:
- State the free tier clearly in the hero or directly under it ("Free forever for basic" vs. "Free trial").
- If there are free-tier limits (e.g., 3 quizzes/day, ads, capped uploads), name them on the landing page **before** the signup form — surfacing limits at signup is a trust violation.
- The freemium offer must be **compelling enough on its own** to drive signups without payment — measured by free-signup-to-active-user conversion, not by upsell rate.
- Address the unspoken objection "is it really free, or is there a catch?" directly in FAQ.

Counter-risk: over-emphasizing "free" without explaining the path to monetization makes the business model look unserious to skeptical students or institutional buyers (e.g., professors evaluating Ritsu for class use). The landing page should answer "free in what sense?" with calm specificity.
