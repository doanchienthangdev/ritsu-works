# Brand promise must be deliverable — Ryanair vs. luxury mismatch

A brand promise is what customers expect to get when they buy. The framework rules that the promise must be deliverable — and that the entire customer experience must be designed to align with the promise. If a business promises premium service but delivers mediocre execution, the brand breaks down and customers stop trusting it permanently. The boundary the founder is committing to is therefore not "what would sound good in marketing" but "what we can actually deliver every time."

Ryanair is cited as the positive example: their promise is "cheap flights, no frills." Every customer interaction is designed around this — they charge for baggage, they have uncomfortable seats, they board quickly. Because the experience aligns with the promise, customers (mostly) accept it. If Ryanair instead promised "a luxury travel experience" but delivered the same product, they would be seen as frauds. The rule generalizes: the cheaper of two mismatched promises (over-claiming or under-delivering) destroys trust irreversibly.
