# Positioning is customer perception, not the tagline or logo

A common conceptual error is treating positioning as a tagline, an elevator pitch, a logo, or a marketing slogan. The Vietnamese summary of the BRAVO module reinforces the same boundary as the English module: positioning is the *perception* a business occupies in the customer's mind — the collection of associations, feelings, beliefs, and expectations a customer has when they think about the business. Marketing artifacts (slogans, taglines, visual identity) are *expressions* of positioning, but they are not positioning itself.

The practical implication for any founder working in a non-English market: translating the slogan does not translate the positioning. Positioning lives in how the customer in that market actually perceives the business — which means it has to be tested with customers in their own language and cultural context, not assumed from a translated tagline.
