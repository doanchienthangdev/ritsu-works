# Founder's personal brand shapes the product brand

A founder's public reputation bleeds into the product's brand perception, especially when the founder is publicly active. The Vietnamese module surfaces Elon Musk → Tesla as the canonical illustration: when the founder tweets controversial things, the company's brand perception shifts with it — sometimes positively, sometimes negatively, depending on the audience's values.

The idea worth preserving for Ritsu: as a solo-founder B2C company, the founder's voice on social and in interviews is part of positioning whether the founder intends it or not. Refusing to be visible doesn't remove the founder from positioning; it just lets the silence shape it. The decision to lean into founder visibility (and choose what to be visible *about*) is a positioning choice in its own right, not a separate "personal branding" track.

This is filed as an idea (not a decision) because the trade-off — founder visibility increases positioning leverage but also concentrates reputational risk in one person — is not resolved by the source material. It is worth revisiting before any major founder-public-statement decision.
