# Sell benefits, not features — and go as deep as you can

Features describe what a product has or does ("adjustable standing desk", "256-bit encryption", "real-time collaboration"). Benefits describe what the customer gets as a result ("reduce back pain", "your data is completely secure", "your team stays in sync"). Customers do not buy features; they buy results and feelings those features enable. The framework rules that positioning copy must translate every feature into a benefit, then keep translating until it reaches an emotionally-resonant outcome.

The feature-benefit bridge can be applied recursively:
- Feature: "Adjustable standing desk"
- Benefit: "Reduce back pain and improve posture"
- Deeper benefit: "Feel healthier and more energetic at the end of the work day"
- Even deeper benefit: "Have the energy to be fully present with your family after work"

Apple is cited as an exemplar: rather than positioning the "A11 Bionic chip with neural engine" (feature), they say "photos and videos process instantly. Your device understands you." The deeper a benefit reaches into the customer's lived experience, the harder it is for competitors to copy with feature-list one-upmanship.
