# Positioning happens whether you do it intentionally or not

A business cannot opt out of positioning. Every customer forms a perception when they encounter the business; the only choice is whether that perception happens intentionally (via deliberate messaging, design, experience) or accidentally (from inconsistent or default choices). Most businesses end up accidentally positioned — they use generic templates, copy competitor messaging, let customer service define the brand, and evolve without intention. Accidental positioning is always weaker than intentional positioning.

In the absence of clear positioning from the business itself, competitors will position it as inferior or generic, customers will make assumptions based on surface cues, analysts will categorize it however is convenient, and employees will use inconsistent positioning. Once accidental positioning takes hold ("the cheap alternative to Salesforce", "that tool nobody's heard of"), repositioning out of it is expensive and risky.

The directive: position yourself first, before the market and competitors do it for you.
