# Psychographics over demographics

Demographics (age, income, location, job title) describe surface attributes. Psychographics (values, beliefs, fears, lifestyle, aspirations) describe behavior drivers. The BRAVO framework rules that psychographics are more predictive than demographics of whether someone will actually buy from you. Two customer avatars with identical demographics can require completely different messaging, products, and positioning if their psychographics diverge.

The cited example: a skincare company's audience of "women 25-35, $60k+ income" splits into two distinct psychographic avatars — "Science-focused Sarah" (values evidence, reads ingredient lists, follows dermatologists) and "Natural-minded Nina" (values sustainability, skeptical of chemicals, follows eco-influencers). Same demographics; opposite product positioning. This is why founders building customer avatars should go deep on values and fears rather than wide on demographic checkboxes.
