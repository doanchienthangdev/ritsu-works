# Credibility signals must match the audience to register as trust

Different audiences trust different signals: B2B buyers value case studies, certifications, team credentials, and customer logos; B2C consumers respond to star ratings, testimonials, media mentions, and user counts; enterprise buyers need SOC 2 and compliance; students respond to peer testimonials, educator endorsement, and usage statistics. A credibility signal that does not match the audience is wasted real estate and may even backfire — a Michelin star is highly credible for a restaurant but meaningless for a financial advisory firm.

Concrete rule: a marketing tool does not need a "Trusted by nurses" badge; it needs "Trusted by marketing professionals." For Ritsu, that means displaying which universities students come from, peer testimonials, and educator endorsements — not generic SaaS badges like SOC 2 (which matter to enterprise buyers but not 19-year-olds).
