# Distribute social proof throughout the journey, not in one section

The instinct to consolidate testimonials into a single "Social Proof" or "Reviews" page is wrong. Social proof works by appearing exactly where a prospect's confidence wavers. Map it to friction points:

- **Hero section** — star rating + user count (sets baseline trust above the fold)
- **Features section** — testimonials from users who love that specific feature
- **Pricing section** — a testimonial from someone in that tier ("Worth every penny for the $X plan")
- **Objection handling** — a testimonial that specifically addresses the objection ("I was worried about X, but...")
- **Checkout/signup** — urgency or momentum signals ("X people signed up this week")

Vague social proof is worthless. "Our customers love us!" is wasted; "4.8 stars from 2,847 verified reviews; 94% of customers renew annually" is persuasive. Specificity beats volume; the more concrete the number, named customer, and quantified outcome, the more it shifts decisions.
