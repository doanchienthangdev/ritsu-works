# For narrow niches, start with publications, not topics

The default guest-post ideation workflow is "generate topics, then find publications that might want them." For narrow or boring niches, invert it: pick 20-30 relevant publications first, study 2-3 months of their content, identify topic patterns and gaps, and only then pitch topics that already fit their editorial calendar. This approach is more efficient than ideating blind because the publication universe in a narrow niche is small and well-defined, while the topic space appears vast but most topics won't fit anywhere.

The principle generalizes: when one side of a many-to-many matching problem is much smaller than the other, start with the small side. For mainstream B2C, topic ideas are the smaller side (audience is vast). For B2B/industrial/niche, publications are the smaller side. Reverse the search direction and the conversion rate climbs.
