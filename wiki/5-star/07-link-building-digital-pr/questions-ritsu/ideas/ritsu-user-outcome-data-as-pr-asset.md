# Ritsu user-outcome data as a digital PR asset

Ritsu's defining digital PR opportunity is publishing aggregated learning-outcome data from its users — test score improvements, retention rates, time-to-mastery, study habit shifts. Possible packagings include: "State of Student Learning 2026" annual survey, subject-specific learning trends, demographic differences in how students learn with AI, AI-tutoring-vs-human-tutoring outcome comparison. Each is the kind of data EdTech journalists (EdSurge, Chronicle of Higher Education) and AI journalists (TechCrunch, MIT Technology Review) actively look for and cite.

The unique leverage: nobody else has Ritsu's specific dataset, and learning-outcome data is exactly the genre that earns high-authority links from university sites, academic publications, and education policy outlets. This compounds with Ritsu's Anthropic relationship — being a notable Claude API user with publishable research data is the kind of profile that earns both ecosystem features and academic citations. Should be tested with a small first publication (a single subject-specific report) before committing to an annual State-of-Learning report.
