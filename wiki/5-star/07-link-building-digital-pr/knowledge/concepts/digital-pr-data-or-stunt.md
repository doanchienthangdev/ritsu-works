# Digital PR: data-driven story or publicity stunt

Digital PR earns links by getting your story, data, or angle into media coverage — not by buying ad placement. The strategy splits into two execution patterns based on what you have:

**If you have data**: package your own data or public data analyzed in a new way as a media story. Clario (a cybersecurity company) analyzed terms-of-service across major platforms and packaged "Facebook is the Worst Offender" — naming a villain. Result: 6,000+ backlinks and 288,000+ monthly visitors. Other patterns: customer surveys with proprietary results ("HVAC Labor Crisis: 2024 Industry Survey"), public data recompiled ("Which US cities have the worst water quality"), or aggregate findings from a calculator/tool you provide.

**If you don't have data**: create a newsworthy moment. Trelleborg manufactured a 364-meter circumference O-ring, applied for a Guinness World Record, and pitched it — earning a Wikipedia link plus broad coverage. Other stunt patterns: setting an industry record, taking a controversial position, sponsoring unique research, hosting a conference, or doing something counterintuitive in your field.

Either path uses the same pitch structure (story-first, give the journalist quotes on a silver platter, offer one publication an exclusive then pitch others) and produces the same compounding benefit: more brand-name searches → higher rankings for branded terms.
