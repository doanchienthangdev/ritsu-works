# Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

## Why CLV Matters
- One of the most important business metrics, yet most marketers ignore it
- ALL fastest growing Exposure Ninja clients focus on CLV without exception
- As digital marketing gets more competitive, businesses must maximize revenue per customer
- Private equity-funded competitors can massively overspend on acquisition → only CLV-focused businesses survive
- Selling to existing customers is generally much cheaper (often free) vs acquiring new ones
- Most marketers are addicted to new customer acquisition — the exciting part
- Meanwhile, existing email lists of 10,000-20,000 sit unused
- CLV = purchase size × purchase frequency × customer duration

## The Two Levers

### Lever 1: Increase Number of Purchases

#### Strategy: Subscriptions
- Nudge customers into recurring subscription
- Example: Athletic Greens — one-time £97 vs subscription £79/month, showing cost per serving
  - Double subscription option with more incentives
  - Subscription = higher CLV with zero re-marketing cost
- Example: Together — subscription as DEFAULT purchase method
  - One-time purchase requires active opt-out
  - Customizable volume and frequency
- Subscription trend is everywhere (baking kits for kids to apocalypse survival items)
- Even hardware companies adding subscriptions:
  - GoPro: cloud backups + store discount subscription
  - Aura Ring: monthly membership for functionality that used to be one-time
- B2B subscriptions: maintenance packages, premium support
  - Example: Telestream/ScreenFlow — premium support subscription (priority tickets, phone support)
- Key question: what can YOUR business offer on subscription? (could be support, community, premium features)

#### Strategy: Renewal/Restock Marketing
- Identify average repurchase frequency
- Start promotion BEFORE customer runs out/expires
- Don't let them re-enter shopping/research phase
- Multi-channel approach:
  - Automated email sequences timed before renewal date
  - Feed user data to Google/Facebook for targeted display/social ads
  - Example: accreditation client — emails + Google Display + Facebook/Instagram ads before renewal
- E-commerce consumables: time restock emails before product runs out
- B2B memberships/licenses: renewal campaigns before expiry

#### Strategy: Upgrade Campaigns
- When you release new versions, run targeted campaigns to existing customers
- Example: Aura Ring v2 → v3
  - Ran promotion specifically to existing owners
  - Offered discount + free lifetime membership (new subscription feature)
  - Much cheaper to get existing customer to upgrade than acquire new customer
  - Marketing message: focus ONLY on what's new (they already know what the product does)
- Example: Telestream/ScreenFlow — each version upgrade = new license purchase at discounted price
  - Focus on new features vs current version, not overall product pitch

### Lever 2: Increase Order Value

#### Strategy: Add-ons
- Example: GoDaddy domain purchase — battle through upsells:
  - Domain protection ($6.99/yr default-ticked), ultimate protection upgrade
  - Website builder (free to start, then hosting upsells)
  - Email addresses (multiple tier packages up to $12.99/month)
  - All add-ons discounted first year (renewal bill higher, but card already on file)
  - Must click "No thanks" to avoid charges (default = yes)
- Example: GoPro — cases, tripods, batteries, cold weather batteries, goose necks, clamps
  - "Accessories village" — continuous upsell after initial purchase
- E-commerce: always think about related products and add-ons

#### Strategy: Upsells
- Example: VistaPrint business cards — masterclass in upselling:
  - Paper thickness (standard vs premium, premium = "recommended")
  - Finish options (matte → glossy → foil accent → embossed)
  - Corner style (standard sharp → rounded at extra fee)
  - Quantity with discount incentives for larger orders
  - Post-checkout: monogrammed products, office supplies, your face on a hot air balloon
- Before AND after checkout = focused on increasing average order value
- Competing in price-sensitive space → must maximize each customer's value to fund acquisition

#### Strategy: Premium Options
- Example: Simba mattresses — three tiers (Core, Hybrid Pro, Lux)
  - Captures every budget level
  - "I'll always default to wanting the best" psychology
  - Also sells pillows, duvets, mattress protectors (cross-sells)
- Monthly payment/split-pay reduces price sensitivity
  - Example: Klarna claims up to 58% increase in average order value
  - £1,279 one-off = sticker shock; £100/month = affordable
  - Split pay everywhere now (Pretty Little Thing: £2 item split into 6 payments)

#### B2B Premium Upsells
- Example: Best Companies workplace survey
  - Basic survey is entry point
  - 152 employee comments collected BUT locked behind "Pro" upgrade
  - Genius: collected data during normal usage, then gated results
  - Customer didn't know Pro existed until they saw locked data they desperately wanted

## Key Principles
- Having these two levers (more purchases + higher value) working together = maximum CLV
- Even one lever can dramatically improve business economics
- Higher CLV → can afford more for customer acquisition → more market share → compounding advantage
- Reducing price sensitivity (split pay, subscriptions, premium tiers) enables higher CLV
- Best results: businesses that systematically optimize both levers
- CLV calculations compound over time: small percentage improvements stack into massive advantages
- Focus on long-term customer value, not short-term transaction profit

## CLV Checklist
1. Subscriptions: can you offer subscription? Is it the default option?
2. Renewal/restock: do you know repurchase frequency? Do you market before expiry?
3. Upgrades: do you promote new versions to existing customers?
4. Add-ons: what complementary products/services can you bundle?
5. Upsells: do you offer premium options? Do you present them at checkout?
6. Premium versions: do you have tiered pricing capturing all budget levels?
7. Split pay: do you offer monthly payment options?

## Advanced CLV Concepts

### Retention-Driven Growth
- Acquiring customers at breakeven/loss is viable if CLV is high enough
- This is how SaaS scales: accept customer acquisition cost (CAC) of $500 if CLV is $5,000+
- Retention metrics (churn rate, expansion revenue) become more important than new customer count
- Calculate payback period: how many months until a customer recovers their acquisition cost

### Monetization Psychology
- Default effects matter: what's the default option? (subscription vs one-time, add-on yes vs no)
- Anchoring: present premium option first, then show cheaper tiers
- Bundling: people value bundles higher than individual items
- Scarcity: "upgrade expires in X days" drives urgency
- Social proof: "X% of customers upgrade to premium" encourages similar behavior

### Channel Optimization for CLV
- Email marketing to existing customers has highest ROI (low cost, warm audience)
- Retargeting campaigns for upsells much cheaper than cold acquisition
- Referral programs increase both acquisition AND CLV (referred customers have higher LTV)
- Customer communities increase stickiness and cross-sell opportunities

### CLV vs CAC
- If CAC = $100 and CLV = $500, you can profitably spend more on acquisition
- If CLV = $200, acquisition budget must be smaller (unsustainable if CAC is $100)
- Increasing CLV first before scaling acquisition prevents profitability issues later
- Many startups fail because they grow customer base without growing CLV
