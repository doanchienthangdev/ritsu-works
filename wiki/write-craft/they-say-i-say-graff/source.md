## What this book gives /write

- Never assert cold — open by naming the view you're correcting ('they say'), then deliver your claim as the answer ('I say'); without the 'they say,' the reader sees WHAT but never WHY.
- No quote, stat, or study speaks for itself: sandwich it — say who's speaking, drop it in, then restate its meaning and tie it to your point. Bare quotes are 'hit-and-run.'
- Plant the naysayer: voice the strongest objection before the reader thinks of it, give it a real hearing, then answer it — a preemptive strike that builds credibility instead of dodging.
- Always answer 'So what? Who cares?' — a clear, correct claim with no stated stakes loses the audience; name who has a stake and the consequence they already care about.
- Templates don't stifle creativity, they free it — they shape HOW you say something while the WHAT stays fully original; master the move, then improvise past it.
