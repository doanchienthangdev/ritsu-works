# Writing craft distilled — Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace (Joseph M. Williams)

## Core thesis on writing
Clarity is not a feeling you chase but a structure you can engineer. Williams's central discovery is that readers judge prose "clear," "choppy," or "graceful" based on a few predictable patterns of word order — chiefly, whether your **characters are the subjects** and your **actions are the verbs**, and whether information flows **old-before-new** from one sentence into the next. Because these patterns are diagnosable and fixable, good style is a learnable craft, not a gift. And because clear writing respects the reader's time, choosing it is finally an ethical act: *"Write to others as you would have others write to you."*

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Diagnose–Analyze–Rewrite** — the universal second-pass edit: underline the first 7-8 words, flag abstract subjects and late verbs, then fix them.
- **Characters→Subjects, Actions→Verbs** — the single highest-leverage clarity move; make every sentence a tiny "who did what" story.
- **Old-Before-New (cohesion)** — open on the familiar, end on the new, chain sentences at the seams; this is what "flow" actually is.
- **Consistent Topic Strings (coherence)** — keep a small set of characters in the opening/subject slots so a passage feels focused.
- **Stress Position** — relocate the idea you most want felt to the end of the sentence, the natural emphasis.
- **Six Principles of Concision** — a delete-and-compress checklist (meaningless, doubled, inferable, phrase-for-word, negatives→affirmatives, useless modifiers).
- **Control Sprawl** — extend long sentences cleanly with resumptive, summative, and free modifiers instead of clause-on-clause chains.
- **Balance & Climactic Close** — parallel structure plus end-weight for the few lines meant to be memorable (headlines, taglines, closers).
- **Real-vs-Invented Rule Triage** — keep real and social rules; ignore invented ones (split infinitives, terminal prepositions) when they hurt clarity.

## Craft principles
- **Make every sentence a little story** — agent as subject, action as verb; never bury the action in a noun or lean on empty verbs (is, has, conducts).
- **Flow lives at the seams** — cohesion is the fit between how one sentence ends and the next begins, not anything inside a single sentence.
- **Simplicity → complexity** — short concrete subject and quick verb up front; long, abstract, technical material at the back, where the reader has momentum.
- **End on weight** — light trailing words (a preposition, a tacked-on attribution) make a sentence deflate; the payoff goes last.
- **Concision is labor** — you must inch through each sentence; it can't be skimmed in. Compression is "style's first grace."
- **Prefer affirmatives** — say what *is*; fold "do not allow" into "prevent."
- **Trim metadiscourse** — say the thing rather than announce that you will.
- **Diagnose, don't trust your ear** — you know your own draft too well to feel its fog.
- **Subjects carry meaning** — "people distrust people" vs "circumstances caused distrust" are different *claims*, not different styles.
- **Clarity is ethical** — the ethos a reader infers from your prose hardens into your reputation.
