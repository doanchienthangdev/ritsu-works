# Writing craft distilled — Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion (Robert Cialdini)

## Core thesis on writing
Persuasive writing works less by stacking evidence than by tripping the right mental shortcut. Cialdini's central finding is that readers, overloaded and busy, run on automatic "click, run" responses — they decide from a single representative cue (a price, a count of others, a deadline, an authority signal) rather than from all the available information. For /write this reframes copy from "explain everything" to "identify the one trigger that carries the case, surface it honestly, and lead with it." Seven universal levers — reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and unity — are the reusable machinery.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Reciprocity Give-First** — give genuine value before you ask; the ask then rides obligation.
- **Rejection-Then-Retreat** — open with a larger request, retreat to the real (smaller) one; concession + contrast.
- **Commitment & Consistency Ladder** — secure a tiny active/public/effortful first yes, then escalate.
- **Social Proof of the Many-Like-You** — cite a concrete count of *similar* others already doing it.
- **Authority Credible-Expert Frame** — front-load real evidence; distinguish "an authority" from "in authority."
- **Scarcity Loss-Frame** — name what the reader will *lose*, time-bound and true.
- **Unity One-of-Us Framing** — invoke shared identity ("one of us"), not mere similarity ("like us").
- **Give-the-Because Reason** — never a bare request; attach a real "because…"

## Craft principles
- **Trip the right mental shortcut** — lead with the single cue that fires the right "click, run" response.
- **Set the contrast before the number** — show the big/expensive thing first; the real ask then looks small.
- **Prove it with the many, don't assert it** — a count of similar peers beats any direct claim.
- **Frame the stakes as a loss** — loss aversion pulls harder than the identical gain.
- **Earn a small yes first** — a tiny commitment rewrites self-image and "grows its own legs."
- **Give before you ask** — an unexpected, customized gift creates a debt the reader wants to repay.
- **Admit a weakness to be believed** — a conceded flaw makes every strong claim more credible.
- **Always attach a reason** — people want a why; supply a real, reader-centered one.
- **Write from inside the tribe** — use the audience's own words and shared-identity category.
- **Use real triggers, never fabricated ones** — fake scarcity/proof/similarity collapse trust permanently.
