# Writing craft distilled — On Writing Well (William Zinsser)

## Core thesis on writing
Writing is "talking to someone else on paper" — anyone who can think clearly can write clearly, about any subject. The enemy is clutter: every needless word, long word, redundant adverb, and passive construction that weakens a sentence. Good writing is the residue of relentless rewriting, and what the writer ultimately sells is not the subject but *who they are* — humanity and warmth that keep a reader (attention span: ~30 seconds) moving from one sentence to the next.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Strip-and-Rebuild (Cut-to-Half Pass)** — bracket every word doing no new work; test by deleting; cut a draft to half its length, then attack the remainder.
- **One-Provocative-Thought Reduction** — bite off one small corner of the subject and leave the reader with exactly *one* new idea, not five.
- **Throwaway-First-Three** — draft past the throat-clearing, then cut to the sentence where you start to sound like yourself.
- **Annotated-Decision Pass** — justify each word and transition; after every sentence ask "what does the reader want to know next?"
- **One-Idea-Per-Sentence** — split any sentence carrying two thoughts; readers process ideas in linear sequence (gold for tutorials/explainers).
- **Stop-at-the-Exit Ending** — when you've made your point, find the nearest exit; refuse the "In sum…" recap; end on a quote, image, or full-circle echo.
- **I-Ness Warmth Pass** — put a person back into institutional copy with "I/we/you" and active verbs (clarity, simplicity, brevity, humanity).

## Craft principles
- **Clear thinking becomes clear writing** — fix the thought before the sentence; ask "What am I trying to say?" then "Have I said it?"
- **Rewriting is the writing** — the first draft is raw material; reshape, tighten, refine until the narrative flows with no trouble.
- **Active verbs are the engine** — they push the sentence forward and force a visible actor; passive voice saps energy.
- **Kill redundant modifiers** — cut adverbs that repeat the verb and adjectives stating a known fact.
- **No little qualifiers** — "a bit," "sort of," "very," "rather" each whittle away the reader's trust; be bold, not kind-of bold.
- **Write by ear** — read everything aloud; vary sentence length; an occasional short sentence lands a punch.
- **The first sentence must hook** — readers want to know, very soon, what's in it for them.
- **Be yourself** — there's no style store; voice is organic, and it *is* the product.
- **Fight clichés and journalese** — reach for the precise, slightly unexpected word; the race is to the original, not the swift.
- **Prefer the short plain word** — "help" over "assistance"; after verbs, concrete nouns are your strongest tools.
- **Ask what it's *really* about** — cut hard-won material that isn't central; readers should sense you know more than you wrote.
- **Trust your material** — let a striking fact or joke speak for itself; no exclamation point to announce it.

## Signature lines

- "Don't be kind of bold. Be bold." (Ch. 10)
