# Writing craft distilled — Scientific Advertising (Claude C. Hopkins)

## Core thesis on writing
Persuasive writing is **salesmanship in print**, governed by measurable laws, not taste. Every line must do what a good salesman would do in front of one selfish buyer: offer service, prove claims with specifics, and earn the decision instead of demanding it. The two unforgivable sins are writing to entertain (seeking applause instead of action) and writing to please the seller instead of the reader. When in doubt, Hopkins's whole method reduces to one test — *"Would it help a salesman sell the goods?"* — and one verdict mechanism: trace the result, keep the winner.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Offer-Service** — lead with what the reader gets + a no-risk trial; never open with "buy ours".
- **The Specific Claim** — replace every superlative with an exact, tested-sounding number; enriches the USP idea.
- **Tell the Full Story** — assume one reading; include every convincing claim; length follows stakes.
- **The Preemptive Claim** — be first to state a true, universal process fact in vivid detail, and own it.
- **Test-Before-Scale** — run small variants against real buyers, measure cost-per-customer, scale only the winner.

## Craft principles
- **Write to sell, not to be admired** — cut any line a salesman wouldn't use.
- **Write to one typical reader**, never "the mass" — the crowd blurs your language.
- **Assume the reader is selfish** — open from their self-interest, not your need to be chosen.
- **Specifics persuade; platitudes evaporate** — "best/most/fastest" signal exaggeration and get discounted.
- **The headline's only job is to flag the right reader** — spend hours on it; a new headline can multiply returns 5–10×.
- **Picture the gain, not the pain** — the positive, aspirational version out-pulls the warning version ~4:1.
- **Exact figures imply you measured** — "78-second shave", "425 calories", "130 formulas tested".
- **Do the research first** — one buried fact often becomes the keystone of the whole piece.
- **Hold one voice** — changing tone forces your best readers to get reacquainted.

## Signature lines
- *"Platitudes and generalities roll off the human understanding like water from a duck."* — Hopkins
- *"They forget they are salesmen and try to be performers. Instead of sales, they seek applause."* — Hopkins
- *"The more you tell the more you sell."* — Hopkins
- *"Genius is the art of taking pains."* — Hopkins
