# Writing craft distilled — The Elements of Style (Strunk & White)

## Core thesis on writing
Plain English style is a small set of enforceable rules of usage and composition, and 95% of bad writing comes from violating a handful of them. Good writing is concise, definite, active, and ordered so that **the reader is aided at every step** — the paragraph, the sentence, and the word are all positioned to make meaning obvious. The masters may break the rules, but only when they pay for the violation with some "compensating merit"; until you can do that reliably, obey.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Topic-Sentence Paragraph (Rule 10)** — claim first, develop in the middle, loop the ending back; never end on a digression.
- **Omit-Needless-Words Edit Pass (Rule 13)** — a fixed deletion checklist ('the fact that', 'who is/which was', 'in a hasty manner'→'hastily', step-chains→one sentence).
- **Positive-Form Rewrite (Rule 12)** — convert 'not'-evasions into the definite positive word ('did not remember'→'forgot').
- **Parallel Construction (Rule 15)** — cast like ideas in like grammatical form so the reader sees the likeness.
- **End-Weight Emphasis (Rule 18)** — put the most important word last; scales to sentences, paragraphs, and whole pieces.

## Craft principles
- **Use the active voice** — direct, vigorous, names the actor; replace 'there is / could be heard' with a real verb.
- **Make every word tell** — no unnecessary words or sentences, but keep needed detail; conciseness ≠ shortness.
- **Prefer the definite, specific, concrete** — kill vague approval-words (effect, feature, factor, nature, case, clever) and say what actually happened.
- **One topic per paragraph** — the break is a signal to the reader that a new step has begun.
- **Keep related words together** — position is the chief signal of relationship; modifiers next to what they modify, relative pronoun next to its antecedent.
- **Leading participle must match the subject** — or the sentence turns ludicrous ('Being in a dilapidated condition, I bought the house').
- **No comma splices; semicolons join equal clauses** — punctuation marks where thoughts begin and end.
- **Avoid a string of loose and/but sentences** — sing-song monotony numbs the reader.
- **One tense in a summary** — shifting reads as uncertainty.

## Signature lines
- "Omit needless words." — Strunk
- "Vigorous writing is concise." — Strunk
- "Make definite assertions. Avoid tame, colorless, hesitating, non-committal language." — Strunk
- "The position of the words in a sentence is the principal means of showing their relationship." — Strunk
