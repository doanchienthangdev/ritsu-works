# Writing craft distilled — Save the Cat! (Blake Snyder)

## Core thesis on writing
Storytelling is a craft with knowable, near-physical 'laws' — not a mystery to be approached with awe. Before you write a word, you must be able to say *what it is* in one sentence; if you can't compress it, you don't have it yet. And every piece must earn attention from strangers fast, hit its structural turns on schedule, and show visible change from open to close.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Blake Snyder Beat Sheet (BS2)** — 15 beats on a fixed page-map that give any long narrative momentum, a turning Midpoint, a real low point ('All Is Lost'), and proof of change.
- **The "What Is It?" Logline (4 components)** — irony + a compelling mental picture + audience/tone + a killer title; write it first and pitch it to real people.
- **Snyder's 10 Story-Types** — classify by structural/emotional DNA (Dude with a Problem, Golden Fleece, Monster in the House…), not by surface label, then study the best of that type.
- **The Board** — plan in ~40 movable scene cards across 4 rows; tag each with an emotional shift (+/-) and a conflict (><).
- **Save the Cat** — earn the reader's allegiance with an early endearing action (or make the antagonist worse).
- **The Pope in the Pool** — bury dull-but-necessary exposition behind an arresting foreground.
- **Is It Primal?** — re-root abstract stakes in survival, mating, protection, or fear of death.
- **Emotional Color Wheel** — deliberately cycle emotions instead of playing one note.

## Craft principles
- **One-sentence test**: inability to compress = you're guessing.
- **The hero leads**: protagonists must be proactive; passive leads die on the page.
- **Show, don't tell**: reveal through action and image, not narrated explanation.
- **Bookend with opposites**: the before/after gap is your proof of change.
- **Take a step back**: start the character as far from the end as possible — getting there *is* the story.
- **One concept per piece** (Black Vet): more does not mean better.
- **One piece of magic** (Double Mumbo Jumbo): ask for exactly one suspension of disbelief.
- **Present danger** (Watch Out for That Glacier): make threats immediate and shown.
- **Everybody arcs**; give minor players a 'Limp and an Eyepatch'; give every voice its own voice (the Bad Dialogue Test); put one clear conflict in every scene.

## Signature lines
- "What is it? is the name of the game… A good What is it? is the coin of the realm." — Snyder
- "if I can't fill in the blank in one or two sentences — I don't have the beat yet! I am just guessing." — Snyder
- "The hero must be proactive. It's The Law. If he's not, he's not a hero." — Snyder
- "When in doubt ask, Is It Primal?" — Snyder
