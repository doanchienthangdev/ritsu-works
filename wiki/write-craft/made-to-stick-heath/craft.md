# Writing craft distilled — Made to Stick (Chip Heath & Dan Heath)

## Core thesis on writing
An idea "sticks" when it is understandable, memorable, and able to change thought or behavior — and stickiness is not a gift, it's a craft you can audit for. Six properties make ideas stick, spelling **SUCCESs**: **S**imple, **U**nexpected, **C**oncrete, **C**redible, **E**motional, **S**tories. The villain that ruins all six is the **Curse of Knowledge**: once you know something you can't imagine not knowing it, so your prose silently goes opaque. Good writing is, first, translation for a reader who hears only your taps, never the tune in your head.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **SUCCESs Stickiness Audit** — pressure-test any draft on all six dimensions; rewrite the misses.
- **Find the Core (Commander's Intent)** — force-rank to the single most important thing; lead with it; cut what competes.
- **Generative Analogy & High-Concept Pitch** — map the new idea onto a schema the reader already owns ("Die Hard on a bus"); keep it generative.
- **WIIFY Self-Interest Lead** — open with the reader's payoff, in second person, spelled out ("benefit of the benefit").
- **Three Inspirational Plots** — pick Challenge (try harder), Connection (belong/help), or Creativity (innovate) to fit the emotion you want.
- **Springboard Story** — tell one true story of the change you want believed; withhold the moral so the reader infers it and buys in.

## Craft principles
- **Beat the Curse of Knowledge** — re-read as a true novice; translate every term you can no longer un-know.
- **Make it concrete** — sensory nouns, named specifics, images; the Velcro theory says more hooks = stickier.
- **Write to the one, not the mass** — readers feel for a named person, not statistics; analysis kills feeling.
- **Statistics show a relationship** — translate numbers to human scale; the relationship sticks, the figure won't.
- **Open a curiosity gap first** — "Here's what you know; now here's what you're missing" before you answer.
- **Surprise by breaking a pattern, then make it postdictable** — violate expectation on the core point, then resolve it.
- **Make the credential testable** — let the reader verify it themselves ("Where's the beef?").
- **Show via simulation** — a story is a flight simulator; turn rules into scenes the reader walks through.
- **Core + compact** — proverb-length, but never compact-over-correct.
- **Appeal to identity, not just incentive** — "what does someone like me do here?"

## Signature lines
- "Once we know something, we find it hard to imagine what it was like not to know it." — Heath & Heath
- "If I look at the mass, I will never act. If I look at the one, I will." — Mother Teresa, quoted by Heath & Heath
- "Stories are like flight simulators for the brain." — Heath & Heath
- "It's more important for people to remember the relationship than the number." — Heath & Heath
