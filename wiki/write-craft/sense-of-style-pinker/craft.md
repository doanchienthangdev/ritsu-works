# Writing craft distilled — The Sense of Style (Steven Pinker)

## Core thesis on writing
Good writing is not obedience to a list of dos and don'ts; it is **reverse-engineered from prose you admire and grounded in how the reading mind works**. Its highest ideal is *classic style*: the writer can see a truth and orients the reader's gaze to it, as one equal pointing something out to another in conversation — "prose is a window onto the world." Most bad writing isn't stupidity or malice but the **curse of knowledge**: the writer can no longer imagine not knowing what they know. The cures are concreteness, calibration, coherence, and revision — plus the humility to show a draft to a real reader.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Classic Style (Window onto the World)** — show abstract ideas as concrete actors doing visible things; the strongest cure for academese.
- **Reverse-Engineer Good Prose** — dissect admired passages phrase-by-phrase, name the move, reuse it.
- **Concrete-Image Test (See It, Feel It)** — swap abstractions for the seeable thing ('the ivory chess set fell off the table').
- **Topic-Then-Comment / Given-Then-New** — open from known info, end on the new; keep one actor threaded across sentences.
- **Arcs of Coherence (Connective Discipline)** — name the link between ideas (because/but/for-example); use parallel syntax (Rule of One Variable) for any comparison.
- **State It in the Positive** — convert negations to their affirmative twins; every 'not' is mental homework.
- **Calibrate to the Reader's Chunks** — banish clubhouse jargon; when unsure, assume too little.
- **Close the Loop** — get a real feedback signal; trying harder to imagine the reader doesn't cure the curse.
- **Signpost Sparingly** — kill metadiscourse; open with a question instead of a scrunched-up table of contents.

## Craft principles
- **Omit needless words** — cut hedges, intensifiers, scare quotes, metaconcepts (level, model, approach).
- **Show with a telling detail** instead of pronouncing the adjective.
- **Start strong, finish strong** — never open on a cliché or a banality.
- **Hedge by choice, not by tic** — qualify (spell out exceptions) rather than sprinkle escape-hatch words.
- **Treat writing as revising** — the form a thought takes for the writer is rarely the form a reader can absorb.
- **Write in a confident voice** — no apologetic quotes, no whinging that the topic is 'complex and controversial.'
- **Address the reader as a competent equal** — clarity is not condescension.
- **Look things up** — intellectual conscientiousness is the deepest style; don't mistake an anecdote for a trend.
- **Keep a moving protagonist in subject position** — eyes on an actor, not a parade of zombie nouns.

## Signature lines
- "Call it the Curse of Knowledge: a difficulty in imagining what it is like for someone else not to know something that you know." — Pinker
- "The curse of knowledge is the single best explanation I know of why good people write bad prose." — Pinker
- "Good writing is understood with the mind's eye." — Pinker
- "The advice in this and other stylebooks is not so much on how to write as on how to revise." — Pinker
