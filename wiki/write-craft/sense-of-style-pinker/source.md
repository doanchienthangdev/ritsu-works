## What this book gives /write

- Classic style is the default cure for jargon: show the idea as a real actor doing a visible thing, and point the reader's gaze to it as if in conversation — 'prose is a window onto the world.'
- The curse of knowledge — you can't imagine not knowing what you know — is the #1 cause of unclear teaching copy; the only reliable fix is to show a draft to a real reader (close the loop), not to try harder to imagine them.
- Concreteness wins: readers understand and remember the specific seeable thing ('the ivory chess set fell off the table') far better than the abstraction, so swap zombie nouns and metaconcepts for verbs and pictures.
- Flow is engineering: open each sentence from given/known info and end on the new, keep one moving protagonist in subject position, and name the logical link (because/but/for-example) — using parallel syntax for every comparison.
- State it in the positive — every negation is mental homework, and stacking two 'nots' often means the opposite of what you intended; this is gold for quiz items and instructions.
