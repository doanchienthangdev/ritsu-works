# Writing craft distilled — On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft (Stephen King)

## Core thesis on writing
Writing is **telepathy** — the transmission of a clear image from the writer's mind into the reader's across time and space. The whole job is to send the picture intact, so anything that muddies the channel (fear, affectation, fancy words, adverbs, passive voice, over-description) is the enemy. King's two convictions: a *competent* writer can become *good* through brutal work, and the work reduces to one Prime Rule — **read a lot and write a lot** — plus a portable toolbox you carry to every job.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **What-If Situation Engine** — generate a story/scenario from one sentence: a character + a predicament ("What if a mother and son are trapped in a stalled car by a rabid dog?").
- **Fossil Excavation** — draft to *discover* the piece (it's buried, you dig it up), not to execute an outline; plot is the "jackhammer" of last resort.
- **Few Well-Chosen Details** — describe with the first 3–4 sensory details and stop; "description begins in the writer's imagination but should finish in the reader's."
- **Reveal Character by Behavior, Not Label** — never state "she was depressed"; show the behavior and let the reader conclude. "If I have to tell you, I lose."
- **2nd Draft = 1st Draft − 10%** — a hard cut quota every revision; "literary Viagra."
- **Closed Door / Open Door** — draft 1 for yourself (door shut), revise for the reader (door open), with a rest period between so you can kill your darlings.
- **The Ideal Reader Pass** — write to one specific named human; watch *where* they set the draft down — that scene is the boring part.
- **Graceful Back Story** — "everyone has a history and most of it isn't interesting"; dramatize context, never dump it.

## Craft principles
- **Omit needless words** (King adopts Strunk's Rule 17 as his own).
- **Prefer the active voice** — "The writer threw the rope, not The rope was thrown by the writer."
- **The adverb is not your friend** — especially in dialogue tags; the best attribution is plain *said*.
- **Use the first word that comes to mind** if apt and colorful — don't dress up vocabulary.
- **Lead expository paragraphs with a topic sentence** — paragraphs are "maps of intent."
- **Tell the truth** — honesty redeems stylistic faults; lying is "the great unrepairable fault."
- **Murder your darlings** — cut even what you love if it slows the story.
- **Fresh similes only** — they must clarify; clichés look lazy, broken ones lose the reader.
- **Fall back to noun + verb** when a sentence collapses under its clauses.
- **Know what it's about** and sharpen that theme in draft 2 ("step back and look at the forest").

## Signature lines
- "I'm convinced that fear is at the root of most bad writing." — King
- "The road to hell is paved with adverbs." — King
