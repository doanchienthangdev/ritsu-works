# Writing craft distilled — Ogilvy on Advertising (David Ogilvy)

## Core thesis on writing
Writing-to-persuade is a craft of information, not decoration: "I do not regard advertising as entertainment or an art form, but as a medium of information." The job is not to be found clever but to be found so interesting that the reader acts. Every choice — headline, first sentence, word length — is judged by one test: did it move the reader, or did it bore them? Ogilvy's whole method is *homework first, facts over adjectives, one human talking to one human*, and a fanatical respect for how real readers actually scan, skip, and misread.

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **Benefit-or-News Headline** — lead with the reader's strongest benefit or genuine news; put the brand name in; telegraph plainly, no puns (*enriches* the existing `ogilvy-headline`).
- **Specifics-Over-Generalities** — swap every vague claim for the exact number/fact; "5%" beats "less than you might suppose."
- **Big-Idea Five-Question Test** — gasp? / wish I'd thought of it? / unique? / on-strategy? / good for 30 years?
- **Do-Your-Homework Pre-Write** — study product, rivals, and real prospects' language before drafting a word.
- **Second-Person-Singular Letter Voice** — write to one reader, alone; open with a grabber.
- **Story-Form Copy** — cast the piece as a true, curiosity-hooking incident that resolves on the benefit.
- **Editorial-Not-Ad Format** — make it look like content, scan-order image→headline→copy, sell in every caption.

## Craft principles
- You cannot bore people into buying — interest is the only currency.
- Address one reader, not a crowd (second person singular).
- Use the words people actually use; test obscure words on a layperson.
- Drop Brag-and-Boast superlatives; substitute a verifiable fact.
- Make the first paragraph a grabber; never open with the obvious.
- Assume miscomprehension — make it crystal clear.
- Project the same image year after year; repeat your winners until they stop working.
- "Positively good," said clearly, can beat "better than them."
- The more (relevant) facts you tell, the more you sell.

## Signature lines
- "You cannot bore people into buying your product. You can only interest them in buying it." — David Ogilvy
- "Unless your headline sells your product, you have wasted 90 per cent of your money." — David Ogilvy
- "There are no dull products, only dull writers." — David Ogilvy
- "The more facts you tell, the more you sell." — Dr. Charles Edwards, quoted by Ogilvy
