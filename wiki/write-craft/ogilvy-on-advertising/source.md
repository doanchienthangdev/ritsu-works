## What this book gives /write

- The headline does 80–90% of the work: lead with a concrete benefit or real news, put the product name in it, and never sacrifice clarity for cleverness.
- Specifics out-sell generalities every time — replace 'better/a lot/the best' with the exact number or fact the reader can believe and remember.
- Write to ONE reader as if penning a personal letter (second person singular), and make the first sentence a grabber — you cannot bore anyone into acting.
- Pick the central idea with five questions: gasp? wish-I'd-thought-of-it? unique? on-strategy? good-for-30-years? — most concepts fail gate 4 (on-strategy).
- Do your homework before writing: the surprising true fact you dig up (Rolls-Royce's 'electric clock at 60 mph') is usually the headline.
