# Writing craft distilled — Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting (Robert McKee)

## Core thesis on writing
A story is the *creative demonstration of truth* — meaning proven through events, never explained in words. Its engine is the **gap** between what a character expects and what reality delivers; its substance is **value-charged change** achieved through **conflict**. Everything McKee teaches is a discipline for keeping a piece *alive* (every unit must turn a value) and *meaningful* (one Controlling Idea governs every keep/cut). Though written for screen, the principles apply to any telling: "stories do not need to be written to be told."

## Frameworks it gives /write
- **The Story Gap (Expectation vs. Result)** — the universal attention engine: action → surprising reaction → gap → harder action. Kills the "non-event."
- **Inciting Incident — Upset the Balance** — open by knocking the world off balance, then form a desire to restore it. A hook formula.
- **Controlling Idea (Value + Cause)** — compress meaning to one sentence ("X happens BECAUSE Y"); it decides what's kept and cut.
- **The Crisis Dilemma** — force a choice between two real values (lesser of two evils / irreconcilable goods); the choice defines character and detonates the climax.
- **The Value-Charge Turn** — name the value's charge at the top and end of each section; unchanged = cut it.
- **The Principle of Antagonism** — strengthen the opposition (Contrary → Contradictory → Negation of the Negation), not the hero; the win is only as big as the wall.

## Craft principles
- **Dramatize; never explain** — prove the idea through experience, not assertion.
- **Convert exposition to ammunition** — let facts arrive as weapons inside a conflict, never as info-dumps.
- **Write text and subtext** — "nothing is what it seems"; on-the-nose writing dies.
- **Withhold to create hunger** — interest comes from withholding, not giving; save secrets for last.
- **Never resolve by coincidence** — deus ex machina is "the writer's greatest sin."
- **Meaning produces emotion** — chase the irreversible value-swing, not spectacle.
- **Talent without craft is fuel without an engine** — apply principles deliberately.
- **Trivial told brilliantly beats profound told badly** — invest in the telling.
- **Pace by alternating tension and release** — relentless escalation exhausts the reader.
- **Lock the ending to the opening** — make the climax feel inevitable via the Spine.

## Signature lines
- "Master storytellers never explain. They do the hard, painfully creative thing—they dramatize." — McKee
- "If I could send a telegram to the film producers of the world, it would be these three words: 'Meaning Produces Emotion.'" — McKee
- "You do not keep the audience's interest by giving it information, but by withholding information." — McKee
- "Talent without craft is like fuel without an engine. It burns wildly but accomplishes nothing." — McKee
