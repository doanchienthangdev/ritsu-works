# Business Game Identification

**Type:** concept · **From:** *The North Star Playbook* (Amplitude, Inc., John Cutler, Ibrahim Bashir, 2024), Ch 1 — About the North Star Framework

## Summary

A workshop technique for identifying which of three fundamental 'games' a product is playing—attention (user engagement time shows satisfaction), transaction (helping users complete purchases and track delivery), or productivity (helping users accomplish a job to do). Identifying the game is described as usually the first point of contention in North Star workshops, but it clarifies team thinking and often changes the entire focus of the North Star.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 1 — About the North Star Framework](../chapters/chapter-01-about-the-north-star-framework.md)** (PDF pp. 6–13, confidence 0.95):

  > Attention game: Your user gets absorbed in your product, and the more time they spend using it shows increased satisfaction.

  > Productivity game: Your user chooses your product because they have a job to do.

  > Defining the game they're playing is usually the first point of contention of the workshop. But it clarifies the teams' thinking and often changes the whole focus of the North Star.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu sits at the intersection of attention (engagement time with quizzes) and productivity (job = master this material for an exam); identifying which game dominates will shape which North Star Metric correctly captures value delivered.
