# Vanity Metric

**Type:** concept · **From:** *The North Star Playbook* (Amplitude, Inc., John Cutler, Ibrahim Bashir, 2024), Ch 2 — The North Star checklist and statement exercise

## Summary

A vanity metric is one that makes a team feel good in the short term but does not reliably predict long-term product success. Examples include Daily Active Users, ad impressions, page views, downloads, and registered users. The chapter advises a 'vanity metric test' to distinguish metrics that mask real retention drivers from those that reflect genuine value.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — The North Star checklist and statement exercise](../chapters/chapter-02-the-north-star-checklist-and-statement-exerci.md)** (PDF pp. 14–22, confidence 0.95):

  > When your North Star changes, you want to be confident that the change is meaningful and valuable. Though some metrics might make you feel good about your performance in the short term, they don't always actually tell you about your product's long-term success.

  > Example Vanity Metrics: • Daily Active Users (DAU) • Ad impressions • Number of downloads • Page views • Registered users
