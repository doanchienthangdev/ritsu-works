# Input Metrics

**Type:** concept · **From:** *The North Star Playbook* (Amplitude, Inc., John Cutler, Ibrahim Bashir, 2024), Ch 1 — About the North Star Framework

## Summary

Inputs are the handful of leading-indicator factors that together produce the North Star Metric. They are equally important to the framework because product teams can move inputs more directly and more quickly than the top-level metric itself. Each input needs its own name and definition, and inputs should be independent enough that movements in one are not immediately felt in the others.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 1 — About the North Star Framework](../chapters/chapter-01-about-the-north-star-framework.md)** (PDF pp. 6–13, confidence 0.95):

  > North Star inputs are a small set of influential, complementary factors that you believe most directly affect your North Star Metric.

  > Inputs are the tools product and marketing teams deploy in their day to day work to influence the North Star. Inputs are independent variables.

- **From [Ch 3 — Get specific: defining your North Star](../chapters/chapter-03-get-specific-defining-your-north-star.md)** (PDF pp. 23–33, confidence 0.95):

  > Inputs are the handful of factors that, together, produce the North Star Metric. They are as important to the North Star Framework as your metric.

  > product teams are better served trying to move one component of a NSM that ladders up to the larger metric but is more of a leading indicator of impact.

## Ritsu relevance

For Ritsu's north star of '100 paying who love', defining input metrics (e.g., activation_rate, day_7_retention, magic_moment_completion_rate) is the direct next step; the framework clarifies these must be independently actionable by specific teams.
