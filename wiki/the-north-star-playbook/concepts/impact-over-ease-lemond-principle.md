# Impact Over Ease — The LeMond Principle

**Type:** concept · **From:** *The North Star Playbook* (Amplitude, Inc., John Cutler, Ibrahim Bashir, 2024), Ch 7 — Reap the benefits of the North Star Framework

## Summary

Adapting Greg LeMond's cycling maxim ('it never gets easier, you just go faster'), the chapter reframes product work: the goal is not to make the work easier but to make it more impactful. Teams should seek meaningful, hard work — the 'true work' — rather than comfort or reduced difficulty.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Reap the benefits of the North Star Framework](../chapters/chapter-07-reap-the-benefits-of-the-north-star-framework.md)** (PDF pp. 59–63, confidence 0.95):

  > We shouldn't expect product work to get easier, but we can find ways to make it better, to focus on meaningful, impactful hard work—the true work.

  > To reframe LeMond: Product work never gets easier, you'll just have more impact—and maybe a bit more sanity, flow, and satisfaction.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's product philosophy (active mastery over passive consumption) mirrors this: the platform does not make studying easier — it makes studying more effective. The LeMond principle validates Ritsu's 'desirable product' positioning over 'pleasing product'.
