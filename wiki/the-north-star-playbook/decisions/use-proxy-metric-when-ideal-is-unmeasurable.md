# Use a Proxy Metric When the Ideal Is Unmeasurable

**Type:** decision · **From:** *The North Star Playbook* (Amplitude, Inc., John Cutler, Ibrahim Bashir, 2024), Ch 2 — The North Star checklist and statement exercise

## Summary

When the most meaningful metric cannot be directly instrumented (e.g., 'customers silently reflecting on films'), teams should identify a measurable proxy that correlates with it (e.g., 'customers sharing insights in community discussions'). The chapter also cautions against prematurely eliminating metrics just because they aren't measurable with current tools.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — The North Star checklist and statement exercise](../chapters/chapter-02-the-north-star-checklist-and-statement-exerci.md)** (PDF pp. 14–22, confidence 0.95):

  > a better North Star Metric might be customers sharing insights in a community discussion—something that can be instrumented and tracked. In this example, the number of customers sharing their insights—a measurable metric—is a proxy metric for the number of customers pondering the films

  > don't eliminate ideas right out of the gate based on this factor—there will be plenty of time for that later.
