# Hubbard's Uncertainty Reduction Fact

**Type:** observation · **From:** *The North Star Playbook* (Amplitude, Inc., John Cutler, Ibrahim Bashir, 2024), Ch 4 — Troubleshooting: fixing issues and avoiding traps

## Summary

Douglas W. Hubbard's book How to Measure Anything is cited to counter the belief that unmeasurable metrics should be discarded. The key insight quoted: when uncertainty is high, very little data is needed to reduce it significantly; the need for large data sets only arises when you already know a lot. This rebuts the 'can we measure that?' trap.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 4 — Troubleshooting: fixing issues and avoiding traps](../chapters/chapter-04-troubleshooting-fixing-issues-and-avoiding-tr.md)** (PDF pp. 34–42, confidence 0.95):

  > Myth: When you have a lot of uncertainty, you need a lot of data to tell you something useful.

  > Fact: If you have a lot of uncertainty now, you don't need much data to reduce uncertainty significantly.
