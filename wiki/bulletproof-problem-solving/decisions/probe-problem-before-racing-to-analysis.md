# Probe the Problem Before Racing to Analysis

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 10 — Becoming a Great Problem Solver

## Summary

The initial problem statement posed by stakeholders is often not the right question. The authors instruct problem solvers to take time upfront to probe boundaries, accuracy requirements, time frames, and forces acting on the problem — and to revise the problem statement iteratively via one-day answers as learning accumulates.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 10 — Becoming a Great Problem Solver](../chapters/chapter-10-becoming-a-great-problem-solver.md)** (PDF pp. 283–288, confidence 0.95):

  > Take the time to probe the problem carefully with the decision makers involved before racing off to conduct analysis.

  > Be ready to revise your problem statement as you learn, refining it iteratively with one-day answers.
