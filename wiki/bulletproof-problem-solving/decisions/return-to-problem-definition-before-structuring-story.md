# Return to the Problem Definition Before Structuring the Story

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story

## Summary

Before constructing the final pyramid narrative, revisit the original problem definition worksheet to confirm: what problem is being solved and how has it evolved, what success criteria did the decision maker set, and whether problem boundaries were honored (or consciously relaxed). This ensures the story answers the decision maker's actual question rather than the question the team ended up working on.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > The starting point for this is to return to your problem definition worksheet and remind yourself:

  > What are the key criteria for success that our decision maker (which may be yourself) set out in advance? It is important to reflect these explicitly in your story.
