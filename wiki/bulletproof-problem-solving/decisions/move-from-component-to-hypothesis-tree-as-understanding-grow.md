# Move from Component to Hypothesis Tree as Understanding Grows

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization

## Summary

Teams should start with simple factor or component trees when knowledge is limited, then iteratively evolve toward hypothesis trees and deductive logic trees as data and analysis deepen understanding. Vague labels are a signal to advance: they do not drive analysis or action. The transition is driven by the ability to state testable hypotheses, not by time elapsed.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization](../chapters/chapter-03-problem-disaggregation-and-prioritization.md)** (PDF pp. 79–116, confidence 0.95):

  > The only rule here is to move when you can from trees with general problem elements to trees that state clear hypotheses to test; vague labels do not drive analysis or action—which is the whole point.

  > Start with simple factor or component trees when you are just starting out and don't know much; later, work toward more complete deductive logic trees and decision trees.
