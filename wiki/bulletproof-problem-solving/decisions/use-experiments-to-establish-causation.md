# Use Experiments (Constructed or Natural) to Establish Causation

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

The chapter prescribes reaching for experiments — either randomised controlled trials or natural experiments — whenever the goal is causal inference rather than correlation description. Experiments are described as 'a powerful and often overlooked tool in the big gun arsenal.' If a constructed experiment is infeasible due to ethics, cost, or timing, analysts should actively search for natural experiments where the world has already created a quasi-experimental structure.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-06-big-guns-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 165–208, confidence 0.95):

  > Experiments are a powerful and often overlooked tool in the big gun arsenal; if you can't make one, sometimes you can find a natural experiment.
