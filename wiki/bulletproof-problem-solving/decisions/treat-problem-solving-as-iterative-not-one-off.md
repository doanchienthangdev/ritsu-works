# Treat Problem Solving as Iterative, Not One-Off

**Type:** decision · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 0 — Introduction: Problem Solving for the Challenges of the Twenty-First Century

## Summary

The seventh pitfall the chapter names is treating problem solving as a linear, one-time process. The authors argue that problems have a 'messiness' that requires cycling back and forth between hypotheses, analysis, and conclusions, each pass deepening understanding. Second and third iterations of issue trees are normal and worthwhile, not signs of failure. This iterative posture is presented as essential to the bulletproof method.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 0 — Introduction: Problem Solving for the Challenges of the Twenty-First Century](../chapters/chapter-00-introduction-problem-solving-for-the-challeng.md)** (PDF pp. 15–30, confidence 0.95):

  > Rarely is a problem solved once and for all. Problems we will discuss often have a messiness about them that takes you back and forth between hypotheses, analysis, and conclusions, each time deepening your understanding.

  > it is okay and worthwhile to have second and third iterations of issue trees as your understanding of a problem changes.
