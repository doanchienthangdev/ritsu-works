# Food Company Sunk-Cost Exit Failure

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 0 — Introduction: Problem Solving for the Challenges of the Twenty-First Century

## Summary

A food products company declined an offer to exit a loss-making business after incurring $125 million in losses because management would only accept recovery of accounting book value. Driven by loss aversion and groupthink among managers with similar backgrounds, they rejected the offer. Several years later they finally exited with losses exceeding $500 million. The case illustrates how sunk-cost bias and groupthink among homogeneous teams can compound financial damage dramatically.
