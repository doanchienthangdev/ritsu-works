# Pacific Salmon Problem Statement Evolution

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 2 — Define the Problem

## Summary

The Gordon & Betty Moore Foundation's salmon conservation team iteratively refined its problem statement from a vague preservation goal to one centered on stock diversity and sufficient populations to utilize available ocean-carrying capacity. The evolution was driven by deeper engagement with scientists, tribal groups, fishing organizations, and conservationists. The case demonstrates how measurability constraints (salmon are harder to count than assumed) force creative re-framing of the outcome metric.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — Define the Problem](../chapters/chapter-02-define-the-problem.md)** (PDF pp. 61–78, confidence 0.95):

  > Most importantly, the team added more emphasis on maintaining salmon stock diversity and full functioning of all the elements of the ecosystem.

  > One of the reasons the foundation selected saving Pacific salmon as one of its first granting programs was the apparent fit with the foundation's filter of measurability, a deeply held core value. After all, you can count fish, right? You can see this coming: It turns out it is a little harder than it looks.
