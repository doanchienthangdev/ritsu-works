# Newspaper Industry Classified Advertising Blind Spot

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 2 — Define the Problem

## Summary

In the 1990s newspaper executives wrongly defined their competitive threat from the Internet as a content-quality battle, when the real attack came from disruption of classified advertising (autos, real estate, employment, personals). Because their problem boundary excluded advertising as the contestable territory, they could not see the threat. The chapter uses this as a direct illustration of how incorrect problem boundary definition creates strategic blindness.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — Define the Problem](../chapters/chapter-02-define-the-problem.md)** (PDF pp. 61–78, confidence 0.95):

  > These managers believed that the problem statement around new competition was: 'Do the new Internet entrants have better content (news and other writing) that would win over readers?'

  > The existing players literally cannot see the threat from new entrants because their mindset, their problem boundary definition, makes them blind to it.
