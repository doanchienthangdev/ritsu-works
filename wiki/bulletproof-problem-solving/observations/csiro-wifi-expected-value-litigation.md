# CSIRO WiFi Patent Litigation — Expected Value Break-Even

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Australia's CSIRO used a backward expected-value calculation to decide whether to defend its WiFi intellectual property in court. With estimated legal costs of $10m and a potential win of $100m, the break-even probability of success was 10%. Because the board judged their actual chances higher than 10%, they proceeded—an example of working backward from an indifference probability rather than forward from a point estimate.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > It worked backward to the break-even probability of success, given its estimates of the costs of court action ($10m) and what they would receive if they prevailed ($100m).

  > The decision was made to pursue action in the courts because the board felt the chances of success were greater than an indifference probability of 10%
