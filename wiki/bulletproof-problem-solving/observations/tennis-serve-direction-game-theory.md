# Tennis Serve Direction — Nalebuff/Dixit Payoff Matrix

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

Barry Nalebuff and Avinash Dixit applied game theory to tennis serving direction, constructing a payoff matrix across four scenarios (serve to forehand/backhand × opponent anticipates/does not anticipate). Their recommendation: serve unpredictably with 40% to forehand and 60% to backhand. Hawkeye data from the 2012 Olympics final showed Murray served with more spatial variation on clutch points than Federer, directing 7 of 8 serves to Federer's backhand — consistent with the theory.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-06-big-guns-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 165–208, confidence 0.95):

  > The recommendations are to remove predictability by making each serve appear random, and to place your serves to your opponent's forehand 40% of the time and to their backhand 60% of the time.

  > Murray served with more spatial variation (less predictability) on clutch points than Federer.
