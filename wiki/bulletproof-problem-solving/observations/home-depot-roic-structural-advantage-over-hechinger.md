# Home Depot's ROIC Structural Advantage Over Hechinger

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization

## Summary

A ROIC tree analysis in the mid-1980s revealed that Home Depot and Hechinger had similar returns on capital but radically different drivers. Home Depot had 15–18% lower prices, 5–7% lower logistics costs, achieved via 20-foot warehouse racks on the retail floor (eliminating backroom labor), direct supplier drop-shipping, and cross-docking. This structural model let Home Depot grow far faster, and by 1997 Hechinger had failed.

- **From [Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization](../chapters/chapter-03-problem-disaggregation-and-prioritization.md)** (PDF pp. 79–116, confidence 0.95):

  > Charles and his team set to work with a comparative analysis of Hechinger and Home Depot and were startled by what they found: What appeared to be similar businesses externally were radically different internally.

  > Home Depot was able to operate with 5–7% less shipping and logistics costs than Hechinger, and negotiate as much as 5% lower costs for the same goods by concentrating volume on orders of fewer retail items.
