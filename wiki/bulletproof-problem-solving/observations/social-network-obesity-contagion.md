# Social Network Obesity Contagion (Christakis & Fowler)

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 9 — Wicked Problems

## Summary

A large-scale US study found obesity spread in quantifiable social network patterns over 32 years: one person becoming obese raised the likelihood of a spouse becoming obese by 37%, siblings by 40%, and friends by 57%. The authors concluded that health improvements in one individual could spread to others, making social-network-based interventions potentially more cost-effective than traditional individual-level approaches.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 9 — Wicked Problems](../chapters/chapter-09-wicked-problems.md)** (PDF pp. 265–282, confidence 0.95):

  > "Obesity may spread in social networks in a quantifiable and discernible pattern that depends on the nature of social ties."

  > "medical and public health interventions might be more cost effective than initially supposed, since health improvements in one might spread to others."
