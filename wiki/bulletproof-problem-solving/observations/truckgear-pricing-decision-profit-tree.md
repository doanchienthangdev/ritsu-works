# Truckgear Start-up Pricing Decision via Profit Lever Tree

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 1 — Learn the Bulletproof Problem Solving Approach

## Summary

A start-up accessory company (Truckgear, selling ~10,000 truck accessories at $550/unit) used a profit lever tree to evaluate whether to raise prices after costs increased. A 7% price increase would yield $385,000 in additional cash profitability but only 650 units of lost sales would neutralize the benefit. Customer survey showed the largest segments were not price-sensitive; competitors were comparably priced; and fixed cost reduction was not feasible. The company proceeded with the modest price increase.

- **From [Ch 1 — Learn the Bulletproof Problem Solving Approach](../chapters/chapter-01-learn-the-bulletproof-problem-solving-approac.md)** (PDF pp. 31–60, confidence 0.95):

  > If the company could hold its current unit sales, a price increase of 7% would yield a $385,000 improvement in cash profitability

  > it would only take a drop in unit sales of 650 to neutralize the benefit of the price increase
