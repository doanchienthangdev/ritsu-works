# Early Hypothesis Writing on the Flight to Calgary Drives Focused Data Gathering

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story

## Summary

A senior McKinsey partner wrote the final presentation on the plane to the first client meeting — before any data was collected — using early-stage hypotheses to anticipate what questions to ask. The authors present this as evidence that guessing answers early, even imperfectly, disciplines and focuses data gathering from the very first client interaction.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > He was furiously scribbling on a pad of paper and ripping off the sheets.

  > Look, I know my hypotheses are early stage and some will be wrong, but if I guess at the answers now, I'll know what questions to ask the client when we meet their team.

  > Imagining a set of hypotheses allowed the team to begin focused data gathering from the first meeting.
