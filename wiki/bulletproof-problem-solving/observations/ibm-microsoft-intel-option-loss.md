# IBM's Failure to Exercise Low-Cost Options in Microsoft and Intel

**Type:** observation · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 8 — Problem Solving with Long Time Frames and High Uncertainty

## Summary

IBM had the financial resources to take large equity positions in both Intel and Microsoft in the 1980s. It took a 20% stake in Intel in 1982 for $625 million but sold out by 1987; ten years later that stake would have been worth $25 billion. IBM had an opportunity to buy 30% of Microsoft for under $300 million in mid-1986; by end of 1996 that stake would have been worth $33 billion. This case illustrates catastrophic failure to maintain low-cost strategic options during a period of disruptive technology.
