# Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) Tree

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization

## Summary

A specialized deductive logic tree that disaggregates a company's return on invested capital into its mathematical drivers: profit margin (return on sales) and capital turnover (sales to invested capital ratio). It is particularly powerful for comparing competitors and understanding how different business models generate equivalent or divergent financial results. The chapter calls it the canonical starting tree for business problems.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization](../chapters/chapter-03-problem-disaggregation-and-prioritization.md)** (PDF pp. 79–116, confidence 0.95):

  > Return on invested capital (ROIC) signals how well a company can turn investments into profit. Arithmetically it is the product of two components: profit margin (sometimes called return on sales) and capital turnover (the ratio of sales to invested capital).

  > For business problems it almost always makes sense to start with a profit lever or return on capital tree.
