# Pyramid Structure

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story

## Summary

The pyramid is the canonical organizing structure for presenting a problem-solving conclusion: governing statement at the top (usually the SOR statement), supported by major arguments at the second tier, with data, interviews, and analysis forming the proof layer below. The chapter traces it to Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle and frames it as a logic tree rotated on its side. It forces every supporting element to be explicitly tied to the governing thought.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > The visual structure we use to represent our story structure is traditionally a pyramid—which is really just one of our logic trees turned on its side.

  > This visual helps show clearly how each element of our argument is supported by data, interviews, and analyses.

  > The pyramid structure helps to structure arguments and support into a powerful story.
