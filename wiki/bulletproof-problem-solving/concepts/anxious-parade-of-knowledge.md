# Anxious Parade of Knowledge (APK)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story

## Summary

APK is the McKinsey term for the failure mode where a team completes analysis but presents detailed findings that never cohere into a real argument or recommendation. It results from reams of data without a clear point of view, unresolved team disagreements, or incomplete models. The authors cite it as the primary risk at the synthesis stage of problem solving.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > As young consultants, we dreaded the nervous presentation of detailed results that never jelled into a real story; in McKinsey this was called the anxious parade of knowledge, or APK.

  > To avoid the dreaded anxious parade of knowledge, use your logic tree structure pyramid to organize a compelling story.
