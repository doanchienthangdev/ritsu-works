# Machine Learning for Prediction

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

Machine learning is positioned as the tool of choice when the goal is accurate prediction rather than causal understanding, when data is large and complex, and when traditional regression struggles with non-linear interactions. Unlike regression, ML algorithms often select relevant feature combinations automatically. The chapter cautions that ML requires large, representative datasets, offers limited interpretability, and embeds any biases present in the training data. Framing the problem and hypotheses first — before applying ML — is emphasised as essential.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-06-big-guns-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 165–208, confidence 0.95):

  > Machine learning techniques are at heart very similar in concept to traditional statistical tools like multiple regression. They often perform much better than these traditional techniques when there is a large amount of data in the right format, and when there are more complex patterns and interactions in the data.

  > machine learning is predicting an outcome, not understanding the solution or how a decision is being made.
