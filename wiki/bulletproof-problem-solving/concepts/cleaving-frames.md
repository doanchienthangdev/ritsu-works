# Cleaving Frames

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization

## Summary

Cleaving frames are pre-existing theoretical constructs or frameworks applied as lenses to quickly and elegantly disaggregate a problem into insightful parts. Expert problem solvers maintain toolkits of such frames and try several to find the one that yields the most insight. Examples span business (price/volume, principal/agent, assets/options, collaborate/compete), policy (regulate/incent, equality/liberty, mitigate/adapt, supply/demand), and personal (work/play, near term/long term) levels.

- **From [Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization](../chapters/chapter-03-problem-disaggregation-and-prioritization.md)** (PDF pp. 79–116, confidence 0.95):

  > Expert practitioners of problem solving often employ existing frameworks or theories to more quickly and elegantly cleave problems into insightful parts.

  > Good problem solvers have toolkits like this that act as lenses to visualize potential solutions. They try on one or more theoretical frames to see which one is likely to be the best fit for the problem at hand.

  > Different frames often yield different insights, so it makes sense to test several.
