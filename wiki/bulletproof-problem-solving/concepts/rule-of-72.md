# Rule of 72

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

The Rule of 72 is a mental-math heuristic for estimating compound growth: divide 72 by the annual growth rate to find how many years it takes for an amount to double. For example, at 5% growth an investment doubles in about 14 years; at 15%, in about five years. The rule breaks down when the growth rate itself changes over the period, which is common in long-horizon forecasting.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > The rule of 72 allows you to estimate how long it takes for an amount to double given its growth rate by dividing 72 by the rate of growth.

  > Where do errors occur with the rule of 72? When there is a change in the growth rate, which of course is often the case over longer periods.
