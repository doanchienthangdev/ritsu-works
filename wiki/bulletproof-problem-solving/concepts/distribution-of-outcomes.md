# Distribution of Outcomes

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Analyzing the full distribution of possible outcomes—not just the mean or base case—is critical to realistic planning. The chapter cites data showing that 90% of infrastructure megaprojects exceeding $1 billion go over budget by 20% for roads and 45% for rail, with true expected overruns near 30%—far beyond the 10% contingency typically applied. The error of anchoring on the mean and under-weighting tails is framed as one of the most common analytic failures.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > We see many common errors that relate to the distribution of outcomes. These include placing too much emphasis on the mean outcome, typically called the base case, and insufficient weight on outcomes that are one or even two standard deviations from the mean.
