# Five-Level Uncertainty Schema

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 8 — Problem Solving with Long Time Frames and High Uncertainty

## Summary

A framework from former McKinsey colleagues that classifies uncertainty into five levels: Level 1 (predictable), Level 2 (binary alternative futures), Level 3 (range of possible futures), Level 4 (true ambiguity — outcomes unforeseeable), and Level 5 (unknown unknowns). Each level implies different problem-solving responses. Levels 1–4 are 'known unknowns'; Level 5 is nearly absolute uncertainty.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 8 — Problem Solving with Long Time Frames and High Uncertainty](../chapters/chapter-08-problem-solving-with-long-time-frames-and-hig.md)** (PDF pp. 225–264, confidence 0.95):

  > uncertainty levels range from level 1, what is a reasonably predictable future, to level 5, the unknown unknowns

  > Level 4 describes true ambiguity, where it is not possible to conceive of all the potential outcomes or to predict them with confidence.

  > Uncertainty levels 1 through 4 are colorfully called "the known unknowns," in contrast to level 5.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's early-stage strategy decisions (pricing experiments, PMF instrumentation, market channel selection) operate at Level 2–3 uncertainty. The schema helps the AI workforce correctly classify which decisions require hedges versus no-regrets moves in `/cla` problem framing.
