# Highest-Level Problem Solving

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 2 — Define the Problem

## Summary

The principle of targeting problem-solving efforts at the broadest organizational level possible rather than optimizing for a sub-unit or partial solution. The chapter illustrates this with a steel company case where the true problem was free cash flow generation, not evaluation of a capital budget, and with the Pacific salmon case where a north-Pacific scale was required because salmon migrate across regional boundaries.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — Define the Problem](../chapters/chapter-02-define-the-problem.md)** (PDF pp. 61–78, confidence 0.95):

  > One of the other important principles in problem definition is to solve the problem at the highest level possible. The insight here is that the most granular and local solutions are often not optimal for the larger organization.
