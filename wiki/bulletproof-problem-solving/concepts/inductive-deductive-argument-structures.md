# Inductive and Deductive Argument Structures

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story

## Summary

Two complementary logical modes for filling in the pyramid's supporting layers. Deductive structures proceed from general principles to specific cases; inductive structures build from observed individual cases to general conclusions. The chapter notes both can coexist in the same pyramid, and Minto's caution that over-reliance on deductive chains can feel labored or pedantic.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > our basic deductive logic (from general principles to individual cases) and inductive logic (from individual observation to general conclusions) approaches to generating answers introduced back in Chapter 3 underpin these two structures.

  > There is no general preference for inductive or deductive reasoning in your pyramid structure, though Minto argues that constant reference to deductive arguments can feel labored or pedantic.
