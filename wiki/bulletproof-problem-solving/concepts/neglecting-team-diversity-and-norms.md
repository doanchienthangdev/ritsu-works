# Neglecting Team Diversity and Cognitive Norms

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 0 — Introduction: Problem Solving for the Challenges of the Twenty-First Century

## Summary

The fourth pitfall is failing to structure teams for good problem solving. Effective teams require diversity of experience and divergent views, open-mindedness, and processes to reduce bias. Groupthink among managers with similar backgrounds and traditional hierarchy is identified as a common business problem that prevents teams from seeing alternatives clearly. The authors reference forecasting research (Tetlock and Gardner's Superforecasting) and McKinsey research on debiasing to support this.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 0 — Introduction: Problem Solving for the Challenges of the Twenty-First Century](../chapters/chapter-00-introduction-problem-solving-for-the-challeng.md)** (PDF pp. 15–30, confidence 0.9):

  > the importance of a diversity of experience and divergent views in the group, having people who are open-minded, a group dynamic that can be either competitive or collaborative, and training and team processes to reduce the impact of biases.

  > Groupthink amongst a team of managers with similar backgrounds and traditional hierarchy made it hard for them see the real alternatives clearly
