# One-Day Answer

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 1 — Learn the Bulletproof Problem Solving Approach

## Summary

A one-day answer is an iterative, running best-guess summary of the problem solution, structured around situation–observation–resolution (SOR). It is kept continuously updated as analyses are completed and serves as the seed for the final governing statement of the pyramid. The authors describe it as critical for avoiding analysis paralysis and driving focused data gathering from day one.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 1 — Learn the Bulletproof Problem Solving Approach](../chapters/chapter-01-learn-the-bulletproof-problem-solving-approac.md)** (PDF pp. 31–60, confidence 0.95):

  > We often use the expression, "What's the one-day answer?" This means we ask our team to have a coherent summary of our best understanding of the problem and a solution path at any point in the project, not just at the end.

  > This process of creating active hypotheses is at the heart of Bulletproof Problem Solving.

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > We hypothesize a one-day answer using a situation-observation-resolution structure and we constantly pressure test this with our analyses under the structure of our workplan and team processes.
