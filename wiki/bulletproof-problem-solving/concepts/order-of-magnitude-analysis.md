# Order of Magnitude Analysis

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Order of magnitude analysis sizes different levers of a problem—estimating minimum, most likely, and maximum values—to determine where analytical effort should focus. In business, this typically means calculating the value of a 10% improvement in price, cost, or volume. It serves as the primary knock-out tool to identify which levers matter most before deeper modeling.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > Order of magnitude analysis is used to prioritize team efforts by estimating the size of different levers.

  > Doing an order of magnitude analysis should provide a minimum, most likely, and maximum estimate, not simply the maximum.
