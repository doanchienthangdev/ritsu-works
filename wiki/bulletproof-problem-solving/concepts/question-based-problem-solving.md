# Question-Based Problem Solving (Sherlock Holmes Framework)

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Question-based problem solving uses structured sequences of who/what/where/when/how/why questions to rapidly narrow root causes and reach rough-cut decisions without large-scale data collection. The chapter demonstrates that three well-chosen questions can resolve complex classification problems—illustrated by a hospital heart-attack triage decision tree (systolic pressure, age, sinus tachycardia) and Rob's knee surgery decision. The approach sits between heuristic sizing and full statistical modeling.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > We have found that the Sherlock Holmes approach of painting a picture of the problem by asking who, what, where, when, how, and why is a powerful root-cause tool to quickly focus problem solving.

  > we can often get to rough-cut solutions in as few as three questions.
