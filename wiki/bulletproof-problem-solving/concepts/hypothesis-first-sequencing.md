# Hypothesis-First Sequencing Before Advanced Analysis

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

The chapter argues that analysts should always sequence their thinking — starting with clear problem framing and initial hypotheses, then summary statistics and data visualization — before escalating to advanced tools. Jumping directly to machine learning or regression without this first-cut phase risks 'boiling the ocean': data-fishing expeditions that do not illuminate root causes. Oxford professor Stephen Roberts is cited as endorsing this scientific-method discipline.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-06-big-guns-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 165–208, confidence 0.95):

  > First-cut data analysis often points to direction of causality and size of impact, which are critical to evaluating the results of complex models later.

  > He advises students not to commence machine-learning analysis until such time as they have developed a clear idea of the structure of the model and testable hypotheses, the essence of the scientific method.
