# Staircase Architecture Dimensions

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 8 — Problem Solving with Long Time Frames and High Uncertainty

## Summary

Three criteria that govern how staircase steps are designed and sequenced: Stretch (how far the step extends beyond the current platform — balancing absorption capacity against competitive speed), Momentum (leveraging early wins to build organizational confidence, especially critical in winner-take-all markets), and Flexibility (picking moves that maximize option value for follow-on steps while avoiding stranded sunk assets, e.g., via variable cost structures like outsourcing).

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 8 — Problem Solving with Long Time Frames and High Uncertainty](../chapters/chapter-08-problem-solving-with-long-time-frames-and-hig.md)** (PDF pp. 225–264, confidence 0.95):

  > Stretch: The distance of the step-out from the established ­platform, the complexity of new capabilities targeted by a step-out

  > Momentum: The positive effect of early success, often based on small moves, on the learning and confidence of the organization.

  > Flexibility: Maintaining fluidity in the face of uncertainty has several features, including picking step-outs that generate the greatest option value for follow-on initiatives
