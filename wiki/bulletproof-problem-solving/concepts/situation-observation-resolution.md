# Situation–Observation–Resolution (SOR) Structure

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story

## Summary

SOR is the three-part narrative frame used to open and govern the problem-solving story. Situation establishes context, observation (or complication) names the tension or what isn't working, and resolution states the recommended action path. The chapter notes audience-dependent ordering: lead with resolution ('What should I do?') when the audience is receptive, or build from situation to resolution when the audience is resistant.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 7 — Synthesize Results and Tell a Great Story](../chapters/chapter-07-synthesize-results-and-tell-a-great-story.md)** (PDF pp. 209–224, confidence 0.95):

  > our bias in most circumstances is to lead by answering the question 'What Should I Do?' and then summarize the situation and key observations that support action.
