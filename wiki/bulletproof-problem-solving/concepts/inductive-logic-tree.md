# Inductive Logic Tree

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization

## Summary

An inductive logic tree works from specific observations toward general principles, the reverse of deductive trees. It is used when the team has good case examples but lacks established general principles. Inductive trees show probabilistic rather than causal relationships. The chapter illustrates this with contested historical artifacts: working backwards from how people assess specific individuals to derive principles.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 3 — Problem Disaggregation and Prioritization](../chapters/chapter-03-problem-disaggregation-and-prioritization.md)** (PDF pp. 79–116, confidence 0.95):

  > We employ inductive logic trees when we do not yet know much about the general principles behind the problems we are interested in, but we do have some data or insights into specific cases.

  > Inductive trees show probabilistic relationships, not causal ones: Just because all the swans you have seen are white, does not mean all swans are white.
