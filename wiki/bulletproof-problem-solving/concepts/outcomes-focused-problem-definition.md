# Outcomes-Focused Problem Definition

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 2 — Define the Problem

## Summary

The principle that a good problem statement is expressed in terms of desired outcomes, not activities or intermediate outputs. The chapter lists this as the first characteristic of a good problem statement and returns to it in the SMART discussion, noting that activity-framed problems often misalign effort from what decision makers actually need.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 2 — Define the Problem](../chapters/chapter-02-define-the-problem.md)** (PDF pp. 61–78, confidence 0.95):

  > Outcomes focused: A clear statement of the problem to be solved, expressed in outcomes, not activities or intermediate outputs.

## Ritsu relevance

Ritsu's north-star metric ('100 paying who love') is deliberately outcome-framed (mastery, retention, love) rather than activity-framed (sessions, uploads) — the same discipline the chapter recommends.
