# Expected Value Calculation

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Expected value is the value of an outcome multiplied by its probability. The chapter introduces both the single-point form (one outcome × its probability) and the fuller form summing all possible outcomes weighted by their probabilities. It is most reliable when the outcome distribution is approximately normal; it misleads when distributions are skewed or long-tailed. A practical variant is working backward to the break-even probability, as CSIRO did in its WiFi patent litigation.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > Expected value is simply the value of an outcome multiplied by its probability of occurring.

  > Single point expected value calculations are most useful when the underlying distribution is normal rather than skewed or long tailed.
