# Marginal Analysis

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Marginal analysis examines the cost or benefit of producing, consuming, or investing in one additional unit rather than looking only at total or average costs. In production, marginal costs often fall rapidly with volume until a step-fixed-cost inflection point is reached. The chapter illustrates this with a hotel scenario where accepting a $30 room offer makes sense even when average cost is $50, because marginal cost is near zero; and with a gym-subsidy case where the decision hinges on the percentage of employees already paying their own way.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > Marginal analysis involves examining the cost or benefit of the next unit.

  > We add units until the marginal benefit of a unit sale is equal to the marginal cost.
