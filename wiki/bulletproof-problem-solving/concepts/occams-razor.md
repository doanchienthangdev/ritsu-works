# Occam's Razor

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses

## Summary

Occam's Razor is the oldest heuristic in the toolkit: favor the simplest hypothesis that fits the facts, selecting the explanation with the fewest assumptions. The chapter illustrates this with a probability argument—four independent 80%-correct assumptions compound to only ~40% joint probability, while two assumptions yield 64%. The principle guards against over-complex explanations but must be relaxed when evidence demands nuance.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 5 — Conduct Analyses](../chapters/chapter-05-conduct-analyses.md)** (PDF pp. 141–164, confidence 0.95):

  > Occam's Razor—favor the simplest solution that fits the facts—which originated in the fourteenth century. It tells us to select the hypothesis that has the fewest assumptions.

  > The important reminder is not to get committed to a simple answer with few assumptions when the facts and evidence are pointing to a more nuanced or complex answer
