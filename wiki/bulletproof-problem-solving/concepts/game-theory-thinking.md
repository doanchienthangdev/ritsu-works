# Game Theory Thinking

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

Game theory is the framework for strategic problems where an actor's payoffs depend on the choices of adversaries or competitors. The chapter describes using payoff matrices and decision trees to map moves and counter-moves, and running 'attack vs defence' workshops covering 18 months of potential strategic sequences. Key constructs include minmax (maximise minimum gain) and maxmin (minimise maximum loss). It is the right tool when competitor reactions to your moves materially shape outcomes.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis](../chapters/chapter-06-big-guns-of-analysis.md)** (PDF pp. 165–208, confidence 0.95):

  > We employ game theory thinking to work through our own choices and competitor choices, set out in a logic tree.

  > Game theorists use terms like minmax and maxmin to describe strategies where you choose an outcome that maximizes your minimum gain or conversely minimizes your maximum loss.
