# Synthesis and Storytelling

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 10 — Becoming a Great Problem Solver

## Summary

The chapter emphasizes that completing a powerful analysis is not the same as solving a problem. Because most real-world problem solving requires persuading stakeholders to act differently, synthesis — turning findings into a compelling narrative — deserves as much effort as the analysis itself. Humans are visual learners who respond to storytelling.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 10 — Becoming a Great Problem Solver](../chapters/chapter-10-becoming-a-great-problem-solver.md)** (PDF pp. 283–288, confidence 0.95):

  > Put as much effort into synthesis and telling the story as doing the analysis.

  > most of the problem solving you will ever do in business, non-profits, or politics involves persuading someone to do something differently.

  > humans are visual learners and love storytelling above all else.
