# Five Core Cognitive Biases in Problem Solving

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 4 — Build a Great Workplan and Team Processes

## Summary

The chapter (citing Kahneman, Lovallo, and behavioral economics) identifies five biases as the most important to address: confirmation bias (falling in love with your hypothesis), anchoring bias (attachment to an initial data range), loss aversion and sunk-cost fallacy, availability bias (using the nearest mental map rather than the right one), and overoptimism. Most other described biases are forms of these five.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 4 — Build a Great Workplan and Team Processes](../chapters/chapter-04-build-a-great-workplan-and-team-processes.md)** (PDF pp. 117–140, confidence 0.95):

  > Confirmation bias is falling in love with your one-day answer. It is the failure to seriously consider the antithesis to your thesis, ignoring dissenting views.

  > Availability bias is use of an existing mental map because it is readily at hand, rather than developing a new model for a new problem.
