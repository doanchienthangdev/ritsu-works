# Situation-Complication-Resolution Structure

**Type:** concept · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 4 — Build a Great Workplan and Team Processes

## Summary

The classic McKinsey one-day-answer structure organizes problem-solving output into three ordered parts: situation (current state of affairs), complication (what changed or went wrong to create the problem), and resolution (best current answer to 'what should we do?'). Later, as findings are synthesized, the order may be reversed to lead with the recommendation.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 4 — Build a Great Workplan and Team Processes](../chapters/chapter-04-build-a-great-workplan-and-team-processes.md)** (PDF pp. 117–140, confidence 0.95):

  > 1. A short description of the situation that prevails at the outset of problem solving.

  > 2. A set of observations or complications around the situation that creates the tension or dynamic that captures the problem.

  > 3. The best idea of the implication or resolution of the problem that you have right now.
