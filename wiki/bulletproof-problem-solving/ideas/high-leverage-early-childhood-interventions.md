# High-Leverage Early Childhood Interventions

**Type:** idea · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 9 — Wicked Problems

## Summary

The chapter surfaces early childhood and prenatal interventions as unusually high-leverage because weight trajectories compound across decades. Infants with rapid weight gain (ages 0–2) are four times more likely to be obese later; 75–80% of obese adolescents become obese adults. Simple, low-cost interventions (nutrition advice, breastfeeding promotion, monthly weight monitoring) at the earliest stage may prevent enormous downstream costs — a principle applicable whenever a problem has compounding causal chains.

## Raw quotes (verbatim, for citation)

- **From [Ch 9 — Wicked Problems](../chapters/chapter-09-wicked-problems.md)** (PDF pp. 265–282, confidence 0.8):

  > infants who experienced rapid weight gain from zero to two years were almost four times more likely to be overweight or obese later in life.

  > There may be extraordinary leverage that can come from simple, straightforward actions to address the issue of child obesity and its knock-on effects.
