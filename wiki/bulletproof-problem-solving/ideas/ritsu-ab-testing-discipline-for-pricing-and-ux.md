# Apply Systematic A/B Testing Discipline to Ritsu Pricing and UX

**Type:** idea · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

The EA SimCity finding — that removing a promotional banner increased purchases by 43% — is a sharp reminder that promotion-first thinking can backfire. Ritsu's current pricing-pull-test (SOP-PRODUCT-010) and free-to-paid trigger detection could benefit from a formal A/B testing decision tree (like the chapter's Exhibit 6.1) to guide when to run an experiment versus when heuristic pricing judgments are sufficient. The chapter's crowdsourcing decision tree (Exhibit 6.10) could also inform whether to use internal experiments or a Kaggle-style external competition for harder prediction problems such as churn forecasting.
