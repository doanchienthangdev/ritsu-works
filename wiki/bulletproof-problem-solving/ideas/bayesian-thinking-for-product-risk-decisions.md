# Apply Bayesian Thinking to Product Risk Decisions at Ritsu

**Type:** idea · **From:** *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn, Robert McLean, 2018), Ch 6 — Big Guns of Analysis

## Summary

The Challenger case shows how excluding inconvenient data (all zero-failure flights) and failing to think conditionally led to catastrophic misjudgment. Ritsu faces analogous risks: early product decisions made on sparse data from self-selected early users (like Challenger engineers looking only at damage-incident flights). A Bayesian updating discipline — explicitly stating the prior, identifying the evidence, and computing a posterior — could prevent premature conclusions from thin N=10 or N=30 cohorts. This is especially relevant for the SOP-PRODUCT-002 'N=10 strangers' gate and the SOP-METRICS-004 Very Disappointed tracking.
