# Positioning (BRAVO Framework) - Critical Questions
## Module 1 of the Exposure Ninja 5-Star Marketing System
### Ritsu-Specific Questions for Deep Understanding

---

## CONTEXT: Understanding Ritsu

Ritsu is an AI learning platform that transforms raw course materials (textbooks, PDFs, lectures) into interactive adaptive learning activities. It serves university students overwhelmed by dense course materials, using 85+ commands and 3,854+ tests. Ritsu's GTM strategy is university pilot launches leading to freemium public launch. The platform offers a CLI input interface (commands like /quiz, /explain) with visual/interactive output. Core value proposition: deep understanding, not memorization. Differentiation from ChatGPT (manual copy-paste), Anki/Quizlet (manual cards), and fragmented tool combinations. Key growth mechanism: share links for viral spreading. Target: price-sensitive university students.

---

## BRAND (B) - Ritsu-Specific Questions 1-15

1. What visual elements, tone, and personality should Ritsu's brand convey to university students who are overwhelmed by dense course materials? (Consider: Does it feel like a helpful study partner? An authority? A accessible tool?)

2. Ritsu's competitors include established tools (Anki, Quizlet) and powerful but clunky solutions (ChatGPT + copy-paste). What visual consistency across the website, app, CLI, and social media would help Ritsu stand out as "the serious alternative"?

3. If Ritsu's brand were a person, what would they be like? Would they be a professor? A peer tutor? An AI mentor? How should this personality manifest in the CLI interface, error messages, and success feedback?

4. Ritsu's brand promise seems to be "transform any course material into deep understanding quickly." How should this promise be reflected visually and in tone across all student touchpoints?

5. Analyze the difference between Ritsu's brand and the brand of its competitors. What visual or tonal elements would make a student immediately recognize "this is Ritsu, not Quizlet"?

6. How should Ritsu's brand personality differ for different audiences? (e.g., students vs. university administrators vs. professors recommending tools). Can Ritsu have one consistent brand?

7. Why might a student's first interaction with Ritsu's CLI interface be more important for brand positioning than the landing page? How does the product experience become brand experience?

8. Ritsu's share link feature is a core growth mechanism. How should the brand feel when a student shares their quiz link with a peer? Should it feel like "I'm sharing a study resource" or "I'm inviting you to use Ritsu"?

9. What happens to Ritsu's brand perception if students experience one bad interaction (e.g., a quiz command fails, an explanation is incorrect)? How do these product moments affect brand?

10. Should Ritsu's brand positioning change if it expands beyond university students (e.g., to professionals doing certification study, or non-native language learners)? Why or why not?

11. How does the founder's reputation (Thang as a solo founder, builder, marketer) affect Ritsu's brand perception? Should Thang be visible in Ritsu's brand?

12. Ritsu's 85+ commands and 3,854+ tests represent immense depth. How should brand positioning communicate this depth without overwhelming students?

13. Consider brand promise for different course materials (STEM vs. humanities, intro vs. advanced, STEM textbooks vs. video lectures). Should Ritsu have one brand promise or tailored promises?

14. How should Ritsu's brand positioning handle the fact that it's powered by AI? Should the AI be front-and-center in the brand identity or invisible?

15. Create Ritsu's complete brand positioning statement, including: visual identity direction, brand personality, brand promise, and how these should be consistent across website, CLI, social media, and product experience.

## REASON FOR EXISTING (R) - Ritsu-Specific Questions 16-30

16. Beyond "building a successful ed-tech company," what is Ritsu's deeper reason for existing? Why does this platform need to exist in the world?

17. The problem Ritsu solves is students struggling with dense, passive course materials. But what's the deeper "why"? Is it about democratizing education? Making learning accessible? Transforming how education works?

18. How does Ritsu's reason for existing differ from competitors? Why is Ritsu's mission different from Quizlet's (memorization tools) or ChatGPT's (general AI assistant)?

19. What would a compelling origin story for Ritsu look like? What problem did Thang (the founder) personally experience that led to building Ritsu?

20. How should Ritsu's "reason for existing" connect emotionally with university students? Why should students care about Ritsu's mission beyond just getting better grades?

21. If Ritsu's mission is "transform deep understanding through interactive learning," how does this differ from just "help students study better"? Why does this difference matter?

22. Should Ritsu's mission address educational equity (making quality learning accessible to all students, regardless of background)? How would this shape positioning?

23. How does Ritsu's reason for existing affect pricing decisions? Can Ritsu charge for premium features while maintaining a mission of educational access?

24. If Ritsu expands to professional development or language learning, does the core "reason for existing" still hold? Or does it need to evolve?

25. How should Ritsu communicate its "reason for existing" to different audiences (students, professors, university administrators)? Does each audience care about the same mission?

26. Ritsu's freemium model (university pilot then public launch) suggests a scaling mission. How does this affect the "reason for existing"? Is it about reach? Impact? Both?

27. How transparent should Ritsu be about its business model and monetization strategy within its reason for existing? Should students know this is a sustainable business?

28. If Ritsu's deeper mission is about transforming education, how should this manifest in product decisions? Should every feature align with this mission?

29. Create Ritsu's "reason for existing" statement. How is this different from "Ritsu is a platform that transforms course materials into interactive learning activities"?

30. How might Ritsu's reason for existing evolve after reaching PMF (product-market fit)? What happens to mission when you're no longer scrappy startup?

## AUDIENCE (A) - Ritsu-Specific Questions 31-50

31. "University students" is too broad. What specific type of university student is Ritsu's primary audience? (Consider: major, year, GPA, motivation, study habits, tech comfort)

32. Create a detailed customer avatar for Ritsu's primary user. Include: major/field, year in school, pain points with current study methods, desires, fears about exams, tech comfort, how they spend study time, where they get study resource recommendations.

33. Are there secondary audiences Ritsu should consider? (e.g., high school students preparing for AP exams, graduate students, professors recommending tools). How should positioning differ for these?

34. Why might a physics major and a history major need different positioning, even though they're both university students? Should Ritsu position differently to STEM vs. humanities students?

35. Ritsu's target audience is "price-sensitive university students." What does this mean for pricing positioning? Should Ritsu be free, freemium, or paid?

36. Where do university students spend time physically and digitally? How should understanding this (dorms, libraries, study groups, Reddit, TikTok, Discord) inform Ritsu's distribution?

37. What language do university students use when talking about studying? How should Ritsu's messaging align with student vocabulary vs. edu-tech industry vocabulary?

38. Analyze the difference between students who prefer passive study tools (reading notes, watching videos) and students who prefer active study. Should Ritsu position to one or both?

39. How might a student's relationship with their course affect Ritsu's appeal? (e.g., a student struggling in a required course vs. a student excited about their major). Should positioning differ?

40. Create multiple customer avatars for Ritsu. How should positioning strategy account for these different avatars?

41. How does understanding that students are "overwhelmed" by course materials affect product development and positioning? What specifically are they overwhelmed by?

42. Where do university students discover study tools? (professor recommendations, peer recommendations, app stores, social media, study group conversations). How should this affect Ritsu's positioning and distribution?

43. What are the objections students have to adopting new study tools? (time to learn, doubt about effectiveness, switching costs from current tools). How should positioning address these?

44. How does professor perception of Ritsu affect student adoption? Should Ritsu position differently to professors than students?

45. What role does social proof play in Ritsu's positioning? If one student uses Ritsu and loves it, will they recommend to peers? How should Ritsu facilitate this?

46. Analyze Ritsu's share link feature from an audience perspective. Why would a student share a quiz with a peer? What needs does sharing satisfy?

47. How might Ritsu's audience change from early pilots (motivated early adopters) to mainstream adoption (procrastinators, skeptics, less tech-savvy students)? Should positioning evolve?

48. Should Ritsu position against specific competitors (e.g., "better than ChatGPT + copy-paste") or position independently? Why?

49. How does a student's current study tool affect their perception of Ritsu? A student using ChatGPT might see Ritsu differently than a student using Anki.

50. What's the relationship between Ritsu's audience (price-sensitive students) and acquisition channel? What channels make sense for reaching this audience affordably?

## VOICE (V) - Ritsu-Specific Questions 51-65

51. Should Ritsu's voice be formal (like a textbook or professor) or casual (like a peer or friend)? What's the right tone for helping students with dense material?

52. Analyze the voice of Ritsu's competitors. How does Quizlet's tone differ from ChatGPT's? Where should Ritsu position on the formal-casual spectrum?

53. How should Ritsu's voice differ across channels? Should CLI output have a different tone than the website landing page? Why or why not?

54. If Ritsu uses humor or casual language, could this undermine the perception that Ritsu takes learning seriously? How do you balance approachability with authority?

55. Ritsu's error messages and failure states are opportunities for voice. Should an incorrect quiz answer be handled with gentle humor, compassionate guidance, or matter-of-fact correction?

56. How should Ritsu's voice acknowledge student stress and overwhelm without being patronizing? What tone conveys empathy without diminishing the learning challenge?

57. Should Ritsu's voice reflect Thang's personal voice (if Thang is visible in the brand)? Or should Ritsu develop a voice independent of the founder?

58. Analyze how command-line interfaces typically "speak" to users. How should Ritsu's CLI voice differ from technical tools like Git or AWS CLI?

59. How should Ritsu's voice change when communicating with professors or university administrators vs. students? Can these be different voices, or must it be consistent?

60. What does Ritsu's voice sound like when a student hasn't made progress on their weak areas? Encouraging? Challenging? Neutral? How does this affect student motivation?

61. Should Ritsu's voice use academic terminology or simpler language? How does this decision affect positioning as "serious learning platform"?

62. Create Ritsu's voice guide, including: tone (formal/casual), perspective (first/second/third person), vocabulary level, examples of what to say/not say, how voice differs across channels.

63. How should Ritsu's voice handle AI-generated content? Should it be transparent that an explanation is AI-generated? Does this affect trust?

64. How does voice consistency affect student trust in Ritsu's learning quality? If explanations sound inconsistent, does this hurt positioning?

65. What's the relationship between voice and student motivation? Can Ritsu's voice alone improve student engagement, or is this purely product?

## OFFERING (O) - Ritsu-Specific Questions 66-85

66. What exactly is Ritsu's offering? Is it the CLI commands? The interactive quizzes? The share feature? The complete experience? How should this be positioned?

67. Ritsu's core feature is transforming course materials into interactive learning activities. Should positioning focus on this feature or on the outcome (deeper understanding)?

68. What are the top 3-5 benefits students should experience from using Ritsu? (Consider: time savings, better grades, deeper understanding, confidence, less anxiety). How should positioning emphasize these?

69. Ritsu's 85+ commands represent power and flexibility. Is this a benefit (you can do anything) or a drawback (too many commands to learn)? How should positioning address this?

70. What is Ritsu's USP (Unique Selling Proposition)? How is it different from Quizlet, ChatGPT, Anki, and fragmented combinations? Define this clearly.

71. Is Ritsu's USP "AI-powered interactive learning" or something more specific (e.g., "transforms passive course materials into active learning instantly")? Which is more compelling?

72. How should Ritsu position its freemium model? Is free access a benefit (low barrier to entry) or a positioning risk (doesn't seem serious/professional)?

73. Ritsu's target audience is price-sensitive students. How should offering be positioned for this audience? Is free/cheap positioning a strength or weakness?

74. What outcomes should Ritsu promise? Better grades? Deeper understanding? Faster study? Confidence? Which should be primary positioning?

75. How should Ritsu position for different use cases? (cramming before exams vs. learning throughout semester, reviewing weak areas vs. entire course review). Is there one positioning or multiple?

76. Create offering positioning statement for Ritsu: What is it? Top 3 benefits? USP? Price positioning? Why students should choose Ritsu over alternatives?

77. How does Ritsu's AI-powered nature affect positioning? Should this be a selling point (cutting-edge) or de-emphasized (not about the tech, about the learning)?

78. What guarantee or promise should Ritsu make? (Better grades? Better understanding? Faster study? Satisfaction guarantee?). How should this be positioned?

79. Ritsu's multiple commands (/quiz, /explain, /summarize, etc.) could be positioned as depth or as complexity. How should Ritsu handle this?

80. How should Ritsu's offering positioning change for different courses or materials? Should positioning differ for organic chemistry vs. history?

81. What's the relationship between Ritsu's offering (interactive learning activities) and its reason for existing (deep understanding)? Are these aligned?

82. How should Ritsu position multi-student sharing and collaboration features? Is this a competitive advantage or just a nice-to-have?

83. How should Ritsu position the learning outcomes compared to current student study methods? What should the comparison highlight?

84. If Ritsu adds features (e.g., spaced repetition, peer collaboration, study groups), how should offering positioning evolve?

85. Create a comparison positioning piece: Ritsu vs. ChatGPT for studying, Ritsu vs. Quizlet for learning, Ritsu vs. manual note-taking. What are the specific advantages in each case?
