# Competitor & Audience Research — The Traffic Lasso (Locations)

## Overview
- Before spending money on traffic, know WHERE your customers are
- The Traffic Lasso framework: Locations, Ad channels, Search, Social, Optimization
- "Locations" = qualitative and quantitative research on where customers hang out and how competitors reach them
- This is the research foundation for all traffic strategies

## Qualitative Research: Competitor Analysis with Semrush

### Domain Overview Analysis
- Enter competitor URL into Semrush
- Review organic search traffic estimates (take with pinch of salt — estimated, not measured)
- Review paid search spending estimates
- Compare different country markets
- Choose desktop vs mobile analysis depending on the business

### Organic Search Analysis
- Check which keywords competitors rank for
- Note branded vs non-branded traffic split
- Identify their best-performing organic pages
- Look at their blog/content strategy through pages driving traffic
- Assess whether their organic rankings are intentional or accidental
- Example: Aura Ring — lots of branded traffic, weak non-branded SEO despite large site

### Backlink Analysis
- Check total backlinks and referring domains
- Order by authority score (representation of link value, scale 0-100)
- Identify link building strategies from top referring domains:
  - Affiliate links (e.g., podcast sponsorships)
  - Wikipedia citations
  - App store links
  - PR and media coverage (CNN, NYT, Bloomberg)
  - Digital PR campaigns using data/surveys
  - Partner and supplier links
- Analyze specific high-authority links to understand strategy
- Example: Aura Ring — 186,000 links from 11,000 domains, mostly from PR, podcasts, app stores
- Link building strategy deduction from competitor analysis

### Content Analysis
- Review main site content quality and quantity
- Check if designers have stripped content (common problem)
- Evaluate keyword usage in headings and copy
- Identify content gaps — topics competitors haven't covered well
- Blog content vs main page content analysis
- Example: Aura Ring — "Adapts and responds to you" heading wastes SEO opportunity; should use target keywords like "health tracking"

### Paid Search Analysis
- Review competitor's Google Ads keywords
- Identify branded vs non-branded spend split
- Evaluate ad copy quality (USPs, benefits, disqualification copy)
- Check landing page relevance for each keyword
- Analyze cost per click for different keywords
- Assess whether competitor's paid strategy is smart or wasteful
- Example: Aura Ring spending $125K/month, $85K on wedding rings (questionable), mostly branded traffic

### Social Media Analysis
- Check all social channels (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter/X, YouTube, TikTok)
- Evaluate posting frequency and consistency
- Assess engagement rates vs follower count
- Note content types (video, images, UGC, branded)
- Check brand consistency across channels
- Identify if they're posting native content or cross-posting same content everywhere
- YouTube: organic content vs ad uploads (short videos = usually ads)
- Facebook Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) — see ALL ads any business is running
  - How frequently updating ads
  - Split testing variants
  - Which channels (FB, Instagram, Messenger, Audience Network)
  - Oldest running ad = control that works = good to study/copy
  - Languages and geographic targeting
- Building a complete picture of competitor priorities

## Quantitative Research: Google Analytics & Audience Analysis

### Traffic Channel Analysis
- All traffic channels view: users, sessions, bounce rate, pages per session, conversion rate, goals
- Identify best performing traffic channels
- Compare organic, paid, social, direct, referral, display traffic
- Benchmark bounce rate (<55-60% typical target)
- Variance between channels reveals traffic quality issues
- Display traffic usually lowest quality (accidental clicks)
- If all channels have high bounce rate = website problem, not traffic problem

### Organic Traffic Deep Dive
- Create segment for organic traffic only
- Go to Behavior > Site Content > Landing Pages
- Compare homepage traffic (branded, high intent) vs blog traffic (informational, lower intent)
- Blog traffic typically has higher bounce rate, lower conversion — that's expected
- Compare individual blog performance to find what resonates
- High traffic + low conversion = opportunity to improve page
- High conversion + low traffic = opportunity to promote more

### Geographic Analysis
- Locations report in Google Analytics (country and city level)
- Identify geographic pockets of strong performance
- Use geographic data to target ads more effectively
- Example: 21% of conversions from London → run harder with London-focused campaigns
- Unexpected geographic performance = expansion opportunity

### Customer Surveys
- Survey new customers: "Where did you find us?"
- In lead capture forms or post-purchase surveys
- Google Analytics shows traffic source but not customer quality
- Surveys reveal where BEST customers come from, not just most traffic
- Combine survey data with analytics for complete picture

### Keyword Research for Audience Understanding
- What people search reveals what they care about
- Use keyword research to understand pain points and priorities
- High-CPC keywords indicate high commercial intent
- Keyword volume indicates topic popularity

## Building the Complete Competitor Picture
- Synthesize all data into a competitor profile
- Identify competitor strengths to avoid and weaknesses to exploit
- Prioritize channels where competitors are weakest
- Example synthesis: Aura Ring = paid social acquisition focused, weak on organic search, PR-driven link profile, generic social content = opportunity in search and native social

---

## Key Framework: The Traffic Lasso Workflow
1. **Locations** (this module) — Where do customers hang out? How do competitors reach them?
2. **Ad Channels** — Which paid channels match your audience locations?
3. **Search** — What keywords should you target?
4. **Social** — How to leverage organic social and community?
5. **Optimization** — How to improve what you've built?

## Why This Matters
- Most marketers skip this research and waste money on wrong channels
- Competitors' successes and failures show you the map
- You're not guessing; you're following data trails
- This research takes 2-4 hours per competitor but saves months of wasted spend
- Foundation for all subsequent traffic work is built here

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Trusting Semrush estimates as absolute truth (they're estimates)
- Ignoring organic traffic in favor of only paid
- Not analyzing geographic variation
- Assuming high traffic = quality customers (ignoring bounce rate, conversion rate)
- Looking at only one competitor (need 3-5 for pattern recognition)
- Skipping customer surveys (traffic source ≠ customer quality)
- Not synthesizing data into actionable insights
- Forgetting that competitor weakness is your opportunity
