# Link Building & Digital PR - Deep Question Bank (Generic)

## Introduction

This is a comprehensive question bank designed to help you think deeply about link building strategy for your specific business. These questions cover all strategies from the course: guest posting, content creation, boring business tactics, digital PR, unlinked mentions, and partner links.

**How to use this**: Pick 1-2 questions per week and write answers in detail. These questions are meant to be uncomfortable and require real thinking. Your answers will form the foundation of your link building strategy.

---

## Part A: Foundational Questions

### Your Business & Link Building Readiness

1. What is the primary business goal that link building should support? (Increased search visibility? Authority in your industry? Lead generation? Hiring? Brand awareness?) How will you measure success?

2. Who is your ideal customer/reader? Describe them in detail: age, role, company size, industry, pain points, goals. Where do they currently consume information?

3. What keywords do you want to rank for? List the top 10-15 keywords you care about. For each, what is the search intent (are people looking for information, or ready to buy)?

4. Who are your 3-5 top competitors? For each, what is their domain authority, estimated organic traffic, and backlink profile? Use Semrush or Ahrefs.

5. What is your current domain authority and backlink profile? How many backlinks do you have? From how many referring domains? What is your spam score?

6. Realistically, how much time and budget can you dedicate to link building? Hours per week? Outsourcing budget? This will determine which strategies to prioritize.

7. Do you have in-house writing capability, or will you need to hire writers? What is your budget for content creation?

8. What is your brand reputation currently? Is your industry perception positive, neutral, or are there reputation challenges? How will this affect outreach?

## Part B: Guest Posting Questions (Strategy 1)

### Ideation

9. Write down 10 topic ideas that would interest your target customer. For each, explain why this topic matters to them and what search volume might be (if searchable).

10. Use Answer The Public or BuzzSumo to research one of your target topics. What are the top 10 questions people ask about this? Which surprised you? Which do you have strong expertise to answer?

11. For your industry, what trends are emerging in the next 6 months? Can you piggyback on any broader trends (mental health, climate, remote work, etc.) and connect them to your niche?

12. If you wanted to write about [your industry] + [current major trend], what would the angle be? Why would a mainstream publication care about this story?

13. What data points or research do you have access to that could support a guest posting pitch? (Customer surveys, case studies, proprietary data, original research)

14. What are you known for as an expert? Write 3-5 expertise areas where you could position as THE authority on a topic. What makes you uniquely qualified in each?

15. What is a contrarian or surprising opinion you have about your industry? Could this be an angle for a guest post pitch? (Attention-grabbing, but not just clickbait)

16. For your target audience, what are the biggest mistakes people make? Could you write an article called "5 Mistakes [Audience] Make [Common Goal]"? What would the mistakes be?

### Finding Publications

17. Google "[your topic] blogs", "[your topic] news", "[your industry] publication", and "[your industry] magazine". Create a list of the top 20 publications that appear. For each, note domain authority (use Moz).

18. For your 3-5 top competitors, use Semrush to pull their backlink reports. Which publications have linked to them? Which publications link to multiple competitors? Add those to your target list.

19. Look at 5 top-ranking websites for your primary keyword. What publications have linked to them? Note these publications.

20. Review your target publication list. For each, answer: Is the audience my target customer? Is the publication mobile-friendly? Do they publish consistently? Do they accept guest posts? (Usually stated in their submission guidelines)

21. For your top 10 publications, analyze their existing backlink profiles using Semrush. How many do follow links do they have? This indicates their authority.

22. For your top 5 publications, read the last 2 months of articles. What topics repeat? What is the typical article length? What types of authors do they feature (practitioners, researchers, celebrities)?

23. Do any of your target publications have a guest posting submission policy? Find and read it. What are the requirements? How do they prefer to be contacted?

### Contacts & Pitching

24. For your top 10 publications, identify the editor(s) and 2-3 frequent contributors. What are their Twitter handles, LinkedIn profiles, emails? Create a contact list.

25. Read one article from each of your top 5 publications. Reference the author. If you were to pitch this person, what would you say about their recent work that would show you've read their stuff (not a mass email)?

26. Draft 5 different guest posting pitch emails, each targeting a different publication. Include: personalized opening, 3 topic ideas, your credentials, and a call to action.

27. What is your "author bio" that would appear on published articles? Write it (50-100 words). Include your expertise, current role, website, and social proof.

28. For your top 3 target publications, what is the timeline you'd expect? (Usually 2-4 weeks from pitch to decision, then 4-8 weeks to publication). Plan accordingly.

29. If 20 pitches = 1 published article (industry average), how many pitches do you need to send to achieve your link building goals? Break down: how many articles do you want published? This determines your pitch volume.

30. What follow-up approach will you take if you don't hear back? After how many days? How many follow-ups? Write your follow-up email template.

### Writing & Publishing

31. For one of your target publications, write the opening two paragraphs of a guest post. Does it match the publication's tone and style? Does it hook the reader immediately?

32. Create an outline for a 2,000-word article on one of your topics. Include: problem/hook, 4-5 main sections with subheadings, real examples or data, and conclusion.

33. What are 3-5 original insights or data points you could include in your guest posts? (Things competitors likely haven't published or discussed)

34. For your best guest post idea, identify 5-10 credible sources (research, studies, expert quotes) you'd cite in the article.

35. If you were to write a guest post, how would you naturally mention your company without it feeling like an advertisement? Write 1-2 sentences that feel native to an editorial piece.

36. What images, videos, or visual assets would enhance your guest posts? Do you have access to these, or would you need to create them?

### Post-Publication & Scaling

37. Once you publish a guest article, how will you promote it? To which audiences/channels? (Your email list, LinkedIn, Twitter, employees to share, etc.)

38. What is your plan for building relationships with editors you work with? How often will you stay in touch? What value will you provide to them?

39. How will you track the impact of your published articles? (Backlinks, organic traffic, lead generation, brand mentions) What is success?

40. If you publish 3-5 guest articles successfully, how will that credential help future outreach? (For next publication, for speaking gigs, for your own marketing?)

## Part C: Content Creation & Link Stealing Questions (Strategy 1B)

### Content Ideation

41. What are the top 20 keywords you want to rank for? For each, what is the current #1 ranking article/page? (Use Google, Ahrefs, or Semrush)

42. For your top 5 target keywords, write a hypothetical "authority" article outline (3,000-5,000 words) that would be SO good it becomes the definitive guide on that topic. What would it include?

43. For your industry, what are 5-10 fundamental "how-to" topics that people desperately need answered? (How to choose X, how to implement Y, how to measure Z)

44. What is one comprehensive topic that your industry/business understands that most people don't? Could you create an "authority" guide that becomes the go-to resource for this?

45. What are the most common questions your sales team gets asked? Could each of these be a 2,000+ word guide on your website?

46. If you created a "definitive guide" for your industry, what would the table of contents look like? (What would it cover?)

47. What original research could you conduct that would be linkable? (Customer survey, competitive analysis, industry analysis, data analysis)

48. Could you create an interactive tool (calculator, assessment, quiz, comparison tool) that would be valuable to your audience and worth linking to?

### Content Production

49. Outline one 3,000-word "authority" article you could write in the next month. What section would take most effort? How long would it take to research and write?

50. For one of your target articles, what visual assets would make it more linkable? (Infographics, charts, diagrams, original images)

51. If you had to rank 10 article ideas by "linkability" (likelihood to be linked to), what order would they be? Why are some more linkable than others?

52. What is your writing process? (Outline → draft → edit → fact-check → publish) How long does each step take for a 2,000-word article?

53. Do you have editorial standards (style guide, fact-checking process, source standards) for your content? If not, what should they be?

### Link Stealing

54. Pick one of your top target keywords. Research the current #1 ranking article. What are its weaknesses? (Outdated, shallow, missing examples, poor structure, no data)

55. For that same article, use Semrush to see its backlinks. How many do follow links does it have? From how many referring domains? Which are the highest authority?

56. Could you write a better version of that article? What would make yours 2-3x better? (More recent data, more examples, better structure, original research, interactive elements)

57. If you created a superior version of that article, which of its backlinks would you reach out to first? (Target the high-authority ones)

58. Write an outreach email pitching your improved version to a site that links to the inferior article. Use the template from the knowledge base.

59. Of the sites linking to that inferior article, which ones are in your ideal customer demographic? (These are the most valuable to convert)

60. How many hours per month could you dedicate to the "link stealing" process? (Research competitor content → create better content → outreach to existing linkers)

## Part D: Boring Business & Trade Publication Questions (Strategies 2-3)

### For "Boring" Businesses

61. Is your business in a "boring" industry? (B2B, industrial, SaaS, services) If so, list 5 reasons why. What competitive advantage does that give you if you do marketing well?

62. What are the top 10 trade publications in your industry? (Search "[industry] magazine", "[industry] journal", "[industry] trade publication")

63. For your top 3 trade publications, spend 1 week reading their recent content. What are the recurring topics? What is the typical article length? Who are the frequent contributors?

64. What unique expertise do you have that someone in your industry might not? (Specific knowledge, experience, data, perspective) How would that be valuable to trade publication readers?

65. If a trade publication editor asked "what can you write about that would be most valuable to our readers?", what would you say?

66. List 10 article ideas specifically for trade publications in your industry. For each, identify which publication it best fits.

67. What is the timeline for getting published in a trade publication vs. a mainstream publication? (Usually faster because smaller staff)

68. Do any trade publications in your industry have a physical print edition? How does that change strategy? (Print backlinks might be less valuable than online)

69. Are there any industry associations that publish journals or magazines? Are these good targets for guest posting?

70. What is the typical compensation for guest posts in trade publications? (Most don't pay, or pay very little. Is that acceptable to you?)

### Digital PR for Your Industry

71. What data do you collect that could be newsworthy? (Customer data, survey results, proprietary research, market analysis)

72. If you surveyed 500 customers/practitioners in your industry, what question would produce the most interesting/surprising results?

73. Is there a counterintuitive opinion you have about your industry? Could this be positioned as a "story" for journalists?

74. What is a Guinness World Record-level stunt that would be feasible for your business? (Not literally a record, but something attention-grabbing)

75. Could you partner with a research organization (university, think tank, non-profit) to produce original research that would be newsworthy?

76. List 10 journalists/bloggers who cover your industry. What have they written about recently? What angles interest them?

77. What is a trending topic in your industry RIGHT NOW that you could add a unique angle to?

78. If you pitched your business as a "story" to a journalist, what would the headline be?

79. What customer stories or case studies do you have that would be compelling to media? (Major transformation, surprising results, unique challenge)

80. What is your plan for distributing a digital PR story? Which journalists would you pitch first? Which publications would you hope to land in?

## Part E: Unlinked Mentions & Partner Links Questions (Strategies 4-5)

### Unlinked Brand Mentions

81. How often is your brand mentioned online currently? Set up Google Alerts for your brand name and track for 1 week. How many mentions per week do you get?

82. Of recent brand mentions, what percentage don't include links to your website? (Rough estimate)

83. Who is mentioning your brand the most? (Competitors, industry blogs, directories, review sites, social media?)

84. What is your template for reaching out to sites that mention you but don't link? Write it now.

85. Of all the places mentioning your brand, which would be most valuable to convert to links? (High authority, relevant audience)

86. Set up Semrush Brand Monitoring (or similar). Review "unlinked mentions" for the past month. How many are there? How many could realistically be converted to links?

87. If you could convert 50% of unlinked brand mentions to links, how many new backlinks would you gain per month?

88. What is your process for following up on unlinked mentions? How often will you check? Who will handle outreach?

89. Are there specific types of sites (directories, review platforms, industry databases) that mention you without linking? Could you do batch outreach to these?

90. How will you track conversion rate on unlinked mention outreach? (What percentage of requests result in added links?)

### Partner & Supplier Links

91. List all companies you have business relationships with: suppliers, partners, integrations, customers who resell, etc. (20+ names ideally)

92. For each of these companies, check their website. How many mention you? How many mention you with links?

93. Create a list of 10-15 partner relationships where you'd like a link but don't have one currently.

94. For each, what would be the most natural way to add a link? (Clients page, partners page, integrations page, logo link from their homepage, etc.)

95. Draft personalized outreach emails to 5 of these partners, asking for a link. Make each specific to their website and relationship.

96. Could you offer reciprocal links? (Link from your site to theirs if they link to you) Does this help your pitch?

97. For strategic partners, could you negotiate a link as part of a broader partnership or deal?

98. What is your timeline for reaching out to partners about links? (Batch approach: do 5-10 per week?)

99. How many links do you estimate you could gain from partner outreach in the next 3 months?

100. Could you create a "partners" or "integrations" page on your site that would incentivize partners to link to you in exchange for being featured?

## Part F: Testimonial Links & Scaling Questions (Strategy 6)

### Killer Testimonials

101. List all software, platforms, and service providers you use and genuinely love. (CRM, accounting software, design tool, marketing platform, etc.)

102. For each, assess: Does this company feature customer testimonials? Do they use them in marketing materials? Would a great testimonial be valuable to them?

103. Identify 5-10 vendors where you'd be happy to give a killer testimonial and where you'd want a reciprocal link.

104. For one vendor, write a testimonial that is specific, credible, and feature-worthy. (Not generic like "great tool". Include metrics, specific benefit, vs. the alternative)

105. When you give these testimonials, what is your ask? (Link in the testimonial? Link on their case study page? Link on their homepage? Multiple placements?)

106. What is the timeline for getting a testimonial published and seeing where it appears? (1-2 weeks to publish, then monitor for link inclusion)

107. Could you give testimonials to 10+ vendors in the next 3 months? What's the realistic number?

108. How many of these testimonial placements would include links? (Estimate: 50-70%)

109. For strategic vendors, could you do more than just a testimonial? (Case study, video testimonial, speaking engagement at their conference?)

110. Are there any vendor relationships where you could provide ongoing content (like being a regular guest contributor or advisor), which would lead to multiple link opportunities?

## Part G: Comprehensive Planning Questions

### Building Your Link Building Plan

111. Rank the 6 strategies by priority for your business. Which 2-3 should you start with? Why?

112. For your top 3 strategies, outline your 90-day plan: What will you execute? Who will own it? What resources do you need? What is the expected outcome?

113. Realistically, how many backlinks do you want to build in the next 12 months? (Estimate based on goals, not wishful thinking)

114. Which strategies have the highest ROI for your specific business? Why?

115. What are your biggest obstacles to executing link building? (Time, expertise, budget, writing capability, list of prospects) How will you solve each?

116. What is your link building budget for the next 12 months? How would you allocate it across strategies?

117. Who will own link building at your company? Is it one person, or distributed? What are their responsibilities?

118. What are the dependencies for your link building strategy? (Do you need to create content first? Identify partnerships first? Build your backlink database?)

119. When will you start? What is your launch date for link building efforts? What is the first action this week?

120. What are your link building metrics and KPIs? (Backlinks per month, referring domains, organic traffic growth, lead growth, keyword rankings) How will you track?

### Testing & Optimization

121. For each strategy, how will you test small-scale before going all-in? (Example: send 5 guest pitches before 50)

122. What is your benchmark for success on each strategy? (Example: guest posting = 20% pitch acceptance rate. If you get 5%, something is wrong.)

123. For underperforming strategies, how long will you give them before pivoting? (3 months? 6 months?)

124. Which strategies will you automate or outsource? Which will you do yourself?

125. How often will you review and adjust your link building strategy? (Monthly? Quarterly?)

## Part H: Industry & Competitive Questions

### Analyzing Your Competitive Landscape

126. For each of your top 3 competitors, what is their link building strategy? (Do they focus on guest posting? Content? Digital PR? What domains link to them?)

127. Where are your competitors getting their best quality links? (Use Semrush to reverse engineer)

128. Which publications link to competitors but NOT to you? Add these to your target list.

129. Which competitors have the most referring domains? What's the ratio of backlinks to referring domains? (This tells you if they're getting many links from few sources vs. diverse sources)

130. For your top 3 competitors, estimate their link building effort. Do they seem to be doing it actively, or did they get lucky?

131. Are there any link building tactics your competitors are using that you haven't considered?

132. Could you differentiate your link building strategy from competitors? (Different angle, different publications, different angle?)

133. What is the "link gap" between you and your closest competitor? How many backlinks behind are you? How many referring domains?

## Part I: Authority & Positioning Questions

### Building Your Expert Positioning

136. How would you explain your expertise in a single sentence to a journalist?

137. What credentials, experience, or data support your expertise?

138. Are there any certifications, awards, or recognition that would boost your credibility for link building?

139. What is a bold prediction you'd make about your industry in the next 2 years?

141. If you became the "go-to expert" for one specific topic in your industry, what would it be?

142. How will link building help establish you as an expert? (Different from just driving traffic)

## Part L: Content Types & Formats

### Exploring Different Content Formats

161. Beyond traditional blog posts, what other formats could you create that would be linkable?
    - Infographics
    - Research reports
    - Industry surveys
    - Video content
    - Podcasts
    - Interactive tools
    - Datasets
    - Frameworks or methodologies
    - Checklists or templates

162. For your audience, which formats would be most likely to be linked to? Why?

163. If you created an infographic on a key topic, what would it visualize?

164. Could you conduct original research (survey, study, data analysis) that would be linkable?

165. Could you create a framework or methodology (like a 5-step process) that others would reference and link to?

166. If you created a free tool or calculator for your audience, what would it help them do?

167. What is the ratio of content creation effort to linkability for different formats?

168. Which formats align with your strengths? (If you're a great writer, written content. If you're good with design, infographics, etc.)

## Part M: Outreach & Relationship Building

### Mastering Outreach

169. For your guest posting targets, how will you personalize each pitch to stand out?

170. What is your timeline from identifying a prospect to sending a pitch?

171. How will you handle rejection or non-responses? (What is your emotional approach?)

172. What is your follow-up cadence? (After how many days? How many follow-ups?)

173. Will you send mass outreach emails or highly personalized ones? (Answer: highly personalized is better, but takes more time)

174. How will you track outreach? (Spreadsheet, CRM, other?)

175. For follow-ups, what is your script/template?

176. How will you qualify prospects before outreach? (Not every publication is worth your time)

177. What is your ideal response time from editors? (Is 2 weeks a success or a failure?)

178. How will you negotiate if an editor says "we'd publish this, but we don't have link placement for guest authors"?

## Part N: Measurement & Analytics

### Tracking Results

179. What is your current organic traffic baseline? (Monthly visits from search)

180. For each backlink you build, what is your estimated impact on organic traffic?

181. What keywords do you expect to improve rankings on in 6 months? 12 months?

182. How will you track backlink growth? (Semrush, Ahrefs, monthly reports?)

183. For each strategy, what is your target number of backlinks in 6 months and 12 months?

184. How will you measure the quality of backlinks you build? (Domain authority? Referral traffic? Ranking impact?)

185. What is the lead value of organic traffic? (How many leads does 100 visits generate? What is the average deal size?)

186. For content you create, how will you measure success? (Traffic? Shares? Links? Leads?)

187. Are there leading indicators you can track monthly? (Pitches sent, published articles, links acquired?) vs. lagging indicators like traffic and rankings?

188. What dashboard will you create to track progress?

189. How often will you review metrics? (Weekly? Monthly? Quarterly?)

190. What is "success" for your link building effort? (Define quantitatively)

## Summary: Next Steps

After answering these questions, you should have:
- A clear understanding of your business, industry, and competitive landscape
- A specific link building strategy with prioritized tactics
- A 90-day action plan with clear ownership and metrics
- A realistic understanding of timeline and resources required
- A measurement system to track progress

Start with questions 1-10 this week. They form the foundation for everything else.
