## 0. The direction in one statement (so-what first)

> **Ritsu's strategic direction is *concentrated market penetration of one deliberately-narrow Blue-Ocean position* — the active-mastery × course-structured-path × your-own-material white space — entered through the US-led deadline-bearing committed STEM/ML masterer, and won on the un-substitutable *system* (the multi-week PATH + 17 activities + concept-level mastery map + named, cited learning-science) that the free rivals structurally lack. We do *deliberately less* on the commoditized doc→quiz spine (Eliminate/Reduce) to fund the value dimensions no rival owns (Raise/Create). We prove the position with N=10 before we scale it.**

The four frameworks all point the same way, and that convergence *is* the confidence:

- **Ansoff** says we are in **Market Penetration** (existing product, existing segment) — the *lowest-risk* quadrant — and that is correct for a true-zero company: the job is to capture the wedge we already serve, not to take on new-product or new-market risk yet.
- **Blue Ocean** says the penetration target is a **genuinely uncontested position** (the active+pathed+tracked corner of the strategy canvas) reachable only by *reconstructing the value-cost frontier* (ERRC), not by out-featuring rivals in the red ocean.
- **STP** says the segment is *locked and narrow* (the deadline-bearing masterer), the targeting is *concentrated/niche-first*, and the position is the learning-science-grounded active-mastery system.
- **Entering-a-new-market / introducing-a-new-product** says the 60-day wedge is *neither* — it is penetration of an existing position; the new-market and new-product moves are the **expansion ladder**, sequenced *after* PMF, with risk rising diagonally.

This doc is the proof of that one statement.

## 1. Ansoff Matrix — the growth vector (which quadrant, and the expansion ladder)

> Framework: `ansoff-matrix.md`. Two axes — Product (existing→new) × Market (existing→new) — with **risk rising on the diagonal** (Penetration lowest, Diversification highest). The framework's #1 pitfall is *misclassifying* a move (calling diversification 'penetration', or calling a premium tier 'product development'). This section places Ritsu's revenue base **now** and stages the ladder **deliberately**.

### 1.1 — Where Ritsu is NOW: Market Penetration (and nothing else)

```

              existing                    new
          ┌───────────────────────┬───────────────────────┐

          │   (lowest risk)       │   (medium risk)        │

          ├───────────────────────┼───────────────────────┤
      new │  PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT   │    DIVERSIFICATION      │
          │  (medium risk)        │    (highest risk)       │
```

**The classification, made explicit (so we don't fool ourselves):**

- **100% of the current plan is Market Penetration.** The product is *built and live* (`product.md` §6 — 17+ activities, 7 modes, Knowledge Map, mastery-tracking, all on ritsu.ai `[OBSERVED]` feature surface); the segment is *chosen* (`icp-summary.md` §0 — the deadline-bearing STEM/ML masterer). The 60-day plan = *sell more of the existing product to more of the existing segment.* That is the lowest-risk quadrant, and it is the **only** quadrant a true-zero company should be in. The whole job right now is **share of one segment**, not breadth.
- **The Free→Plus→Pro→Ultra tiering is Penetration, NOT Product Development.** Per the framework's exact pitfall ('a premium tier of the same software is Market Penetration, not Product Development'): the paid tiers gate *volume + depth of the same product* for the *same buyer* — they deepen penetration, they do not open a new quadrant. `[INFERRED]`

### 1.2 — The expansion ladder (risk rising diagonally — sequenced, not simultaneous)

The framework's discipline is to **fund each quadrant from the previous** (the Amazon sequence: penetrate → develop markets → develop products → diversify). Ritsu's ladder, with the **gate** on each rung:

| Rung | Ansoff quadrant | Ritsu move | Risk | Gate (do NOT start before) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 (now)** | **Market Penetration** | Capture the US STEM/ML masterer wedge with the built product | **lowest** | — (this is the current plan) |
| **1** | **Market Development** | Same product → adjacent *jobs/subjects* (medicine/pre-med — `icp §4b` CORE, **proven WTP next door**; then CFA/law STRONG) and the **English intermarket** (UK/CA/AU/IN — same psychographic, `positioning.md` §5) | medium | wedge PMF: **≥100 paying retained 30d + activation ≥40%** (`north-star.md`) |
| **2** | **Product Development** | New activity types / deeper Knowledge Map / new tutoring modes for the *same* wedge | medium | a *validated* feature gap from SOP-PRODUCT-002 N=10 observation (don't build ahead of demand) |
| **3** | **Diversification** | **Educator B2B** then **API platform** — new product *and* new customer (`charter.md` §1/§3) | **highest** | the B2C wedge is a *proven, retained* business (charter.md: 'activates once the consumer wedge is proven') |

**So-what:** Ansoff hands the direction one instruction and one warning. The instruction: **stay in Penetration; do not diagonal-jump.** The warning (the medicine/pre-med adjacency, `icp-summary.md` §4b, is the tempting exception): med is a *Market Development* move (same product, new sub-segment) and is **risk-justified only as a parallel N=10 co-beachhead** — it de-risks R1 because med WTP is *already proven* ($300–500 on UWorld/Anki-add-ons/Sketchy `[INFERRED]`), but it is still a deliberate rung-1 move, not a free lunch, and it carries med's own unforgiving-accuracy cost. The diversification rungs (educator B2B, API) are the `[HYPOTHESIS]` $1B-shape ladder (`charter.md` §1) — **named so they are not pursued early**, exactly the Ansoff anti-pattern.

## 2. Blue Ocean — the ERRC value-cost reconstruction + Strategy Canvas

> Framework: `blue-ocean-strategy.md`. The Four Actions (Eliminate / Reduce / Raise / Create) + the Strategy Canvas. The framework's hard rule: **a real blue-ocean grid has at least one Eliminate AND one Create** — otherwise it is just incremental red-ocean improvement. This is the move the worked Ritsu example in the Domont `process.md` (§ 'Worked End-to-End Example') sketched; here it is landed as the canonical artifact.

### 2.1 — The ERRC grid for Ritsu

The grid is built directly from the diagnosis: the competitive frame is `positioning.md` §5 ('learning-science-grounded AI mastery', deliberately *exiting* the commodity 'AI study tool' category), and the cell-by-cell logic is the §3 NotebookLM teardown in `market-and-competitive-landscape.md` (what is commoditized vs what is a real moat).

| Action | Factor | Why (the value-cost logic) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ELIMINATE** | The **generic AI-chat / open-ended-Q&A format** | It is ChatGPT's commodity zone (perceptual map bottom-left) — high token cost, no structured-mastery value, and it plots Ritsu on top of a free incumbent. *Ritsu is not a chatbot you paste into* (`product.md` §5). Eliminating it removes cost AND sharpens the position. | `positioning.md` §9; `product.md` §5 |
| **REDUCE** | **Flashcard / doc→quiz commoditization** (competing on "we also make quizzes/flashcards") | The free shadow rival (NotebookLM) owns this spine for $0 (`market-and-competitive-landscape.md` §3.2 — 'commoditized, do NOT lead on this'). Quizzes/flashcards stay as *table-stakes inputs* to the path, but Ritsu *reduces* its competitive emphasis on them below the industry norm — it refuses to fight the red-ocean quiz war. | `icp-summary.md` §8 R2; `market-and-competitive-landscape.md` §3.2 |
| **RAISE** | **Learning-science rigor + transparent, page/timestamp citations** (above every rival) | This is the **preemptive POD** (`positioning.md` §6) — authority compounds, features don't; ChatGPT copies a feature in a week, not months of cited-method authority. Raised *above industry standard* because it is the one cell a free, big-tech rival has no incentive to build. Doubles as the accuracy POP defense (the #1 SERVQUAL bar). | `positioning.md` §6; `product.md` §6.7 |
| **CREATE** | **(a) The <60-second magic moment** (upload → structured plan → first activity) AND **(b) the ordered, difficulty-graded, tracked MULTI-WEEK PATH across the user's own course material** | Two value dimensions the category has never offered as a *system*: the <60s activation event (`product.md` §7) and — *the load-bearing creation* — the **per-course, multi-week, tracked mastery path** (NotebookLM is per-*document* and un-pathed; Coursera is pre-built and passive). This is the uncontested white space. | `market-and-competitive-landscape.md` §3.2/§4; `product.md` §6.4/§7 |

**Grid validity check (the framework's gate):** ≥1 Eliminate (generic AI-chat) ✅ and ≥1 Create (the magic moment + the multi-week PATH) ✅ — so this is a *strategic* reconstruction of the value-cost frontier, not a feature list. The Eliminate/Reduce pair *funds* the Raise/Create pair: by refusing to compete on chat and quiz commodity, Ritsu spends its scarce solo+AI capacity on the path + authority no one else owns.

### 2.3 — The non-customer pool (the framework's demand step)

Blue Ocean demands you name the **non-customers** the new curve unlocks. Ritsu's largest tier (per the framework's 'three tiers of non-customers'): the **deadline-bearing masterer who has rejected the existing tools** — uses free NotebookLM/ChatGPT but finds them un-pathed and un-tracked for a *multi-week course*; tried Anki but quit on the manual-card overhead; can't afford a $30–80/hr tutor (`icp-summary.md` §5). They are *studying anyway*, *paying for nothing that fits*, and sit **exactly** in the per-course-path gap NotebookLM serves worst. **The validity caveat (the framework's #1 failure mode — 'most blue-ocean failures are demand assumptions never validated'):** this non-customer demand is **R1, unproven at true-zero** — the new value curve must be tested with these non-customers (the N=10 watch, SOP-PRODUCT-002) *before* capital scales. The canvas shows *where* the uncontested space is; it does **not** prove the demand is there.

**So-what:** Blue Ocean converts the position into an explicit value-cost choice: **win by doing less on the commodity spine and creating the per-course mastery path + authority no rival owns** — and validate that white space is *demand*, not just empty, with N=10.

## 3. STP — the locked direction (Segment → Target → Position)

> Framework: `stp-model.md` (Kotler). ### 3.1 — Segment (behavioral/psychographic, MASDA-tested)

The market is segmented by the **JOB and the psychographic**, not demographics (the framework's #1 pitfall, *already avoided* in `icp-summary.md` §4b): the job = *master dense, graded, multi-week material before a high-stakes deadline via active practice*; the psychographic = the learner who believes *"I'm not smart enough"* and is wrong (the method is the problem). Fit-weighted tiers (`icp-summary.md` §4b): **CORE** = ML/AI (launch beachhead) + hard-STEM + medicine/pre-med (90–95); **STRONG** = CFA/law (82–84); **MODERATE** = quant-social-science/grad-quals (76–78); **WEAK/out** = essay-humanities (~55). MASDA passes on M/A/S/A; **D (Differentiable — do US learners respond like the model?) is the #1 open risk** (`positioning.md` §5, `icp-summary.md` §3), to validate in Phase A. `[HYPOTHESIS]` on D.

### 3.2 — Target (concentrated / niche-first)

Targeting strategy = **concentrated** (the framework's 'focus beats breadth in early-stage'; `positioning.md` §5 'large share of one segment, not a small share of everyone'). The **single target for the first 100 paying** = the **US-led, English-speaking, deadline-bearing committed STEM/ML masterer** (`icp-summary.md` §0 wedge). The *paying core* (recurring graded deadline = durable WTP) is targeted; the open-courseware reach-layer (soft WTP) is the funnel top, not the target; the English intermarket + adjacent jobs are the **expansion frame** (Ansoff rung-1, §1.2). **Anti-targets** (deliberate exclusion — `icp-summary.md` §7): enterprises, K-12, edutainment/streak-seekers, casual viewers, hobby learners, under-13.

### 3.3 — Position (the Moore-format statement, benefit-led)

The canonical positioning statement (`positioning.md` §0), in the framework's Moore template, benefit-led (the framework's pitfall 'features-not-benefits' *already avoided*):

> **For** the US-led English-speaking deadline-bearing committed STEM/ML masterer **who** must *truly master* dense, graded, multi-week material — not merely pass — **Ritsu is** the learning-science-grounded AI study companion **that** turns any source into an active-learning loop + a tracked multi-week mastery path in 30 seconds. **Unlike** free NotebookLM / ChatGPT (un-pathed, per-document) or Quizlet/Anki (flashcards only), **Ritsu** is the per-course mastery *system* — the ordered path + 17 activity types + concept-level mastery map + named, cited learning-science — built on the belief that *AI can't learn for you, but the right system makes you learn 10×.*

This statement passes the **brand-substitution test** (`positioning.md` §3 — swap in 'Quizlet'/'ChatGPT' and it breaks) and is the contract every touchpoint serves. **It is locked; this doc does not re-open it.**

**So-what:** STP gives the engines one inheritable line: *one segment (the deadline-bearing masterer), one concentrated target (US-led, paying-core), one benefit-led position (the per-course mastery system, learning-science-grounded).* Validation owed: MASDA-D + the position's resonance, via Phase-A interviews + the message-test.

## 4. Entering-a-new-market & Introducing-a-new-product — framing the 60-day wedge

> The Domont Step-1.7 names two situational lenses — *entering a new market* and *introducing a new product*. The disciplined answer for the 60-day wedge is that **it is neither** — and getting that classification right is the whole point (the Ansoff anti-pattern, §1). This section states the framing precisely so the backlog does not mis-scope the launch.

### 4.1 — The 60-day wedge is Market PENETRATION, not new-market entry, not new-product introduction

| Lens | Does it apply to the 60-day wedge? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Entering a new market** (existing product → new segment/geo) | **NO — not yet** | The US STEM/ML masterer is the *chosen, existing* target segment (`icp-summary.md` §0), and the product already serves it. New-market entry = the Ansoff **rung-1** moves (med/pre-med adjacent, the English intermarket) — sequenced *after* wedge PMF, not part of the 60-day plan. |
| **Introducing a new product** (new product → existing/new customer) | **NO** | The product is *built and live* (`product.md` §6, `[OBSERVED]` on ritsu.ai). There is no new product to introduce — there is an *existing* product to penetrate a segment with. New-product introduction = the Ansoff **rung-2/3** moves (new activity types; educator B2B; API). |
| **Market Penetration** (existing product → existing segment) | **YES — this is the 60-day plan** | Capture share of the already-chosen wedge with the already-built product. Lowest risk. The *only* correct framing for true-zero. |

### 4.2 — The two situational lenses *do* apply — to the expansion ladder (so they're staged, not skipped)

The lenses are not irrelevant; they are **future-dated**, and naming them now keeps them from being pursued early:

- **Entering-a-new-market discipline** applies to **Ansoff rung-1** (med/pre-med + English intermarket). When that rung opens (post-PMF gate), the new-market checklist is: re-run MASDA for the new sub-segment; re-validate the position resonates (med's accuracy bar is unforgiving — `icp-summary.md` §4b); confirm the channels reach them (med = r/medicalschool, Anki-native communities). The med co-beachhead N=10 is the *one* new-market probe allowed *in parallel* now — because it de-risks R1, not because it widens scope.
- **Introducing-a-new-product discipline** applies to **Ansoff rung-2/3** (new activities; educator B2B; API). When those open, the new-product checklist is: SOP-PRODUCT-002 N=10 observation *before* the build (`product.md` §12 — the PG gate); validate the new product serves the *same* belief; price it against its *own* job. The diversification rungs (educator B2B, API) additionally require the B2C wedge to be a *proven, retained* business first (`charter.md` §3).

**So-what:** the 60-day plan is **penetration of an existing position with an existing product** — the lowest-risk Ansoff quadrant — and the new-market / new-product moves are explicitly the *next* rungs, gated and sequenced. Mis-labeling the wedge as 'entering a new market' or 'launching a new product' would import risk the company should not carry at true-zero; this section forecloses that error.

## 5. Where we compete + why we win (the explicit statement the backlog inherits)

The four frameworks converge on a single, falsifiable competitive thesis. This is the sentence the 4 acquisition engines, the SOPs, and the 30-artifact backlog inherit **without re-deriving** — the strategic-direction contract.

### 5.1 — WHERE we compete

**In the uncontested Blue-Ocean position** at the intersection of three value dimensions — **active mastery × course-structured multi-week path × the user's own material.** (The §4 perceptual map renders the first two as its two axes — *passive↔active* and *generic↔course-pathed* — and names *your-own-material* as the third white-space property in §4.3; together they bound the corner no rival occupies.) Bounded by: the **frame** = 'learning-science-grounded AI mastery' (deliberately *exiting* the commodity 'AI study tool' red ocean, `positioning.md` §5); the **segment** = the US-led deadline-bearing committed STEM/ML masterer (Ansoff Penetration, STP concentrated target); the **non-customers we unlock** = deadline-bearers who study with free tools that don't fit a multi-week *course* (§2.3). We do **not** compete in the active-but-generic zone (where free NotebookLM dominates) or the passive-generic zone (ChatGPT's commodity) — drifting there plots us on top of a free rival and we lose.

### 5.2 — WHY we win (if we win)

**On the un-substitutable *system* + the switching-cost + authority moat that the free rivals structurally lack** (Porter's forced response to high Buyer-Power + high Substitutes, `market-and-competitive-landscape.md` §5):

1. **The system, not the feature** (Blue Ocean Create/Raise + ST-1): the ordered multi-week PATH + 17 activity types + concept-level mastery map + named cited learning-science. Free NotebookLM has the *spine* (quiz/flashcard/grounded-answer); it lacks the *system*. We compete **only** on the system — never 'we also make quizzes.'
2. **Switching costs** (Porter response to Buyer Power + ST-2): accumulated cross-session mastery state + the growing Knowledge Map + the user's generated activity library = *sunk progress* — the only defense against a buyer who can always walk to free.
3. **Compounding authority** (the preemptive POD + Blue Ocean Raise + SO-3): months of learning-science + citation discipline that a free big-tech rival has no incentive to build. Authority compounds; features get copied in weeks.

### 5.3 — WHY we might lose (the gates, not afterthoughts)

The direction is **prove-then-scale**, because the two load-bearing joints are unproven (`market-and-competitive-landscape.md` §7; `icp-summary.md` §8):

- **R1 — WTP unproven.** At true-zero (0 paying strangers `[OBSERVED]`), the entire revenue thesis is a `[HYPOTHESIS]`. The Blue-Ocean white space is only valuable if it is *demand*. **Gate: N=10 US-stranger activation ≥40% + pay-at-first-limit observed (SOP-PRODUCT-002)** before acquisition spend scales.
- **R2 — free NotebookLM.** The strongest rival is free, Google-grade, and closing the gap (April-2026 mastery-tracking + Socratic guide) — it commoditized Ritsu's *original* product. **Gate: the message-test** — does the PATH/mastery/learning-science pitch convert *against* a NotebookLM-free comparison? ## 2.4 — Blue Ocean strategic sequence — the adoption-hurdle gate (the leg ERRC stops short of)
