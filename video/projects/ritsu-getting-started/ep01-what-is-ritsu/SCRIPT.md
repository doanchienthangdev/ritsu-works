## The spine of this episode

**Everything you want to learn is currently outside you. Learning is the move
from outside to inside. That move is called mastery, and it is the only hard part.**

This is not a new position — it is the live tagline made explicit:
**"Turn *Raw Materials* Into *True Mastery*."** Raw materials are outside. Mastery is
inside. Draft 1 opened on a tactical pain (*"you read it twice and can't explain it"*)
and left the brand's actual claim on the table.

Three consequences for the edit:

- **`OUTSIDE` / `INSIDE` is a recurring visual**, not a one-off line. It returns at the
  commands beat and again at the score. Same two words, same two positions on screen.
- **The VO must use the same two words the screen uses.** Not "out there / in here",
  not "external / internal" — **outside** and **inside**, no articles, matching the
  labels exactly. This is the vocabulary Ep 5, Ep 10 and Ep 11 all reuse; if Ep 1 installs
  a synonym, every callback afterwards costs the viewer a small translation. The warm,
  bodily version of the idea is carried one line earlier by *"inside your head"* — which
  frees this line to do the naming.
- **The abundance of material — including AI-generated material — is framed as the
  problem, not the promise.** Everyone else sells *more content, faster*. We say more
  outside content was never the win.
- **The study stack is reframed.** Not "we replace five apps" (a feature list) but
  "those five apps were you hand-building a bridge from outside to inside, at midnight,
  while also trying to learn." Ritsu *is* the bridge.

**The line we must not cross:** this cannot read as anti-AI, from an AI product. The
division of labour is explicit — *AI multiplies what's outside; only you can build
what's inside; Ritsu uses AI to run the conversion.* Coherent with `positioning.md` §1,
*"AI can't learn for you."*

### The four verbs are the series' table of contents

The definition in shot 3b — *"so you understand it deeply, can practise it, keep hold
of it, and use it in the right situation"* — is not decoration. Each verb is an episode:

| Verb | Where it gets delivered | Product surface |
|---|---|---|
| **UNDERSTAND** | Ep 4, Ep 6 | `Learning` commands (22) — `/why` `/eli5` `/derive` `/intuition` |
| **PRACTISE** | Ep 6, Ep 7 | `Assessment` commands (9) — `/quiz` `/exercise` `/solve` |
| **RETAIN** | Ep 8 | `Review` — per-unit spaced scheduling |
| **APPLY** | Ep 7 | `/adaptive` with **Bloom** set to `Apply` / `Analyze` / `Evaluate` / `Create` |

Ep 11 closes the series by calling all four back. Set them up properly here and that
close costs eight seconds and lands hard. The four words appear on screen in shot 3b
and **do not appear again until Ep 11** — that is what makes the callback work.

## Read-through (the clean script, no production notes)

> Use this block for the VO recording. Every UI string in it was verified on the live
> app 2026-07-30.

Everything you're trying to learn is outside you right now.

The chapter. The lecture you sat through. The paper you saved and never opened. It's
all out there — on a shelf, in a tab, in a folder called "read later."

And there has never been more of it. Textbooks, courses, videos. And now AI writes
more in a second than you could read in a year.

But none of that is learning. That's supply.

And reading it isn't learning either. That's consuming.

Real learning is turning raw material into structured knowledge inside your head — so
you understand it deeply, can practise it, keep hold of it, and use it in the right
situation. So you own it.

That's the move from outside to inside. And it has a name. **Mastery.**

And it's the hardest thing you will ever do with a document, because nothing does it
for you.

You've read the chapter twice. You still couldn't explain it to anyone. That's not a
memory problem. That's the gap. Nothing carried it across.

So people build the bridge by hand. Highlight in one app. Flashcards in a second.
Paste it into a chatbot. Notes in a third. Quiz yourself, explain it back, work the
problems — all of it correct, all of it known for decades, and all of it more work
than the studying itself.

Which is why almost nobody finishes it.

Ritsu is that bridge. One tool, doing the one job: taking what's outside, and putting
it inside you.

Watch. One chapter, one sentence about what I want, and thirty seconds.

I'm not going to speed this up. This is real time.

There it is. A plan, built from my document — three units, about two hours, pitched at
my level, aimed at the thing I actually said I wanted: to learn this fast, for an exam.

But a plan is still outside. It's a better-organised outside.

This is where the crossing happens.

A chat window answers you. Ritsu makes you work — and the work is the only thing that
ever moves an idea from the page into you.

Every one of these is an activity you *do*. Not a paragraph you read.

Forty-two of them, and they're not decoration — `/eli5` is the Feynman Technique.
`/adaptive` runs on Bloom's taxonomy. `/counter-example` asks you where the idea stops
being true.

And it keeps score of what made it across. Not how long you sat there. Not how much you
opened.

Thirty ideas that used to be outside. They aren't anymore.

That's Ritsu. Your material, converted into the thing you own.

Over the next ten videos we'll build this together. By the end you'll have a study
plan, a map of what's actually in your head, a review schedule that defends it, and a
certificate that only unlocks once you genuinely have it.

There will always be more to read than you can read. That was true before AI, and it's
absurd now. The scarce thing was never the material.

It's the part where it becomes yours.

Next video: your first session, start to finish. Bring a PDF.

## Notes to the editor

**Shots 1–3b are the episode.** If the viewer only remembers one image it must be the
full left side and the empty right side. Do not rush shot 3 — the beat of silence on
the empty `INSIDE` is doing more work than any line of copy.

**Shots 3 and 3a kill two different illusions, in the order people hold them.**
*"That's supply"* answers *"what is all that material, if it isn't learning?"* —
it names the **pile**. *"That's consuming"* answers the objection the viewer is already
making in their head — ***"but I do read it"*** — and names the **behaviour**. The second
is the more painful one and the more common one, which is why it comes second and gets
its own image. Shot 3a's document must visibly travel and visibly **fail to cross**:
motion without transfer is the entire idea in one loop.

`supply` also earns its keep at the other end of the episode — it is the setup for the
closing line *"the scarce thing was never the material."* Supply ↔ scarce is a
deliberate pair. Do not replace `supply` with `consuming`; the script needs both words
doing different jobs.

**Shot 2 must not look aspirational.** The instinct will be to make the multiplying
documents look impressive — glossy, fast, exciting. That inverts the meaning. It should
feel like a rising tide. Desaturate as it multiplies.

**The `OUTSIDE`/`INSIDE` split appears exactly three times** — shot 3 (empty), shot 11
(plan is still on the left), shot 14 (30 items finally cross). Three, not four. A fourth
turns a device into a tic.

**Shot 11 is new and is the most important addition in this draft.** Conceding that
*even Ritsu's own plan is still outside* is the line that buys credibility for
everything after it. It is also true. Do not cut it for runtime.

**Shot 9 — music out.** Silence over a real loading bar reads as confidence. Music over
it reads as an ad.

**Shot 13 is where we could lose the ICP.** A rigor-respecting learner reads a big count
as feature-bloat. The number gets one second; the *evidence* gets twelve.

**Do not say "seventeen activities" or "seven modes".** Both are stale marketing copy
and neither survives contact with the app. See `02-script-ideas.md` §3.

**Music:** bed enters on shot 2 and rises with the pile. **Full stop on shot 3** — the
empty INSIDE is silent. Re-enters low under the avatar. Out entirely for shot 9.
Returns on the plan reveal. Resolves on shot 14 as the items cross.

**Runtime.** This drafts at ~3:10 against a 2:45 target. Recover 25s from: shot 6 (the
five planks can be four, −4s), shot 10 (chips can light in pairs, −4s), shot 13 (drop
`/counter-example` from the spoken list, keep it on screen, −5s), shot 15 (the four-item
recap can become three, −6s), shot 2 (−6s). **Never cut shots 1, 3, 9, or 11.**

## What changed from draft 1, and why

| Draft 1 | Draft 2 | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Opened on *"you read it twice and can't explain it"* | Opens on **everything you want to learn is outside you** | Draft 1's hook was a tactical pain any study app could claim. The new one states the actual job and is the live tagline made explicit |
| No mention of content abundance | **Shot 2** — more material than ever, and AI writes more every second | Reframes the competitive story: everyone sells *more content faster*; we say more outside content was never the win. Hard to copy without contradicting themselves |
| 16s on "active recall / spaced repetition / Feynman are decades old, but slow" | Compressed to ~7s, folded into the hand-built-bridge beat | The new frame already does that work. Keeps the added framing nearly runtime-neutral |
| Study stack implied as a feature comparison | **Shot 6** — five planks of a bridge you're building yourself at midnight | Stops it being a feature list; makes the replacement claim mean something |
| Plan reveal → straight to commands | **New shot 11** — *"a plan is still outside"* | The strongest credibility beat in the episode: we disqualify our own output first |
| `≈ 30 concepts mastered` as a stat | Same number, now **30 items crossing the line** | Gives the metric a meaning the viewer has been holding for two minutes |
| Closed on *"turned into the work that makes it stick"* | Closes on **"the scarce thing was never the material — it's the part where it becomes yours"** | Lands the thesis instead of restating the mechanism |
