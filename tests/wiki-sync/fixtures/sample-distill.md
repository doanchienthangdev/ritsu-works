## On the concept of compound interest

**Compound interest** is the accumulation of interest on both the original principal and the previously accrued interest. It is the mechanism by which small recurring gains grow exponentially over long time horizons. Albert Einstein is widely (and probably apocryphally) quoted as calling it "the eighth wonder of the world." The mathematical formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) where P is principal, r is rate, n is compounding periods per year, and t is years.

## On dollar-cost averaging

**Dollar-cost averaging** is the investment strategy of buying a fixed dollar amount of an asset at regular intervals regardless of price. It removes the need to time the market and reduces the impact of volatility on the average purchase price. Over a long enough horizon, dollar-cost averaging tends to outperform attempts at market timing for non-professional investors.

## On the time value of money

**Time value of money** is the concept that a dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future because today's dollar can earn interest. This is the foundation of present-value and future-value calculations in finance.

## Observations from historical market data

Vanguard's research across multiple decades found that **investors who held diversified index funds through full market cycles outperformed 87% of actively-managed mutual funds over 15-year periods**, after fees. This is one of the most replicated findings in personal finance research.

Studies of retail investor behavior consistently show that **the average individual stock investor underperforms the S&P 500 by approximately 3-5 percentage points per year over multi-decade horizons** due to a combination of trading costs, poor market timing, and behavioral biases (selling at lows, buying at highs).

## A decision-grade framework

Given the above evidence, **a reasonable default investment strategy for a non-professional investor is: low-cost broad-market index funds, held for decades, with dollar-cost averaging via automated monthly contributions, and minimal trading activity**. This is not financial advice; it is a synthesis of the observations above into an actionable default that the evidence supports.
